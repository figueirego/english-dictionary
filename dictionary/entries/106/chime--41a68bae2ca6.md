## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /tʃaɪm/ — source labels: UK

### Forms

- chimes — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A musical instrument producing a sound when struck, similar to a bell (e.g. a tubular metal bar) or actually a bell. Often used in the plural to refer to the set&#58; the chimes.

   **Example:** Hugo had a recording of someone playing the chimes against a background of surf noise that she found calming.

   **Example:** Sylvia was a chime player in the school orchestra.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; music

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An individual ringing component of such a set.

   **Example:** Peter removed the C♯ chime from its mounting so that he could get at the dust that had accumulated underneath.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A small bell or other ringing or tone-making device as a component of some other device.

   **Example:** The professor had stuffed a wad of gum into the chime of his doorbell so that he wouldn't be bothered.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The sound of such an instrument or device.

   **Example:** The copier gave a chime to indicate that it had finished printing.

   **Example:** Chimes sing Sunday morn.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A small hammer or other device used to strike a bell.

   **Example:** Strike the bell with the brass chime hanging on the chain next to it.

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /tʃaɪm/ — source labels: UK

### Forms

- chimes — present; singular; third-person
- chiming — participle; present
- chimed — participle; past
- chimed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make the sound of a chime.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The microwave chimed to indicate that it was done cooking.

   **Example:** I got up for lunch as soon as the wall clock began chiming noon.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause to sound in harmony; to play a tune, as upon a set of bells; to move or strike in harmony.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To utter harmoniously; to recite rhythmically.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To agree; to correspond.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The other lab's results chimed with mine, so I knew we were on the right track with the research.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To make a rude correspondence of sounds; to jingle, as in rhyming.
