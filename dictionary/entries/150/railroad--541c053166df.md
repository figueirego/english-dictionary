## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈɹeɪlɹoʊd/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˈɹeɪɹoʊd/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˈɹeɪlɹəʊd/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- railroads — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A permanent track consisting of fixed metal rails to drive trains or similar motorized vehicles on.

   **Usage:** Philippines; US

   **Example:** Many railroads roughly follow the trace of older land or water roads.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The transportation system comprising such tracks and vehicles fitted to travel on the rails, usually with several vehicles connected together in a train.

   **Usage:** US

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A single, privately or publicly owned property comprising one or more such tracks and usually associated assets

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** Railroads can only compete fully if their tracks are technically compatible with and linked to each other.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A procedure conducted in haste without due consideration.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** The lawyers made the procedure a railroad to get the signatures they needed.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** railway (Britain; Commonwealth; Ireland); **Source context (verbatim):** of Nations

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To transport via railroad.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To operate a railroad.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The Thatcherite experiment proved the private sector can railroad as inefficiently as a state monopoly.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To work for a railroad.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** She used to railroad for the Southern Pacific.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To travel by railroad.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To engage in a hobby pertaining to railroads.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To manipulate and hasten a procedure, as of formal approval of a law or resolution.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The majority railroaded the bill through parliament, without the customary expert studies which would delay it till after the elections.

   **Usage:** transitive
