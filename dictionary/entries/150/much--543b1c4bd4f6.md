### Forms

- more — comparative
- most — superlative

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To a great extent.

   **Example:** I don't like melon much, and I don’t much care for milk chocolate either&#58; I find them both much too sweet.

   **Example:** However much I eat, I never get fat.

   **Example:** He left her, much to the satisfaction of her other suitor.

   **Example:** That boyfriend of yours is much like the others.

   **Example:** My English was much the worst, and I'm certainly not much good at math either, of that I'm very much aware.

   **Example:** Both candidates, who are much of an age, say much the same thing, but the youngest shows much the commoner behavior of the two.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To a great extent.
   1. for emphasis or to indicate a great difference

      **Example:** He is much fatter than I remember him.

      **Example:** Honestly, I can't stand much more of this.

      **Example:** a much warmer room for much nicer people

      **Source context (verbatim):** with a comparative adjective

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. Often; frequently.

   **Example:** Does he get drunk much?

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. Used to indicate or compare extent.

   **Usage:** 'this much'

   **Example:** I don't like Wagner as much as I like Mozart.

   **Source context (verbatim):** in combinations such as 'as much'

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. Combining with an adjective or (occasionally) a noun, used in a rhetorical question to mock someone for having the specified quality.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Example:** Jamie's always preaching about how we need to save a planet when she drives literally everywhere she goes. Like, hypocritical much?

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. Almost.

   **Usage:** obsolete
