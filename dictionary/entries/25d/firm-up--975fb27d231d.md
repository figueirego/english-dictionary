## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make (tentative plans) more definite.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Can we firm up plans for the barbecue Sunday?

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To become more definite.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** Let me know as soon as your plans firm up.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of tentative plans

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make (muscles) more toned through physical exercise.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He hopes to firm up his biceps a bit before meeting the girls.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become more toned through physical exercise.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** After weeks of working out, his biceps finally began to firm up a bit.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of muscles

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To become firmer in consistency.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Cook the omelet until the top has started to firm up.
