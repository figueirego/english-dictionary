### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To physically rotate (usually around a vertical axis) for a half turn (180 degrees), a whole turn (360 degrees), or an indefinite amount.

   **Usage:** ergative

   **Example:** At the end of the road, we turned around and walked back to the hotel.

   **Example:** The world turns around once every twenty-four hours.

   **Example:** Turn around once or twice so I can see your new dress.

   **Example:** A wheel turns around on an axle.

   **Example:** If you kids don't cut it out I will turn this car around!

   **Source synonyms:** twirl

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To change drastically in a fundamental way, often for the better; to change to the opposite (opinion or position).

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** She turned her position around and now she is in favor of the merger.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To reverse an expected outcome (of a game, etc.), usually from a losing outcome to a winning one; to return (a business, department, etc.) to effectiveness, profitability, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The new management team has really turned the company around 180 degrees, and they expect a good profit next year.

   **Example:** The team really turned it around in the second half for a great win.

   **Example:** They were way ahead but the game turned around on them and they lost 4–3.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; hobbies; lifestyle; management; sports

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To convert (an agent) to work for one's own side.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** espionage; government; military; politics; war

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To suddenly change or reverse one's opinion, point of view, stated position, behaviour, etc.

   **Usage:** colloquial; idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** You can't just turn around and say that it was all my fault.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To consider from a different viewpoint.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Let's turn that around and look at it from another angle.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an idea

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. (often with a unit of time) To produce; to output; to generate.

   **Usage:** colloquial; idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** We can turn around 500 units by next week.
