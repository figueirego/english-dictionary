## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To flatten and shape (metal) by hammering.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** A smith hammers out iron on an anvil.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To create (something) by hammering metal; to repair (something made of metal that has become bent or dented) by hammering.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I watched a tinsmith hammer out a funnel.

   **Example:** The fender was so badly damaged that it couldn’t be hammered out.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To remove (something tightly in place) by hammering.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The bolt wouldn’t budge so she tried hammering it out.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To produce (a sound or signal) by hammering.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The kids are forever hammering out “Heart and Soul” on the piano.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To type (something) on a keyboard.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To create or develop (an idea, plan, piece of writing, etc.) with effort.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** It took me all night to hammer out the first draft of my essay.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To come to (a difficult agreement or settlement) after much arguing and conflict.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** Two years after the flood, my lawyer managed to hammer out a settlement with my insurance company over the damages.
