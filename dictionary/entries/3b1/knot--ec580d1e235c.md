### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A looping of a piece of string or of any other long, flexible material that cannot be untangled without passing one or both ends of the material through its loops.

   **Example:** Climbers must make sure that all knots are both secure and of types that will not weaken the rope.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A tangled clump of hair or similar.

   **Example:** The nurse was brushing knots from the protesting child's hair.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A maze-like pattern.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A non-self-intersecting closed curve in (e.g., three-dimensional) space that is an abstraction of a knot (in sense 1 above).

   **Example:** A knot can be defined as a non-self-intersecting broken line whose endpoints coincide&#58; when such a knot is constrained to lie in a plane, then it is simply a polygon.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

   **Example:** I got into a knot when I inadvertently insulted a policeman.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The whorl left in lumber by the base of a branch growing out of the tree's trunk.

   **Example:** When preparing to tell stories at a campfire, I like to set aside a pile of pine logs with lots of knots, since they burn brighter and make dramatic pops and cracks.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Local swelling in a tissue area, especially skin, often due to injury.

   **Example:** Jeremy had a knot on his head where he had bumped it on the bedframe.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We knotted the ends of the rope to keep it from unravelling.
