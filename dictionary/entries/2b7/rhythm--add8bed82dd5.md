### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The variation of strong and weak elements (such as duration, accent) of sounds, notably in speech or music, over time; a beat or meter.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Dance to the rhythm of the music.

   **Example:** The audience clapped in rhythm as I sang.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A specifically defined pattern of such variation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Most dances have a rhythm as distinctive as the Iambic verse in poetry

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A flow, repetition or regularity.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Once you get the rhythm of it, the job will become easy.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The tempo or speed of a beat, song or repetitive event.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** We walked with a quick, even rhythm.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The musical instruments which provide rhythm (mainly; not or less melody) in a musical ensemble.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The Baroque term basso continuo is virtually equivalent to rhythm

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A regular quantitative change in a variable (notably natural) process.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The rhythm of the seasons dominates agriculture as well as wildlife

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Controlled repetition of a phrase, incident or other element as a stylistic figure in literature and other narrative arts; the effect it creates.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The running gag is a popular rhythm in motion pictures and theater comedy

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A person's natural feeling for rhythm.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** That girl's got rhythm, watch her dance!
