### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; To secure (someone or something) in a locked enclosure.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The dog won't escape now we've locked him in.

   **Example:** The dog won't escape now we've locked him in the kitchen.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To fix the value of (something potentially variable).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I've locked in a rate of 5%.

   **Example:** I've got a 5% rate locked in.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To prevent (someone or something) from escaping, deteriorating, or switching to an alternative.

   **Usage:** colloquial; transitive

   **Example:** Our new foil packets keep the flavour of the crisps locked in.

   **Example:** Companies find various ways to try to lock in a customer base by preventing the products or services from being fully fungible.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To synchronize (especially a rhythm section) into a groove.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; music

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To focus entirely (on something).

   **Usage:** colloquial; intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** I need to lock in for this exam I'm taking tomorrow.

   **Example:** The missile's path is now locked in.
