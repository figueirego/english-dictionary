### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To deliberately take hold of; to grab or capture.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** clasp; grasp; grip; beclasp (dated); begrasp; begrip; belock (archaic); clasp; clench; clinch; cling; clutch; enclasp; fang (archaic; dialectal); fondle; glaum (dialectal); grab; grabble; grasp; grip; hang on; hold on; hold tight; hold; nab; nim (obsolete); seize; stranglehold; take

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To take advantage of (an opportunity or circumstance).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** jump on

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To take possession of (by force, law etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to seize smuggled goods

   **Example:** to seize a ship after libeling

   **Source synonyms:** attach; arrogate; commandeer; confiscate; impress

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To have a sudden and powerful effect upon.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** a panic seized the crowd

   **Example:** a fever seized him

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Alternative spelling of seise (“to vest ownership of an estate in land”).

   **Usage:** alt-of; alternative; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

   **Alternative form of:** [seise](../1d6/seise--75b3344d806a.md)

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To bind, lash or make fast, with several turns of small rope, cord, or small line.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to seize two fish-hooks back to back

   **Example:** to seize or stop one rope on to another

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To fasten, fix.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To lay hold in seizure, by hands or claws (+ on or upon).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** to seize on the neck of a horse

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To have a seizure.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To bind or lock in position immovably; see also seize up.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Rust caused the engine to seize, never to run again.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To submit for consideration to a deliberative body.

   **Usage:** UK; intransitive

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. \[with of\] To cause (an action or matter) to be or remain before (a certain judge or court).

   **Example:** This Court will remain seized of this matter.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law
