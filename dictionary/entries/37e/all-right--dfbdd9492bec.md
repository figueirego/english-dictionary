## Interjection · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˌɔːlˈɹaɪt/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation; UK
- enPR: ôl'rīt' — source labels: Received-Pronunciation; UK
- IPA: /ˌaː(l)ˈɹiːt/ — source labels: Northumbria; UK
- IPA: /ˌɔlˈɹaɪt/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˌɑlˈɹaɪt/ — source labels: General-American; cot-caught-merger

### Forms

- alright — alternative; nonstandard; proscribed; sometimes
- allright — alternative; misspelling

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. Used to affirm, indicate agreement, or consent.

   **Example:** All right, let's go then.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. Used to indicate support, favor or encouragement.

   **Example:** All right! They scored!

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. Used to fill space or pauses.

   **Example:** All right, so what you suggest we do next?

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. Used as a general lead-in or beginning.

   **Example:** All right, let's get started.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. Used to express exasperation or frustration, often reduplicated or with already.

   **Example:** All right, already! Let me finish what I was doing first, and then we can talk.

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. Term of greeting, equivalent to how are you or hello.

   **Usage:** UK; informal

   **Example:** All right, mate, how are things with you and the missus?
