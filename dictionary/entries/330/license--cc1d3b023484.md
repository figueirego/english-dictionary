### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A legal document giving official permission to do something; a permit.

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The legal terms under which a person is allowed to use a product, especially software.

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Freedom to deviate deliberately from normally applicable rules or practices (especially in behaviour or speech).

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Excessive freedom; lack of due restraint.

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Ellipsis of driver's license.

   **Usage:** US; abbreviation; alt-of; countable; ellipsis; uncountable

   **Example:** In order to enter the building, I need to show my license.

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈlaɪ.səns/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- licenses — present; singular; third-person
- licensing — participle; present
- licensed — participle; past
- licensed — past
- licence — alternative; Commonwealth; noun

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To authorize officially.

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** I am licensed to practice law in this state.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. (applied to a piece of intellectual property)
   1. To give formal authorization to use.

      **Usage:** US; transitive

      **Example:** It was decided to license Wikipedia under the GFDL.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. (applied to a piece of intellectual property)
   1. To acquire authorization to use, usually in exchange for compensation.

      **Usage:** US; transitive

      **Example:** The filmmakers licensed several iconic 80's songs for the soundtrack.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To give permission or freedom to; accept.

   **Usage:** US; transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To permit (as grammatically correct).

   **Usage:** US; transitive

   **Example:** No English adverbs have mandatory complements, and most don't even license optional ones.
