### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A band worn around the waist to hold clothing to one's body (usually pants), hold weapons (such as a gun or sword), or serve as a decorative piece of clothing.

   **Example:** As part of the act, the fat clown's belt broke, causing his pants to fall down.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; clothing; fashion; lifestyle; manufacturing; textiles

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A band used as a restraint for safety purposes, such as a seat belt.

   **Example:** Keep your belt fastened; this is going to be quite a bumpy ride.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A band that is used in a machine to help transfer motion or power.

   **Example:** The motor had a single belt that snaked its way back and forth around a variety of wheels.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Anything that resembles a belt, or that encircles or crosses like a belt; a strip or stripe.

   **Example:** a belt of trees; a belt of sand

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A trophy in the shape of a belt, generally awarded for martial arts.

   **Example:** the heavyweight belt

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A collection of small bodies (such as asteroids) which orbit a star.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. One of certain girdles or zones on the surface of the planets Jupiter and Saturn, supposed to be of the nature of clouds.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A band of armor along the sides of a warship, protecting the ship's vital spaces.

   **Example:** The battleship was protected by a twelve-inch belt just above the waterline.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; nautical; politics; transport; war

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To fasten a belt on; to encircle with a belt.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Edgar belted himself in and turned the car's ignition.

   **Example:** The rotund man had difficulty belting his pants, and generally wore suspenders to avoid the issue.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To encircle; to surround.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The small town was belted by cornfields in all directions.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To invest (a person) with a belt as part of a formal ceremony such as knighthood.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To hit with a belt.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The child was misbehaving so he was belted as punishment.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To hit someone or something, especially forcefully; to bash.

   **Usage:** colloquial; transitive

   **Example:** The angry player belted the official across the face, and as a result was ejected from the game.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To hit a pitched ball a long distance, usually for a home run.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He belted that pitch over the grandstand.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To scream or sing in a loud, strong manner.

   **Usage:** informal; transitive

   **Example:** He belted out the national anthem.

   **Source context (verbatim):** normally belt out

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To drink quickly, often in gulps.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He belted down a shot of whisky.
