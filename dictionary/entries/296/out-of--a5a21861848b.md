### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. From the inside to the outside of.

      **Example:** The audience came out of the theater.

      **Example:** The cat jumped out of the basket.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. So as no longer to be in a given condition or state.

      **Example:** I have fallen out of love with you.

      **Example:** They will soon be out of business.

      **Example:** This train will be going out of service at the next station.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. From a thing or place as a source, place of origin etc.

      **Usage:** informal

      **Example:** He ate out of a big bowl.

      **Example:** Turns out he's some rapper out of New York called Buster Bigmouth.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. Stating the port in which a boat has been registered.

      **Example:** There's the Titanic out of Liverpool.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. Taken from among; expressing a fraction of (a larger number);

      **Example:** Only three out of a thousand are born with this rare disease.

      **Example:** Out of the entire class, only Cynthia completed the work.

      **Example:** Users rated this product a 9 out of 10.

      **Example:** Only one out of three new students come(s) back.

      **Source synonyms:** in

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Expressing motion away, literal or figurative; opposed to into.
   1. Born from a given mother.

      **Usage:** now chiefly horse breeding

      **Example:** She's a lovely little filly, by Big Lad, out of Damsel in Distress.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Expressing position outside, literal or figurative; opposed to in.
   1. Not within a given space, area etc.

      **Example:** His feet rested out of the water.

      **Example:** Is your mother out of hospital?

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Expressing position outside, literal or figurative; opposed to in.
   1. Not in (a given state, condition).

      **Example:** I'm rather out of practice right now.

      **Example:** He cannot see you because he's feeling out of spirits today.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Expressing position outside, literal or figurative; opposed to in.
   1. Without; no longer in possession of.

      **Example:** Sorry, we're out of bread.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. From a given cause or motivation.

   **Example:** I laughed out of embarrassment.

   **Example:** She only did it out of love for him.

   **Example:** She asked the question out of mere curiosity.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. From a given material as means of construction.

   **Example:** It's made out of mahogany.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. In a manner based in but not always in (a certain place); (loosely) in.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** They're out of Tampa and they cover the Southeast.

   **Example:** He works out of the main office.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. expression of how distant a person, an event or object is.

   **Usage:** informal; often

   **Example:** Five months out of the election

   **Example:** Several yards out of the ocean

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To an extreme degree.

   **Example:** We're gonna blow the crap out of it.

   **Example:** I could eat the heck out of some spaghetti.
