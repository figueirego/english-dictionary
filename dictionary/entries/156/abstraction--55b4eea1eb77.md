### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of abstracting, separating, withdrawing, or taking away; withdrawal; the state of being taken away.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The act of abstracting, separating, withdrawing, or taking away; withdrawal; the state of being taken away.
   1. The taking surreptitiously for one's own use part of the property of another; purloining.

      **Usage:** countable; euphemistic; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The act of abstracting, separating, withdrawing, or taking away; withdrawal; the state of being taken away.
   1. Removal of water from a river, lake, or aquifer.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A separation from worldly objects; a recluse life; the withdrawal from one's senses.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** a hermit’s abstraction

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The act of focusing on one characteristic of an object rather than the object as a whole group of characteristics; the act of separating said qualities from the object or ideas.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Abstraction is necessary for the classification of things into genera and species.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Any characteristic of an individual object when that characteristic has been separated from the object and is contemplated alone as a quality having independent existence.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A member of an idealized subgroup when contemplated according to the abstracted quality which defines the subgroup.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. The act of comparing commonality between distinct objects and organizing using those similarities; the act of generalizing characteristics; the product of said generalization.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. An idea or notion of an abstract or theoretical nature.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** to fight for mere abstractions

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Absence or absorption of mind; inattention to present objects; preoccupation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. An abstract creation, or piece of art; qualities of artwork that are free from representational aspects.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** art; arts

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. A separation of volatile parts by the act of distillation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. An idea of an idealistic, unrealistic or visionary nature.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. The result of mentally abstracting an idea; the product of any mental process involving a synthesis of&#58; separation, despecification, generalization, and ideation in any of a number of combinations.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. The merging of two river valleys by the larger of the two deepening and widening so much so, as to assimilate the smaller.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; geology; natural-sciences

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable
