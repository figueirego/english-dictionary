## Noun

### Forms

- lock-ins — plural
- lockin — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A sleepover party, usually held in a public place such as a church or school, in which the participants are not allowed to leave until the next morning.

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** The youth group will have their annual lock-in this weekend.

   **Source synonyms:** wakeover

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An illegal but widely-tolerated invitation-only gathering in a pub, after the end of licensing hours, to allow regular customers the opportunity to enjoy further drinking time.

   **Usage:** Ireland; UK

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A situation in which a business has adopted a certain standard or product from a specific manufacturer and integrated it into their process, thus making it very costly to change to a different standard or supplier.

   **Example:** vendor lock-in

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The situation where an investor cannot trade without incurring an undesirable penalty such as taxation.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance
