### Senses

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Something actual as opposed to invented.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** establish the facts

   **Example:** hard facts

   **Example:** facts and figures

   **Example:** In this story, the Gettysburg Address is a fact, but the rest is fiction.

   **Example:** The report is based on verified facts.

   **Example:** She presented the facts in a clear, logical way.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Something which is real.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Gravity is a fact, not a theory.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something concrete used as a basis for further interpretation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Let's look at the facts of the case before deciding.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An objective consensus on a fundamental reality that has been agreed upon by a substantial number of experts.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** There is no doubting the fact that the Earth orbits the Sun.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Information about a particular subject, especially actual conditions and/or circumstances.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The facts about space travel.

   **Example:** Addition facts include 2 + 2 = 4 and 3 + 4 = 7.

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An individual value or measurement at the lowest level of granularity in a data warehouse.
