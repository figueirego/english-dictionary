### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To possess, own.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I have a house and a car.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To hold, as something at someone's disposal.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Look what I have here—a frog I found on the street!

   **Example:** Do you have the key?

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To include as a part, ingredient, or feature.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The stove has a handle. The shirt has sleeves.

   **Example:** The words cow and dog have three letters.

   **Example:** A government has three branches&#58; the executive, legislative, and judicial.

   **Example:** The movie has lots of action.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Used to state the existence or presence of someone in a specified relationship with the subject.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I have two sisters.

   **Example:** She doesn’t have any friends.

   **Example:** I have a really mean boss.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To consume or use up (a particular substance or resource, especially food or drink).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I have breakfast at six o'clock.

   **Example:** You've already had five drinks!

   **Example:** She's had more than enough time already.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To undertake or perform (an action or activity).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Can I have a look at that?

   **Example:** He's having a tantrum about it.

   **Example:** I’m going to have a bath now.

   **Example:** Let’s have a game of tiddlywinks.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To be scheduled to attend, undertake or participate in.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** What class do you have right now? I have English.

   **Example:** Fred won’t be able to come to the party; he has a meeting that day.

   **Example:** I have a lot of work to do.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To experience, go through, undergo.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We had a hard year last year, with the locust swarms and all that.

   **Example:** He had surgery on his hip yesterday.

   **Example:** I’m having the time of my life!

   **Example:** I hope you have a wonderful birthday.

   **Example:** This year we're having Christmas with my wife's family in Thunder Bay.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To be afflicted with, suffer from.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He had a cold last week.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Used in forming the perfect aspect.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; with-past-participle

   **Example:** I have already eaten today.

   **Example:** Mom's gone real mad, she has.

   **Example:** The universe is expanding and has been since its beginning.

   **Example:** I will have left by the time you get here.

   **Example:** This is the first (and last) time I've eaten lobster.

   **Example:** I’ve worked here for exactly four years, one month and 10 days.

   **Example:** The dogs have eaten your dinner, and I ate the last of the frozen burgers this morning.

   **Example:** Uncle José has crashed the car again. He ran into a tree.

   **Example:** The American Revolution has influenced every democratic process in Europe since the 19th c.

   **Example:** I'm going to take a bath after I've answered these emails.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. Used as an interrogative verb before a pronoun to form a tag question, echoing a previous use of 'have' as an auxiliary verb or, in certain cases, main verb. (For further discussion, see the appendix English tag questions.)

   **Example:** They haven’t eaten dinner yet, have they?

   **Example:** Your wife hasn’t been reading that nonsense, has she?

   **Example:** He has some money, hasn’t he?

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. See have to.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; with-infinitive; with-to

   **Example:** I have to go.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To give birth to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The couple always wanted to have children.

   **Example:** My wife is having the baby right now!

   **Example:** My mother had me when she was 25.

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To obtain.

   **Usage:** informal; passive; usually

   **Example:** The substance you describe can't be had at any price.

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. To engage in sexual intercourse with.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He’s always bragging about how many women he’s had.

<a id="r1-s16"></a>

**Source sense R1.S16**

16. To accept as a romantic partner.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Despite my protestations of love, she would not have me.

<a id="r1-s17"></a>

**Source sense R1.S17**

17. To cause to, by a command, request or invitation.

   **Usage:** transitive; with-infinitive

   **Example:** They had me feed their dog while they were out of town.

   **Example:** Her very boyfriend is the person the criminal has do most of her dirty deeds.

   **Example:** The floor has had oil dropped all over it.

<a id="r1-s18"></a>

**Source sense R1.S18**

   **Usage:** transitive; with-adjective

   **Example:** I'd better have my watch repaired today&#58; tomorrow I have to have my hair cut before having pictures taken for my passport.

   **Example:** The lecture’s ending had the entire audience in tears.

   **Example:** Jim has his eyes closed.

   **Usage:** transitive; with-infinitive

   **Example:** I’ve had three people today tell me my hair looks nice.

**Source sense R1.S20**

20. To depict as being.

   **Usage:** transitive; with-adjective

**Source sense R1.S21**

   **Usage:** UK; slang; transitive

   **Example:** I could have him!

<a id="r1-s22"></a>

**Source sense R1.S22**

   **Usage:** UK; slang; transitive

   **Example:** You broke the window! Teacher’ll have you for that!

<a id="r1-s23"></a>

**Source sense R1.S23**

   **Usage:** transitive; dated outside Ireland

   **Example:** I have no German.

<a id="r1-s24"></a>

**Source sense R1.S24**

24. To feel or be (especially painfully) aware of.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Dan certainly has arms today, probably from scraping paint off four columns the day before.

<a id="r1-s25"></a>

**Source sense R1.S25**

25. To trick, to deceive.

   **Usage:** informal; often; passive; transitive

   **Example:** I bought a laptop online but it never arrived. I think I've been had!

   **Example:** You had me alright! I never would have thought that was just a joke.

<a id="r1-s26"></a>

**Source sense R1.S26**

   **Usage:** transitive; often in continuous tenses

   **Example:** The child screamed incessantly for his mother to buy him a toy, but she wasn't having any of it.

**Source sense R1.S27**

27. To believe, buy, be taken in by.

   **Usage:** often; transitive

   **Example:** I made up an excuse as to why I was out so late, but my wife wasn't having any of it.

   **Source context (verbatim):** used in the negative

<a id="r1-s28"></a>

**Source sense R1.S28**

28. To host someone; to take in as a guest.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Thank you for having me!

<a id="r1-s29"></a>

**Source sense R1.S29**

29. To get a reading, measurement, or result from an instrument or calculation.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** What do you have for problem two?

   **Example:** I have two contacts on my scope.

<a id="r1-s30"></a>

**Source sense R1.S30**

30. To consider a court proceeding that has been completed; to begin deliberations on a case.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We’ll schedule closing arguments for Thursday, and the jury will have the case by that afternoon.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a jury

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s33"></a>

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Ah! Now I have it!

<a id="r1-s34"></a>

**Source sense R1.S34**

   **Usage:** nonstandard; transitive; non-native speakers' English
