### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Reduced form of 'so that', used to express purpose; in order that.

   **Usage:** form-of; reduced

   **Example:** I got an earlier train to work so I'd have plenty of time to prepare for the meeting.

   **Example:** Eat your broccoli so you can have dessert.

   **Form of:** 'so that'

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. As a result; for that reason; therefore; because of this; due to this.

   **Example:** I was hungry, so I asked if there was any more food.

   **Example:** He ate too much cake and so he fell ill.

   **Example:** He wanted a book, so he went to the library.

   **Example:** “I need to go to the bathroom.” ― “So go!”

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to connect previous conversation or events to the following question.

   **Example:** So how does this story end?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Used to introduce a rhetorical question.

   **Example:** “We'd like to visit but I don't know if we can afford a hotel.” — “So who's staying in a hotel? Stay with us.”

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Provided that; on condition that; as long as.

   **Usage:** archaic

### Related words

### Senses

<a id="r4-s1"></a>

**Source sense R4.S1**

1. Used after a pause for thought to introduce a new topic, question or story, or a new thought or question in continuation of an existing topic.

   **Example:** So, let's go home.

   **Example:** So, what'll you have?

   **Example:** So, there was this squirrel stuck in the chimney...

   **Example:** So, everyone wants to know – did you win the contest or not?

   **Source synonyms:** look; [well](../29d/well--a7722b765d15.md); see; hey

<a id="r4-s2"></a>

**Source sense R4.S2**

2. Used as a question to ask for further explanation of something said, often rhetorically or in a dismissive or impolite manner.

   **Example:** "You park your car in front of my house every morning." — "So?"

<a id="r4-s3"></a>

**Source sense R4.S3**

3. Used as a meaningless filler word to begin a response to a question.

   **Example:** What are you doing? / So I'm just fixing this shelf.

   **Example:** What time does the train leave? / So it leaves at 10 o'clock.

<a id="r4-s4"></a>

**Source sense R4.S4**

4. Be as you are; stand still; used especially to cows; also used by sailors.

   **Usage:** archaic
