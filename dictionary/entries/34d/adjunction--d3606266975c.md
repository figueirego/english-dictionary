### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of joining; the thing joined or added.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The joining of personal property owned by one to that owned by another.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The process of adjoining elements to an algebraic structure (usually a ring or field); the result of such a process.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The ring obtained after the adjunction of the elements a,b and y to the ring R may be denoted R\#91;a,b,y\#93;.

   **Example:** The field adjunction \#92;mathbb\#123;Q\#125;(\#92;pi) can be obtained from \#92;mathbb\#123;Q\#125; by adjoining \#92;pi to \#92;mathbb\#123;Q\#125;.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** algebra; mathematics; number-theory; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A relationship between a pair of categories that makes the pair, in a weak sense, equivalent.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** category-theory; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

5. A natural isomorphism between a pair of functors satisfying certain conditions, whose existence implies a close relationship between the functors and between their (co)domains; the natural isomorphism, functors, and their (co)domains thought of as a single object.
