### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To pillage, take or destroy all the goods of, by force (as in war); to raid, sack.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The mercenaries plundered the small town.

   **Example:** The shopkeeper was plundered of his possessions by the burglar.

   **Source synonyms:** ravage; rifle

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To take (goods) by pillage.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The mercenaries plundered all the goods they found.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To take by force or wrongfully; to commit robbery or looting, to raid.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** “Now to plunder, mateys!” screamed a buccaneer, to cries of “Arrgh!” and “Aye!” all around.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make extensive (over)use of, as if by plundering; to use or use up wrongfully.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The miners plundered the jungle for its diamonds till it became a muddy waste.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To take unexpectedly.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To amass ill-gotten wealth (by public officials) through a combination or series of overt criminal acts.

   **Usage:** Philippines; criminal law
