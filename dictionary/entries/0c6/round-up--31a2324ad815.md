### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To collect or gather (something) together.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** The city hall needs to round up all the wrongly parked bikes across the city.

   **Example:** They rounded up a group of experts and got their opinions.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To collect or gather (something) together.
   1. To gather (livestock such as cattle, sheep, geese, etc.) together, such as by encircling them.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** In the autumn we round up all the hill sheep and bring them down into the barn.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To collect or gather (something) together.
   1. To arrest or detain a group of people based on collective (rather than individualized) cause or suspicion, often as a form of targeted persecution.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; transitive

      **Example:** During the Holocaust, the Nazis rounded up Jews into ghettos and concentration camps.

      **Example:** Major Strasser has been shot. Round up the usual suspects.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To round (a number) to the smallest integer that is not less than it, or to some other greater value, especially a whole number of hundreds, thousands, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The total is $24,995 — let's round it up to $25,000.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** arithmetic

   **Source antonyms:** round down
