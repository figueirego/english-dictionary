## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈtɪns(ə)l/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation; Scotland
- IPA: /ˈtɪnsəl/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- tinsels — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A shining fabric used for ornamental purposes.
   1. A silk or wool fabric with gold or silver thread woven into it; brocade.

      **Usage:** obsolete; uncountable; usually

      **Source synonyms:** baldacchin

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A shining fabric used for ornamental purposes.
   1. A very thin, gauzelike cloth with gold or silver (or, later, copper) thread woven into it, or overlaid with thin metal plates.

      **Usage:** obsolete; uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A thin, shiny foil for ornamental purposes which is of a material made of metal or resembling metal; especially, narrow glittering strips of such a material, often strung on to thread, and traditionally at Christmastime draped on Christmas trees, hung from balustrades or ceilings, or wrapped around objects as a decoration.

   **Usage:** broadly; uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Anything shining and gaudy; especially something superficially shiny and showy, or having a false lustre, and more pretty than valuable.

   **Usage:** broadly; figuratively; uncountable; usually
