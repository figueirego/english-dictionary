   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

3. The vocalisations of a Eurasian magpie, Pica pica.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

4. The vocalisations of various birds or other animals.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An intermittent noise, as from vibration.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Proper brake adjustment will help to reduce the chatter.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. In national security, the degree of communication between suspect groups and individuals, used to gauge the degree of expected terrorist activity.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** The NSA is concerned about increased chatter between known terror groups.

**Source sense R1.S7**

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

**Source sense R1.S8**

   **Usage:** collective; countable; usually

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- chattering — participle; present
- chattered — participle; past
- chattered — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To talk idly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** They knitted and chattered the whole time.

   **Source synonyms:** chat; natter

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To make a noise by rapid collisions.

   **Usage:** intransitive; usually; machinery

   **Example:** He was so cold that his teeth were chattering.

   **Example:** The software in modern machine tools can often predict and prevent movements that would cause the cutting tool to chatter.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of teeth

   **Source synonyms:** clatter; gnash; grind; pink

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To utter sounds which somewhat resemble language, but are inarticulate and indistinct.
