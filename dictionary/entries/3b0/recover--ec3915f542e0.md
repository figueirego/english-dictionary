### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To get back, to regain (a physical thing; in astronomy and navigation, sight of a thing or a signal).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After days of inquiries, he finally recovered his lost wallet.

   **Example:** For days telescopes surveyed the skies to recover the small asteroid.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To salvage, to extricate, to rescue (a thing or person).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** They recovered three of the explorers very much alive, then another, wracked with severe hypothermia, who was taken to hospital.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To replenish to, resume (a good state of mind or body).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** At the top of the hill I asked to stop for a few minutes to recover my strength.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To obtain a positive judgement; to win in a lawsuit.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The plaintiff recovered in his suit, being awarded declaratory relief and a clearing of his name.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To gain as compensation or reparation, usually by formal legal process.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to recover damages in trespass; to recover debt and costs in a legal action or that is owing

   **Example:** to recover lands in ejectment or common recovery

   **Example:** recover one's investment

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

   **Source synonyms:** recoup

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To reach (a place), arrive at.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To restore to good health, consciousness, life etc.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To make good by reparation; to make up for; to retrieve; to repair the loss or injury of.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

   **Example:** to recover lost time

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To regain one's composure, balance etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Spinning round, he caught a stone with his ankle, but recovered quickly before turning to face me.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To get better, to regain health or prosperity.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I was hurt, but I knew I’d recover, given time.

   **Example:** Without calling in Business Recovery experts, the company saw trade and investor confidence recover sharply in the wake of the crisis.

   **Example:** I lost out in the deal, but I quickly recovered financially

   **Example:** It takes time and good health to recover from injury, surgery, a bereavement and emotional turmoil

   **Source context (verbatim):** followed by "from" to show what caused the bad feeling

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To recover from.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

   **Example:** To the end of his days, he never fully recovered his daughter's death.

   **Source context (verbatim):** without "from"
