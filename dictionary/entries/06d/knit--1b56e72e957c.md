### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To turn thread or yarn into a piece of fabric by forming loops that are pulled through each other. This can be done by hand with needles or by machine.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** to knit a stocking

   **Example:** The first generation knitted to order; the second still knits for its own use; the next leaves knitting to industrial manufacturers.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To create a stitch by pulling the working yarn through an existing stitch from back to front.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Stitches that are knitted look like little V’s when seen from the front.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To join closely and firmly together.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The fight for survival knitted the men closely together.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become closely and firmly joined; become compacted.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To grow together.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** All those seedlings knitted into a kaleidoscopic border.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To combine from various elements.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The witness knitted together his testimony from contradictory pieces of hearsay.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To heal following a fracture.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I’ll go skiing again after my bones knit.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of bones

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To form into a knot, or into knots; to tie together, as cord; to fasten by tying.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To draw together; to contract into wrinkles.

   **Usage:** transitive
