### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To remove a piece from a whole by breaking or snapping.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** She unwrapped the slab of chocolate and broke off a piece.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To become detached by breaking or snapping.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** A chunk of rock broke off from the cliff face.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To discontinue abruptly.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Both families want the lovers to break off any relationship they may have.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To end abruptly, either temporarily or permanently.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Our school doesn't break off until July.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To play the first shot in a frame of snooker, billiards or pool.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; billiards; games; hobbies; lifestyle; snooker; sports

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To give (someone) (something); to allow (someone) to take (something); to grant (someone) a share or portion of (something).

   **Usage:** slang; transitive; African-American Vernacular

   **Example:** If you help, I'll break you off a piece of the proceeds.
