### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make something loose.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to relax a rope or cord

   **Example:** to relax the muscles or sinews

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make something loose.
   1. To relieve from constipation; to loosen; to open.

      **Usage:** dated; transitive

      **Example:** An aperient relaxes the bowels.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To become loose.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** expand

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To relieve (someone or someone's mind) of stress; to enable to rest; to calm down.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Amusement relaxes the mind.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To rest and become relieved of stress.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I like to relax by listening to music.

   **Source synonyms:** chill out; unwind

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To make something less severe or tense.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to relax discipline

   **Example:** to relax one's attention or endeavours

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To become less severe or tense.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Tensions between the two countries have relaxed somewhat in recent weeks.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To make something (such as codes and regulations) more lenient.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To become more lenient.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of codes and regulations
