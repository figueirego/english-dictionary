*English*

## Verb

### Forms

- sees through — present; singular; third-person
- seeing through — participle; present
- saw through — past
- seen through — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To perceive visually through something transparent.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Their fabric is so thin that I can see through these curtains.

   **Example:** We saw through the water with ease; it was as clear as glass.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To not be deceived by something that is false or misleading; to understand the hidden truth about someone or something.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** I'm surprised she doesn't see through his lies.

   **Example:** I can see through his poker face. He isn't fooling anyone.

   **Source synonyms:** tumble (to sth)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To recognize someone's true motives or character.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** In that moment, I finally saw through her; this petition drive had nothing to do with her love for animals, and everything to do with impressing Michael, the cute intern.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To provide support or cooperation to (a person) throughout a period of time; to support someone through a difficult time.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** And may we all, citizens the world over, see these events through.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To do something until it is finished; to continue working on (something) until it is finished.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Despite her health problems, Madame Prime Minister saw the project through.

   **Source synonyms:** see out

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To constitute ample supply for one for.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; idiomatic

   **Example:** Those chocolates should see us through the holiday season.
