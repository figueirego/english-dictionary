### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A meaning of a word or phrase that is (subjectively) suggested or implied (implicit), as contrasted with a denotation, which is the explicit and objective meaning. A characteristic of words or phrases, or of the contexts that words and phrases are used in.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The word "advisedly" has a connotation of "wisely", although it denotes merely "intentionally" and "deliberately."

   **Example:** The word "happy" has a positive connotation, while "sad" has a negative connotation.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences; semantics

   **Source synonyms:** intension; intent

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The attribute or aggregate of attributes connoted by a term, as contrasted with a denotation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The two expressions "the morning star" and "the evening star" have different connotations but the same denotation (i.e. the planet Venus).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; logic; mathematics; philosophy; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** intension; intent
