### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To declare or find innocent or not guilty.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** absolve; clear; exculpate; exonerate

   **Source antonyms:** condemn; convict; adjudge; convict; condemn; damn; deem (obsolete); doom; judge (obsolete); sentence

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To discharge (for example, a claim or debt); to clear off, to pay off; to fulfil.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. to discharge, release, or set free from a burden, duty, liability, or obligation, or from an accusation or charge.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The jury acquitted the prisoner of the charge.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To bear or conduct oneself; to perform one's part.

   **Usage:** reflexive

   **Example:** The soldier acquitted herself well in battle.

   **Example:** The orator acquitted himself very poorly.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To clear oneself.

   **Usage:** reflexive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. past participle of acquit.

   **Usage:** archaic; form-of; participle; past; transitive

   **Form of:** [acquit](../2e3/acquit--b8e9f6796778.md)

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To release, to rescue, to set free.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To pay for; to atone for.

   **Usage:** obsolete; rare; transitive
