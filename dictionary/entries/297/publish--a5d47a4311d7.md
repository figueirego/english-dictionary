### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To issue (something, such as printed work) for distribution and/or sale.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The Times published the investigative piece about the governor both in print and online.

   **Example:** Most of the sketches Faulkner published in 1925 appeared in the Sunday magazine section of the New Orleans Times-Picayune.

   **Example:** The State combined public information strategies and published billboards, pamphlets, and newsletter articles under the campaign theme, Give 'Em the Boot.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To announce to the public.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The Secretary of Health and Human Services published a press release on May 22, 2013.

   **Example:** The Bolshevik government published an announcement of the tsar's death.

   **Example:** No newspaper published the victim's name.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To issue the work of (an author).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Grove Press published many avant-garde authors.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To disseminate (a message) publicly via a newsgroup, forum, blog, etc.

   **Usage:** Internet; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To issue a medium (e.g. publication).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Major city papers still publish daily.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To have one's work accepted for a publication.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** She needs to publish in order to get tenure.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To be made available in a printed publication or other medium.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The article first published online, then in print the next day.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of content

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To make (information such as an event) available to components that wish to be notified (subscribers).
