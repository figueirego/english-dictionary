## Noun · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /deɪt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- dates — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The addition to a writing, inscription, coin, etc., which specifies the time (especially the day, month, and year) when the writing or inscription was given, executed, or made.

   **Example:** the date of a letter, of a will, of a deed, of a coin, etc.

   **Example:** US date &#58; 05/24/08 = Tuesday, May 24th, 2008. UK date &#58; 24/05/08 = Tuesday 24th May 2008.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A specific day in time at which a transaction or event takes place, or is appointed to take place; a given point of time.

   **Example:** the date for pleading

   **Example:** Do you know the date of the wedding?

   **Example:** We had to change the dates of the festival because of the flooding.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. A point in time.

   **Example:** You may need that at a later date.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. An assigned end; a conclusion.

   **Usage:** rare

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. A given or assigned length of life; duration.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. A pre-arranged meeting.

   **Example:** I arranged a date with my Australian business partners.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. One's companion for social activities or occasions, especially a romantic partner.

   **Example:** I brought Melinda to the wedding as my date.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. A romantic meeting or outing with a lover or potential lover, or the person so met.

   **Example:** We really hit it off on the first date, so we decided to meet the week after.

   **Example:** The cinema is a popular place to take someone on a date.

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /deɪt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- dates — present; singular; third-person
- dating — participle; present
- dated — participle; past
- dated — past

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To note the time or place of writing or executing; to express in an instrument the time of its execution.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to date a letter, a bond, a deed, or a charter

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To note or fix the time of (an event); to give the date of.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To determine the age of something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to date the building of the pyramids

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To take (someone) on a date, or a series of dates.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To have a steady relationship with; to be romantically involved with.

   **Usage:** broadly; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** go out; see

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To have a steady relationship with each other; to be romantically involved with each other.

   **Usage:** broadly; reciprocal

   **Example:** They met a couple of years ago, but have been dating for about five months.

   **Source synonyms:** go out; see

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. To make or become old, especially in such a way as to fall out of fashion, become less appealing or attractive, etc.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** This show hasn't dated well.

   **Example:** The comedian dated himself by making quips about bands from the 1960s.

   **Source synonyms:** age; elden; obsolesce

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. To have beginning; to begin; to be dated or reckoned.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** with from
