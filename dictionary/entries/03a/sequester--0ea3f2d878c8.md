### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To separate from all external influence; to seclude; to withdraw.

   **Example:** The jury was sequestered from the press by the judge's order.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To separate in order to store.

   **Example:** The coal burning plant was ordered to sequester its CO₂ emissions.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To set apart; to put aside; to remove; to separate from other things.

   **Source synonyms:** allocate; appropriate; shelve; assign; dedicate; detach; designate; earmark; preselect; put aside; secern (archaic); secernate (archaic); separate (obsolete); sepose (obsolete); sequester; set apart; set aside

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To prevent an ion in solution from behaving normally by forming a coordination compound.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To temporarily remove (property) from the possession of its owner and hold it as security against legal claims.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To cause (one) to submit to the process of sequestration; to deprive (one) of one's estate, property, etc.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To remove (certain funds) automatically from a budget.

   **Usage:** US; transitive

   **Example:** The Budget Control Act of 2011 sequestered 1.2 trillion dollars over 10 years on January 2, 2013.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; law; politics

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To seize and hold enemy property.

   **Usage:** international law

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To withdraw; to retire.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To renounce (as a widow may) any concern with the estate of her husband.

   **Usage:** dated
