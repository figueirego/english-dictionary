### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Sameness, identicalness; the quality or fact of (several specified things) being the same.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Benjamin Franklin established the identity of electricity and lightning.

   **Source context (verbatim):** in technical usage

   **Source synonyms:** identicalness; identicality; sameness; identicality; identicalness; identity; sameliness; sameness; uniformity

   **Source antonyms:** unidenticality; nonidentity; difference; differentiation; differentness; distinction; distinguishedness; nonidentity; unidenticality; diversity

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The difference or character that marks off an individual or collective from the rest of the same kind; selfhood; the sense of who something or someone or oneself is, or the recurring characteristics that enable the recognition of such an individual or group by others or themselves.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I've been through so many changes, I have no sense of identity.

   **Example:** This nation has a strong identity.

   **Source synonyms:** individuality; beinghood; identity; individuality; ipseity; personality; personhood; personness; selfdom (archaic); selfhood; selfness; selfship

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A name or persona—a mask or appearance one presents to the world—by which one is known.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** This criminal has taken on several identities.

   **Example:** In this show, the competitor's identity will remain secret until after the vote.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An equation which always holds true regardless of the choice of input variables.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The equation (x+y)(x−y) = x²−y² is an algebraic identity. It is true regardless of the values of x and y.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Any function which maps all elements of its domain to themselves.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** algebra; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** identity function

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An element of an algebraic structure which, when applied to another element under an operation in that structure, yields this second element.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Zero is the identity for the addition of real numbers.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** algebra; mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A well-known or famous person.

   **Usage:** Australia; New-Zealand; countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** celebrity; personality
