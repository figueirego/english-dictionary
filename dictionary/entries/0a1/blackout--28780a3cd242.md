### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A temporary loss of consciousness.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** blankout; faint; syncope

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A temporary loss of memory.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** blankout

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A large-scale power failure, and resulting loss of electricity to consumers.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** power cut; outage

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An instance of censorship, especially a temporary one.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** media blackout

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An intentional outage of a website or other online service, typically as a form of protest.

   **Usage:** Internet; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** the 2012 English Wikipedia blackout

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The mandatory blocking of all light emanating from buildings, as well as outdoor and street lighting as a measure against aerial bombing or naval attack, as imposed during, e.g., World War II.

   **Usage:** countable; historical; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The blocking out of as much light as possible.

   **Usage:** attributive; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** blackout blinds; blackout curtains

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A mass murder committed, usually in an urban area, to eliminate potential witnesses of a previous crime.

   **Usage:** countable; rare; slang; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** criminology; human-sciences; law; sciences
