### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make something internal; to incorporate it in oneself.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

   **Example:** You are not going to get good at socialising by approaching social situations like it's a formula where you do 1, 2 and 3 from a book, but books can definitely help you internalize useful things if you get what I'm saying.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make something internal; to incorporate it in oneself.
   1. To process new information in one's mind.

      **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make something internal; to incorporate it in oneself.
   1. To refrain from expressing (a negative emotion), to one's psychological detriment; to bottle up.

      **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To store (a string or other structure) in a shared pool, such that subsequent items with the same value can share the same instance.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; programming; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** intern

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance
