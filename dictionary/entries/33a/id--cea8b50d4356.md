## Suffix · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɪd/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- -ide — alternative; archaic

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Forming the names of dynasts, being suffixed to the name of their progenitors and meaning “descendant of”.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** Perseus + -id → Perseid (dynast)

   **Example:** Solomon + -id → Solomonid

   **Example:** Umayya + -(i)d → Umayyad

   **Example:** Genghis + -id → Genghisid

   **Example:** Osman + -id → Osmanid

   **Example:** \[Ismail\] Safavi + -(i)d → Safavid

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** history; human-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Forming the common names of members of a taxon which has a name ending in -idae.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** A felid is a member of Felidae.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; natural-sciences; taxonomy

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Forming the names of cusps or other features of lower (mandibular) teeth.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** protocone (“cusp of upper molars”) + -id → protoconid (“equivalent for lower molars”)

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; dentistry; history; human-sciences; medicine; natural-sciences; paleontology; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Forming nouns from Latin or Greek roots, including certain plant names modelled on Latin sources.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; botany; natural-sciences

## Suffix · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɪd/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- -ide — alternative; archaic

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. Forming names of feminine equivalents or of feminine descendants from a masculine name.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** Titan + -id → Titanid, literally “female Titan”

   **Example:** Nereus + -id → Nereid, literally “sprung of Nereus”

   **Example:** Oceanus + -id → Oceanid, literally “sprung of Oceanus”

   **Example:** Perseus + -id → Perseid, literally “sprung of Perseus” (an epithet of Alcmene, grand-daughter of Perseus)

   **Example:** Danaus + -id → Danaid, literally “sprung of Danaus”

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; mysticism; mythology; philosophy; sciences

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. Forming the names of epic poems.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** Aeneas + -id → Aeneid

   **Example:** Achilles + -id → Achilleid

   **Example:** Perseus + -id → Perseid (epic poem)

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** literature; media; publishing

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Forming common names of meteors from their apparent constellation of origin.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** Perseus + -id → Perseid (meteor)

   **Example:** Gemini + -id → Geminid

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɪd/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- -ide — alternative; archaic

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. of or pertaining to; appended to various foreign words to make an English adjective or noun form. Often added to words of Greek, sometimes Latin, origin.

   **Usage:** morpheme; not productive outside zoology

   **Example:** arachno- + -id → arachnid

   **Example:** falcon + -id → falconid

   **Source synonyms:** -al; -an; -ite; -ar; -ese; -ic; -ish; -like; -oid; -ory; -ous; -y
