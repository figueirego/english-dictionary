## Verb

### Forms

- wears out — present; singular; third-person
- wearing out — participle; present
- wore out — past
- worn out — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To cause (something) to become damaged, useless, or ineffective through continued use, especially hard, heavy, or careless use.

   **Example:** You're going to wear out that game if you keep playing so rough with it.

   **Example:** He wears a pair of tennis shoes out every summer.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To deteriorate or become unusable or ineffective due to continued use, exposure, or strain.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The old tractor finally wore out.

   **Example:** My shoes wear out quickly now that I walk to work.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To exhaust; to cause or contribute to another's exhaustion, fatigue, or weariness, as by continued strain or exertion.

   **Example:** You kids are wearing me out!

   **Example:** Trying to keep up with politics wears me out.

   **Example:** Our physical advantage allowed us to wear the other team out and win.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become exhausted, tired, fatigued, or weary, as by continued strain or exertion.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I'm wearing out, guys. Time to go to sleep.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Of apparel, to display in public.

   **Example:** Those sweatpants are great for loafing around the house, but they're not meant to be worn out.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Of a shirt, to not tuck into the pants; to wear in a casual manner.

   **Example:** A dress shirt should be tucked in, but a t-shirt can be worn out.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To punish by spanking.

   **Usage:** Southern-US
