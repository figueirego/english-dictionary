### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To grant citizenship to someone not born a citizen.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To acclimatize an animal or plant.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make natural.

   **Example:** Custom naturalizes labour or study.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To limit explanations of a phenomenon to naturalistic ones and exclude supernatural ones.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To make (a word) a natural part of the language, using the native homologue of each phoneme (and often for each morpheme) of the imported word (e.g., native inflections).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In English, foreign words are typically written in italics until they are naturalized.

   **Example:** English speakers have naturalized the French word "café".

   **Example:** English orthography often (but not invariably) drops the diacritics from words that it has naturalized from other languages.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** paronymize

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To study nature.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive
