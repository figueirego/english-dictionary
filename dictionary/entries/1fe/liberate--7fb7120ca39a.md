   1. To free (someone or something) from discriminatory or oppressive attitudes, inhibition, or restraint.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** You need to free your mind and liberate yourself from prejudice.

      **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Often followed by from&#58; to allow or cause (someone or something) to be free; to set free, to release.
   1. To free (a place such as a country, or the residents thereof) from an occupying force, or from oppression such as unjust rule.

      **Usage:** ironic; sometimes; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Often followed by from&#58; to allow or cause (someone or something) to be free; to set free, to release.
   1. To cause (energy or a substance) to be released through a chemical reaction or physical decomposition.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** Since the reaction liberates a large amount of chlorine gas, a powerful ventilation system is recommended.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To take (property belonging to someone else) by force or theft; to loot, to rob, to steal.

   **Usage:** euphemistic; informal; transitive

   **Example:** We didn’t need IDs. We just liberated these beers from the back of the shop.
