   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** said of people towards animals

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To rise up on the hind legs.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The horse was shocked, and thus reared.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To get angry.

   **Usage:** intransitive; usually

   **Example:** The skunk reared up on its hind legs when it faced a fox.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with "up"

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To rise high above, tower above.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To raise physically or metaphorically; to lift up; to cause to rise, to elevate.

   **Usage:** literary; transitive

   **Example:** Poverty reared its ugly head. (appeared, started, began to have an effect)

   **Example:** The monster slowly reared its head.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To construct by building; to set up

   **Usage:** rare; transitive

   **Example:** to rear defenses or houses

   **Example:** to rear one government on the ruins of another.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To raise spiritually; to lift up; to elevate morally.

   **Usage:** rare; transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To lift and take up.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**
