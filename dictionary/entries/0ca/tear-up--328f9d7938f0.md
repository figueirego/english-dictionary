### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To tear into pieces.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The student tore up his test after he found out his mark of 20%.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cancel or annul, or to cause the cancellation or annulment of (e.g. an agreement or contract).

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** I finally persuaded the landlord to tear up the lease and move to a month-by-month rental.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To damage.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** The lacrosse practice really tore up the field.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To succeed dramatically in (an area of endeavor) or against.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** In his first year, his hitting tore up the league's opposing pitchers.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To wrench out of the ground.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We tore up the weeds from the allotment.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To have intense penetrative sex with.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive; vulgar

   **Example:** Chad tore up Stacy after that party.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To brutally assault.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** Jason tore up Todd after finding out that he was having sex with his sister.
