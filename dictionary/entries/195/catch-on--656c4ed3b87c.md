### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Chiefly in the form catch oneself on&#58; to cause (oneself) to get or regain a proper appreciation of a situation; to behave in the expected, sensible way; to stop fooling around or being silly.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; transitive (reflexive); Ireland (especially Northern Ireland); transitive (reflexive); Ireland (especially Northern Ireland)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Chiefly followed by to&#58; to cling or hold on to something; to get stuck on something; to take hold.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Chiefly followed by to&#58; to cling or hold on to something; to get stuck on something; to take hold.
   1. Of food being cooked&#58; to become stuck to the cooking vessel and brown or burn slightly.

      **Usage:** New-England; dated; idiomatic; intransitive

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** cooking; food; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Of an idea, a thing, etc.&#58; to become fashionable or popular; also, to become established as standard; to become commonplace.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** It’s a crummy idea, and I certainly hope it doesn’t catch on.

   **Example:** At first, many people didn’t like that kind of music, but after a while it caught on.

   **Source antonyms:** go out of fashion; go the way of the dinosaurs

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Followed by to&#58; to recognize the potential of something; also, to take advantage of or use something (especially an idea, a trend, etc.).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Chiefly followed by to&#58; to (begin to) understand; to realize.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; intransitive

   **Example:** He didn’t have to explain; I caught on right away.

   **Example:** She’s been catching on pretty well.

   **Source synonyms:** cotton on; glom on
