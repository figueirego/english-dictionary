### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A violin, a small unfretted stringed instrument with four strings tuned (lowest to highest) G-D-A-E, usually held against the chin, shoulder, chest or on the upper thigh and played with a bow (see also usage notes below).

   **Example:** When I play it like this, it's a fiddle; when I play it like that, it's a violin.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any of various other bowed stringed instruments, particularly those of the violin family when played non-classically.

   **Usage:** proscribed; usually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A violinist, or fiddler, in a band.

   **Example:** He is first fiddle in the band.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Something resembling a violin, or fiddle, in shape
   1. A dock (Rumex pulcher) with leaves supposed to resemble the musical instrument.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Something resembling a violin, or fiddle, in shape
   1. A long pole pulled by a draft animal to drag loose straw, hay, etc.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Something resembling a violin, or fiddle, in shape
   1. A rack for drying pottery after glazing.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A clown; an unserious person entertaining a group.

   **Usage:** figuratively

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈfɪd(ə)l/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- fiddling — participle; present
- fiddled — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To play the fiddle or violin, particularly in a folk or country style.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** to fiddle while Rome burns

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To fraudulently manipulate (records, accounts, etc.) in order to cheat or swindle.

   **Usage:** informal; transitive

   **Example:** Fred was sacked when the auditors caught him fiddling the books.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To fidget or play; to fuss; to idly amuse oneself, to act aimlessly, idly, or frivolously, particularly out of nervousness or restlessness; see also fiddle with.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Sit up straight and stop fiddling!

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. Synonym of tinker (“to make small adjustments or improvements”); see also fiddle with.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** I don't exactly know how to fix this lawnmower; I'm really just fiddling.

   **Source synonyms:** tinker (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To do odd jobs for money.

   **Usage:** UK; intransitive; obsolete; slang
