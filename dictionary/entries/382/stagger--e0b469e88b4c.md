## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈstæɡə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈstæɡɚ/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˈsteɪ̯ɡɚ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- staggers — present; singular; third-person
- staggering — participle; present
- staggered — participle; past
- staggered — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To sway unsteadily, reel, or totter.
   1. In standing or walking, to sway from one side to the other as if about to fall; to stand or walk unsteadily; to reel or totter.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** She began to stagger across the room.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To sway unsteadily, reel, or totter.
   1. To cause to reel or totter.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** The powerful blow of his opponent's fist staggered the boxer.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To sway unsteadily, reel, or totter.
   1. To cease to stand firm; to begin to give way; to fail.

      **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. Doubt, waver, be shocked.
   1. To begin to doubt and waver in purposes; to become less confident or determined; to hesitate.

      **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. Doubt, waver, be shocked.
   1. To cause to doubt and waver; to make to hesitate; to make less steady or confident; to shock.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** He will stagger the committee when he presents his report.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. Have multiple groups doing the same thing in a uniform fashion, but starting at different, evenly spaced, times or places (attested from 1856).
   1. To arrange (a series of parts) on each side of a median line alternately, as the spokes of a wheel or the rivets of a boiler seam.

      **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. Have multiple groups doing the same thing in a uniform fashion, but starting at different, evenly spaced, times or places (attested from 1856).
   1. To arrange similar objects such that each is ahead or above and to one side of the next.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** We will stagger the starting positions for the race on the oval track.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. Have multiple groups doing the same thing in a uniform fashion, but starting at different, evenly spaced, times or places (attested from 1856).
   1. To schedule in intervals or at different times.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** We will stagger the run so the faster runners can go first, then the joggers.
