### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A general title of respect for an adult male.
   1. With a surname.

      **Example:** This is Mister Smith, assistant to the President.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A general title of respect for an adult male.
   1. With a full name.

      **Example:** This is Mister James Smith, assistant to the President.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A general title of respect for an adult male.
   1. With a first name only.

      **Usage:** regional or dated subservient

      **Example:** Will Mister Robert be staying for dinner?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An official form of address to a male president of a nation.

   **Example:** Mister President

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A formal address to any male official of an organization.

   **Example:** Mister Secretary

   **Example:** Mister Treasurer

   **Example:** Mister Attorney

   **Example:** Mister Justice

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An official title of a military man, usually anyone below rank of captain.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A male warrant officer or cadet in the United States Military Academy at West Point.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An informal title that is used to create a nickname, placeholder name, or other moniker for a male.

   **Example:** Mister Suave

   **Example:** He's Mister Baseball around here.

   **Example:** Hey, Mister magenta-shirt over there looks a lot like your cousin Bill.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A title used to designate the (male) winner of certain kinds of competition.

   **Example:** The Mister Universe finals will be held in Los Angeles.

   **Example:** And now I can reveal that this year's Mister Personality is ... Stanley Cheeseworth!

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Used by itself as a familiar term of address to a man whose name is unknown, or sometimes even if the name is known.

   **Usage:** colloquial; sometimes hostile; sometimes written 'mister'; sometimes hostile; sometimes written 'mister'

   **Example:** 'Scuse me Mister, do you have the time?

   **Example:** Now you listen to me, Mister. If I see you round here again, there'll be trouble.
