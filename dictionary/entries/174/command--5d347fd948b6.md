### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To order, to give orders; to compel or direct with authority.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** The soldier was commanded to cease firing.

   **Example:** The king commanded his servant to bring him dinner.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To have or exercise supreme power, control, or authority (especially military over; to have under direction or control.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** to command an army or a ship

   **Source synonyms:** lead; captain

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To require with authority; to demand, order, enjoin.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** he commanded silence

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. to dominate through ability, resources, position, etc.; to overlook.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** This observation deck commands a breathtaking view of San Francisco Bay.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To exact, compel, or secure by influence; to deserve, claim.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** A good magistrate commands the respect and affections of the people.

   **Example:** Justice commands the respect and affections of the people.

   **Example:** The best goods command the best price.

   **Example:** This job commands a salary of £30,000.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To hold; to control the use of.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The fort commanded the bay.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To have a view (of), as from a superior position.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; archaic

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**
