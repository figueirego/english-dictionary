### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To close (remove a gap) completely or fully.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Some flowers close up at night to stay protected from the cold.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To move nearer together so that a gap is removed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The crowd closed up and I couldn't get through to the train.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To enclose, confine.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To shut a building or a business for a period of time.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** We finally managed to close up the shop for the night at about 10 o'clock.

   **Example:** The car factory has closed up for the August holidays.

   **Source synonyms:** close

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Of a cut or other wound&#58; To heal.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** With stitches, the cut should close up in a week to ten days.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To become less 'open' or communicative; to shrink back.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** to close up emotionally

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To stop talking.

   **Usage:** Australia; intransitive; slang
