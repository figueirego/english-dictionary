### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A thicket of bushes or other shrubbery, especially one planted as a fence between two portions of land, or to separate the parts of a garden.

   **Example:** He trims the hedge once a week.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A barrier (often consisting of a line of persons or objects) to protect someone or something from harm.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A mound of earth, stone- or turf-faced, often topped with bushes, used as a fence between any two portions of land.

   **Usage:** Cornwall; Devon; UK; West-Country

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A noncommittal or intentionally ambiguous statement.

   **Usage:** especially applied linguistics and pragmatics

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Contract or arrangement reducing one's exposure to risk (for example the risk of price movements or interest rate movements).

   **Example:** The asset class acts as a hedge.

   **Example:** A hedge is an investment position intended to offset potential losses/gains that may be incurred by a companion investment. In simple language, a hedge is used to reduce any substantial losses/gains suffered by an individual or an organization.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. With indication of a person's upbringing, or professional activities, taking place by the side of the road; being third-rate, poor, shoddy.

   **Usage:** Ireland; UK; attributive; figuratively

   **Example:** He then traced them from place to place, till at last he found two of them drinking together, with a third person, at a hedge-tavern near Aldersgate.
