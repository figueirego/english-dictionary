### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To reconcile at the end of a shift; to compare receipts of items sold to records of credit card, check and cash placed into the drawer, verifying that correct change was given out by the clerk.

   **Usage:** US

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To retire; to exchange gambling chips for money when finished gambling.

   **Usage:** US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** gambling; games

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To exchange possession of any commodity or idea for cash.

   **Usage:** broadly

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To explain what is entailed by an idea or proposition.

   **Usage:** broadly; often in analytic philosophy

   **Example:** Hume cashed out the concept of causation into such experiential terms as spatiotemporal contiguity, temporal succession, and constant conjunction.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To give up on something.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** If you can't even succeed with him, you might as well cash out.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** cash in — reconcile at end of shift; cash up — reconcile at end of shift
