### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Something homologous; a homologous organ or part, chemical compound, chromosome, gene, or cultural element.
   1. A phoneme, morpheme, or word shared by two languages or dialects, via cognation or naturalization.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Something homologous; a homologous organ or part, chemical compound, chromosome, gene, or cultural element.
   1. One of a group of similar DNA sequences that share a common ancestry, or the peptides or proteins that they encode; the counterpart gene or protein in another taxon evolved from a common ancestor (molecularly it may be either identical or similar).

      **Example:** TP53 is the human homolog of murine Trp53.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; genetics; medicine; natural-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something homologous; a homologous organ or part, chemical compound, chromosome, gene, or cultural element.
   1. A member of a homologous series.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; organic-chemistry; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Something homologous; a homologous organ or part, chemical compound, chromosome, gene, or cultural element.
   1. A belief, practice, concept, or artifact that has a counterpart in another culture.

      **Usage:** cultural anthropology
