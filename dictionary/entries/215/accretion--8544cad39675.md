### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Increase by natural growth, especially the gradual increase of organic bodies by the internal addition of matter; organic growth; also, the amount of such growth.

   **Usage:** also; figuratively; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** accrescence

   **Source antonyms:** nonaccretion

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. (Gradual) increase by an external addition of matter; (countable) an instance of this.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** A mineral augments not by growth, but by accretion.

   **Source synonyms:** accrescence; accumulation

   **Source antonyms:** nonaccretion

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. (Gradual) increase by an external addition of matter; (countable) an instance of this.
   1. The process by which material is added to a geological feature; specifically, to a tectonic plate at a subduction zone.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; geology; natural-sciences

      **Source synonyms:** accrescence; accumulation

      **Source antonyms:** nonaccretion; attrition; erosion

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Followed by of&#58; external addition of matter to a thing which causes it to grow, especially in amount or size.

   **Usage:** also; figuratively; uncountable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The process of separate particles aggregating or coalescing together; concretion; (countable) a thing formed in this manner.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** The accretion of particles forms a solid mass.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The process of separate particles aggregating or coalescing together; concretion; (countable) a thing formed in this manner.
   1. The formation of planets, stars, and other celestial bodies by the aggregating of matter drawn together by gravity; also, the growth of a celestial body through this process.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astrophysics

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Something gradually added to or growing on a thing externally.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively

   **Example:** accretion of ice

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Something gradually added to or growing on a thing externally.
   1. A substance which has built up on the surface of an object, rather than become embedded in it.

      **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

      **Example:** Accretions of dirt on clothing are often left in place by conservators because they can provide additional details about the artefact’s importance or history.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Increase in property by the addition of other property to it (for example, gain of land by alluvion (“the deposition of sediment by a river or sea”) or dereliction (“recession of water from the usual watermark”), or entitlement to the products of the property such as interest on money); or by the property owner acquiring another person’s ownership rights; accession; (countable) an instance of this.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law; property

   **Source synonyms:** accrual; accruer

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Increase of an inheritance to an heir or legatee due to the share of a co-heir or co-legatee being added to it, because the latter person is legally unable to inherit the share.

   **Usage:** uncountable; inheritance law
