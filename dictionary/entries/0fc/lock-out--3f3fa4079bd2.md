### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To inadvertently prevent (a person, particularly oneself) from entering a place, by leaving it locked.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I've locked myself out of my room again!

   **Source antonyms:** let in; lock in

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To prevent from accessing a data structure.

   **Example:** The synchronize keyword locks out all other threads trying to access the object.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. (industrial) To prevent (a person, including oneself) from powering up or turning on a machine when doing so would be dangerous (accomplished with objects such as padlocks or zip ties placed through the switch handle, for example)

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. (colloquial, sometimes humorous) To be unable to concentrate.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Bro, I am totally locked out on this assignment.
