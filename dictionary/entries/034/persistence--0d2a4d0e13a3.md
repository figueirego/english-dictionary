### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The property of being persistent.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** You've got to admire her persistence. She's asked him out every day for a month even though she keeps turning him down.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Of data, the property of continuing to exist after the termination of the program.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Once written to a disk file, the data has persistence&#58; it will still be there tomorrow when we run the next program.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computer; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; science; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Continuation of the previous day's weather (particularly temperature and precipitation statistics).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** climatology; meteorology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The number of times an operation can be iteratively applied to a number before it reaches a permanently constant state.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The persistence of the number 39 under the operation of multiplying the digits of the number is three, because 3x9 = 27, 2x7 = 14, and 1x4 = 4, and no further iterations will change the number again.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences
