### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Any activity designed to develop or hone a skill or ability.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The teacher told us that the next exercise is to write an essay.

   **Source synonyms:** practice; practicing; drill; drilling

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Activity intended to improve physical, or sometimes mental, strength and fitness.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I like to do my exercises every morning before breakfast. I do calisthenics for physical exercise, and I do crosswords for mental exercise.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Activity intended to improve physical, or sometimes mental, strength and fitness.
   1. Such activity of physical nature, for physical fitness (plus also mental benefit).

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** Swimming is good exercise.

      **Example:** She does stomach exercises every day.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Activity intended to improve physical, or sometimes mental, strength and fitness.
   1. Such activity of mental nature, for neurocognitive health.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** Such exercises as puzzles and chess can help keep the mind sharp as we age.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A setting in action or practicing; employment in the proper mode of activity; exertion; application; use.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The law guarantees us the free exercise of our rights.

   **Example:** We urge the exercise of patience and restraint.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The performance of an office, ceremony, or duty.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I assisted the ailing vicar in the exercise of his parish duties.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. That which gives practice; a trial; a test.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To exert for the sake of training or improvement; to practice in order to develop.

   **Example:** to exercise troops or horses; to exercise one's brain with a puzzle

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To perform physical activity for health or training.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I exercise at the gym every day.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To use (a right, an option, etc.); to put into practice.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** exercise caution

   **Example:** The tenant exercised his option to renew the tenancy.

   **Example:** She is going to exercise her right to vote.

   **Source synonyms:** assert

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To occupy the attention and effort of; to task; to tax, especially in a painful or vexatious manner; harass; to vex; to worry or make anxious.

   **Usage:** now often passive voice

   **Example:** exercised with pain

   **Example:** It is not a question that has exercised me greatly in the past.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To set in action; to cause to act, move, or make exertion; to give employment to.

   **Usage:** obsolete
