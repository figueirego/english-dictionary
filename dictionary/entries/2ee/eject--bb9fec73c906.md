### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To compel (a person or persons) to leave.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The man started a fight and was ejected from the bar.

   **Example:** Andrew was ejected from his apartment for not paying the rent.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To throw out or remove forcefully.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In other news, a Montreal man was ejected from his car when he was involved in an accident.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To compel (a sports player) to leave the field because of inappropriate behaviour.

   **Usage:** US; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To forcefully project oneself or another occupant from an aircraft (or, rarely, another type of vehicle), typically using an ejection seat or escape capsule.

   **Usage:** intransitive; usually

   **Example:** The pilot lost control of the plane and had to eject.

   **Example:** As the crippled jet spiralled down, the pilot pulled the escape handle, ejecting first his rear-seater, then himself.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To cause (something) to come out of a machine.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Press that button to eject the video tape.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To cause (something) to come out of a machine.
   1. To unmount (a drive).

      **Usage:** transitive; by analogy

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To come out of a machine.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I can't get this cassette to eject.
