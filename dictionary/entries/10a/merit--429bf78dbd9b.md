### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A claim to commendation or a reward.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A mark or token of approbation or to recognize excellence.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** For her good performance in the examination, her teacher gave her ten merits.

   **Source antonyms:** demerit

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something deserving or worthy of positive recognition or reward.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** His reward for his merit was a check for $50.

   **Source synonyms:** excellence; value; worth

   **Source antonyms:** demerit

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The sum of all the good deeds that a person does which determines the quality of the person's next state of existence and contributes to the person's growth towards enlightenment.

   **Usage:** Jainism; uncountable

   **Example:** to acquire or make merit

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Buddhism; lifestyle; religion

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Usually in the plural form the merits&#58; the substantive rightness or wrongness of a legal argument, a lawsuit, etc., as opposed to technical matters such as the admissibility of evidence or points of legal procedure; (by extension) the overall good or bad quality, or rightness or wrongness, of some other thing.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Even though the plaintiff was ordered by the judge to pay some costs for not having followed the correct procedure, she won the case on the merits.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The quality or state of deserving retribution, whether reward or punishment.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete
