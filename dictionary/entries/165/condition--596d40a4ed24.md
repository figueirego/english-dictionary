## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /kənˈdɪʃ(ə)n/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- conditions — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A state or quality.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** National reports on the condition of public education are dismal.

   **Example:** The condition of man can be classified as civilized or uncivilized.

   **Source synonyms:** way

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A state or quality.
   1. A particular state of being.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** Hypnosis is a peculiar condition of the nervous system.

      **Example:** Steps were taken to ameliorate the condition of slavery.

      **Example:** Security is defined as the condition of not being threatened.

      **Example:** Aging is a condition over which we are powerless.

      **Source synonyms:** way

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A state or quality.
   1. The situation of a person or persons, particularly their social and/or economic class, rank.

      **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

      **Example:** A man of his condition has no place to make requests.

      **Source synonyms:** way

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A state or quality.
   1. The health status of a medical patient.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** My aunt couldn’t walk up the stairs in her condition.

      **Source synonyms:** way; fettle

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A state or quality.
   1. The health status of a medical patient.
      1. A certain abnormal state of health; a malady or sickness.

         **Usage:** countable; uncountable

         **Source synonyms:** way; fettle

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A requirement or prerequisite.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Environmental protection is a condition for sustainability.

   **Example:** What other planets might have the right conditions for life?

   **Example:** The union had a dispute over sick time and other conditions of employment.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A logical clause or phrase that a conditional statement uses. The phrase can either be true or false.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A clause in a contract or agreement indicating that a certain contingency may modify the principal obligation in some way.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /kənˈdɪʃ(ə)n/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- conditions — present; singular; third-person
- conditioning — participle; present
- conditioned — participle; past
- conditioned — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To subject to the process of acclimation.

   **Example:** I became conditioned to the absence of seasons in San Diego.

   **Example:** Heat pumps condition the air on the first floor.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To subject to different conditions, especially as an exercise.

   **Example:** They were conditioning their shins in their karate class.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To make dependent on a condition to be fulfilled; to make conditional on.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To place conditions or limitations upon.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To shape the behaviour of someone to do something.

   **Example:** The children were conditioned to speak up if they had any disagreements.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To treat, especially hair with hair conditioner.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To contract; to stipulate; to agree.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To test or assay, as silk (to ascertain the proportion of moisture it contains).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To put under conditions; to require to pass a new examination or to make up a specified study, as a condition of remaining in one's class or in college.

   **Usage:** US; transitive

   **Example:** to condition a student who has failed in some branch of study

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** colleges; education

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**

10. To impose upon an object those relations or conditions without which knowledge and thought are alleged to be impossible.
