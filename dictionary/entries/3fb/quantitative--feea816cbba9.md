### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Of a measurements and data types&#58; based on some quantity or number rather than on some quality.

   **Example:** quantitative data

   **Example:** A yes-no data field is qualitative, whereas a number-value data field is quantitative.

   **Source synonyms:** nonqualitative; unqualitative

   **Source antonyms:** nonquantitative; unquantitative

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Of logical analysis&#58; based on quantification as well as qualification, rather than solely the latter&#58; thus, not ignorant of magnitudes (large or small).

   **Example:** quantitative analysis

   **Example:** It's easy to say, qualitatively, that an undesirable thing should be banned and punishable; but the law must often also be quantitative if it is to be fair, which may sometimes mean that some small number of parts per trillion is an acceptable amount.

   **Source antonyms:** nonquantitative; unquantitative

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Of a form of analysis, that determines the amount of some element or compound in a sample.

   **Example:** quantitative analysis

   **Example:** The lab tests that are currently commercially available for this pollutant can tell us a yes-no answer as to whether it was detected, but they cannot provide a quantitative result; a new generation of quantitative assays is under development.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

   **Source antonyms:** nonquantitative; unquantitative
