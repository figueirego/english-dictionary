   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. The change in pronunciation of historically or variably consonant (typically sonorant) sounds as vowels. For example, the syllabic /l/ in words like people or the coda one in words like cold or coal are variably realized as a high back vowel or glide—\[ʊ\], \[u\], \[ɤ\] or \[o\]—in many dialects of English in the US, UK, and the Southern Hemisphere. For example, in African American Vernacular English, one common pronunciation of the words "people", "cold", and "coal" is \[pʰipʊ\], \[kʰoɤd\], or \[kʰoɤ\] respectively.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; phonology; sciences
