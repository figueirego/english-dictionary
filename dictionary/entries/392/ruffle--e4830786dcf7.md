### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make a ruffle in; to curl or flute, as an edge of fabric.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Ruffle the end of the cuff.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To disturb; especially, to cause to flutter.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The wind ruffled the papers.

   **Example:** Her sudden volley of insults ruffled his composure.

   **Source synonyms:** perturb; trouble; unsettle; agitate; freak; sting; bother; bring down; discompose; unstring; disconcert; enturbulate (nonstandard); disturb; mortify (obsolete); perturb; put out; ruffle; throw off balance; unsettle; upset; flurry

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To grow rough, boisterous, or turbulent.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To become disordered; to play loosely; to flutter.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To be rough; to jar; to be in contention; hence, to put on airs; to swagger.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To make into a ruff; to draw or contract into puckers, plaits, or folds; to wrinkle.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To erect in a ruff, as feathers.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To beat with the ruff or ruffle, as a drum.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war
