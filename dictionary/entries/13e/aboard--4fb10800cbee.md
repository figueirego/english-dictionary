## Adverb · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈbɔːd/ — source labels: UK
- IPA: /əˈbɔɹd/ — source labels: US

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. On board; into or within a ship or boat; hence, into or within a railway car.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** We all climbed aboard.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. On or onto a horse, a camel, etc.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** to sling a saddle aboard

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. On base.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** He doubled with two men aboard, scoring them both.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Into a team, group, or company.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** The office manager welcomed him aboard.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Alongside.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** The ships came close aboard to pass messages.

   **Example:** The captain laid his ship aboard the enemy's ship.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport
