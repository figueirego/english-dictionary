### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To rend (a solid material) by holding or restraining in two places and pulling apart, whether intentionally or not; to destroy or separate.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He tore his coat on the nail.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To injure as if by pulling apart.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He tore some muscles in a weight-lifting accident.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To destroy or reduce abstract unity or coherence, such as social, political or emotional.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He was torn by conflicting emotions.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make (an opening) with force or energy.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** A piece of debris tore a tiny straight channel through the satellite.

   **Example:** His boss will tear him a new one when he finds out.

   **Example:** The artillery tore a gap in the line.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To remove by tearing, or with sudden great force.

   **Usage:** often; transitive

   **Example:** Tear the coupon out of the newspaper.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with off or out

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To demolish.

   **Usage:** transitive; with-down

   **Example:** The slums were torn down to make way for the new development.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of structures

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To become torn, especially accidentally.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** My dress has torn.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To move or act with great speed, energy, or violence.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He went tearing down the hill at 90 miles per hour.

   **Example:** The tornado lingered, tearing through town, leaving nothing upright.

   **Example:** He tore into the backlog of complaints.

   **Usage:** intransitive
