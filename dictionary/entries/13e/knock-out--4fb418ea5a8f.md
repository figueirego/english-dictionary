### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To strike or bump (someone or something) out.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** I accidentally knocked out the glass in my picture frame.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To render unconscious, as by a blow to the head.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** The boxer knocked out his opponent in the third round.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To put to sleep.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; transitive

      **Example:** The allergy pill knocked him out for a good three hours.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To fall asleep, especially suddenly.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; intransitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To exhaust.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; transitive

      **Example:** Running errands all day really knocked him out.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To impress, surpass or overwhelm (someone).

      **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To defeat or kill (someone).

      **Usage:** slang; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To cause a mechanism to become non-functional by damaging or destroying it.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** The antitank gun knocked out the enemy tank.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To strike or render unconscious
   1. To eliminate from a contest or similar.

      **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To communicate (a message) by knocking.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The prisoner knocked out a message on the wall for the prisoner in the adjoining cell.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To complete, especially in haste; knock off.

   **Usage:** informal; transitive

   **Example:** They knocked out the entire project in one night.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To lose the scent of hounds in fox-hunting.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To leave college after hours—after half-past ten at night when the doors have been locked.

   **Usage:** obsolete; Oxford University slang

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To obtain or earn (something, often money or food).

   **Usage:** Australia; transitive

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

   **Usage:** UK; slang; transitive
