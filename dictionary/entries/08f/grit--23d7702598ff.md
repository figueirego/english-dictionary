## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɡɹɪt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A collection of hard small materials, such as dirt, ground stone, debris from sandblasting or other such grinding, or swarf from metalworking.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** The flower beds were white with grit from sand blasting the flagstone walkways.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A collection of hard small materials, such as dirt, ground stone, debris from sandblasting or other such grinding, or swarf from metalworking.
   1. Sand or a sand–salt mixture spread on wet and, especially, icy roads and footpaths to improve traction.

      **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Small, hard, inedible particles in food.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** These cookies seem to have grit from nutshells in them.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A measure of the size of abrasive grains, such as those on sandpaper, and thus their relative coarseness or fineness; the smaller the number, the coarser the abrasive&#58; thus, 60 is rough, 600 is fine, and 3000 is ultrafine.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** I need a sheet of 100 grit sandpaper.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A hard, coarse-grained siliceous sandstone; gritstone. Also, a finer sharp-grained sandstone, e.g., grindstone grit.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; geology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Strength of mind; courage or fearlessness; fortitude.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; uncountable

   **Example:** That kid with the cast on his arm has the grit to play dodgeball.
