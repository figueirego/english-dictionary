## Adjective · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /leɪt/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /læɪt/ — source labels: Australia; New-Zealand
- IPA: /let/ — source labels: Scotland
- IPA: /leːt/ — source labels: Wales

### Forms

- later — comparative
- latest — superlative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Near the end of a period of time.

   **Example:** The seedlings appeared to be coming along nicely until a late frost killed them.

   **Example:** The meeting is convened for late morning.

   **Example:** In Southern U.S., "evening" is used for middle to late afternoon.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Specifically, near the end of the day.

   **Example:** It was getting late and I was tired.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Associated with the end of a period.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** Late Latin is less fully inflected than Classical Latin.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Not arriving or occurring until after an expected time.

   **Example:** The flowers were late in blooming because of the prolonged cold weather.

   **Example:** Panos was so late that he arrived at the meeting after Antonio, who had the excuse of being in hospital for most of the night.

   **Example:** The heavy snow made all the trains late.

   **Source synonyms:** tardy

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Levied as a surcharge on a payment which has not arrived by a specified deadline.

   **Example:** The power company suspended late fees during the pandemic.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Not having had an expected menstrual period.

   **Example:** I'm late, honey. Could you buy a test?

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Recently deceased, dead&#58; used particularly when speaking of the dead person's actions while alive. (Generally must be preceded by a possessive or an article, commonly “the”; see usage notes. Can itself only precede the person's name, never follow it.)

   **Usage:** euphemistic; not-comparable

   **Example:** Her late husband had left her well provided for.

   **Example:** Mary was entitled to the crown by her late father’s testament.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Existing or holding some position not long ago, but not now; departed, or gone out of office.

   **Usage:** dated

   **Example:** the late bishop of London

   **Example:** the late administration

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Recent — relative to the noun it modifies.

   **Example:** the late war

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Of a star or class of stars, cooler than the sun.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

**Source synonyms:** late

**Source antonyms:** early; mid; middle

## Adverb · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /leɪt/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /læɪt/ — source labels: Australia; New-Zealand
- IPA: /let/ — source labels: Scotland
- IPA: /leːt/ — source labels: Wales

### Forms

- later — comparative
- latest — superlative

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. After a deadline has passed, past a designated time.

   **Example:** We drove as fast as we could, but we still arrived late.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. Formerly, especially in the context of service in a military unit.

   **Example:** Colonel Easterwood, late of the 34th Carbines, was a guest at the dinner party.

   **Example:** The Hendersons will all be there / Late of Pablo Fanque's Fair / What a scene!

   **Example:** Linda Smith, late of 13 Oxford Street.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. Not long ago; just now, recently.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** belatedly — past a designated time; tardy; erenow — formerly; freshly — not long ago
