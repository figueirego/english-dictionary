### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To dominate by accumulating; to become dominant by accumulating.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** The businessmen rolled up the market by buying most of the major players.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make something into a particular shape, especially cylindrical or fold-like.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The shopkeeper had to roll up the poster to make it easier to carry.

   **Example:** When they told you not to fold, spindle, or mutilate a punchcard, the spindling referred to rolling it up.

   **Example:** He rolled up his shirt sleeves.

   **Source antonyms:** unroll; unscroll; roll down

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make something into a particular shape, especially cylindrical or fold-like.
   1. To pack up into a bundle or bindle.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Source synonyms:** pack up; pack

      **Source antonyms:** unroll; unscroll; roll down; unroll; unpack

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make something into a particular shape, especially cylindrical or fold-like.
   1. To create a cigar or cigarette, or a joint.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** The audacity of that man rolling up in court was astounding.

      **Source antonyms:** unroll; unscroll; roll down

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To raise (a car window, rolling door, or rolling security barrier).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The shopkeeper had to roll up the security barrier to open the shop.

   **Source synonyms:** wind up

   **Source antonyms:** roll down; wind down

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To roll the dice necessary to create a character for a game, especially a role-playing game.

   **Usage:** intransitive; roleplaying games

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To arrive by vehicle, usually by car.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** We thought Jim would be late for the wedding, but then we saw him roll up in front of the church in his Mercedes.

   **Example:** Don't be rolling up to my door without calling ahead.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To swap out for a higher strike.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an option
