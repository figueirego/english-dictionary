### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To ooze or pass slowly through pores or other small openings, and in overly small quantities; said of liquids, etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Water has seeped through the roof.

   **Example:** The water steadily seeped in through the thirl.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To enter or penetrate slowly; to spread or diffuse.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** Woe seeped through her heart thinking of what had befallen their ethnic group.

   **Example:** Fear began to seep into the local community over the contamination of their fishpond.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To diminish or wane away slowly.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** The resistance movement against the invaders had slowly seeped away.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. (of a crack etc.) To allow a liquid to pass through, to leak.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The crack is seeping water.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To soak.

   **Usage:** Scotland
