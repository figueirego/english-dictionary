### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To specify, especially in detail.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** They call out 304 stainless steel in the drawing, but the part was made from aluminum.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To order into service; to summon into service.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** The Governor called out the National Guard.

   **Source synonyms:** summon

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To yell out; to vocalize audibly; announce.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To challenge (someone).
   1. To challenge (someone) to a duel.

      **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To challenge (someone).
   1. To challenge (someone) to a fight.

      **Usage:** slang; transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To challenge (someone).
   1. To criticize or denounce (someone); to bring up previously unspoken criticisms about (someone); to point out (someone's) faults.

      **Usage:** colloquial; idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** He was very insulting. Finally Jack called him out and shut him up.

      **Example:** She called them out on their lies.

      **Source synonyms:** denounce; point out; charge

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To contact one's workplace and announce that one is unable to attend work. Regionalism short for call out sick; much more commonly&#58; call in sick.

   **Usage:** New-Jersey; New-York; intransitive; Connecticut
