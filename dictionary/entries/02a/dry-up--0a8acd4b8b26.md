## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To become dry (often of weather); to lose water.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I'll go shopping when it dries up.

   **Example:** Last summer the lake completely dried up.

   **Source synonyms:** desiccate; dehydrate

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause to become dry.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The heatwave dried up all the rivers.

   **Source synonyms:** desiccate; dehydrate

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To manually dry dishes and utensils.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; intransitive

   **Example:** I'll dry up if you wash up.

   **Source synonyms:** dry; wipe up

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To deprive someone of (something vital).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The bankruptcy rumor dried up his sales.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To gradually decrease and eventually cease.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** When our money dried up, we had to get proper jobs.

   **Example:** After the stock market crash, the easy financing dried up.

   **Source synonyms:** wither away

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To stop talking because one has forgotten what one was going to say.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** This surprised me so much that I dried up for a moment.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To stop talking because one has forgotten what one was going to say.
   1. To forget one's lines.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Source context (verbatim):** of an actor

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To stop talking or drop a topic.

   **Usage:** 1930s US slang

   **Example:** Oh, dry up, you old fuddy-duddy!

   **Source synonyms:** shut up; clam up
