### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To deploy or release (a new film or software, etc.); to launch (a product or service), especially in a gradual fashion across multiple regions.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** We plan to roll out the new version in September.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To leave a place at a leisurely pace.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Source antonyms:** roll in

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see roll, out.

   **Example:** The cook rolled out the dough with a rolling pin.

   **Example:** They rolled out the red carpet to welcome the visiting dignitaries.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To use up too much energy when first bowled and to therefore not finish strongly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** bowling; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a ball

   **Source synonyms:** burn up; burn out

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To swap out for a farther expiration date while keeping the same strike.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an option
