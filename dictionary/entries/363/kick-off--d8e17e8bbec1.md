## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make the first kick in a game or part of a game.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** The players kick off for the third quarter and the clock starts.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To start; to launch; to set in motion.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; idiomatic

   **Example:** Let's kick off this project with a planning meeting.

   **Example:** The project kicked off with an energy-sapping meeting.

   **Source synonyms:** start off

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To dismiss; to expel; to remove from a position.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I got kicked off the team after a string of poor performances

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To die or quit permanently.

   **Usage:** US; colloquial; euphemistic; idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** It's a wonder that old dog hasn't kicked off yet.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To shut down or turn off suddenly.

   **Usage:** US; idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** The washer was working fine until it kicked off in the middle of a cycle.

   **Example:** The circuit breaker, a power failure, and the e-stop button are the only things we can think of that might have caused that pump to kick off when it did.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To suddenly become more active.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** The party kicked off when the third bottle of wine was opened.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To force the weaning of a bovine cow's calf by restricting the calf's access to its mother's udders, whether by literally kicking it away or another method.

   **Usage:** US; idiomatic; slang; transitive; ranching

   **Example:** A week after we kicked off her calf that cow was still bawling.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To be overcome with anger, to start an argument or a fight; to behave aggressively.

   **Usage:** UK; colloquial; idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** When she called him a drunk, it was the last straw. He just kicked off.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To have a fight or argument start; to fight or argue.

   **Usage:** colloquial; idiomatic; impersonal; intransitive

   **Example:** It really kicked off in town when the team lost.
