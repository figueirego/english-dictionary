### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A form of the letter e modified by the addition of a diacritical “tail”&#58; ⟨ę⟩.
   1. Used in Latin for a long ē that represents an etymological ⟨ae⟩ or ⟨oe⟩ diphthong, both of which diphthongs had phonologically merged into ⟨ē⟩ by the early Mediaeval period.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A form of the letter e modified by the addition of a diacritical “tail”&#58; ⟨ę⟩.
   1. Used in Middle and Early Modern Irish for ⟨e⟩, ⟨ae⟩, and ⟨ea⟩.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A form of the letter e modified by the addition of a diacritical “tail”&#58; ⟨ę⟩.
   1. Used in Old Norse for /æ(ː)/, representing the Proto-Germanic \*a (as opposed to the Proto-Germanic \*e).
