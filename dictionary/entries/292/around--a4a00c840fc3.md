## Preposition · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈɹaʊnd/ — source labels: Canada; General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /əˈɹuːnd/ — source labels: Northumbria
- IPA: /əˈɹæɔnd/ — source labels: Australia
- IPA: /əˈɹæʊnd/ — source labels: New-Zealand

### Forms

- round — alternative
- arownd — alternative; obsolete
- ron — alternative
- 'round — alternative; contraction

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Forming a circle or closed curve containing (something).

   **Example:** She wore a gold chain around her neck.

   **Example:** I planted a row of lilies around the statue.

   **Example:** The jackals began to gather around the carcass.

   **Source synonyms:** environ (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Centred upon; surrounding; regarding.

   **Example:** We could use some clarification around the new rules.

   **Example:** There has been a lot of controversy around the handling of personal information.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of abstract things

   **Source synonyms:** environ (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Following the perimeter of a specified area and returning to the starting point.

   **Example:** We walked around the football field.

   **Example:** She went around the track fifty times.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Following a path which curves near an object, with the object on the inside of the curve.

   **Example:** The road took a brief detour around the large rock formation, then went straight on.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Near; in the vicinity of; approximately.

   **Example:** I left my keys somewhere around here.

   **Example:** I left the house around 10 this morning.

   **Example:** I don't want you around me.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. At or to various places within or throughout.

   **Example:** The pages from the notebook were scattered around the room.

   **Example:** Those teenagers like to hang around the mall.

   **Example:** She went around the office and got everyone to sign the card.

   **Example:** The estate agent showed me around the property.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. So as to avoid something.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** Our product designs around the existing patents to avoid infringing them.

## Adverb · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈɹaʊnd/ — source labels: Canada; General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /əˈɹuːnd/ — source labels: Northumbria
- IPA: /əˈɹæɔnd/ — source labels: Australia
- IPA: /əˈɹæʊnd/ — source labels: New-Zealand

### Forms

- round — alternative
- arownd — alternative; obsolete
- ron — alternative
- 'round — alternative; contraction

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. So as to form a circle or trace a circular path, or approximation thereof.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** High above, vultures circled around.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. So as to surround or be near.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Everybody please gather around.

   **Example:** There isn't another house for miles around.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. Nearly; approximately; about.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Around a thousand people attended.

   **Example:** An adult elephant weighs around five tons.

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. From place to place.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** There are rumors going around that the company is bankrupt.

   **Example:** Look around and see what you find.

   **Example:** We moved the furniture around in the living room.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. From one state or condition to an opposite or very different one; with a metaphorical change in direction; bringing about awareness or agreement.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** The team wasn't doing well, but the new coach really turned things around.

   **Example:** He used to stay up late but his new girlfriend changed that around.

   **Example:** The patient was unconscious but the doctor brought him around quickly.

   **Example:** I didn't think he would ever like the new design, but eventually we brought him around.

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. So as to partially or completely rotate; so as to face in the opposite direction.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually; spin

   **Example:** Turn around at the end of this street.

   **Example:** She spun around a few times.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with turn

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. Used with verbs to indicate repeated or continuous action, or in numerous locations or with numerous people.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** I asked around, and no-one really liked it.

   **Example:** Shopping around can get you a better deal.

   **Example:** When are you going to stop whoring around, find a nice girl, and give us grandchildren?

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. Used with certain verbs to suggest unproductive activity.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** sit around, mess around, loaf around
