## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /pæt͡ʃ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A piece of cloth, or other suitable material, sewed or otherwise fixed upon a garment to repair or strengthen it, especially upon an old garment to cover a hole.

   **Example:** His sleeves had patches on the elbows where different fabric had been sewn on to replace material that had worn away.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A small piece of anything used to repair damage or a breach; as, a patch on a kettle, a roof, etc.

   **Example:** I can't afford to replace the roof, which is what it really needs. I'll have the roofer apply a patch.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A piece of any size, used to repair something for a temporary period only, or that it is temporary because it is not meant to last long or will be removed as soon as a proper repair can be made, which will happen in the near future.

   **Example:** Before you can fix a dam, you have to apply a patch to the hole so that everything can dry off.

   **Example:** "This patch should hold until you reach the city," the mechanic said as he patted the car's hood.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A small, usually contrasting but always somehow different or distinct, part of something else (location, time, size)

   **Example:** The world economy had a rough patch in the 1930s.

   **Example:** To me, a normal cow is white with black patches, but Sarah's from Texas and most of the cows there have solid brown, black, or red coats.

   **Example:** Doesn't that patch of clouds looks like a bunny?

   **Example:** When ice skating, be sure to stay away from reeds&#58; there are always thin patches of ice there, and you could fall through.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A small area, a small plot of land or piece of ground.

   **Usage:** specifically

   **Example:** scattered patches of trees or growing corn

   **Example:** There was a blackberry patch down by the creek, and his grandparents called the pasture down there the cow patch.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A local region of professional responsibility.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A small piece of black silk stuck on the face or neck to heighten beauty by contrast, worn by ladies in the 17th and 18th centuries; an imitation beauty mark.

   **Usage:** historical

**Source sense R1.S8**

### Pronunciation

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To mend by sewing on a piece or pieces of cloth, leather, or the like.

   **Example:** My coat needs patching.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To mend with pieces; to repair by fastening pieces on.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To make out of pieces or patches, like a quilt.

   **Example:** I patched together this quilt.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To join or unite the pieces of; to patch the skirt.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To employ a temporary, removable electronic connection, as one between two components in a communications system.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To repair or arrange in a hasty or clumsy manner

   **Usage:** generally with the particle "up"

   **Example:** The truce between the two countries has been patched up.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To make the changes a patch describes; to apply a patch to the files in question. Hence&#58;
   1. To fix or improve a computer program without a complete upgrade.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To make the changes a patch describes; to apply a patch to the files in question. Hence&#58;
   1. To make a quick and possibly temporary change to a program.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

   **Example:** I'll need to patch the preamp output to the mixer.
