<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An agreement between two or more parties, to perform a specific job or work order, often temporary or of fixed duration and usually governed by a written agreement.

   **Example:** sign a contract

   **Example:** write up a contract

   **Example:** read a contract

   **Example:** countersign a contract

   **Example:** legally-binding contract

   **Example:** unwritten contract

   **Example:** Marriage is a contract.

   **Example:** hired on a short-term contract

   **Source synonyms:** compact; pact

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An agreement between two or more parties, to perform a specific job or work order, often temporary or of fixed duration and usually governed by a written agreement.
   1. A period of work agreed to under a temporary contract.

      **Example:** He worked a six-month contract on an oil rig.

      **Source synonyms:** compact; pact

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An agreement which the law will enforce in some way. A legally binding contract must contain at least one promise, i.e., a commitment or offer, by an offeror to and accepted by an offeree to do something in the future. A contract is thus executory rather than executed.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The document containing such an agreement.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A part of legal studies dealing with laws and jurisdiction related to contracts.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An order, usually given to a hired assassin, to kill someone.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** The mafia boss put a contract out on the man who betrayed him.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The declarer's undertaking to win the number of tricks bid with a stated suit as trump.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** bridge; games

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- enPR: kəntrăkt — variety unspecified in the source labels
- IPA: /kənˈtɹækt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- contracts — present; singular; third-person
- contracting — participle; present
- contracted — participle; past
- contracted — past

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To draw together or nearer; to shorten, narrow, or lessen.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** The snail’s body contracted into its shell.

   **Example:** to contract one’s sphere of action

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To shorten by omitting a letter or letters or by reducing two or more vowels or syllables to one.

   **Example:** The word “cannot” is often contracted into “can’t”.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** grammar; human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To make an agreement or contract; to covenant.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The company contracted with the council to build 200 new houses.

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To enter into a contract with (someone or something).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We have just contracted new pest control services.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To enter into (an agreement) with mutual obligations; to make (an arrangement).

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To bring on; to incur; to acquire.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** She contracted the habit of smoking in her teens.

   **Example:** to contract a debt

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. To gain or acquire (an illness).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. To draw together so as to wrinkle; to knit.

<a id="r3-s9"></a>

**Source sense R3.S9**

9. To betroth; to affiance.
