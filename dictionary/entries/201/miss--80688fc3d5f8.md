## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /mɪs/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- misses — present; singular; third-person
- missing — participle; present
- missed — participle; past
- missed — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To fail to hit, catch, grasp, etc.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; physical

   **Example:** I fired the gun, but the bullet missed the target.

   **Example:** I tried to kick the ball, but missed.

   **Example:** She tried to grab hold of the end of the rope, but she missed.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To avoid hitting.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; physical

   **Example:** The driver swerved and just managed to miss the chicken crossing the road.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To fail to achieve or attain.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The company missed all its sales targets.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To fail to experience, attend, partake, take advantage of, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Joe missed the meeting this morning.

   **Example:** I usually watch the Oscars Ceremonies, but I missed it this year.

   **Example:** I'm starving! I missed breakfast this morning.

   **Example:** I only left the queue for a moment and now I've missed my turn.

   **Example:** This offer is too good to miss.

   **Example:** to miss an opportunity, to miss a chance

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To avoid or escape.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The car just missed hitting a passer-by.

   **Example:** We missed disaster by the skin of our teeth.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To become aware of the loss or absence of; to feel the want or need of, sometimes with regret; to feel sadness at the absence of somebody or something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I miss you! Come home soon!

   **Example:** I miss going for walks along the beach.

   **Example:** I'm not going to the party, but I don't think I'll be missed.

   **Example:** I had left my wallet at home, but I only missed it when I got to the metro.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To fail to understand.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to miss the joke

   **Source antonyms:** catch

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To fail to notice; to have a shortcoming of perception; overlook.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** So I'm just going over my early notes, see if I missed anything.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To be too late to connect with or meet something or someone (a means of transportation, a deadline, etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I missed the plane!

   **Example:** "Is Polly still here?" — "No, sorry, you've just missed her. She left a couple of minutes ago."

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To be wanting; to lack something that should be present (see also adjectival missing).

   **Usage:** transitive; mostly continuous tenses

   **Example:** The car is missing essential features.

   **Example:** This jigsaw is missing several pieces.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To spare someone of something unwanted or undesirable.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive; African-American Vernacular

   **Example:** Miss me with that nonsense!

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To fail to help the hand of a player.

   **Example:** Player A&#58; J7. Player B&#58; Q6. Table&#58; 283. The flop missed both players!

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; poker

   **Source context (verbatim):** said of a card

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To fail to score (a goal).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To go wrong; to err.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. To be absent, deficient, or wanting.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete

## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /mɪs/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- misses — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. A failure to physically hit.

   **Example:** In eight shots at the target he had six misses.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A failure to obtain or accomplish something; a failure to succeed.

   **Example:** After four top-ten singles, the band's next release was a miss.

   **Example:** Your answer isn't exactly right, but it's a very close miss.

   **Example:** Jones put the penalty shot over the bar — what a terrible miss.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. An act of avoidance (usually used with the verb give).

   **Example:** I think I’ll give the meeting a miss.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. Someone or something whose loss or absence is felt.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** Top striker Smith is out injured and will be a big miss for United.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. The situation where an item is not found in a cache and therefore needs to be explicitly loaded.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. A foul shot that fails to hit the target ball, where the player has, in the referee's judgement, not made every effort to play a legal shot; in addition to conceding points for the foul, the player can be made to play the shot again.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; games; hobbies; lifestyle; snooker; sports

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. Error, fault; misdeed, wrongdoing, sin.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. Hurt or harm from a mistake or accident.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. Loss, lack want; hence, the feeling of loss.

   **Usage:** obsolete
