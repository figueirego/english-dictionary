### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To fall asleep.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** After two glasses of whiskey, Tom soon dropped off in front of the television.

   **Source synonyms:** drift off; doze off; crash out; doze off; drift off; drop off; fall asleep; get off (British); go night-night (childish; colloquial); go to sleep; nod off; nod out; sack out; zone

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To deliver; to deposit or leave; to allow passengers to alight.

   **Usage:** US; especially; idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Can you drop the kids off at school?

   **Example:** I'll drop off your books when I see you tonight.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To fall off&#58;
   1. To drop from, fall from.

      **Usage:** also; figuratively; transitive

      **Example:** The leaves were slowly dropping off the tree.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To fall off&#58;
   1. To drop, fall.

      **Usage:** also; figuratively; intransitive

      **Example:** The leaves hung on until the end of November, then dropped off quickly.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To end a connection with a telephone queue, either by hanging up or after being served or processed.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** As soon as a caller drops off, we'll have an open line and maybe you can reach us then.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To lessen or reduce.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Sales have dropped off in recent months.

   **Source synonyms:** fall off; taper off; dwindle

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To die.

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive; slang

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To abandon or give up on (something); to be abandoned or given up on.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Example:** My lawyer said some of those charges will be dropped off.

   **Example:** If you ignore them, the fees won't just drop off.
