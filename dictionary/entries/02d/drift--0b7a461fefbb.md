### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To move slowly, especially pushed by currents of water, air, etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The boat drifted away from the shore.

   **Example:** The balloon was drifting in the breeze.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To move haphazardly without any destination.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He drifted from town to town, never settling down.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To deviate gently from the intended direction of travel.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** This car tends to drift left at high speeds.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To drive or carry, as currents do a floating body.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To drive into heaps.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** A current of wind drifts snow or sand

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To accumulate in heaps by the force of wind; to be driven into heaps.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Snow or sand drifts.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To make a drift; to examine a vein or ledge for the purpose of ascertaining the presence of metals or ores; to follow a vein; to prospect.

   **Usage:** US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; mining

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To enlarge or shape, as a hole, with a drift.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To oversteer a vehicle, causing loss of traction, while maintaining control from entry to exit of a corner. See Drifting (motorsport).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** automotive; transport; vehicles
