### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. One who controls something.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any electric or mechanical device for controlling a circuit or system.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; electronics; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The chief accounting officer which audits, and manages the financial affairs of a company or government; a comptroller.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A mechanism that controls or regulates the operation of a machine, especially a peripheral device in a computer.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computer-hardware; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A hardware device designed to allow the user to play video games.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** video-games

   **Source synonyms:** game controller

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An iron block, usually bolted to a ship's deck, for controlling the running out of a chain cable. The links of the cable tend to drop into hollows in the block, and thus hold fast until disengaged.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The person who supervises and handles communication with an agent in the field.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** espionage; government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. The subject of a control verb. See Control (linguistics)

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. In software applications using the model-view-controller design pattern, the part or parts of the application that treat input and output, forming an interface between models and views.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences; software
