## Verb

### Forms

- sorts out — present; singular; third-person
- sorting out — participle; present
- sorted out — participle; past
- sorted out — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To clarify by reviewing mentally.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** It's a bit confused at the moment, I'll try to sort it out later.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To arrange.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Could you call Dave and sort out a meeting for tomorrow?

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To fix (a problem).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The computer won't let me delete that file; could you sort it out?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To organise or separate into groups, as a collection of items, so as to make tidy.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Could you sort out your wardrobe and put the clothes you no longer use in one pile to give away and another to throw away?

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To separate from the remainder of a group; often construed with from.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We need to sort out the problems we can solve from the ones we can't.

   **Example:** They've already sorted out the students in group A, so we just need to worry about groups B and C.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To take action to stop someone who has been causing trouble, often by physically attacking them.

   **Usage:** UK; slang; transitive

   **Example:** If you do that again, I'll soon sort you out.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To provide (somebody) with a necessity, or a solution to a problem.

   **Usage:** UK; slang

   **Example:** Hey man, I want some weed. —I'll sort you out, mate.

   **Example:** We really need to sort Chris out with a girlfriend.
