## Noun · Record 1

### Forms

- double-dips — plural
- double dip — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An ice cream cone with two scoops of ice cream.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** We each got double-dips with chocolate on top and strawberry on the bottom.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A hill that levels off for a while about halfway down.

   **Usage:** roller coasters

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Ellipsis of double-dip recession (“return to recession after a short period of growth”).

   **Usage:** abbreviation; alt-of; ellipsis

   **Example:** The UK is currently experiencing a double-dip.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** economics; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** W-shaped recession

   **Alternative form of:** double-dip recession

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The drawing of a government pension or benefit for one job while also working in the government at another job, or of two pensions at the same time as a result of reaching the retirement criteria twice for the same entity.

   **Usage:** informal

## Verb · Record 2

### Forms

- double-dips — present; singular; third-person
- double-dipping — participle; present
- double-dipped — participle; past
- double-dipped — past
- double dip — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To dip a piece of food (e.g. a chip) into a communal sauce container after already having taken a bite of the food.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** I don't mind double-dipping when eating with my family, but I'd be embarrassed to do it when out with friends.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To be illegitimately compensated a second time for the same activity.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** He got caught double-dipping by billing the government directly as well as the primary contractor.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To draw a government pension or benefit for one job while also working in the government at another job, or to draw two pensions at the same time as a result of reaching the retirement criteria twice for the same entity.

   **Usage:** informal

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To re-release a movie or TV series, sometimes as a compilation or with additional features.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; film; media; television

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To use a single debt instrument to obtain interest tax expense (and therefore a lower tax base) in two or more tax jurisdictions.

   **Usage:** US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To defeat a team twice in the finals.

   **Example:** SPC Ladies double-dipped the Sudbury Storm in the NSA Canadian World Series.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; games; hobbies; lifestyle; softball; sports

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To enroll in a single class which will fulfill two different requirements of a course of study.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** education; higher-education

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To buy the same game twice, especially on different platforms.

   **Example:** I usually don't double-dip, but this port convinced me to with its additional content and improved graphics.
