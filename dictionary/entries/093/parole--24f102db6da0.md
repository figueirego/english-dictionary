### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Originally, one's oath or word of honour, given as a condition of release from custody; now specifically, describing the release of a former prisoner under certain conditions, especially the promise of good behaviour.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually; with-on

   **Example:** He will be on parole for nearly two more years.

   **Example:** He was released on parole.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Conditional release of a prisoner (now especially before the end of a custodial sentence), or the term or state of such release; the system governing such releases.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A word of honor, especially given by a prisoner of war, to not engage in combat if released.

   **Usage:** historical; uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A watchword or code phrase; (military) a password given only to officers, distinguished from the countersign, which is given to all guards.

   **Usage:** archaic; uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Language in use, as opposed to language as a system.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The permission for a foreigner who does not meet the technical requirements for a visa to be allowed to enter the U.S. on humanitarian grounds.

   **Usage:** US; uncountable; usually; immigration law

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Alternative form of parol.

   **Usage:** alt-of; alternative; uncountable; usually

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law
