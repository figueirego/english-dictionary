### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A small, light vehicle with runners, used recreationally, mostly by children, for sliding down snow-covered hills; no draft animal pulls it.

   **Example:** The child zoomed down the hill on his sled.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A vehicle on runners, used for conveying loads over the snow or ice, and often pulled by sled dogs.

   **Usage:** Canada; US

   **Example:** "Mush!" he yelled at the dogs pulling the sled.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A snowmobile.

   **Usage:** Canada; US; slang

   **Example:** We saw them go by on their sleds this afternoon, headed toward their grandfather's quarter section.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A car (automobile) or truck, usually called so with the implication of sledlike traits&#58; heavy, low-slung, and prone to going fast but not braking or cornering particularly well.

   **Usage:** Canada; US; figuratively; slang

   **Example:** He was a bit reckless and frankly we were nervous about riding with him in that big old sled of his, a Cadillac with a V-8.

   **Example:** That sled had manual drum brakes on all four corners.

   **Source synonyms:** land yacht; boat

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A piece of exercise equipment, used in American football training etc., that provides resistance as it is moved over a flat surface.
