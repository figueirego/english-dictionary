**Source sense R1.S1**

2. Pertaining to a species, as a taxon or taxa at the rank of species.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; natural-sciences; taxonomy

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Special, distinctive or unique.

   **Usage:** in-compounds; sometimes

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. intended for, or applying to, a particular thing.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Serving to identify a particular thing (often a disease or condition), with little risk of mistaking something else for it.

   **Example:** a highly specific test

   **Example:** specific and nonspecific symptoms

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Being a remedy for a particular disease on a deeper level, rather than just masking the symptoms.

   **Example:** Quinine is a specific medicine in cases of malaria.

   **Example:** Any improvement in secondary sciatica is probably due to the analgesic action of the sodium salicylate, but in primary sciatica, in all likelihood “rheumatic,” the effect of the sodium salicylate appears to be specific rather than symptomatic.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Limited to a particular antibody or antigen.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Of a value divided by mass (e.g. specific orbital energy).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Similarly referring to a value divided by any measure which acts to standardize it (e.g. thrust specific fuel consumption, referring to fuel consumption divided by thrust).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A measure compared with a standard reference value by division, to produce a ratio without unit or dimension (e.g. specific refractive index is a pure number, and is relative to that of air).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics
