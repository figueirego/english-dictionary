### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A number of domestic animals assembled together under the watch or ownership of a keeper.

   **Usage:** collective

   **Example:** a herd of cattle

   **Example:** a herd of sheep

   **Example:** a herd of goats

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any collection of animals gathered or travelling in a company.

   **Usage:** collective

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A crowd, a mass of people or things; a rabble.

   **Usage:** derogatory; usually

   **Example:** I was never one to follow the herd.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A collection of things, especially vehicles.

   **Usage:** collective

   **Example:** Herds of buses spread across the streets.

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /hɝd/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- herding — participle; present

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To unite or associate in a herd; to feed or run together, or in company.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Sheep herd on many hills.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To unite or associate in a herd

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To manage, care for or guard a herd

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He is employed to herd the goats.

   **Source synonyms:** wrangle

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To associate; to ally oneself with, or place oneself among, a group or company.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To move, or be moved, in a group. (of both animals and people)

   **Example:** On alighting at the station, we were all herded over the footbridge and through a side exit.
