### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An uncomfortable or painful feeling due to recognition or consciousness of one's own impropriety or dishonor, or something being exposed that should have been kept private.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** When I realized that I had hurt my friend, I felt deep shame.

   **Example:** The teenager couldn’t bear the shame of introducing his parents.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Something to regret.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** It was a shame not to see the show after driving all that way.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Reproach incurred or suffered; dishonour; ignominy; derision.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The cause or reason of shame; that which brings reproach and ignominy.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. That which is shameful and private, especially private parts.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The capacity to be ashamed, inhibiting one from brazen behaviour; due regard for one's own moral conduct and how one is perceived by others; restraint, moderation, decency.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Don't you have any shame?

### Senses

   **Example:** I was shamed by the teacher's public disapproval.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Stop shaming others about their food choices.
