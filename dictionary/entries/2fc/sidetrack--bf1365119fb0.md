## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈsaɪdˌtɹæk/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- sidetracks — plural
- side-track — alternative
- side track — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A second, relatively short length of track just to the side of a railroad track, joined to the main track by switches at one or both ends, used either for unloading freight, or to allow two trains on a same track to meet (opposite directions) or pass (same direction); a railroad siding.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any auxiliary railroad track, as differentiated from a siding, that runs adjacent to the main track.

   **Usage:** sometimes

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A smaller tunnel or well drilled as an auxiliary off a main tunnel or well.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; mining

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An alternate train of thought, issue, topic, or activity, that is a deviation or distraction from the topic at hand or central activity, and secondary or subordinate in importance or effectiveness.

   **Example:** Stay focused on the story; you keep getting lost in all of these little sidetracks.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** railroad siding — small railroad track for unloading, passing; siding — small railroad track for unloading, passing; lay-by (UK) — small railroad track for unloading, passing

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈsaɪdˌtɹæk/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- sidetracks — present; singular; third-person
- sidetracking — participle; present
- sidetracked — participle; past
- sidetracked — past
- side-track — alternative
- side track — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To divert (a locomotive or train) on to a lesser used track in order to allow other trains to pass.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To divert or distract (someone) from a main issue or course of action with an alternate or less relevant topic or activity; or, to use deliberate trickery or sly wordplay when talking to (a person) in order to avoid discussion of a subject.

   **Example:** Sorry I'm late. I got sidetracked helping my friend move some furniture.

   **Example:** The politician sidetracked the reporter with a story about duck hunting instead of a direct response to the question that was asked.

   **Example:** I hope you can sidetrack the teacher with questions so we don't have to take the exam.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To sideline; to push aside; to divert or distract from, reducing (something) to a secondary or subordinate position.

   **Example:** The project was sidetracked in favor of a more popular program.

   **Example:** He has sidetracked this debate for far too long.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To deviate briefly from the topic at hand.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Just to sidetrack a little bit from the subject I will explain my reasoning.

   **Example:** To sidetrack for a moment, let me commend this team for their outstanding efforts.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** digress — deviate from the topic at hand
