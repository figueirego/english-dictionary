## Verb

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To sweep up (a mess) thoroughly with a broom.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The janitor broomed up the shards of glass.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To strike (a length of wood) repeatedly such that the top of it becomes frayed and worn.

   **Usage:** dated

   **Example:** Most of the fence-posts had been broomed up so badly they weren't usable.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a length of wood

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To show signs of a witch's broom (mistletoe) infection; to have the witch's broom infection spread and worsen.

   **Usage:** dated

   **Example:** I knew the yew tree was done for when the infection broomed up its branches.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an infected tree
