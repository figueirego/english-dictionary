### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Small time; operating on a small scale; involving small amounts of money; petty or cheap.

   **Usage:** US; colloquial; idiomatic

   **Example:** I bought my new ride from some nickel and dime used-car salesman.

   **Example:** Don't waste your time with that; their operations are nickel and dime.

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To charge, or be charged, several unexpected small amounts of money, often in the form of fees, taxes, or related expenses to a venture, which when taken as a whole add up to a significant unexpected cost.

   **Usage:** US; colloquial; idiomatic

   **Example:** I got nickel and dimed to death by the phone company's sneaky extra charges.

   **Example:** It seems like a great offer, but they will just nickel and dime you until you've spent more than retail anyway.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To wear down in small increments; to quibble or obsess endlessly with (someone) over trifles.

   **Usage:** US; colloquial; figuratively; idiomatic
