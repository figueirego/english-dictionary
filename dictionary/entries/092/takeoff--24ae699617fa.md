### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A launch or ascent into the air or into flight, such as of an aircraft, rocket, bird, high-jumper etc.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The flight was smooth, but the takeoff was a little rough.

   **Example:** That high-jumper's takeoff needs some work.

   **Example:** The mute swan, being one of the world's heaviest flying birds, can have a laborious takeoff.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The spot from which one takes off; in particular, the place from which a jumper rises in leaping.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The initial movement a surfer makes to start riding a wave.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports; surfing

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A parody or lampoon of someone or something.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Weird Al's song "Lasagna" is a takeoff on the popular song "La Bamba".

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A quantification, especially of building materials.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I'll give you an estimate after I do the quantity takeoffs for the trusses and structural steel.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The removal of sheets from the press.

   **Usage:** UK; countable; historical; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** media; printing; publishing
