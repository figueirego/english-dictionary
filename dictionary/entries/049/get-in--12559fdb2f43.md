### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see get, in.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To get into or inside something, literally or figuratively.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Hurry up and get in the car!

   **Example:** He tried to go after the ball but couldn't get in the game.

   **Example:** You'd better get in gear. We've got work to do!

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To enter a place; to gain access.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** If you want to get in, you'll need the gate code and a key.

   **Example:** Teens walk the redlight district but can never get in.

   **Example:** They hacked his password and finally got in.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To secure membership at a selective school.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** All of our students who applied to university got in.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To be elected to some office.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** Do the early results say our candidate will get in?

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To fit (words) into a conversation etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To become dark earlier as a result of seasonal change; to draw in.

   **Example:** Winter's on the way, and the nights are getting in.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of evenings or nights

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To arrive.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** What time does your flight get in?

   **Example:** It was gone 9 o'clock by the time I got in from work last night.
