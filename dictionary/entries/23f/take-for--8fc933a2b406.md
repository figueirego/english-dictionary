## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To regard as.

   **Example:** Does he take me for a fool?

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To consider mistakenly.

   **Example:** Sorry, I took you for someone else.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To defraud; to rip off.

   **Usage:** colloquial

   **Example:** Pinkett is angry that George betrayed his trust and took him for $100K.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Bring (a person) to acquire (a thing).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Jim took his daughter for ice cream.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To account for; to charge for; to scan.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** ”Have you taken for a bag?” “Yes, I’ve scanned it and charged you an extra 25p, you would’ve had to pay £4.75 but now that’ll be £5.00 please.”

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To go towards.

   **Usage:** chiefly Herefordshire

   **Example:** The fox took for Westhide wood.

   **Example:** The coward chose to take for the hills, rather than stand and fight.
