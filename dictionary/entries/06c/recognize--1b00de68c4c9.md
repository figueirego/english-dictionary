### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To match (something or someone which one currently perceives) to a memory of some previous encounter with the same person or thing.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

   **Example:** I recognized his face immediately, although his voice was different.

   **Source synonyms:** [place](../207/place--81df63580cc9.md)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To acknowledge the existence or legality of; to treat as valid or worthy of consideration.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

   **Example:** The US and a number of EU countries are expected to recognize Kosovo on Monday.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To acknowledge or consider (as being a certain thing or having a certain quality or property).

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive; or with clause

   **Example:** Slavery is widely recognized as immoral.

   **Example:** I recognize that my behaviour has been unacceptable.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To realize or discover the nature of something; apprehend quality in.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To show formal appreciation of, as with an award, commendation etc.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; transitive

   **Example:** His services were recognized in a testimonial.

   **Example:** The soldier was recognized in dispatches.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To review; to examine again.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; obsolete

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To reconnoiter.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US; obsolete

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To have the property to bind to specific antigens.

   **Usage:** British; English; Oxford; US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences
