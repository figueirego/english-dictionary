## Noun

### Forms

- blind spots — plural
- blindspot — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The place where the optic nerve attaches to the retina, and so where the retina cannot detect light; the portion of the visual field that corresponds to this site.

   **Example:** We all have a blind spot in our vision, but our brains usually compensate for it, such that we usually don't consciously notice it.

   **Source synonyms:** optic disc; optic disk; scotoma

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any portion of the visual field in which the vision is impaired (by ocular disease).

   **Example:** He has a few blind spots in his eyesight because of his diabetic retinopathy.

   **Source synonyms:** scotoma

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. In driving, the part of the road that cannot be seen in the rear-view mirror or side-view mirror.

   **Example:** When he changed lanes, he sideswiped a car that was in his blind spot.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** automotive; transport; vehicles

   **Source synonyms:** blindside

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The part of the railway/tramway track that cannot be seen in the side mirror or the side window.

   **Example:** When he changed tracks, he sideswiped a railway worker that was in his blind spot.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

   **Source synonyms:** blindside

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. In a stadium or auditorium, any location affording those seated or standing there only an obstructed visual or auditory experience.

   **Example:** Our seats turned out to be in a blind spot behind a pillar blocking our view on the stage.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An inability to recognize a fact or think clearly about a certain topic, especially because of a prejudice.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** He loves her so much that he has a blind spot when it comes to her faults.

   **Source synonyms:** scotoma

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A subject or area about which one is uninformed or misinformed, often because of a prejudice or lack of appreciation.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** The new study addresses the blind spot created by previous research having excluded healthy adult subjects.

   **Example:** I have a blind spot for math.

   **Source synonyms:** scotoma

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A location where radio reception and/or transmission is significantly poorer than in surrounding locations.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** communications; electrical-engineering; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; telecommunications

   **Source synonyms:** dead spot

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A location around an earthquake epicentre that is prone to earthquake.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; geology; natural-sciences; seismology

   **Source synonyms:** blind zone; dead spot

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. The flaw of building that cannot be seen when collapsing because poor structure, built not according specified standards, or not earthquake-proof.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture

   **Source synonyms:** dead spot
