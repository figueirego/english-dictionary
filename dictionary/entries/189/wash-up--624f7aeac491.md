### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To clean the utensils, dishes etc. used in preparing and eating a meal.

   **Usage:** UK; ambitransitive

   **Example:** You wash up tonight and I'll dry.

   **Example:** I'm not washing up all these dishes.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To wash one's hands or face, often around mealtimes.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive

   **Example:** Dinner is almost ready, so go and wash up.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To carry (an object) to land.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The tide washes up a lot of driftwood.

   **Example:** The whale was washed up on the beach.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of water

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To be carried by water to land.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Some dangerous chemicals washed up on the beach.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To arrive in a place; to end up somewhere.

   **Usage:** broadly; intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To beat up (someone).

   **Usage:** slang; transitive; African-American Vernacular
