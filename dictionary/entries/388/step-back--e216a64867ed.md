### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see step, back.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To stop what one is doing and evaluate the current situation.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** Perhaps we should step back for a second and think about solving this problem a different way.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To prevent oneself from becoming emotionally involved in a certain situation.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** As a therapist sometimes you have to step back from your clients' lives.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. to depart driving the train following the train they arrived into the station driving, so as to decrease service turnaround time.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a driver at a terminal station

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To retreat from one's duties in a job; to reduce one's duties, often as a prelude to leaving a position; to take a back seat.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** He's stepping back from the role to let you start in it.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To quietly abandon a belief.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** You need to step back from the threat you made.
