## Verb

### Forms

- takes over — present; singular; third-person
- taking over — participle; present
- took over — past
- taken over — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To assume control of something, such as a business or enterprise, and sometimes by force.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To adopt a further responsibility or duty.

   **Example:** He will take over the job permanently when the accountant retires.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To relieve someone temporarily.

   **Example:** My husband is taking over the accounts department during the holiday period, while the chief accountant is away.

   **Example:** If you will take over driving, I'd like to get some sleep.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To buy out the ownership of a business.

   **Example:** Acme Motors is to take Jetcar Industries over this week, if all goes as planned.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To appropriate something without permission.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To annex a territory by conquest or invasion; to conquer.

   **Example:** Ancient Rome took over lands throughout the known world.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To become more successful than (someone or something else).

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Buzz Lightyear has taken over Woody as the most popular children's toy.

   **Example:** Tiger Woods has taken over as the top golfer.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see take, over.

   **Example:** He took the car over to the garage.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** overtake — to become more successful than; surpass — to become more successful than; commandeer — to assume control, sometimes by force
