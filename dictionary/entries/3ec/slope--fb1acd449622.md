## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /sloʊp/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /sləʊp/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- slopes — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A surface that either inclines or declines in a regular manner from the perspective of an observer.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** the slope

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An area of ground that tends evenly upward or downward.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I had to climb a small slope to get to the site.

   **Example:** a steep slope

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The degree to which a surface tends upward or downward.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The road has a very sharp downward slope at that point.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The ratio of the vertical and horizontal distances between two points lying on the line.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The slope of this line is 0.5

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a line; with respect to a fixed coordinate system

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The slope of the line tangent to the curve at the given point.

   **Usage:** countable; proscribed; sometimes; uncountable; see Usage notes

   **Example:** The slope of a parabola increases linearly with x.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a curve at a given point

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The angle a roof surface makes with the horizontal, expressed as a ratio of the units of vertical rise to the units of horizontal length (sometimes referred to as run).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The slope of an asphalt shingle roof system should be 4&#58;12 or greater.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. a concept or idea which suggests a type of oblique or accelerating progression

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The habit of lying can quickly become a slippery slope.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An East or Southeast Asian person, especially one that is Chinese.

   **Usage:** countable; ethnic; offensive; slur; uncountable; vulgar

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A hasty departure.

   **Usage:** UK; countable; dated; slang; uncountable

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /sloʊp/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /sləʊp/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- slopes — present; singular; third-person
- sloping — participle; present
- sloped — participle; past
- sloped — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To tend steadily upward or downward.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The road slopes sharply down at that point.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To form with a slope; to give an oblique or slanting direction to; to incline or slant.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to slope the ground in a garden; to slope a piece of cloth in cutting a garment

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To try to move surreptitiously.

   **Usage:** UK; colloquial; usually

   **Example:** I sloped in through the back door, hoping my boss wouldn't see me.

   **Source context (verbatim):** followed by a preposition

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To hold a rifle at a slope with forearm perpendicular to the body in front holding the butt, the rifle resting on the shoulder.

   **Example:** The order was given to "slope arms".

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war
