### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Supposing that, assuming that, in the circumstances that; suppositive used to introduce a condition that may be (or prove to be) either true or false.

   **Example:** I'll do it next week if I have time.

   **Example:** Fetch me a cold beer if there's one in the fridge.

   **Example:** If I give you the money, will you buy it for me?

   **Example:** If you prefer, I can come tomorrow instead.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Supposing that, assuming that, in the circumstances that; suppositive used to introduce a condition that may be (or prove to be) either true or false.
   1. A keyword that invokes conditional processing&#58; in the event that a given condition is true, execute the given statement(s) (otherwise execute other statements).

      **Example:** if A then B else C

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Supposing that; used with past or past perfect subjunctive to indicate a counterfactual or hypothetical condition.

   **Example:** If she hadn't told me, I wouldn't know.

   **Example:** If he understood how this made me feel right now, he'd be O.K. with it too.

   **Example:** If I was King of England, I'd make you my Queen.

   **Example:** She could have used my car if the battery hadn't been flat.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Supposing that; used with past or past perfect subjunctive to indicate a counterfactual or hypothetical condition.
   1. Even if; even in the circumstances that.

      **Usage:** excessive; usually

      **Example:** I wouldn't marry you if you were the last man on earth.

      **Example:** You're finishing your chowder if you sit there all afternoon!

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Considering the fact that; given that; introducing a condition that is known to be true.

   **Example:** The drain's blocked — and if the drain's blocked, the water won't flow.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. When; whenever; every time that.

   **Example:** If you heat water to 100° C, it boils.

   **Example:** If it rains, it pours.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Although; used to introduce a concession; may..but.

   **Example:** He was a great friend, if a little stingy at the bar.

   **Example:** She won her team's admiration, if not the award, for her performance.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. While; used to introduce a contrast.

   **Usage:** literary

   **Example:** If tea is expensive, coffee is completely unaffordable.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Whether; used to introduce a noun clause, an indirect question, that functions as the direct object of certain verbs.

   **Usage:** proscribed; sometimes

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Introducing a relevance conditional; in case.

   **Example:** I have leftover cake if you want some.

   **Example:** If you want to go home, I have the car keys.
