   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Usage:** uncountable

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈleɪ.bə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈleɪ.bɚ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- labours — present; singular; third-person
- labouring — participle; present
- laboured — participle; past
- laboured — past
- labor — alternative; US

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To toil, to work.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** grind

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To belabour, to emphasise or expand upon (a point in a debate, etc).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I think we've all got the idea. There's no need to labour the point.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To be oppressed with difficulties or disease; to do one's work under conditions which make it especially hard or wearisome; to move slowly, as against opposition, or under a burden.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To suffer the pangs of childbirth.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To pitch or roll heavily, as a ship in a turbulent sea.
