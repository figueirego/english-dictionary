### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To become encouraged, reinvigorated, or cheerful; to summon one's courage or spirits; to pluck up courage.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** I realized I needed to buck up and tackle the problem head-on.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To encourage; to hearten.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** I knew I had to try and buck up the rest of my team as well.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To do better.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** You better buck up or you'll never make it.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To dress oneself up smartly; compare (obsolete) buck ("a fop, dandy")

   **Usage:** dated; idiomatic; intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To pass on to higher authority for resolution. See also pass the buck.

   **Usage:** colloquial; idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** He started bucking up everything to management when he didn't get a raise.

   **Example:** He just bucked everything risky up to management.

   **Example:** Instead of dealing with the customer's complaint himself, he just bucked it up to his boss.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To hurry up.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To hit or fight (someone).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; slang; transitive; African-American Vernacular
