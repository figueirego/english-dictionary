## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /dɛsk/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- desks — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A table, frame, or case, in past centuries usually with a sloping top but now usually with a flat top, for the use of writers and readers. It often has a drawer or repository underneath.

   **Example:** She organized her notes neatly on her desk before starting the project.

   **Example:** The desk was cluttered with papers and books, making it hard to concentrate.

   **Example:** He sat at his desk for hours, trying to finish the report.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A reading table or lectern to support the book from which the liturgical service is read, differing from the pulpit from which the sermon is preached; also (especially in the United States), a pulpit. Hence, used symbolically for the clerical profession.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A department tasked with a particular topic or focus in certain types of businesses, such as newspapers and financial trading firms.

   **Example:** the city desk, the sports desk

   **Example:** the options desk, the equities desk

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Ellipsis of mixing desk.

   **Usage:** abbreviation; alt-of; ellipsis

   **Alternative form of:** [mixing desk](../2c9/mixing-desk--b2450fc6d3ec.md)

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A station for a string player in an orchestra, consisting of a chair and a music stand, or a row of such stations.

   **Example:** The first four desks of cellos play separate parts, making a lush, Impressionist harmony.
