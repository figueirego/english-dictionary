### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To lead by persuasion or influence; incite or prevail upon.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** entice; inveigle; put someone up to something

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause, bring about, lead to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** His meditation induced a compromise. Opium induces sleep.

   **Source synonyms:** bring about; instigate; prompt; stimulate; trigger; provoke; abet (obsolete); actuate; goose; fuel; light a fire under; send; sting; bring about; cheer (obsolete); draw; drive; encourage; egg on (idiomatic); engender; evoke; excite; foment; goad; grill (Scotland; US; obsolete); ignite (figuratively); impel; incite; induce; inflame (figuratively); instigate; invoke; lash; motive; motivate; move (obsolete); obligate; oblige; push; promote; prompt; provoke; rouse; set off; spark off; spur; spur on; stimulate; stir; stir up; stoke (figuratively); tempt; urge; whet (obsolete); whip up

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To induce the labour of (a pregnant woman).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cause or produce (electric current or a magnetic state) by a physical process of induction.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To infer by induction.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; logic; mathematics; philosophy; sciences

**Source sense R1.S6**
