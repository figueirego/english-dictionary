## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- enPR: dĭm — variety unspecified in the source labels
- IPA: /dɪm/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- dims — present; singular; third-person
- dimming — participle; present
- dimmed — participle; past
- dimmed — past

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To make something less bright.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He dimmed the lights and put on soft music.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To become darker.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The lights dimmed briefly when the air conditioning was turned on.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To render dim, obscure, or dark; to make less bright or distinct.

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To deprive of distinct vision; to hinder from seeing clearly, either by dazzling or clouding the eyes; to darken the senses or understanding of.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To diminish, dull, or curtail.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** All these setbacks had started to dim the hopes of the students.

   **Example:** Nothing will dim their spirit of resilience.

   **Example:** A glut might dim the outlook for grain futures.
