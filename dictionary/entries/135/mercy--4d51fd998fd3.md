### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Relenting; forbearance to cause or allow harm to another.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** She took mercy on him and quit embarrassing him.

   **Source synonyms:** ruth; kindness; leniency; quarter

   **Source antonyms:** mercilessness; ruthlessness; cruelty

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Forgiveness or compassion, especially toward those less fortunate.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Have mercy on the poor and assist them if you can.

   **Source synonyms:** pity; sympathy; bowels (archaic); commiseration; compassion; empathy; fellow feeling; mercy; pity; remorse (obsolete); rue (archaic; dialectal); ruth (archaic); sympathy; tenderheartedness; loving-kindness

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A tendency toward forgiveness, pity, or compassion.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Mercy is one of his many virtues.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Instances of forbearance or forgiveness.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A blessing; something to be thankful for.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** It was a mercy that we were not inside when the roof collapsed

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A children's game in which two players stand opposite with hands grasped and twist each other's arms until one gives in.

   **Usage:** uncountable
