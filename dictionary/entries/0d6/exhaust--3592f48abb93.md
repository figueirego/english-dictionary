### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To draw or let out wholly; to drain completely.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The water was exhausted out of the well.

   **Example:** Moisture of the earth is exhausted by evaporation.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To empty by drawing or letting out the contents.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to exhaust a well

   **Example:** to exhaust a treasury

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To use up; to deplete, drain or expend wholly, or use until the supply comes to an end.

   **Usage:** figuratively; literally; transitive

   **Example:** My grandfather seemingly never exhausts his supply of bad jokes.

   **Example:** to exhaust one's resources

   **Example:** to exhaust all possibilities

   **Example:** You're exhausting my patience.

   **Example:** I exhausted my strength walking up the hill.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To tire out; to wear out; to cause to be without any energy.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The marathon exhausted me.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To discuss thoroughly or completely.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** That subject has already been exhausted.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To subject to the action of various solvents in order to remove all soluble substances or extractives.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to exhaust a drug successively with water, alcohol, and ether

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To expel (as exhaust).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To discharge or escape (as exhaust).

   **Usage:** intransitive
