### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To allow to enter; to grant entrance (to), whether into a place, into the mind, or into consideration.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to admit a serious thought into the mind

   **Example:** to admit evidence in the trial of a cause

   **Example:** A ticket admits one into a playhouse.

   **Example:** They were admitted into his house.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To allow (someone) to enter a profession or to enjoy a privilege; to recognize as qualified for a franchise.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to admit an attorney to practice law

   **Example:** The prisoner was admitted to bail.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To concede as true; to acknowledge or assent to, as an allegation which it is impossible to deny (+ to).

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** the argument or fact is admitted

   **Example:** He admitted his guilt.

   **Example:** She admitted taking drugs / she admitted to taking drugs.

   **Example:** She was trapped into admitting the truth.

   **Source synonyms:** own up; confess; allow

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To be capable of; to permit. In this sense, "of" may be used after the verb, or may be omitted.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The words do not admit such a construction.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To give warrant or allowance, to grant opportunity or permission.

   **Usage:** intransitive; with-of

   **Example:** Circumstances do not admit of this.

   **Example:** The text does not admit of this interpretation.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To allow to enter a hospital or similar facility for treatment.

   **Usage:** transitive
