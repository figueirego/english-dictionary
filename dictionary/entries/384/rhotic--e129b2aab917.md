### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. That allows the phoneme /ɹ/ to follow a vowel, as in bar (/bɑːɹ/) and bard or barred (/bɑːɹd/); (of an English speaker) who speaks with such an accent.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Rhotic speech is common in Ireland, Scotland, much of the United States, Canada, West Country England, and many parts of the north and west of England.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an English accent

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Having a sound quality associated with the letter R; having the sound of any of certain IPA symbols, including /ɹ/, /ɻ/, /ɚ/, /ɝ/ and /r/.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** In the IPA, a rhotic vowel (aka R-coloured vowel, retroflex vowel, vocalic r or rhotacised vowel) is indicated by the affixing of a hook diacritic ( ˞ ) to the right of the regular symbol for the vowel. The rhotic consonants are /r/, /ɾ/, /ɹ/, /ɻ/, /ʀ/, /ʁ/, /ɽ/ and /ɺ/.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; phonetics; phonology; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a phoneme

   **Source synonyms:** retroflex
