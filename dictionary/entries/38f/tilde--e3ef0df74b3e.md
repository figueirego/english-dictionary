### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A diacritical mark ⟨˜⟩ placed above a letter to modify its pronunciation.
   1. In Spanish, ⟨ñ⟩ is a palatalized ⟨n⟩, for example in ⟨cañón⟩.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A diacritical mark ⟨˜⟩ placed above a letter to modify its pronunciation.
   1. In Portuguese, ⟨ã⟩ and ⟨õ⟩ are nasalized vowels, for example in ⟨canção⟩.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A diacritical mark ⟨˜⟩ placed above a letter to modify its pronunciation.
   1. The Vietnamese tone mark dấu ngã, which is placed above a vowel to indicate a creaky rising tone (thanh ngã).

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A diacritical mark ⟨˜⟩ placed above a letter to modify its pronunciation.
   1. The apex, a curved diacritic used in the 17th century to mark final nasalization in the early Vietnamese alphabet. It was an adoption of the Portuguese tilde.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.
   1. The character encoded as decimal 126 in the 1967 ASCII character set, and later in the 1992 Unicode character set.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.
   1. A punctuation mark that indicates range (from a number to another number). This use is common in Asia, where the symbol in this case is also called a wave dash.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.
   1. In lexicography, the ⟨~⟩ symbol is used to indicate the repetition of the topical word or item. In this case, the symbol is also called a swung dash.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.
   1. May be used to represent approximation, in English prose and in mathematics. For example, “My dog weighs ~30 pounds.”

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A symbol ⟨~⟩, with various names and uses, also known as swung dash or wave dash. In the computer industry, various other names may be used, such as squiggle and twiddle.
   1. An alternate form of the logical negation operator, which is usually written as ¬.
