### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A hummed tune, i.e. created orally with lips closed.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An often indistinct sound resembling human humming.

   **Example:** They could hear a hum coming from the kitchen and found the dishwasher on.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Busy activity, like the buzz of a beehive.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Unpleasant odour.

   **Usage:** UK; slang

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An imposition or hoax; humbug.

   **Usage:** dated

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A kind of strong drink.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A phenomenon, or collection of phenomena, involving widespread reports of a persistent and invasive low-frequency humming, rumbling, or droning noise not audible to all people.

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈhʌm/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- hums — present; singular; third-person
- humming — participle; present
- hummed — participle; past
- hummed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make a sound from the vocal cords without pronouncing any real words, with one's lips closed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** We are humming happily along with the music.

   **Source synonyms:** croon

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To express by humming.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to hum a tune

   **Example:** The team hummed “We Shall Overcome” as they came back onto the field after the break.

   **Source synonyms:** buzz; croon

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To drone like certain insects naturally do in motion, or sounding similarly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** bumble; buzz; whirr

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To buzz, be busily active like a beehive.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The streets were humming with activity.

   **Source synonyms:** bustle; buzz; hustle

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To produce low sounds which blend continuously.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To reek, smell bad.

   **Usage:** UK; slang

   **Example:** This room really hums — have you ever tried spring cleaning, mate?

   **Source synonyms:** funk; stink; funk; honk; hum (UK; slang); niff; pong; reek; smell; stink; stink to high heaven; whiff

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To flatter by approving; to cajole; to deceive or impose upon; to humbug.

   **Usage:** UK; dated; slang; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** blandish; inveigle; sweet-talk; blandander (Ireland; dated); blandish; butter up (figuratively); cajole; sweeten; fast-talk; canoodle (figuratively); carny (dialectal); coax; collogue; croodle (UK; dialectal; obsolete); engle (obsolete); fleech (Scotland); honey around (figuratively); honey up (dated; figuratively); hum (UK; dated; slang); ingle (obsolete); inveigle; soap (dated; slang); soft-sawder (idiomatic); soft-soap (idiomatic); stroke (US; figuratively); sweet-talk (idiomatic); tweedle (obsolete); twist; wheedle; whilly (Scotland; archaic); word (obsolete)
