### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see turn, off.

   **Example:** He turned off from me and faced the far wall.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To power down, to switch off, to put out of operation, to deactivate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Turn off the machine and unplug it when you leave.

   **Example:** In some people, this gene has been turned off.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To power down, to switch off, to put out of operation, to deactivate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).
   1. To deactivate a heat source cooking a particular item.

      **Usage:** broadly; transitive

      **Example:** You can turn off the green beans now.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To power down, to switch off, to put out of operation, to deactivate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).
   1. To become deactivated; to become powered down.

      **Usage:** intransitive; transitive; usually

      **Example:** My computer turned off!

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a machine

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To power down, to switch off, to put out of operation, to deactivate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).
   1. To rotate a tap or valve so as to interrupt the outflow of liquid or gas.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** Remember to turn the tap off once you've finished so you don't waste water.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To power down, to switch off, to put out of operation, to deactivate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).
   1. To dismiss; to fire.

      **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

      **Example:** The butler turned off that foolish maid.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To bore, repulse, disgust, or discourage (someone).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** That book's plot was so predictable, it turned me off.

   **Example:** Cigarette smoking really turns me off.

   **Example:** The smell of the trash bag is turning me off.

   **Example:** Her harsh words might turn them off from participating any further.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To leave a road; to exit a highway or other main road.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** You'll need to turn off before we get to the county line.

   **Example:** Turn off at the next exit so we can have lunch.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To avert; to stop (something).

   **Usage:** dated; transitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To turn out; to produce.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To execute by hanging.

   **Usage:** obsolete; slang; transitive

   **Example:** The thief was turned off by the hangman.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To raise, unblocking the way and signaling that a train may proceed.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a railroad semaphore

   **Source antonyms:** turn on
