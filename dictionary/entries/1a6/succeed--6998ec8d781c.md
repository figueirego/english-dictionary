### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To follow something in sequence or time.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Autumn succeeds summer.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To replace or supplant someone in order vis-à-vis an office, position, or title.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The king's eldest son succeeds his father on the throne.

   **Example:** After a contentious election, Jones succeeded Smith as president of the republic.

   **Source synonyms:** take the place of

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To come after or follow; to be subsequent or consequent; (often with to).

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To come in the place of another person, thing, or event; to come next in the usual, natural, or prescribed course of things; to follow; hence, to come next in the possession of anything; (often with to).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Following the death of his mother, he succeeded to the throne.

   **Example:** So, if the issue of the elder son succeed before the younger, the crown (or&#58; property) falls to me.

   **Source synonyms:** supersede (formal)

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To come in the place of another person, thing, or event; to come next in the usual, natural, or prescribed course of things; to follow; hence, to come next in the possession of anything; (often with to).
   1. To ascend the throne after the removal or death of the occupant.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** Princess Buttercup succeeded to the throne as queen after King Willoughby died.

      **Source synonyms:** supersede (formal)

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To prevail in obtaining an intended objective or accomplishment; to prosper as a result or conclusion of a particular effort.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The persecution of any righteous practice has never succeeded in the face of history; in fact, it can expedite the collapse of the persecutory regime.

   **Example:** She succeeded in her efforts to repair the tank.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To prosper or attain success and beneficial results in general.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** voted most likely to succeed

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To turn out, fare, do (well or ill).

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To support; to prosper; to promote or give success to.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To descend, as an estate or an heirloom, in the same family; to devolve; (often with to).

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To fall heir to; to inherit.

   **Usage:** obsolete; rare; transitive

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To go down or near (with to).

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete; rare
