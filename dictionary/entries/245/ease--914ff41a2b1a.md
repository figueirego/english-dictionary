### Pronunciation

- enPR: ēz — source labels: US

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To free (something) from pain, worry, agitation, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He eased his conscience by confessing.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To alleviate, assuage or lessen (pain).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To give respite to (someone).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The provision of extra staff eased their workload.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To loosen or slacken the tension on a line.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We eased the boom vang, then lowered the sail.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To reduce the difficulty of (something).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We had to ease the entry requirements.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To move (something) slowly and carefully.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He eased the cork from the bottle.

   **Source antonyms:** maneuver

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To lessen in intensity.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The pain eased overnight.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To proceed with little effort.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The car eased onto the motorway.

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To reduce speed.

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**
