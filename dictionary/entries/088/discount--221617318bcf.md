### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To sell at a reduced price.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Sales were slow even after the shop discounted the product.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To deduct from an account, debt, charge, etc.

   **Usage:** rare; transitive

   **Example:** Merchants sometimes discount five or six per cent for prompt payment of bills.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To disregard or regard as unimportant.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Owing to his reputation, they discounted his comments.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To lend money upon, deducting the discount or allowance for interest.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The banks discount notes and bills of exchange.

   **Example:** The company's directors were chagrined to learn that banks were suddenly refusing to discount its notes.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To take into consideration beforehand; to anticipate and form conclusions concerning (an event).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The market has dropped, discounting changes in interest rates.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To believe, or act as though one believes, that one's own feelings are more important than the reality of a situation.

   **Usage:** transactional analysis

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; psychology; sciences
