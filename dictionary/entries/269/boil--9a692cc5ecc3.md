**Source sense R2.S1**

   **Example:** Add the noodles when the water comes to the boil.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. An instance of boiling.

   **Example:** Surface water will do, but give it a good boil before drinking it.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

   **Example:** a down-home boil with plenty of crab

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** a down-home boil at the town hall

   **Usage:** collective; nonstandard; rare

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /bɔɪ(ə)l/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- boiling — participle; present

- boilt — nonstandard; past; regional

- boyle — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To heat to the point where it begins to turn into a gas.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Boil some water in a pan.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of liquids

   **Source synonyms:** seethe; well; plaw; cook; do

   **Source antonyms:** condense

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To cook in boiling water.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Boil the eggs for three minutes.

   **Example:** Is the rice boiling yet?

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To begin to turn into a gas, seethe.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Pure water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of liquids

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To bring to a boil, to heat so as to cause the contents to boil.

   **Usage:** New-Zealand; UK; informal; transitive

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To be uncomfortably hot.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** It’s boiling outside!

   **Source context (verbatim):** of weather; used only in progressive tenses

   **Source synonyms:** bake; scorch; swelter

   **Source antonyms:** freeze

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To feel uncomfortably hot.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** I’m boiling in here – could you open the window?

   **Source context (verbatim):** used only in progressive tenses

   **Source synonyms:** seethe; bake; stew

   **Source antonyms:** freeze

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. To form, or separate, by boiling or evaporation.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to boil sugar or salt

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. To steep or soak in warm water.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r3-s9"></a>

**Source sense R3.S9**

9. To be agitated like boiling water; to bubble; to effervesce.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** the boiling waves of the sea

   **Source context (verbatim):** of liquids

<a id="r3-s10"></a>

**Source sense R3.S10**

10. To be moved or excited with passion; to be hot or fervid.

   **Example:** His blood boils with anger.
