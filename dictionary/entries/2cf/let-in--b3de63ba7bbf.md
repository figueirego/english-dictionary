### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To let someone or something come in; to admit someone or something in.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He opened the window to let in the air.

   **Source antonyms:** keep out; lock out

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To let someone or something come in; to admit someone or something in.
   1. To divulge one's inner thoughts to (someone), making oneself emotionally vulnerable to them; to open up to (someone).

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Source antonyms:** keep out; lock out

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To associate with.

   **Usage:** transitive; Oxford University slang

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cheat or victimize.

   **Usage:** UK; obsolete; slang

   **Example:** He let me in heavily.
