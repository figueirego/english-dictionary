### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Any disturbed state of the atmosphere causing destructive or unpleasant weather, especially one affecting the earth's surface involving strong winds (leading to high waves at sea) and usually lightning, thunder, and precipitation.

   **Example:** The boat was torn to pieces in the storm, and nobody survived.

   **Source synonyms:** cyclone; tempest

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any disturbed state of the atmosphere causing destructive or unpleasant weather, especially one affecting the earth's surface involving strong winds (leading to high waves at sea) and usually lightning, thunder, and precipitation.
   1. A heavy fall of precipitation (hail, rain, or snow) or bout of lightning and thunder without strong winds; a hail storm, rainstorm, snowstorm, or thunderstorm.

      **Usage:** broadly

      **Source synonyms:** cyclone; tempest

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any disturbed state of the atmosphere causing destructive or unpleasant weather, especially one affecting the earth's surface involving strong winds (leading to high waves at sea) and usually lightning, thunder, and precipitation.
   1. Synonym of cyclone (“a weather phenomenon consisting of a system of winds rotating around a centre of low atmospheric pressure”).

      **Usage:** broadly

      **Source synonyms:** cyclone; tempest; cyclone (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Any disturbed state of the atmosphere causing destructive or unpleasant weather, especially one affecting the earth's surface involving strong winds (leading to high waves at sea) and usually lightning, thunder, and precipitation.
   1. A period of frosty and/or snowy weather.

      **Usage:** Canada; Scotland; US; broadly; dated

      **Source synonyms:** cyclone; tempest

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Any disturbed state of the atmosphere causing destructive or unpleasant weather, especially one affecting the earth's surface involving strong winds (leading to high waves at sea) and usually lightning, thunder, and precipitation.
   1. A disturbed state of the atmosphere between a severe or strong gale and a hurricane on the modern Beaufort scale, with a wind speed of between 89 and 102 kilometres per hour (55–63 miles per hour; 10 on the scale, known as a "storm" or whole gale), or of between 103 and 117 kilometres per hour (64–72 miles per hour; 11 on the scale, known as a "violent storm").

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** climatology; meteorology; natural-sciences

      **Source synonyms:** cyclone; tempest

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A heavy expulsion or fall of things (as blows, objects which are thrown, etc.).

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** a storm of bullets

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A violent agitation of human society; a domestic, civil, or political commotion.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** The proposed reforms have led to a political storm.

   **Source synonyms:** tempest; tumult

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A violent commotion or outbreak of sounds, speech, thoughts, etc.; also, an outpouring of emotion.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** a storm of protest

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Chiefly with a qualifying word&#58; a violent attack of disease, pain, physiological reactions, symptoms, etc.; a paroxysm.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** asthmatic storm cytokine storm

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; pathology; sciences

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Ellipsis of storm window (“a second window (originally detachable) attached on the exterior side of a window in climates with harsh winters, to add an insulating layer of still air between the outside and inside”).

   **Usage:** Canada; US; abbreviation; alt-of; ellipsis; in-plural

   **Alternative form of:** [storm window](../287/storm-window--a1fa1669d780.md)
