## Verb · Record 1

### Forms

- catches up — present; singular; third-person
- catching up — participle; present
- caught up — participle; past
- caught up — past
- catch-up — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To pick up suddenly.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To entangle (something).

   **Usage:** transitive; chiefly passive voice

   **Example:** The speaker wires have got caught up with the wires from the lights.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To involve in (something); to embroil.

   **Usage:** broadly; transitive; chiefly passive voice

   **Example:** That's why she got caught up in addiction.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To be brought up to date with news.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I hadn't seen her for so long. It was great to catch up.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To bring (someone) up to date with the news.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After Alice's vacation, her boss caught her up on policy changes.

   **Example:** I have to miss the end of the meeting, but you can catch me up later.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To reach something that had been ahead.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Rose was three seconds behind, but managed to catch up with the race leader and eventually won.

   **Example:** I'll go ahead and you can catch up with me later.

   **Example:** I'll go ahead and you can catch me up later.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To compensate for or make up a deficiency.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** After a slow start, they've been catching up lately.

   **Example:** The sales quarter ends soon and we've got to try to catch up.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To finally overtake or overpower (someone or something) after a long period of approaching or threatening (them or it). (Said especially of self-destructive behaviours that take years to bring about negative outcomes.)

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** His years of drinking were catching up with him.

   **Example:** The company's creative accounting had finally caught up with it.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of some inevitable phenomenon; with with
