## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The amount of money taken as sales transacted in a given period.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The company had an annual turnover of $500,000.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The frequency with which stock is replaced after being used or sold, workers leave and are replaced, a property changes hands, etc.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** High staff-turnover can lead to low morale amongst employees.

   **Example:** Job dissatisfaction results in high turnover.

   **Example:** Those apartments have a high turnover because they are too close to the railroad tracks.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A semicircular pastry made by turning one half of a circular crust over the other, enclosing the filling (such as fruit).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** They only served me one apple turnover for breakfast.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A loss of possession of the ball without scoring.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The Nimrods committed another dismaying turnover en route to another humiliating loss.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A measure of leg speed&#58; the frequency with which one takes strides when running, typically given in strides per minute.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An act or process of turning something over.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** a turnover in leadership

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The act or result of overturning something; an upset.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** a bad turnover in a carriage

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable
