## Adjective

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɪnˈfɛk.ʃəs/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- more infectious — comparative
- most infectious — superlative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Caused by an agent that enters the host's body (such as a bacterium, virus, parasite, or prion); often, also, transmitted among hosts via any of various routes (for example, contact, droplet-borne, airborne, waterborne, foodborne, fomite-borne, or bloodborne).

   **Example:** Cancer is usually not infectious.

   **Example:** More infectious diseases like the flu are usually less potent.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; pathology; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an illness

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Able to infect others; capable of transmitting pathogens.

   **Usage:** typically of a person

   **Example:** Despite feeling better, the patient is still infectious.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; pathology; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Spreading quickly from one person to another.

   **Example:** infectious smile

   **Example:** Her enthusiasm for work can be really infectious.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of feelings and behaviour

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Memorable and invoking excitement or interest.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** Pop music is more infectious than elevator music.

**Source antonyms:** non-infectious; noninfectious
