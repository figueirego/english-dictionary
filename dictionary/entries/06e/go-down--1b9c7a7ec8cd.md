### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.

   **Example:** go down to the grave

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.
   1. Synonym of set, to disappear below the horizon.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** It'll be cooler once the sun goes down.

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a heavenly body

      **Source synonyms:** set (synonym; synonym-of); to disappear below the horizon (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.
   1. To decrease; to change from a greater value to a lesser one.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** The unemployment rate has gone down significantly in recent months.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.
   1. To fall (down); to fall to the floor.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** The boxer went down in the second round, after a blow to the chin.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.
   1. To crash.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** The plane went down thirty miles from shore.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To descend; to move from a higher place to a lower one.
   1. To sink.

      **Example:** SMS Pommern went down with all hands at Jutland.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a ship or boat

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To be received or accepted.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The news didn't go down well with her parents.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To be received or accepted.
   1. To be pleasant, etc., when eaten or drunk.

      **Usage:** UK; colloquial; intransitive

      **Example:** That meal went down a treat.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To be blamed for something; to be the scapegoat; to go to prison.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Rodney's not here; after the shootout, he went down and won't be back for at least a year.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To be recorded or remembered (as).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Today will go down as a monumental failure.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To take place, happen.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To attack another gang.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang; African-American Vernacular

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a gang

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To perform oral sex.

   **Usage:** intransitive; with-on

   **Example:** He felt nervous about going down on his girlfriend for the first time.

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To stop functioning, to go offline.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Did the server just go down again? We'll have to reboot it.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. To be soundly defeated.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang

   **Example:** You guys are going down!

<a id="r1-s16"></a>

**Source sense R1.S16**

16. To physically leave one's university, either permanently or in some other non-transient sense (such as following the end of term).

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive; Oxbridge slang

   **Example:** Following the death of her mother, she went down from Cambridge for a few days so as to attend the funeral.
