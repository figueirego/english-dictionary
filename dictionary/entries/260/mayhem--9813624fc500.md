### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The criminal offence of maiming (“seriously wounding”) a person such that their ability to defend or protect themselves is weakened or completely removed; (countable) an instance of this.

   **Usage:** UK; historical; uncountable; usually; criminal law

   **Example:** to commit mayhem

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The criminal offence of maiming a person by depriving them of the use of any of their limbs.

   **Usage:** US; broadly; uncountable; usually; criminal law

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Often preceded by to cause or make&#58; great confusion, destruction, disorder, or trouble; chaos, turmoil; (countable) an instance of this.

   **Usage:** figuratively; uncountable; usually

   **Example:** In all the mayhem, some children were separated from their parents.

   **Example:** What if the legendary hero Robin Hood had been born into the mayhem of the 20th century?

   **Source synonyms:** bedlam; car crash (figuratively); chaos; confusion; disarray; disorder; disorganization; dog's breakfast (Commonwealth; idiomatic); dog's dinner (British; idiomatic; slang); free-for-all (idiomatic); fuddle; hash; hot mess (US; idiomatic; slang); imbroglio; mash; maze (figuratively); mess; misorder; mommick (US; dialectal); muddle; mullock (British; dialectal); muss; shambles; shemozzle; snarl (figuratively); tangle (figuratively); topsy-turviness; upfuckery (rare; vulgar); ado; argy-bargy (UK); bangarang; bobbery (dated); brouhaha; bunfight; bustle; bust-up; chaos; ferment; clamour; clatter; commotion; disruption; disturbance; flap; flutter; foofaraw; fracas; furor; fuss; hobbleshaw (obsolete); hoo-ha; hoo-hah; hubbub; hullabaloo; hurly-burly; kerfuffle; mayhem; merry hell; outcry; palaver; pandemonium; pother; quarrel; riot; rowdydow (archaic); ruckus; rumpus; shemozzle; shindy (slang); splatterdash; stir; stooshie (Scotland); three-ring circus; to-do; tomfoolery; trouble; tumult; turmoil; unrest; uproar; upstir

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Violent conduct, especially the violent infliction of injury on a person or thing.

   **Usage:** figuratively; uncountable; usually
