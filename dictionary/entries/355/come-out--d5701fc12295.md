### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see come, out.

   **Example:** The mouse came out of the hole.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To be discovered; to be revealed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** It finally came out that he had been lying all the time.

   **Example:** Her lies will eventually come out.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To be published or released; to be issued; to be broadcast for the first time.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** My new book comes out next week.

   **Example:** Her interview comes out in the next edition of the newspaper.

   **Example:** The song came out on radio in 1967.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make a formal debut in society.

   **Usage:** dated; historical

   **Example:** My daughter comes out in the spring.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a debutante

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To end up or result; to turn out to be.

   **Usage:** copulative

   **Example:** There were a lot of problems at the start, but it all came out well in the end.

   **Example:** The photos came out fine.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To walk onto the field at the beginning of an innings.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a batsman

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To come out of the closet.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal

   **Example:** He came out to his parents as gay last week.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To originate in; to derive from; to be taken from out of or to have arrived from.

   **Example:** That comes out of my paycheck.

   **Example:** He's the best player to come out of Ohio.

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To express one's opinion openly.

   **Example:** I came right out and told the boss what I thought of him.

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To become visible in the sky as a result of clouds clearing away.

   **Usage:** moon or stars

   **Example:** It's quite warm now the sun has come out.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of the sun

**Source sense R1.S11**
