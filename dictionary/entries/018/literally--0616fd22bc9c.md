### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. In a literal manner; exactly as stated, word for word.
   1. Without overstatement or understatement, or false or misleading words.

      **Example:** He's prone to exaggeration, so don't take what he says literally.

      **Example:** There are literally millions of individual pieces of space debris orbiting Earth.

      **Source synonyms:** ad verbum

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. In a literal manner; exactly as stated, word for word.
   1. With phrasings that might normally be used or understood as figurative&#58; truly; not figuratively; not as an idiom or metaphor.

      **Example:** When I saw on the news that there would be no school tomorrow because of the snowstorm, I literally jumped for joy, and hit my head on the ceiling fan.

      **Example:** I didn't have time to repair the wall, so I literally just papered over the cracks.

      **Source synonyms:** ad verbum; actually; really; unfiguratively; unmetaphorically; above a bit; actually; all things considered (idiomatic); as a matter of fact; by right (obsolete); certainly; come to think of it (idiomatic); de facto; definitely; de jure; fact is (idiomatic); forsooth (archaic; poetic); frankly; honestly; in actuality; in all honesty; indubitably; in fact; in faith (archaic); in point of fact; in reality; in truth; indeed; legitimately; literally; not gonna lie; properly; really; right; simply; soothfast (obsolete); soothly (archaic); surely; to be frank; to be honest; TBH; tbh; to tell the truth (idiomatic); truly; truthfully; undoubtedly; verily (archaic); very much; wis (dialectal)

      **Source antonyms:** figuratively; idiomatically; metaphorically; virtually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. In a literal manner; exactly as stated, word for word.
   1. Draws attention to a pun or other wordplay involving an idiom.

      **Example:** My daughter's pet rabbit had babies, and now we've literally got rabbits coming out of our ears.

      **Source synonyms:** ad verbum

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. As an intensifier.
   1. Used as a general intensifier or dramatiser, sometimes tending towards a meaningless filler.

      **Usage:** colloquial

      **Example:** I had no idea, so I was literally guessing.

      **Example:** I was literally having breakfast when she arrived.

      **Example:** She was literally like, "What?", and I was literally like, "Yeah".

      **Example:** Literally who is this?

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. As an intensifier.
   1. Used as an intensifier with statements or terms that are in fact meant figuratively and not word for word as stated.

      **Usage:** proscribed; sometimes

      **Example:** He was so surprised, he literally jumped twenty feet in the air.

      **Example:** On 9/11 people were literally glued to their TV sets.

      **Example:** People can't even express their opinions freely in this country any more. Literally 1984!

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Used as a generic downtoner&#58; just, merely.

   **Usage:** colloquial

   **Example:** It's not even hard⁠ to make—you literally just put it in the microwave for five minutes and it's done.

   **Example:** It won't take me long to get back, 'cause the store's literally two blocks away.

   **Source synonyms:** merely; barely (archaic); but; just; literally; merely; no more than; nothing but; only; simply
