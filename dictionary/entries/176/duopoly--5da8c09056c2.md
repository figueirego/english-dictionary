### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An economic condition in which two sellers exert most control over the market of a commodity.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Modern examples of duopoly include the American markets for credit cards (Visa and MasterCard), smartphones (Apple and Google), soft drinks (Coca-Cola and Pepsi), and airplanes (Airbus and Boeing).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** economics; sciences

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The domination of a field of endeavour by two entities or people.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** First past the post voting has long maintained American politics as an effective duopoly where power simply alternates between two main parties.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A situation in which two or more radio or television stations in the same city or community share common ownership.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; media

   **Source synonyms:** twinstick (Canada)
