### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To repeatedly hit a person.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To hit a person so that they become unconscious (knocked out).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'm going to punch out that SOB and leave him lying on the ground bleeding.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To use a punch to remove a piece of material or to remove a piece already scored.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I spent all day punching out the die cut decals.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To leave a workplace by punching a timecard.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive

   **Example:** I'm going to punch out a bit early today.

   **Source antonyms:** clock in; clock on; [punch in](../113/punch-in--44faa79ea195.md)

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To leave a workplace.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To eject from an airplane.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** With the engine on fire he had to punch out and hit the silk.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To extract data from a computer by the use of a keyboard.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To rule (by an umpire) that a pitch is a called third strike, often done emphatically.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Jones punched out the batter to start in the sixth.
