### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of appearing or coming into sight; the act of becoming visible to the eye.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** His sudden appearance surprised me.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A thing seen; a phenomenon; an apparition.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** There was a strange appearance in the sky.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The way something looks; personal presence

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** aspect; mien; make; face

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Apparent likeness; the way which something or someone appears to others.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Some people say I'm shallow because I care so much about my appearance.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. That which is not substance, essence, hypostasis; the outward reality as opposed to the underlying reality

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Catholicism teaches that the Eucharist, while remaining under the physical appearance of bread and wine, becomes really and truly the body and the blood of Christ.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; lifestyle; philosophy; religion; sciences; theology

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The act of appearing in a particular place, or in society, a company, or any proceedings; a coming before the public in a particular character.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** A person makes his appearance as an historian, an artist, or an orator.

   **Example:** David Beckham's first appearance with the LA Galaxy at Giants Stadium against the New York Red Bulls last night drew a crowd of 66237.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. An instance of someone coming into a court of law to be part of a trial, lawsuit or other proceeding, either in person or represented by an attorney or such like; a court appearance

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Chiefly used by nurses&#58; the act of defecation by a patient.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The patient had a small bowel obstruction and there was no appearance until after the obstruction resolved.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences
