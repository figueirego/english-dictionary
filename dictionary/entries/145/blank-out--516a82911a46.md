## Verb

### Forms

- blanks out — present; singular; third-person
- blanking out — participle; present
- blanked out — participle; past
- blanked out — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To become blank.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** He removed the paper from the heat, and the paper blanked out.

   **Example:** The screen blanked out during the power cut.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To become blank.
   1. To temporarily lose memory.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

      **Example:** He blanked out five minutes into the meeting.

      **Example:** I'm blanking out on your name, I'm afraid.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cause (something) to become blank.
   1. To erase (something), leaving a blank in its place.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** The teacher blanked out some words from the text, which the students had to fill in.

      **Example:** They had to blank out five obscenities before airing the song.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cause (something) to become blank.
   1. To erase from one's memory.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** He blanked out the abuse he had suffered as a child.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To get even; to neutralise.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Beckham's second-half goal blanked out Filippi's first-half header to put the score at 1-1.
