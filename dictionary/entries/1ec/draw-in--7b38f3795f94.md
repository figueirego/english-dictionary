### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To attract.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Their concerts draw in big crowds.

   **Example:** The campfire drew in numerous unwary moths.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To get (someone) involved.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** They drew in the quiet boy who hadn't wanted to participate.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To approach.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** They saw that the night was quickly drawing in, so they pitched their tent.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of night/darkness/winter

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become dark earlier as a result of seasonal change.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** It's that time of year again when the evenings really start to draw in.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of nights or evenings

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To reduce or contract; to become shorter.
