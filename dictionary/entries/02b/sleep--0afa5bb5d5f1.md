**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To rest in a state of reduced consciousness.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** You should sleep eight hours a day.

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To have sexual intercourse (see sleep with).

   **Usage:** euphemistic; idiomatic

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** This caravan can sleep four people comfortably.

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To be careless, inattentive, or unconcerned; not to be vigilant; to live thoughtlessly.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Usage:** euphemistic; idiomatic; intransitive

6. To be, or appear to be, in repose; to be quiet; to be unemployed, unused, or unagitated; to rest; to lie dormant.

## Noun · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- enPR: slēp — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The state of reduced consciousness during which a human or animal rests in a daily rhythm.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** I really need some sleep.

   **Example:** We need to conduct an overnight sleep test to diagnose your sleep problem.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. An act or instance of sleeping.

   **Usage:** countable; informal

   **Example:** I’m just going to have a quick sleep.

   **Example:** a sound night’s sleep

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. A night.

   **Usage:** countable; informal; metonymically; uncountable

   **Example:** There are only three sleeps till Christmas!

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. Rheum, crusty or gummy discharge found in the corner of the eyes after waking, whether real or a figurative objectification of sleep (in the sense of reduced consciousness).

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. A state of plants, usually at night, when their leaflets approach each other and the flowers close and droop, or are covered by the folded leaves.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; botany; natural-sciences

   **Source synonyms:** nyctinasty; nyctitropism

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. The hibernation of animals.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable
