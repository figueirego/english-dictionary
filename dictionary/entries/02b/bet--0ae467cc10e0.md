### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A wager, an agreement between two parties that a stake (usually money) will be paid by the loser to the winner (the winner being the one who correctly forecast the outcome of an event).

   **Example:** Dylan owes Fletcher $30 from an unsuccessful bet.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A candidate (for elections and pageants) or competitor (in multinational sports).

   **Usage:** Philippines; figuratively; informal

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Indicating a degree of certainty, or that something can be relied upon.

   **Example:** It’s a safe bet that it will rain tomorrow.

   **Example:** There's a decent bet that we'll be able to reach the top of that hill in an hour.

   **Example:** It’s an even bet that Jim will come top of the maths test tomorrow.

   **Example:** There's a good bet that Sally will arrive later.

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To stake or pledge upon the outcome of an event; to wager.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; transitive

   **Example:** I('ll) bet I can run faster than you.

   **Example:** I bet you dinner at the Ritz that you can't run faster than me.

   **Example:** I bet(ted) her £5 it wouldn't rain all week.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To be sure of something; to be able to count on something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Fancy going for a drink after work? / You bet I do!

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To place money into the pot in order to require others do the same, usually only used for the first person to place money in the pot on each round.
