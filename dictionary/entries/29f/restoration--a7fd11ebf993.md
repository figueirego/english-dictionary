### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The process of bringing an object back to its original state; the process of restoring something.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** foreskin restoration

   **Example:** The restoration of this painting will take years.

   **Example:** The restoration of this medieval church involved undoing all the Victorian modifications.

   **Source synonyms:** deconversion; restitution; step back; deconversion; regression; restitution; restoration; reversion; step back (idiomatic)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The result of such a process, such as a dental restoration (a dental prosthesis).

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** These restorations were especially impressive and were reported in a leading prosthodontics journal.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The return of a former monarchy or monarch to power, usually after having been forced to step down.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The restoration of the House of Stuart took place a few years after the death of Cromwell.

   **Example:** The restoration of the Kingdom of Spain took place immediately after the death of Franco.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The return of a socioeconomic formation in the role of the dominant mode of production.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The restoration of capitalism in Russia gave rise to unemployment.

   **Example:** The restoration of capitalism in Eastern Europe made way for improvement in human rights.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The receiving of a sinner to divine favor.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** lifestyle; religion; theology
