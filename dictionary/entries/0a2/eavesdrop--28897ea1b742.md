### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To hear (intentionally) a conversation one is not intended to hear; to listen in.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** I hope nobody was eavesdropping on our conversation last night, since it got so personal.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To listen for another organism's calls, so as to exploit them.

## Noun · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈiːvzˌdɹɒp/ — source labels: UK
- IPA: /ˈivzˌdɹɑp/ — source labels: US

### Forms

- eavesdrops — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The dripping of rain from the eaves of a house.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The space around a house on which such water drips.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. A concealed aperture through which an occupant of a building can surreptitiously listen to people talking at an entrance to the building.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. The act of intentionally hearing a conversation not intended to be heard.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** Were you having a little eavesdrop on us last night?
