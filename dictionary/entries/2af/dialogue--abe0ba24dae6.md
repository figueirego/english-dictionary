### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A conversation or other form of discourse between two or more individuals.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Melinda and Bill maintained a dialogue via email over the course of their long-distance relationship.

   **Example:** Start up a dialogue

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. In a dramatic or literary presentation, the verbal parts of the script or text; the verbalizations of the actors or characters.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The movie had great special effects, but the dialogue was lackluster.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** authorship; broadcasting; communications; film; journalism; literature; media; publishing; television; writing

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A literary form, where the presentation resembles a conversation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** A literary historian, she specialized in the dialogues of ancient Greek philosophers.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; philosophy; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The relationship between two texts or artworks interpreted as responding to each other (regardless of whether this is actually possible).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** art; arts; historiography; history; human-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Nonstandard form of dialog (“dialog box”).

   **Usage:** alt-of; countable; nonstandard; uncountable

   **Example:** Once the My Computer dialogue opens, select Local Disk (C&#58;), then right click and scroll down.
