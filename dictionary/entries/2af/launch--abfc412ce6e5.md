**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To throw (a projectile such as a lance, dart or ball); to hurl; to propel with force.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** fling; hurl; bung (Australia; UK); cast; chuck; chunk (Southern-US; slang); cook (UK; dialectal; obsolete); dash; dump; feck; fling; jerk (obsolete); heave; hield; hoy (Australia; Northumbria); huck; hurl; hurtle (archaic; literary); launch; lob; peck (regional); peg (slang); pick (obsolete); pitch; precipitate; project; quoit; shy; [slight](../171/slight--5c7dad260e0b.md); skew (Yorkshire); sling; thrill (obsolete); throw; toss; traject; warp (obsolete; rare); whang (Australia; UK); whip; whop; wing; yeet (slang)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To pierce with, or as with, a lance.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** lance; pierce

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cause (a vessel) to move or slide from the land or a larger vessel into the water; to set afloat.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The navy launched another ship.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cause (a rocket, balloon, etc., or the payload thereof) to begin its flight upward from the ground.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** NASA launched several unmanned rockets before launching any of the Mercury astronauts.

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To send out; to start (someone) on a mission or project; to give a start to (something); to put in operation

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Our business launched a new project.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To start (a program or feature); to execute or bring into operation.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Double-click an icon to launch the associated application.

**Source sense R1.S7**

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Usage:** intransitive
