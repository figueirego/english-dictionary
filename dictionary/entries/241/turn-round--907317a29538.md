## Verb

### Forms

- turns round — present; singular; third-person
- turning round — participle; present
- turned round — participle; past
- turned round — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To revolve or rotate around a centre.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The body was turning round slowly as it fell.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To turn so as to be facing in the opposite direction.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** It seemed that there was someone behind me; but when I turned round, no one was there.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To change one's opinion or attitude (especially when becoming hostile etc.).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** You can't turn round and blame me all of a sudden.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make revolve, rotate.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To put into an opposing position; to reverse.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He turned the glass round and examined the other side.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To make (a ship, airplane etc.) ready for departure.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The stopover is very short, and crews will have about 15 minutes to turn the plane round.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To process; to complete work on (something), especially with a view to sending it on in a finished state.

   **Usage:** colloquial; transitive

   **Example:** New figures are in. We need to turn these round ASAP&#58; the boss wants a report tonight.
