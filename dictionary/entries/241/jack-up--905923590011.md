### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To raise, hoist, or lift a thing using a jack, or similar means.

   **Example:** He jacked the car up to change the tire

   **Example:** The oil rig can be jacked up higher when the hydraulic legs touch the sea floor.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To raise, increase, or accelerate; often said of prices, fees, or rates.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** I can't believe they're going to jack up the price of gasoline again — and after they already raised it twenty cents a gallon!

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To ruin; wreck; mess up; screw up; sometimes as a bowdlerized substitution for fuck up.

   **Usage:** colloquial

   **Example:** I'm not letting him use my computer again; he always jacks it up.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To give up; to abandon (something, e.g. a job, contract)

   **Usage:** Australia; West-Country; dialectal; intransitive; obsolete; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** jig up; throw up; chuck up; discontinue; jack in

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To organise something.

   **Usage:** New-Zealand

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To shoot, especially in the context of a poor shot opportunity.

   **Usage:** colloquial

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; basketball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To improve or embellish on (something).

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** I jacked up the recipes with some fresh ingredients.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To refuse to follow an order.

   **Usage:** informal

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To criticize, discipline or reprimand.

   **Usage:** informal

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To inject a drug into (oneself) intravenously.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; slang
