   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle; obsolete

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

3. To be able to go.

   **Usage:** intransitive; no-past-participle; no-present-participle; poetic

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To have permission to, be allowed. Used in granting permission and in questions to make polite requests.

   **Usage:** defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** you may smoke outside

   **Example:** may I sit there?

   **Example:** May we request that patrons kindly take their seats.

   **Source synonyms:** can; could; might

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Granting the admissibility of a supposition, in a way that can be semantically either subjunctive or indicative.
   1. Expressing a present possibility; possibly.

      **Usage:** defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

      **Example:** he may be lying

      **Example:** Schrödinger's cat may or may not be in the box.

      **Example:** Sam may be intelligent, but he isn't wise. \[This speaker does not know with certainty whether Sam is intelligent, but the speaker allows the possibility.\]

      **Example:** A female crocodile may lay up to forty eggs. \[Typical occurrence.\]

      **Source synonyms:** could; might

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Granting the admissibility of a supposition, in a way that can be semantically either subjunctive or indicative.
   1. Expressing a disjunctive or contrastive relation between indicative statements.

      **Usage:** defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

      **Example:** You may be my boss, but that doesn't mean you can insult me.

      **Example:** A&#58; Sigh. I'm bummed that Stephen Hawking died. B&#58; Well, he may have died, but he's still alive in our hearts. \[This speaker does not doubt that Stephen has died; nonetheless, the verb inflection is not different.\]

      **Source synonyms:** might

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Expressing a wish (with present subjunctive effect).

   **Usage:** defective; no-past-participle; no-present-participle; present; subjunctive; chiefly solemn

   **Example:** may you win; may the weather be sunny; long may your reign last

   **Example:** Let us pray that peace may soon return to our war-torn homeland.

   **Source synonyms:** might

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Used in modesty, courtesy, or concession, or to soften a question or remark.

   **Usage:** defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle
