### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. What, of those mentioned or implied.

   **Usage:** interrogative

   **Example:** Which song shall we play?

   **Example:** They couldn't decide which song to play.

   **Example:** Which one is bigger?

   **Example:** Show me which one is bigger.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The/Any ... that; whichever.

   **Example:** Take which one you want.

   **Example:** You may go which way you please.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Designates the one(s) previously mentioned.

   **Usage:** relative; formal outside certain phrases

   **Example:** He once owned a painting of the house, which painting would later be stolen.

   **Example:** She only speaks Chinese, which language I can hardly follow.

   **Example:** For several seconds he sat in silence, during which time the tea and sandwiches arrived.

   **Example:** I'm thinking of getting a new car, in which case I'd get a red one.

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. What one or ones (of those mentioned or implied).

   **Usage:** interrogative

   **Example:** Which is which?

   **Example:** By now, you must surely know which is which.

   **Example:** Which is bigger, the red one or the blue one?

   **Example:** I'm unable to determine which is bigger.

   **Example:** Which's your spouse? -The one in trousers.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The/Any ones that; whichever.

   **Example:** Please take which you please.

   **Source context (verbatim):** not in common use

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. In a non-restrictive relative clause, referring to something previously mentioned.
   1. Referring to a preceding noun.

      **Usage:** relative

      **Example:** I dropped a saucepan, which knocked over the eggs, which went all over the kitchen floor.

      **Example:** I found my camera, which I thought I'd lost, under the bed.

      **Example:** I entered the room, at the far end of which was a small table.

      **Example:** I am enclosing an application form, which I should be grateful if you'd sign and return.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. In a non-restrictive relative clause, referring to something previously mentioned.
   1. Referring to a preceding noun.
      1. Used of people (now generally who, whom, that; which remains possible with words also referred to by it such as baby, child).

         **Usage:** archaic; relative

         **Example:** She had a young child, which cried incessantly.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. In a non-restrictive relative clause, referring to something previously mentioned.
   1. Referring to a preceding statement.

      **Usage:** relative

      **Example:** The front door was open, which concerned me.

      **Example:** He had to leave, which was very difficult.

      **Example:** She saved my life, for which I am eternally grateful.

      **Example:** You were late, which was bad, but what's worse, you haven't even apologized yet.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. In a restrictive relative clause, referring to a noun previously mentioned.

   **Usage:** proscribed; relative; sometimes

   **Example:** This is the letter which I received.

   **Example:** This is the letter in which he explains his decision.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. In a restrictive relative clause, referring to a noun previously mentioned.
