### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To release.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The students were let out of school early.

   **Example:** If you go into the aviary, don't let out any of the birds!

   **Example:** I was able to let the butterfly out of the window.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Of a school&#58; to finish for the day or term, allowing the pupils to go home.

   **Usage:** Canada; US; intransitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To allow to operate at higher speed by adjusting controls.

   **Example:** He let out the reins when they were a mile from the barn.

   **Example:** The engineer let out the throttle after the train crossed the bridge.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To rent out.

   **Example:** We let out the house and went on a two-year world trip.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To enlarge by adjusting one or more seams.

   **Example:** After the holidays he had to have his suits let out.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of clothing

   **Source antonyms:** take in

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Of sound, to emit.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** The dog let out a yelp.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To disclose.

   **Example:** He accidentally let out the location for the meeting.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To begin to tell a story.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To lay off or fire someone (from a job).

   **Usage:** dated; slang; transitive
