### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The state of being insusceptible to something; notably&#58;
   1. Protective resistance against disease.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** Some people have better immunity to diseases than others.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The state of being insusceptible to something; notably&#58;
   1. An exemption from specified duties, such as payments or services.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** Feudal privileges often included tax and other immunities.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The state of being insusceptible to something; notably&#58;
   1. An exemption from prosecution.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** The prosecutor offered the lieutenant immunity for all the crimes he would testify having known to be planned by the elusive drug baron.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The state of being insusceptible to something; notably&#58;
   1. An exemption from penance.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** lifestyle; religion

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The state of being insusceptible to something; notably&#58;
   1. An exemption given to a player from losing or being withdrawn from play.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** After winning the last round the player was granted immunity which allowed him to stay in the game even after receiving the fewest points.

      **Source context (verbatim):** in games and competitions

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A resistance to a specific thing.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** Superbugs are bacteria that develop an immunity to antibiotics.
