## Verb

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To wait or delay.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** Hold up a minute. I want to check something.

   **Source synonyms:** hold on; hang on

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To impede; detain.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I've got to get to work now. Why are you holding me up?

   **Example:** What is holding up traffic?

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To impede; detain.
   1. To detain by threatening, usually with a weapon, in order to commit robbery.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** The guy tried to hold up a bank.

      **Source synonyms:** stick up

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To support or lift.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Hold up the table while I slide this underneath.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To support or lift.
   1. To highlight, as if lifting up for display.

      **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

      **Example:** His lectures held up Napoleon as an example of the phenomenon.

      **Example:** held up for public ridicule

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To support or lift.
   1. To fulfil or complete one's part of an agreement.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

      **Example:** I don't think he's holding up his end of the bargain.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To persist.
   1. To withstand; to stand up to; to survive.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

      **Example:** hold up to scrutiny

      **Example:** hold up to heavy use

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To persist.
   1. To keep up; not to fall behind; not to lose ground.

      **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To persist.
   1. To maintain composure despite hardship.

      **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

      **Example:** how're you holdin up?

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To persist.
   1. To continue to be seen as favorable, to avoid seeming dated.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** Bride of Frankenstein is an old film, but it holds up.

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a work
