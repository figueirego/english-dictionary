### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Any of a number of festivals held just before the beginning of Lent.

   **Example:** Carnival of Brazil

   **Example:** Venice Carnival

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A festive occasion marked by parades and sometimes special foods and other entertainment.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A traveling amusement park, called a funfair in British English.

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** We all got to ride the merry-go-round when they brought their carnival to town.

   **Example:** When the carnival came to town, every one wanted some cotton candy.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A context in which transgression or inversion of the social order is given temporary license. Derived from the work of Mikhail Bakhtin.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; sciences; social-science; sociology

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A gaudily chaotic situation.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** a carnival of idiocy
