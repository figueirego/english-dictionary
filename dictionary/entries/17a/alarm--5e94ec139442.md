## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- alarms — plural
- alarum — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A summons to arms, as on the approach of an enemy.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any sound or information intended to give notice of approaching danger; a warning sound to arouse attention; a warning of danger.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A device intended to warn or give notice of approaching danger.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The fire alarm is located high on the wall to prevent tampering.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A sudden attack; a disturbance.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Sudden surprise with fear or terror caused by the apprehension of danger; a feeling of heightened alertness to risk.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** His actions caused alarm among all the other people in the store, who hurried to leave.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A mechanical device for awaking people or rousing their attention.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The clock radio is a friendlier version of the cold alarm by the bedside.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. An instance of an alarm ringing, beeping or clanging, to give a noise signal at a certain time.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** Last night's alarm turned out to be nothing, but it was terrifying at the time.

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈlɑːm/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /əˈlɑɹm/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /əˈlɑ(rə)m/ — source labels: South-Asia

### Forms

- alarms — present; singular; third-person
- alarming — participle; present
- alarmed — participle; past
- alarmed — past
- alarum — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To call to arms for defense.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To give (someone) notice of approaching danger or necessary action; to rouse to vigilance; to put on the alert.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The sudden drop in temperature should alarm you into taking precautions.

   **Example:** Her illness started to alarm the doctors, who ordered more tests.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To produce a warning of approaching danger or necessary action; to emit a signal intended to rouse a recipient to vigilance or put them on the alert.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a device

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To surprise with apprehension of danger; to fill with anxiety in regard to threatening evil; to excite with sudden fear.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The loud bang in the hallway seemed to alarm everyone in the building.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To keep in excitement; to disturb.

   **Usage:** transitive
