## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈskɛəkɹəʊ/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈskɛɚˌkɹoʊ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- scarecrows — plural
- scare-crow — alternative; obsolete
- skarcrow — alternative; obsolete

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An effigy, typically made of straw and dressed in old clothes, fixed to a pole in a field to deter birds from eating crops or seeds planted there.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A person or animal regarded as resembling a scarecrow (sense 1) in some way; especially, a tall, thin, awkward person; or a person wearing ragged and tattered clothes.

   **Usage:** broadly; derogatory

   **Source synonyms:** beanpole

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Synonym of crow scarer (“a farmhand employed to scare birds from the fields”).

   **Usage:** dated

   **Source synonyms:** crow scarer (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Anything that appears terrifying but presents no danger; a paper tiger.

   **Usage:** figuratively

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Military equipment or tactics used to scare and deter rather than cause actual damage.

   **Usage:** figuratively; historical; World War II

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The black tern (Chlidonias niger).

   **Usage:** UK; dialectal; obsolete

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The hooded crow (Corvus cornix).

   **Usage:** UK; dialectal; obsolete

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈskɛəkɹəʊ/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈskɛɚˌkɹoʊ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- scarecrows — present; singular; third-person
- scarecrowing — participle; present
- scarecrowed — participle; past
- scarecrowed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To cause (a person, their body, etc.) to look awkward and stiff, like a scarecrow (noun sense 1).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause (a person, their body, etc.) to look awkward and stiff, like a scarecrow (noun sense 1).
   1. To splay (one's arms) away from the body, like the arms of a scarecrow.

      **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To frighten or terrify (someone or something), as if using a scarecrow.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To spoil the appearance of (something, such as the landscape or a view), as scarecrows may be regarded as doing.

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive
