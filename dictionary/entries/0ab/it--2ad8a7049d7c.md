### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The third-person singular neuter personal pronoun used to refer to an inanimate object, abstract entity, or non-human living thing.

   **Example:** Take this book and put it on the shelf.

   **Example:** Take each day as it comes.

   **Example:** I found a poor little cat. It seems to be half starving.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A third-person singular personal pronoun used to refer to a baby or child, especially of unknown gender.

   **Example:** She took the baby and held it in her arms.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A third-person singular pronoun used to refer to an unspecified person.

   **Usage:** rare

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An affectionate third-person singular personal pronoun.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A third-person singular personal pronoun used to refer to an animate referent who is transgender or non-binary.

   **Usage:** derogatory; offensive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Refers to someone being identified, often on the phone, but not limited to this situation.

   **Example:** It's me, John.

   **Example:** Somebody wanted a drink, didn't they? Who was it?

   **Example:** It is I, your king.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The impersonal pronoun, used without referent as the subject of an impersonal verb or statement (known as the dummy pronoun, dummy it or weather it).

   **Example:** It is nearly 10 o’clock.

   **Example:** It’s 10&#58;45.

   **Example:** It’s very cold today.

   **Example:** It’s lonely here without you.

   **Example:** It seems so.

   **Example:** It blew all night.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. The impersonal pronoun, used without referent, or with unstated but contextually implied referent, in various short idioms or expressions.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The impersonal pronoun, used without referent, or with unstated but contextually implied referent, in various short idioms or expressions.
   1. Referring to a desirable quality or ability, or quality of being successful, fashionable or in vogue.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. The impersonal pronoun, used without referent, or with unstated but contextually implied referent, in various short idioms or expressions.
   1. Referring to sexual intercourse or other sexual activity.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. Sex appeal, especially that which goes beyond physical appearance.

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. The impersonal pronoun, used as a placeholder for a delayed subject, or less commonly, object; known as the dummy pronoun (according to some definitions), anticipatory it or, more formally in linguistics, a syntactic expletive. The delayed subject is commonly a to-infinitive, a gerund, or a noun clause introduced by a subordinating conjunction.

   **Example:** It’s not worth talking to you.

   **Example:** It is easy to see how she would think that.

   **Example:** It was felt that I'd be the right man for the job.

   **Example:** I find it odd that you would say that.

   **Example:** It is hard seeing you so sick.

   **Example:** He saw to it that everyone would vote for him.

   **Example:** It is not clear if the report was true.

   **Example:** She made it clear what she expected by the weekend, which made it a problem to juggle her and other customers' jobs at the same time.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. All or the end; something after which there is no more.

   **Example:** Are there more students in this class, or is this it?

   **Example:** That's it—I'm not going to any more candy stores with you.

   **Example:** Well, looks like this is it. We're all going to die.

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. Followed by an omitted and understood relative pronoun&#58; That which; what.

   **Usage:** obsolete
