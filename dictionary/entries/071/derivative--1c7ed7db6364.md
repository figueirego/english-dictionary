<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. One of the two fundamental objects of study in calculus (the other being integration), which quantifies the rate of change, tangency, and other qualities arising from the local behavior of a function.
   1. The derived function of f(x)&#58; the function giving the instantaneous rate of change of f; equivalently, the function giving the slope of the line tangent to the graph of f. Written f'(x) or (df)/(dx) in Leibniz's notation, ̇f(x) in Newton's notation (the latter used particularly when the independent variable is time).

      **Example:** The derivative of x² is 2x; if f(x)\#61;x², then f'(x)\#61;2x

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** calculus; mathematics; sciences

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a function of a single variable f(x)

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. One of the two fundamental objects of study in calculus (the other being integration), which quantifies the rate of change, tangency, and other qualities arising from the local behavior of a function.
   1. The value of such a derived function for a given value of its independent variable&#58; the rate of change of a function at a point in its domain.

      **Example:** The derivative of f(x)\#61;x³ at x\#61;2 is 12.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** calculus; mathematics; sciences

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. One of the two fundamental objects of study in calculus (the other being integration), which quantifies the rate of change, tangency, and other qualities arising from the local behavior of a function.
   1. Any of several related generalizations of the derivative&#58; the directional derivative, partial derivative, Fréchet derivative, functional derivative, etc.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** calculus; mathematics; sciences

      **Source context (verbatim):** of more general classes of functions
