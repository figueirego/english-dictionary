### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A thing that has physical existence but is not alive.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Objective; goal, end or purpose of something.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The noun phrase which is an internal complement of a verb phrase or a prepositional phrase. In a verb phrase with a transitive action verb, it is typically the receiver of the action.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** grammar; human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A person or thing toward which an emotion is directed.

   **Example:** Mary Jane had been the object of Peter's affection for years.

   **Example:** The convertible, once the object of his desire, was now the object of his hatred.

   **Example:** Where's your object of ridicule now?

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A person or thing toward which an emotion is directed.
   1. A cause for attention or concern.

      **Example:** the object of a strongly worded censure

      **Example:** Money is no object to him.

      **Source context (verbatim):** in the negative

      **Source synonyms:** focus

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An instantiation of a class or structure.

   **Usage:** object-oriented programming

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. An instance of one of the two kinds of entities that form a category, the other kind being the arrows (also called morphisms).

   **Example:** Similarly, there is a category whose objects are groups and whose arrows are the homomorphisms from one group to another.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** category-theory; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To disagree with or oppose something or someone; (especially in a Court of Law) to raise an objection.

   **Usage:** intransitive
