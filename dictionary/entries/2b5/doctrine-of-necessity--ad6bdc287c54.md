## Noun

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Necessarianism, especially as espoused by Joseph Priestley.

   **Usage:** metaphysics

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; lifestyle; philosophy; religion; sciences; theology

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A principle whereby a normally criminal act is justified by the necessity of preserving something of greater utilitarian value than that lost or sacrificed; not to be confused with self-defence.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The principle that, in a situation of emergency or exigent circumstance, a state may legitimately act in ways that would normally be illegal.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; law; politics

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The principle that the laws, of governance in action, should be deemed valid insofar as they do not contradict the constitution. Validated on the basis that maintenance of government is of greater utilitarian value than maintenance of the law.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; law; politics

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The principle that a state in immediate peril to its existence, from a situation not of its own doing, may in extremis be justified in violating a right of another state.

   **Usage:** international law

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; politics

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** necessarianism — doctrine of philosophical necessity; duress of circumstances — principle of preservation of greater utilitarian value
