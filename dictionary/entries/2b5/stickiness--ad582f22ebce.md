### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The property of sticking or adhering; adhesion.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Warmth and humidity, as on a muggy day.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Of prices or wages&#58; the tendency to stay the same despite changes in the economy.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** economics; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The presence of unique attributes that make a product indisposable and valuable to its owner.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually; interface design

   **Example:** The stickiness of a cellphone might be measured by its ability to tell correct time, locate its user, and allow its user to remain connected regardless of location.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; marketing

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A research measure that captures the extent to which viewers wish to spend more time looking at or reading a print advertisement.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** The low stickiness rating helped explain why our ad’s brand linkage rating was so low&#58; people were turned off by the blurriness of the main photo.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** advertising; business; marketing

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually
