### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To acquire, bring in, or introduce.

   **Example:** The ship took on cargo in Norfolk yesterday.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To acquire, bring in, or introduce.
   1. To obtain the services of (a person) in exchange for remuneration; to give someone a job.

      **Example:** Taking on a first employee should be done with care and consideration.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To begin to have or exhibit.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** In the dark, the teddy bear took on the appearance of a fearsome monster.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To assume or take responsibility for.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** I'll take on the project if no one else will.

   **Example:** He took on the children's welfare as an extra charge.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To assume or take responsibility for.
   1. To accept (feedback).

      **Usage:** UK; idiomatic

      **Example:** Thanks for your comments, I'll take them on.

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To attempt to fight, compete with, or engage with.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** I don't recommend taking on that bully, since he's bigger than you are.
