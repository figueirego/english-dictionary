### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A pair of two similar or equal things; couple.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. One of two or more different words in a language derived from the same etymological root but having different phonological forms (e.g., toucher and toquer in French or shade and shadow in English). See also Appendix&#58;Glossary\#doublet.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. In textual criticism, two different narrative accounts of the same actual event.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** literature; media; publishing

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An imitation gem made of two pieces of glass or crystal with a layer of color between them.

   **Usage:** lapidary

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A word or phrase set a second time by mistake.

   **Usage:** US

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** media; printing; publishing

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A quantum state of a system with a spin of ½, such that there are two allowed values of the spin component, −½ and +½.

   **Usage:** quantum mechanics

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A word (or rather, a halfword) consisting of two bytes.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A very small flowering plant, Dimeresia howellii.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; botany; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A word ladder puzzle.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. An arrangement of two lenses for a microscope, designed to correct spherical aberration and chromatic dispersion, thus rendering the image of an object more clear and distinct.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. Either of two dice, each of which, when thrown, has the same number of spots on the face lying uppermost.

   **Example:** to throw doublets
