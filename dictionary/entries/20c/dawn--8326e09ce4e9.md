### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To begin to brighten with daylight.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** A new day dawns.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To start to appear or become obvious.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** I don’t want to be there when the truth dawns on him.

   **Example:** The realization dawned on him that few would pass that final exam.

   **Source synonyms:** glimpse

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To begin to give promise; to begin to appear or to expand.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The morning twilight period immediately before sunrise.

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The rising of the sun.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Source synonyms:** break of dawn; break of day; daybreak; day-dawn; dayspring; sunrise

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. The time when the sun rises.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** She rose before dawn to meet the train.

   **Example:** The platoon braced itself for the dawn offensive.

   **Source synonyms:** break of dawn; break of day; crack of dawn; daybreak; day-dawn; dayspring; sunrise

**Source sense R2.S4**

   **Example:** The dawn of civilization didn't imply twilight of barbarity.
