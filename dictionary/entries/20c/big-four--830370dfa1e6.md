### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The four top Allied powers of World War I and their leaders&#58; David Lloyd George of Britain, Georges Clemenceau of France, Vittorio Emanuele Orlando of Italy and Woodrow Wilson of the United States, who met at the Paris Peace Conference in 1919 at Versailles.

   **Usage:** historical; plural; plural-only

   **Source synonyms:** Council of Four; The Four Nations

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A group of four entrepreneurs who formed the Central Pacific Railroad.

   **Usage:** plural; plural-only

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A jazz rhythm derived from the marching band beat.

   **Usage:** plural; plural-only

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; music

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Any group of four major or largest corporations in a given industry, especially when such a small group has oligopoly or near-oligopoly.
   1. The four largest banks in any of various countries, or (internationally) the Federal Reserve, People's Bank of China, Bank of Japan, and European Central Bank.

      **Usage:** plural; plural-only

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Any group of four major or largest corporations in a given industry, especially when such a small group has oligopoly or near-oligopoly.
   1. The four largest corporations of Big Tech during the 2000s–2020s, usually defined as Alphabet (f.k.a. Google), Meta (f.k.a. Facebook), Amazon, and Apple.

      **Usage:** plural; plural-only

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; engineering; finance; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; technology

      **Source synonyms:** Gang of Four

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Any group of four major or largest corporations in a given industry, especially when such a small group has oligopoly or near-oligopoly.
   1. The four largest global auditing firms, comprising Deloitte, Ernst &amp; Young (EY), KPMG, and PwC.

      **Usage:** plural; plural-only

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** accounting; business; finance

   1. The four largest railway companies in the United Kingdom in the period 1923–1947&#58; the Great Western Railway (GWR); the London, Midland and Scottish Railway (LMS); the London and North Eastern Railway (LNER); and the Southern Railway (SR).
