## Verb · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈmɛləʊ/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈmɛloʊ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- mellowing — participle; present
- mellowed — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To cause (fruit) to become soft or tender, specifically by ripening.

   **Usage:** US; informal; transitive

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To cause (food or drink, for example, cheese or wine, or its flavour) to become matured and smooth, and not acidic, harsh, or sharp.

   **Usage:** US; informal; transitive

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To soften (land or soil) and make it suitable for planting in.

   **Usage:** US; informal; regional; transitive; archaic except UK

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To reduce or remove the harshness or roughness from (something); to soften, to subdue, to tone down.

   **Usage:** US; figuratively; informal; transitive

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To cause (a person) to become calmer, gentler, and more understanding, particularly from age or experience.

   **Usage:** US; figuratively; informal; transitive

   **Example:** The fervour of early feeling is tempered and mellowed by the ripeness of age.

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To cause (a person) to become slightly or pleasantly drunk or intoxicated.

   **Usage:** US; figuratively; informal; transitive; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. Followed by out&#58; to relax (a person); in particular, to cause (a person) to become pleasantly high or stoned by taking drugs.

   **Usage:** US; also; figuratively; informal; reflexive; transitive

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. To mature and lose its harshness or sharpness.

   **Usage:** US; informal; intransitive; or its flavour

   **Source context (verbatim):** of food or drink

<a id="r3-s9"></a>

**Source sense R3.S9**

9. To be rendered soft and suitable for planting in.

   **Usage:** US; informal; intransitive; regional; archaic except UK

   **Source context (verbatim):** of soil

<a id="r3-s10"></a>

**Source sense R3.S10**

10. To lose harshness; to become gentler, subdued, or toned down.

   **Usage:** US; figuratively; informal; intransitive

<a id="r3-s11"></a>

**Source sense R3.S11**

11. To relax; in particular, to become pleasantly high or stoned by taking drugs.

   **Usage:** US; figuratively; informal; intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** followed by out; of a person
