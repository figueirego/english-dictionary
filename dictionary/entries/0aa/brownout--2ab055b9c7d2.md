### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A period of low alternating current line voltage, causing a reduction in illumination.

   **Example:** When demand for electricity exceeds the available supply, a brownout occurs.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

   **Source synonyms:** dimout

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A temporary dimming of vision, usually with a brown hue and accompanied by loss of peripheral vision or tunnel vision.

   **Example:** On rising suddenly from her chair she experienced a brownout and had to sit down again quickly.

   **Source synonyms:** grayout; greyout

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A temporary closing of a fire station, usually due to budget restrictions.

   **Example:** Fire departments across the country have been forced to rely on rolling brownouts because of the recession.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** firefighting; government

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A power outage during the day; distinguished from a blackout, which occurs at night.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A moment or period where something is weakened, diminished or less effective.

   **Usage:** figuratively; uncommon

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A blackout, regardless of time of day.

   **Usage:** Australia; Hong-Kong; Philippines
