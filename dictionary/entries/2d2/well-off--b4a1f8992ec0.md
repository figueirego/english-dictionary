### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Of a person&#58; in fortunate circumstances, especially having financial security, at any level from intermediate (comfortably off) to high (wealthy).

   **Example:** He is very well off as a result of his stock portfolio containing some big winners over the years.

   **Source synonyms:** well-heeled; comfortable; well-endowed; wealthy; bow-legged wi' brass (Yorkshire; humorous; idiomatic); comfortably off; filthy rich; loaded; fat; minted (British; Ireland; slang); moneyed; moneybags; of means; oofy (UK; dated; slang); rich; rich as Croesus; rolling; rolling in dough; rolling in it; upper crust; wealthy; well-endowed; well-heeled; well off; well-to-do

   **Source antonyms:** badly off; ill off; almsless; arm; badly off; bankrupt; beggared; beggarly; boracic; broke; broken; broker than the Ten Commandments; depauperate (obsolete); destitute; dirt poor; disadvantaged; down and out; down at heel; down on one's luck; down on one's uppers; empty-handed; farthingless; feeling the pinch; flat; goodless; groatless (archaic; rare); hard up; impecunious; impoverished; in need; indigent; insolvent; lower-class; moneyless; necessitous; needy; oofless (UK; dated; slang); pauperized; penniless; penurious; pinched; pok kai (Malaysia; slang); poor; poor as a church mouse; poor as a rat; poor as Job; possessionless; poverty-ridden; poverty-stricken; shillingless; skint; stone-broke; stony-broke; strapped; stuck (archaic; slang); threadbare (archaic; obsolete); unaffluent; unrich; unwealthy; wealthless

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Of a person or thing&#58; in a good position or circumstance.

   **Usage:** archaic; dialectal

   **Example:** He would be well off if he would simply quit this place and never look back.

   **Source synonyms:** doing well
