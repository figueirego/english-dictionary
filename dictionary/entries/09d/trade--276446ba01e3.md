## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /tɹeɪd/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- trades — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The buying and selling of goods and services on a market.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** commerce

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A particular instance of buying or selling, or a series of related transactions executed as a single investment.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** I did no trades with them once the rumors started.

   **Source synonyms:** deal; barter

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An idea or strategy for an investment on a market.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** "Sell America" is the latest trade on Wall Street.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An instance of bartering items in exchange for one another.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Those who perform a particular kind of skilled work.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The skilled trades were the first to organize modern labor unions.

   **Source synonyms:** business

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Those engaged in an industry or group of related industries.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** It is not a retail showroom. It is only for the trade.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The skilled practice of a practical occupation.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** He learned his trade as an apprentice.

   **Source synonyms:** craft

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An occupation in the secondary sector, as opposed to an agricultural, professional or military one.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** After failing his entrance exams, he decided to go into a trade.

   **Example:** Most veterans went into trade when the war ended.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The business given to a commercial establishment by its customers.

   **Usage:** UK; uncountable

   **Example:** Even before noon there was considerable trade.

   **Example:** Trade has been down in this cold weather.

   **Example:** The gift shop relies on passing trade from tourists.

   **Source synonyms:** business; takings; custom; footfall; patronage

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Steady winds blowing from east to west above and below the equator.

   **Usage:** countable; in-plural; uncountable

   **Example:** They rode the trades going west.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. A publication intended for participants in an industry or related group of industries.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Rumors about layoffs are all over the trades.

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /tɹeɪd/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- trades — present; singular; third-person
- trading — participle; present
- traded — participle; past
- traded — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To engage in trade.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** This company trades (in) precious metal.

   **Example:** He withdrew money to his bank account after several years of trading stocks on E-Trade.

   **Source synonyms:** deal

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To be traded at a certain price or under certain conditions.

   **Usage:** copulative; intransitive

   **Example:** Apple is trading at $200.

   **Example:** ExxonMobil trades on the NYSE.

   **Example:** The stock is trading rich relative to its sector.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To give (something) in exchange (for).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Will you trade your precious watch for my earring?

   **Source context (verbatim):** with for

   **Source synonyms:** exchange; swap; switch; truck

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To mutually exchange (something) (with).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The rival schoolboys traded insults and punches.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To use or exploit a particular aspect, such as a name, reputation, or image, to gain advantage or benefit.

   **Usage:** transitive; with-on

   **Example:** Some musicians try to trade on their past success by playing the same hits over and over again.

   **Source synonyms:** exploit; capitalize on; take advantage of; use; leverage; benefit from; make use of; milk

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To give someone a plant and receive a different one in return.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** agriculture; business; horticulture; lifestyle

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To do business; offer for sale as for one's livelihood.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source synonyms:** do business

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To have dealings; to be concerned or associated (with).

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To recommend and get recommendations.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** exchange
