### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. (of an employer) To dismiss (workers) from employment, e.g. at a time of low business volume or through no fault of the worker, often with a severance package.

   **Usage:** US; idiomatic; transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To place all or part of a bet with another bookmaker in order to reduce risk.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a bookmaker

   **Source synonyms:** hedge one's bets

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cease, quit, stop (doing something).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Lay off the singing, will you! I'm trying to study.

   **Example:** When are you gonna lay off smoking?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To stop bothering, teasing, or pestering someone; to leave (someone) alone.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Just lay off, okay! I've had enough!

   **Example:** Things have been better since the boss has been laying off a little.

   **Example:** I told him to lay off me but he wouldn't stop.

   **Example:** Lay off it, already!

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. In painting, to apply gentle strokes to smooth a wet coat of paint so as to remove visible roller- or brush-marks, commonly using a dry brush; a similar technique, but using a loaded laying-off brush, may produce a smooth coat of paint when using a roller or the usual brush techniques would leave marks.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** He shows me how to lay off the paint — and moves his paintbrush across the section he had already painted, again and again.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** art; arts

   **Source synonyms:** tip off

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To plan out (a navigational course) using a chart.

   **Usage:** transitive
