### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To set free (a person or group) from the oppression or restraint of another; to liberate.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To set free (a person or group) from the oppression or restraint of another; to liberate.
   1. To cause (a place) to be free from the colonization or rule of another entity.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** to emancipate a colony

      **Source synonyms:** decolonize

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To set free (a person or group) from the oppression or restraint of another; to liberate.
   1. Often followed by from&#58; chiefly with reference to slavery in the United States, and in Central and South America&#58; to set free (oneself or someone) from imprisonment, or from serfdom or slavery.

      **Usage:** also; reflexive; transitive

      **Example:** to pass a law emancipating slaves

      **Source synonyms:** disenslave; enfranchise; manumit; unenslave

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To set free (a person or group) from the oppression or restraint of another; to liberate.
   1. To release (a minor) from the legal authority and custody which a parent or guardian has over them; also (Ancient Rome, historical), to release (a child) from the legal authority of the paterfamilias.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** The child was emancipated from her parents

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Often followed by from&#58; to free (oneself or someone, or something) from some constraint or controlling influence (especially when evil or undue); also, to free (oneself or someone) from mental oppression.

   **Usage:** also; figuratively; reflexive; transitive

   **Example:** Education can emancipate us from error or prejudices.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To place (something) under one's control; specifically (chiefly reflexive), to cause (oneself or someone) to become the slave of another person; to enslave; also, to subjugate (oneself or someone).

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To become free from the oppression or restraint of another.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete
