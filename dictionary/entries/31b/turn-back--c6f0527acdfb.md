## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To reverse direction and retrace one's steps.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Realising he had forgotten his briefcase, he turned back and re-entered the office.

   **Source synonyms:** about turn; about face

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause to reverse direction and retrace one's steps.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The barrage of machine-gun fire turned back the encroaching soldiers.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To return to a previous state of being.

   **Example:** He stopped drinking for a couple of years, but now he has turned back to his old ways.

   **Example:** Once we take this decision, there's no turning back.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To prevent, or refuse to allow, passage or progress.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The soldiers turned back all the refugees at the frontier.

   **Source synonyms:** drive away; repel; turn away; stop

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To adjust to a previous setting.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In Autumn we normally turn the clocks back one hour.

   **Example:** I love that song&#58; turn back to it!

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To fold something back; to fold down.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** When you make the bed, please always turn the sheet back over the blanket.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To give back; to return.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive
