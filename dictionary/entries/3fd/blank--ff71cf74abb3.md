### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To make void; to erase.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I blanked out my previous entry.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To ignore (a person) deliberately.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** She blanked me for no reason.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To render ineffective by blanketing with turbulent airflow, such as from aircraft wake or reverse thrust.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** At high angles of attack, the shuttle’s rudder is blanked by the fuselage and wings, forcing it to use its RCS thrusters for yaw control.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The team was blanked.

   **Example:** England blanks Wales to advance to the final.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To become blank.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To experience a temporary lapse of memory; to be temporarily unable to remember a particular fact. (Commonly used in the first person, present progressive tense, and commonly followed by on to create a transitive phrasal verb.)

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** I’m blanking on her name right now.

   **Example:** She asked him a simple question during the interview, and he blanked.
