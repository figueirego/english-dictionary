## Adjective · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ʌnˈkaʊntəbl̩/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. So many as to be incapable of being counted.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** The reasons for our failure were as uncountable as the grains of sand on a beach.

   **Source synonyms:** countless; uncounted; numberless; innumerable; countless; innumerable; numberless; uncountable; uncounted; unnumberable; unnumbered; untold

   **Source antonyms:** countable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Incapable of being put into one-to-one correspondence with the natural numbers or any subset thereof.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Cantor’s “diagonal proof” shows that the set of real numbers is uncountable.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

   **Source antonyms:** countable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. That cannot be used freely with numbers or the indefinite article, and therefore usually takes no plural form. Example&#58; information.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Many languages do not distinguish countable nouns from uncountable nouns.

   **Example:** One meaning in law of the usually uncountable noun "information" is used in the plural and is countable.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** grammar; human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a noun

   **Source synonyms:** noncount; non-count

   **Source antonyms:** countable
