### Senses

**Source sense R1.S1**

3. A gait of a four-legged animal between walk and canter, a diagonal gait (in which diagonally opposite pairs of legs move together).

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /tɹɑt/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /tɹɒt/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- trots — present; singular; third-person
- trotting — participle; present
- trotted — participle; past
- trotted — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To move along briskly; specifically, to move at a pace between a walk and a run.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I didn't want to miss my bus, so I trotted the last few hundred yards to the stop.

   **Example:** The dog trotted along obediently by his master's side.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To move at a gait between a walk and a canter.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a horse

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To cause to move, as a horse or other animal, in the pace called a trot; to cause to run without galloping or cantering.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To bid against (a person) at an auction, so as to raise the price of the goods.

   **Usage:** UK; archaic; slang; transitive

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** run; jog — to walk rapidly; pace — to walk rapidly
