### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To come out of the water, in order to rest

   **Source context (verbatim):** said of a seal or sea-lion

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To pull on an outhaul in order to extend (a sail) along a spar, or to pull on a reef-tackle to reef (a sail).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To turn sharply in order to deviate from the current heading or course.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To pull a boat out of the water; (by extension, loosely) to drydock a ship.

   **Example:** They managed to haul out their motorboat by paying a local to drag it ashore with a line hitched to a truck.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see haul, out.
