### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To draw air into (inhale), and expel air from (exhale), the lungs in order to extract oxygen and excrete waste gases.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To take in needed gases and expel waste gases in a similar way.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Fish have gills so they can breathe underwater.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To inhale (a gas) to sustain life.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** While life as we know it depends on oxygen, scientists have speculated that alien life forms might breathe chlorine or methane.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To live.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** I will not allow it, as long as I still breathe.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To draw something into the lungs.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Try not to breathe too much smoke.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To expel air from the lungs, exhale.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** If you breathe on a mirror, it will fog up.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To exhale or expel (something) in the manner of breath.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The flowers breathed a heady perfume.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To give an impression of, to exude.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The decor positively breathes classical elegance.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To whisper quietly.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He breathed the words into her ear, but she understood them all.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To pass like breath; noiselessly or gently; to emanate; to blow gently.

   **Example:** The wind breathes through the trees.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To inspire (scripture).

   **Usage:** chiefly Evangelical and Charismatic Christianity

   **Source context (verbatim):** with God as agent

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To exchange gases with the environment.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Uncork the wine and leave it on the table for a few minutes to allow it to breathe.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. Of a material etc., to allow gases to pass through.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Garments made of certain new materials breathe well and keep the skin relatively dry during exercise.

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To rest; to stop and catch one's breath.

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. To stop, to give (a horse) an opportunity to catch its breath.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** At higher altitudes you need to breathe your horse more often.

<a id="r1-s16"></a>

**Source sense R1.S16**

16. To exercise; to tire by brisk exercise.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s17"></a>

**Source sense R1.S17**

17. To passionately devote much of one's life to (an activity, etc.).

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** ―Do you like hiking? ―Are you kidding? I breathe hiking.
