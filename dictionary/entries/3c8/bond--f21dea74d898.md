## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /bɒnd/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /bɑnd/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A document constituting evidence of a long-term debt, by which the bond issuer (the borrower) is obliged to pay interest when due, and repay the principal at maturity, as specified on the face of the bond certificate. The rights of the holder are specified in the bond indenture, which contains the legal terms and conditions under which the bond was issued. Bonds are available in two forms&#58; registered bonds, and bearer bonds.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A documentary obligation to pay a sum or to perform a contract; a debenture.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Investors face a quandary. Cash offers a return of virtually zero in many developed countries; government-bond yields may have risen in recent weeks but they are still unattractive. Equities have suffered two big bear markets since 2000 and are wobbling again. It is hardly surprising that pension funds, insurers and endowments are searching for new sources of return.

   **Example:** Many say that government and corporate bonds are a good investment to balance against a portfolio consisting primarily of stocks.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A partial payment made to show a provider that the customer is sincere about buying a product or a service. If the product or service is not purchased the customer then forfeits the bond.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A physical connection which binds, a band.

   **Usage:** countable; in-plural; often; uncountable

   **Example:** The prisoner was brought before the tribunal in iron bonds.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An emotional link, connection or union; that which holds two or more people together, as in a friendship; a tie.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** They had grown up as friends and neighbors, and not even vastly differing political views could break the bond of their friendship.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Moral or political duty or obligation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A link or force between neighbouring atoms in a molecule.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Organic chemistry primarily consists of the study of carbon bonds, in their many variations.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A binding agreement, a covenant.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** You could rely on him. His word was his bond.

   **Example:** Herbert resented his wife for subjecting him to the bonds of matrimony; he claimed they had gotten married while drunk.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The state of being stored in a bonded warehouse

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** liquor bottled in bond

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A bail bond.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The bailiff released the prisoner as soon as the bond was posted.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. Bond paper.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; by ellipsis

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. Any constraining or cementing force or material.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** A bond of superglue adhered the teacups to the ceiling, much to the consternation of the cafe owners.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. In building, a specific pattern of bricklaying, based on overlapping rows or layers to give strength.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; construction; manufacturing

   **Source synonyms:** bondwork

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

   **Usage:** Scotland; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. A heavy copper wire or rod connecting adjacent rails of an electric railway track when used as a part of the electric circuit.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To connect, secure or tie with a bond; to bind.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The gargantuan ape was bonded in iron chains and carted onto the stage.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause to adhere (one material with another).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The children bonded their snapshots to the scrapbook pages with mucilage.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To form a chemical compound with.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Under unusual conditions, even gold can be made to bond with other elements.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To guarantee or secure a financial risk.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The contractor was bonded with a local underwriter.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To form a friendship or emotional connection.

   **Example:** The men had bonded while serving together in Vietnam.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To put in a bonded warehouse; to secure (goods) until the associated duties are paid.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To lay bricks in a specific pattern.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; construction; manufacturing

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To make a reliable electrical connection between two conductors (or any pieces of metal that may potentially become conductors).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** A house's distribution panel should always be bonded to the grounding rods via a panel bond.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics
