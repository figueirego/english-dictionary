**Source sense R3.S2**

2. simple past of may
   1. Used to indicate a desired past action that was not done.

      **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

      **Example:** You might have warned me about the thunderstorm.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. Used to indicate conditional or possible actions; would perhaps/maybe.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** Well, I might go to the party on Sunday, but I haven't decided yet.

   **Source synonyms:** [may](../3b9/may--ee4d988c65de.md)

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. Used concessively to admit something before making a more accurate or important statement; although

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** I might be in a wheelchair, but I still want to be treated as a lady.

   **Source synonyms:** [may](../3b9/may--ee4d988c65de.md)

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. Used in polite requests for permission.

   **Usage:** archaic; auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle; often

   **Example:** "Might I take the last biscuit? I wonder if I might have a little more coffee too." "Yes you may, help yourself.".

   **Source synonyms:** could; can; [may](../3b9/may--ee4d988c65de.md)

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. Used to express certainty.

   **Usage:** UK; auxiliary; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** Yeah, I think we might need something a bit sturdier.
