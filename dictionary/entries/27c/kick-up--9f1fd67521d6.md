## Verb

### Forms

- kicks up — present; singular; third-person
- kicking up — participle; present

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To rear back; to become more active or restless; to speed up.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a horse

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To raise, to increase (a price).

   **Usage:** US; broadly; figuratively; informal; transitive

   **Example:** The rent has been kicked up again.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To stir up (trouble), to cause (a disturbance).

   **Usage:** figuratively; informal; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make more exciting.

   **Usage:** colloquial; transitive

   **Example:** kick it up a notch

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To show anger (about something).

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** He kicked up about it when they told him the train had been cancelled.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To function improperly; to show signs of disorder; (of an illness) to flare up.

   **Usage:** US; informal; intransitive

   **Example:** The car is kicking up.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To move sharply upward.

   **Usage:** machinery

   **Example:** See, that rod there is supposed to kick up to engage the gear.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To pass (something, such as a proposal or a share of a bribe) up a hierarchy or chain of command.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Example:** You have to kick up some money to the boss.

   **Example:** Let me kick your offer up and see what the execs say.

   **Source synonyms:** run up the flagpole

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see kick, up.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** You could tell where he had been by the cloud of dust he had kicked up.
