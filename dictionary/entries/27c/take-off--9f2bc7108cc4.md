   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He took off his shoes.

   **Example:** Tomorrow the doctor will take the cast off her arm.

2. To leave the ground and ascend into the air or into flight.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The plane has been cleared to take off from runway 3.

   **Example:** That high-jumper takes off from her left foot.

   **Example:** Mute swans need a long stretch of water in order to take off.

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To stand up on a surfboard and begin to surf a breaking wave.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports; surfing

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become successful, to flourish.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The business has really taken off this year and has made quite a profit.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To depart.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I'm going to take off now.

   **Example:** Take off, loser!

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To imitate (somebody), often in a satirical manner.

   **Usage:** transitive; usually

   **Example:** Much of his act involves taking off the Prime Minister and other politicians.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To take (a specified time period) as a vacation or break from work or other activity.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'd like to take next Friday off to attend a wedding.

   **Example:** He decided to let his mother take a night off from cooking, so he took her and his siblings out to dinner.

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To absent oneself from work or other responsibility, especially with permission.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive

   **Example:** If you take off for Thanksgiving you must work Christmas and vice versa.

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To quantify.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'll take off the concrete and steel for this construction project.

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive; slang
