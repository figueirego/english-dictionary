<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The process of gathering the ripened crop; harvesting.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The constant rain made the harvest a nightmare this year.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** agriculture; business; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The yield of harvesting, i.e., the gathered crops or fruits.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** This year's cotton harvest was great but the corn harvest was disastrous.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The product or result of any exertion or course of action; reward or consequences.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The surveillance mission yielded a healthy harvest of intel.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The season of gathering ripened crops; specifically, the time of reaping and gathering grain.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The third season of the year; autumn; fall.

   **Usage:** UK; countable; dialectal; uncountable

   **Example:** Harvest is usually very damp and rainy.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A modern pagan ceremony held on or around the autumn equinox, which is in the harvesting season.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** lifestyle; paganism; religion

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We harvested the apples in September already.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To take a living organism as part of a managed process to gather food or resources, often with the intention of maintaining a healthy population.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** An efficient rifle or shotgun can harvest a deer for venison.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The rising star harvested well-deserved acclaim, even an Oscar under 21.
