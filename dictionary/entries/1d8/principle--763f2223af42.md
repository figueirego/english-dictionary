### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A fundamental assumption, fundamental law or guiding belief.

   **Example:** We need some sort of principles to reason from.

   **Source synonyms:** premise

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A rule used to choose among solutions to a problem.

   **Example:** The principle of least privilege holds that a process should only receive the permissions it needs.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Moral rule or aspect.

   **Usage:** sometimes pluralized

   **Example:** I don't doubt your principles.

   **Example:** You are clearly a person of principle.

   **Example:** It's the principle of the thing; I won't do business with someone I can't trust.

   **Source synonyms:** tenet; value

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A rule or law of nature, or the basic idea on how the laws of nature are applied.

   **Example:** Bernoulli's principle

   **Example:** The Pauli Exclusion Principle prevents two fermions from occupying the same state.

   **Example:** The principle of the internal combustion engine

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A fundamental essence, particularly one producing a given quality.

   **Example:** Many believe that life is the result of some vital principle.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A fundamental essence, particularly one producing a given quality.
   1. A chemical compound within plant or animal tissue that is characteristic of it and more or less peculiar to it, such that it defines the character of that tissue from a human viewpoint (as for example nicotine in tobacco).

      **Example:** the active principle

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**
