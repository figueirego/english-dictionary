### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To do as a requirement; indicates that the sentence subject is required as an imperative or directive to execute the sentence predicate, with failure to do so resulting in a failure or negative consequence.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** You must arrive in class on time.

   **Example:** This door handle must be rotated fully.

   **Example:** Everybody told me I must stop worrying, but I couldn't, because I knew that somehow there was still something wrong about the situation.

   **Example:** You really must come and see us soon, pal. - We'll see&#58; I can borrow my dad's car, but I must bring it back before midnight.

   **Example:** The physician said that I must / had to / would have to stop smoking.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To do with certainty; indicates that the speaker is certain that the subject will have executed the predicate.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** If it has rained all day, it must be very wet outside.

   **Example:** Where would you have put the keyring? You couldn’t have lost it again! - I must have, because it's nowhere to be found.

   **Example:** I said it must be pretty late and I really must go.

   **Example:** She's being taken to the police car now , so they must be going to arrest her.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to indicate that something is very likely, probable, or certain to be true.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** The children must be asleep by now.

   **Example:** There's the doorbell. It must be Dad.
