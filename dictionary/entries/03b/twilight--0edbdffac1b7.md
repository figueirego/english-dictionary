## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈtwaɪˌlaɪt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- twilights — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The soft light in the sky seen before the rising and (especially) after the setting of the sun, occasioned by the illumination of the earth’s atmosphere by the direct rays of the sun and their reflection on the earth.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I could just make out her face in the twilight.

   **Source synonyms:** half-light

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The time when said light is visible; the period between daylight and darkness.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** It was twilight by the time I got back home.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any faint light through which something is seen.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The time when the sun is less than 18° below the horizon.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An in-between or fading condition through which something is perceived.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; figuratively; uncountable

   **Example:** The twilight of one's life

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** blue hour — period between daylight and darkness; gloaming; crepuscule (archaic); crepusculum; dimmet (UK; dialectal); evenglome (literary; rare); glooming; tweenlight (dialectal; rare); twilight
