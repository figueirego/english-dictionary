### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To bend (any thin material, such as paper) over so that it comes in contact with itself.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Next, fold the paper over, so the right half is on top of the left half.

   **Example:** The newspaper was folded under his arm.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make the proper arrangement (in a thin material) by bending.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** If you fold the sheets, they'll fit more easily in the drawer.

   **Example:** The clothes were folded by the housekeeper.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To draw or coil (one’s arms, a snake’s body, etc.) around something so as to enclose or embrace it.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To stir (semisolid ingredients) gently, with an action as if folding over a solid.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Fold the egg whites into the batter.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** cooking; food; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To become folded; to form folds.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Cardboard doesn't fold very easily.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To fall over; to collapse or give way; to be crushed.

   **Usage:** informal; intransitive

   **Example:** The chair folded under his enormous weight.

   **Source synonyms:** buckle; cave; cave in; crumple

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To give way on a point or in an argument.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** He folded that point and didn't bring it up again.

   **Example:** He kept saying it was my fault, but when I brought up Sausalito, he folded.

   **Source synonyms:** buckle; cave; cave in; crumple

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To withdraw from betting.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; poker

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To abandon completing one's hand and play in a defensive manner to avoid dealing in.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** With no hearts in the river and no chance to hit his straight, he folded.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** board-games; games; mahjong

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To withdraw or quit in general.

   **Usage:** broadly; intransitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To fail, to collapse, to disband.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. Of a company, to cease to trade.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The company folded after six quarters of negative growth.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To double or lay together (one’s arms, hands, wings, etc.) so as to overlap with each other.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He folded his arms in defiance.

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. To plait or mat (hair) together.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. To enclose in a fold of material, to swathe, wrap up, cover, enwrap.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s16"></a>

**Source sense R1.S16**

<a id="r1-s17"></a>
