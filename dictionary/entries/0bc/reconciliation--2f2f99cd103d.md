### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The re-establishment of friendly relations; conciliation, rapprochement.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** He longed for reconciliation with his estranged father, but painful memories made him feel unready to do so.

   **Source synonyms:** reconcilement; reconciling (noun); saught (Britain; dialectal)

   **Source antonyms:** irreconciliation; unreconciliation

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The process of comparing and resolving apparent differences between sets of accounting records, or between accounting records and bank statements, receipts, etc.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** accounting; business; finance

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Religious senses.
   1. The end of estrangement between a human and God as a result of atonement.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Christianity

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Religious senses.
   1. The reconsecration of a desecrated church or other holy site.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Christianity

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Religious senses.
   1. Admission of a person to membership of the church, or readmission after the person has previously left the church.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Catholicism; Christianity; Roman-Catholicism

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Religious senses.
   1. Ellipsis of sacrament of reconciliation (“a sacrament (sacred ritual) also called confession, involving contrition by a person, confessing sins to a priest, penance performed by the person, and absolution granted by the priest”).

      **Usage:** abbreviation; alt-of; countable; ellipsis; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Catholicism; Christianity; Roman-Catholicism

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable
