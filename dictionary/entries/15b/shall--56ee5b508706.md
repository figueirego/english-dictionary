### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used before a verb to indicate the simple future tense in the first person singular or plural.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** I shall sing in the choir tomorrow.

   **Example:** I hope that we shall win the game.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Used similarly to indicate determination or obligation in the second and third persons singular or plural.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** Things haven't turned out as I intended they should, but I still intend that you shall take over the business.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used in questions with the first person singular or plural to suggest a possible future action.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle

   **Example:** What shall I do? Shall I give you a hand?

   **Example:** Shall we go out later?

   **Example:** I'll hold that for you, shall I?

   **Example:** Let us examine that, shall we? - Yeah let's.

   **Example:** Shall we see you before you set out?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To owe.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; defective; modal; no-past-participle; no-present-participle; obsolete

   **Example:** I thee shall a hundred golden coins; th'amount thou shalt the Queen is much higher.

   **Example:** He should þe Princess his Life, for she had deliver'd him from þe Poison deadly.
