### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make (something, such as a surface) bright, shiny, and smooth by, or (by extension) as if by, rubbing; to polish, to shine.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In pottery, a stone is sometimes used to burnish a pot before firing, giving it a smooth, shiny look.

   **Source synonyms:** buff; furbish; rub

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Of a stag&#58; to remove the velvet (“skin and fine fur”) from (its antlers) by rubbing them against something; to velvet.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make (someone or something) appear positive and highly respected.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become bright, glossy, and smooth; to brighten, to gleam, to shine forth.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. The making of something bright, shiny, and smooth by, or (by extension) as if by, rubbing; (countable) an instance of this; a burnishing, a polishing, a shining.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** With a good burnish, the old table should fetch a higher price.
