## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /smæʃ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- smashes — present; singular; third-person
- smashing — participle; present
- smashed — participle; past
- smashed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To break (something brittle) violently.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The demolition team smashed the buildings to rubble.

   **Example:** The flying rock smashed the window to pieces.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To be destroyed by being smashed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The crockery smashed as it hit the floor.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To hit extremely hard.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He smashed his head against the table.

   **Example:** Bonds smashed the ball 467 feet, the second longest home run in the history of the park.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To ruin completely and suddenly.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The news smashed any hopes of a reunion.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To defeat overwhelmingly; to gain a comprehensive success over.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The Indians smashed the Yankees 22-0.

   **Example:** I really smashed that English exam.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To deform through continuous pressure.

   **Usage:** US; transitive

   **Example:** I slowly smashed the modeling clay flat with the palm of my hand.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To have sex with.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive; vulgar

   **Example:** Would you smash her?

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. Synonym of go to smash (“to go to ruin; to fail disastrously”).

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive; slang

   **Source synonyms:** go to smash (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To pass counterfeit money.

   **Usage:** Ireland; UK; obsolete; slang
