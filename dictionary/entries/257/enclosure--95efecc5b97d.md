### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Something that is enclosed, i.e. inserted into a letter or similar package.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** There was an enclosure with the letter — a photo.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The act of enclosing, i.e. the insertion or inclusion of an item in a letter or package.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** The enclosure of a photo with your letter is appreciated.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An area, domain, or amount of something partially or entirely enclosed by barriers.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** He faced punishment for creating the fenced enclosure in a public park.

   **Example:** The glass enclosure holds the mercury vapor.

   **Example:** The winning horse was first into the unsaddling enclosure.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The act of separating and surrounding an area, domain, or amount of something with a barrier.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** The enclosure of public land is against the law.

   **Example:** The experiment requires the enclosure of mercury vapor in a glass tube.

   **Example:** At first, untrained horses resist enclosure.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The act of restricting access to ideas, works of art or technologies using patents or intellectual property laws.

   **Usage:** broadly; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The post-feudal process of subdivision of common lands for individual ownership.

   **Usage:** uncountable; British History

   **Example:** Strip-farming disappeared after enclosure.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The area of a convent, monastery, etc where access is restricted to community members.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** lifestyle; religion
