### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. (of a wheel, sled runner, or vehicle tracks) To slide along the ground, without the rotary motion that wheels or tracks would normally have.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To slide in an uncontrolled manner as in a car with the brakes applied too hard, the wheels sliding with limited spinning.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** They skidded around the corner and accelerated up the street.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To operate an aircraft in a banked sideslip with the nose yawed towards the low wing.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Don't use excessive rudder when turning, especially at low airspeed, as this causes your plane to skid through the turn, which can cause you to very rapidly enter a spin if the inner wing stalls.

   **Example:** Because of the jammed ailerons, the pilot had to use careful rudder inputs to skid his plane in order to turn it so he could get lined up with the runway.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To protect or support with a skid or skids.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To cause to move on skids.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To check or halt (wagon wheels, etc.) with a skid.

   **Usage:** transitive
