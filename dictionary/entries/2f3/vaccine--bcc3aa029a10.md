## Noun · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /vækˈsin/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˈvækˌsin/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- vaccines — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. A substance given to stimulate a body's production of antibodies and provide immunity against a disease without causing the disease itself in the treatment, prepared from the agent that causes the disease (or a derivative of it; or a related, also effective, but safer disease), or a synthetic substitute; also, a dose of such a substance.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The process of vaccination; immunization, inoculation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** My dog has had two vaccines this year.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. The material taken from cowpox pustules used for vaccination against smallpox.

   **Usage:** countable; historical; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. The disease cowpox, especially as a source of material for vaccination against smallpox.

   **Usage:** also; countable; obsolete; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** immunology; medicine; sciences

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. Something defensive or protective in nature, like a vaccine (sense 1.1).

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. A software program which protects computers against, or detects and neutralizes, computer viruses and other types of malware; an antivirus.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences
