### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used to express the future tense, sometimes with an implication of volition or determination when used in the first person. Compare shall.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle

   **Example:** Do not forget, will you?

   **Example:** Will you be doing the shopping this evening? If so, will you do mine too, please?

   **Example:** Won't you have another glass of wine? — No, I think I’ll go to bed.

   **Example:** Can somebody lend me a hand? — I will.

   **Example:** I'm going to quit smoking. I really will!

   **Example:** The President will arrive at 10.00 — Will she be wanting lunch?

   **Example:** If you will come this way, I’ll show you your bedroom.

   **Example:** I said I’d help, and help I will.

   **Example:** If your sis won’t be here on Thu, we’d better cancel the booking. — I will pray that she arrives on time.

   **Example:** That'll be £69.99, please. Last for ever this pair of jeans sure will.

   **Example:** This breakthrough will mean that we spend less on the electricity bill.

   **Example:** We'll finish ours sooner than you (do/will).

   **Example:** Dad, will you take me to the park? Will you, please? — Will you be quiet! I'm on the phone.

   **Example:** I'll hold that for you, shall I?

   **Example:** If my plans change, I will let you know.

   **Example:** If we don't go now, I will be late for my class.

   **Example:** They will've forgotten what I look like by now.

   **Example:** The baby will surely have green eyes, because both parents have.

   **Example:** If you bring your tents, we will all camp on the coast.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To be able to, to have the capacity to.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle

   **Example:** Unfortunately, only one of these gloves will actually fit over my hand.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Expressing a present tense or perfect tense with some conditional or subjective weakening&#58; "will turn out to", "must by inference".

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle

   **Example:** He will be home by now. He always gets home before 6 o'clock.

   **Example:** I can't find my umbrella. I will've left it at home this morning.

   **Example:** I’ll kill anybody who touches my car.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To habitually do (a given action).

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle

   **Example:** Boys will be boys.

   **Example:** People will talk.

   **Example:** I will fall in love with the wrong women time and again.

   **Example:** The shapes of clouds will often remind us of animals.

   **Example:** Most nights I'll read a little before going to sleep.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To choose or agree to (do something); used to express intention but without any temporal connotations, often in questions and negation.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; no-past-participle

   **Example:** Will you marry me?

   **Example:** What will you drink?

   **Example:** I’ve told him three times, but he won’t take his medicine.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To wish, desire (something).

   **Usage:** literary; no-past-participle; transitive; uncommon

   **Example:** Do what you will.

   **Example:** God willed it.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To wish or desire (that something happen); to intend (that).

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive; no-past-participle

   **Example:** Consider, if you will, the possibility that the sherry glasses were misplaced accidentally.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Implying will go.

   **Usage:** archaic; no-past-participle

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. One's independent faculty of choice; the ability to be able to exercise one's choice or intention.

   **Example:** Of course, man's will is often regulated by his reason.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The act of choosing to do something; a person’s conscious intent or volition.

   **Example:** Most creatures have a will to live.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. One's intention or decision; someone's orders or commands.

   **Example:** Eventually I submitted to my parents' will.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. Firmness of purpose, fixity of intent

   **Source synonyms:** determination; firmness; resoluteness; resolve

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. A formal declaration of one's intent concerning the disposal of one's property and holdings after death; the legal document stating such wishes.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

   **Source synonyms:** testament; last will; last will and testament

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. That which is desired; one's wish.

   **Usage:** archaic

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. Desire, longing. (Now generally merged with later senses.)

   **Usage:** archaic

   **Example:** He felt a great will to make a pilgrimage to the Holy Land.

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To instruct (that something be done) in one's will.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To bequeath (something) to someone in one's will (legal document).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He willed his stamp collection to the local museum.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To exert one's force of will (intention) in order to compel, or attempt to compel, something to happen or someone to do something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** All the fans were willing their team to win the game.

   **Example:** Her parents were watching her run, willing her on.
