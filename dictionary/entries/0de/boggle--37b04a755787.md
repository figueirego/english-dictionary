### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. (literally or figuratively) to stop or hesitate as if suddenly seeing a bogle.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** The dogs went on, but the horse boggled at the sudden appearance of the strange beast.

   **Example:** The horror of the deed and its consequences boggle the imagination.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To be bewildered, dumbfounded, or confused.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He boggled at the surprising news.

   **Example:** The mind boggles.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To confuse or mystify; overwhelm.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The vastness of space really boggles the mind.

   **Example:** The oddities of quantum mechanics can boggle the minds of students and experienced physicists alike.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To embarrass with difficulties; to palter or equivocate; to bungle or botch

   **Usage:** US; dialectal

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To dissemble; to play fast and loose (with someone or something).

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To wiggle the eyes as a result of bruxing.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a rat
