### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To spontaneously roll suddenly and rapidly to one side or the other upon entering a stall (due to one wing stalling slightly before the other does, combined with the reduced effectiveness of ailerons for roll control at high angles of attack).

   **Example:** In addition to the large decrease in lift caused by the stall itself, further performance degradation can be expected as the airplane rolls off, as the spoilers on the high wing deploy in an attempt to fight the plane's sudden rolling motion.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an aircraft

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To come off (something) with a rolling motion.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** My pen rolled off the table.

   **Example:** I can't believe that that skateboarder just rolled right off that ledge like the six-foot drop was nothing!

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To take (something) off (i.e., remove it) via a rolling motion.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The truck driver rolled off a fresh dumpster and left it sitting at the curb to be filled tomorrow.

   **Source antonyms:** roll on

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To come from an assembly line.

   **Example:** rolled off the line

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To show reduced response at the upper or lower limits of a frequency range.

   **Example:** rolled-off highs

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; electronics; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics
