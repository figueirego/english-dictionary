### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An act of bluffing; a false expression of the strength of one’s position in order to intimidate or deceive; braggadocio.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** That is only bluff, or That is only a bluff.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An attempt to represent oneself as holding a stronger hand than one actually does.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** John’s bet was a bluff&#58; he bet without even so much as a pair.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; poker

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The card game poker.

   **Usage:** US; dated; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. One who bluffs; a bluffer.

   **Usage:** countable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Pretense, excuse.

   **Usage:** countable; dated; slang; uncountable

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /blʌf/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /blɐf/ — source labels: Australia; New-Zealand
- IPA: /blʊf/ — source labels: Northern-England

### Forms

- bluffs — present; singular; third-person
- bluffing — participle; present
- bluffed — participle; past
- bluffed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make a bluff; to give the impression that one’s hand is stronger than it is.

   **Example:** John bluffed by betting without even a pair.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; poker

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To frighten, deter, or deceive with a false show of strength or confidence; to give a false impression of strength or temerity in order to intimidate or gain some advantage.

   **Usage:** by analogy

   **Example:** The government claims it will call an election if this bill does not pass. Is it truly ready to do so, or is it bluffing?

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To perform or achieve by bluffing.

   **Example:** We bluffed our way past the guards.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To give false information intentionally, to lie (to someone), to deceive; to put on an act.

   **Usage:** Manglish; Singlish
