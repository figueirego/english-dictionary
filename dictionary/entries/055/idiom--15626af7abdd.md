### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A manner of speaking, a mode of expression peculiar to a language, language family, or group of people.

   **Example:** In English, idiom requires the indefinite article in a phrase such as "she's an engineer", whereas in Spanish, idiom forbids it.

   **Example:** Some of the usage prescriptions improved clarity and were kept; others that yielded discordant violations of idiom were eventually revised.

   **Source synonyms:** idiomaticness; idiomaticity

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A manner of speaking, a mode of expression peculiar to a language, language family, or group of people.
   1. A programming construct or phraseology that is characteristic of the language.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; programming; sciences

      **Source synonyms:** idiomaticness; idiomaticity

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A language or language variety; specifically, a restricted dialect used in a given historical period, context, etc.

   **Example:** In the idiom of the day, they were sutlers, although today they'd probably be called vendors.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A phrasal expression that is unreliably understood literally, requiring pre-established cultural or domain-specific context to infer its intended meaning.

   **Example:** She often spoke in idioms, pining for salad days and complaining about pots calling the kettle black.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An artistic style (for example, in art, architecture, or music); an instance of such a style.

   **Example:** the idiom of the expressionists
