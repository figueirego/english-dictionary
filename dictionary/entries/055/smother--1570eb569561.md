### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To suffocate; stifle; obstruct, more or less completely, the respiration of something or someone.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He smothered her by pressing his hand over her mouth.

   **Source synonyms:** suffocate; choke; stifle

   **Source antonyms:** ventilate

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To extinguish or deaden, as fire, by covering, overlaying, or otherwise excluding the air.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to smother a fire with ashes

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To reduce to a low degree of vigor or activity; suppress or do away with; extinguish

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The committee's report was smothered.

   **Source synonyms:** stifle; cover up; conceal; hide

   **Source antonyms:** release

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cook in a close dish.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** beefsteak smothered with onions

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** cooking; food; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To daub or smear.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To be suffocated.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** She is smothered by the rope.

   **Source synonyms:** choke; stifle

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To breathe with great difficulty by reason of smoke, dust, close covering or wrapping, or the like.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. to burn very slowly for want of air; smolder.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a fire

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. to perish, grow feeble, or decline, by suppression or concealment; be stifled; be suppressed or concealed.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To get in the way of a kick of the ball.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; games; hobbies; lifestyle; soccer; sports

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To get in the way of a kick of the ball, preventing it going very far. When a player is kicking the ball, an opponent who is close enough will reach out with his hands and arms to get over the top of it, so the ball hits his hands after leaving the kicker's boot, dribbling away.

   **Usage:** Australian rules football
