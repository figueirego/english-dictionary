**Source sense R1.S1**

1. (Of food) intended to be eaten off the premises from which it was bought.

   **Usage:** Commonwealth; Hong-Kong; Ireland; Philippines; UK; not-comparable; sometimes

## Noun · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈteɪkəweɪ/ — source labels: Canada; General-American; Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈtæɪkəwæɪ/ — source labels: Australia; New-Zealand

### Forms

- takeaways — plural
- take-away — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. A restaurant that sells food to be eaten elsewhere.

   **Usage:** Australia; New-Zealand; South-Africa; UK

   **Example:** If you're hungry, there's a takeaway just around the corner.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A meal which has been purchased and has been carefully packaged as to be taken and consumed elsewhere.

   **Usage:** Australia; New-Zealand; UK

   **Example:** I fancy an Indian takeaway tonight.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. The preliminary part of a golfer′s swing when the club is brought back away from the ball.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** golf; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A concession made by a labor union in the course of negotiations.

   **Usage:** US

5. An idea from a talk, presentation, etc., that the listener or reader should remember and consider.
