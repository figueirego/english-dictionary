## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /eɪd͡ʒ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- ages — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The amount of time that some being has been alive, or that some thing has been in existence, as measured from its birth or origin until the present or until some other given reference point. (Often measured in number of years; alternatively in months, days, hours, etc.; see also the usage notes)

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** "What is the age of your oldest child?" — "He's ten." (ten years old)

   **Example:** What were their ages at the time of their marriage?

   **Example:** We can determine the age of fossils using radiometric dating.

   **Example:** What is the present age of the earth?

   **Example:** I have a daughter your age, and I tell her when I was your age I was already working.

   **Example:** You should play with kids the same age as you.

   **Example:** At your age, your mom and I'd already found a job.

   **Example:** We both have kids under age four.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The state of being old; the latter part of life.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Wisdom doesn't necessarily come with age, sometimes age just shows up all by itself.

   **Source synonyms:** old age; dotage; senility; seniority; age; chair days (obsolete); codgerhood; eld (archaic; poetic); elderliness; fogeydom; geezerdom (US; slang); golden years (euphemistic; idiomatic); old age; oldhood; senescence; senectitude; superannuation; twilight years (idiomatic); sunset years (idiomatic); vetustity (rare)

   **Source antonyms:** youth

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any particular stage of life.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** the age of infancy

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The time of life at which some particular power or capacity is understood to become vested.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** the age of consent; the age of discretion

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Maturity; especially, the time of life at which one attains full personal rights and capacities.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** to come of age; she is now of age

   **Source synonyms:** majority; adulthood

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A particular period of time in history, as distinguished from others.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** the golden age of cinema; the first age of colonialism; a bygone age

   **Source synonyms:** epoch; time; era; age; cycle; day (figuratively); epoch; era; generation; Great Year; interval; period; Platonic year; time

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A particular period of time in history, as distinguished from others.
   1. The time or era in history when someone or something was alive or flourished.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** the age of Pericles; the age of the dinosaurs

      **Source synonyms:** epoch; time; era; age; cycle; day (figuratively); epoch; era; generation; Great Year; interval; period; Platonic year; time

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

   1. A great period in the history of the Earth.

      **Usage:** countable

      **Example:** The Bronze Age was followed by the Iron Age.

**Source sense R1.S9**

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /eɪd͡ʒ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- ages — present; singular; third-person
- aging — participle; present
- ageing — participle; present
- aged — participle; past
- aged — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To grow aged; to become old or older; to show marks of age.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He grew fat as he aged.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To grow aged; to become old or older; to show marks of age.
   1. To suffer the passage of time so as to later be viewed or turn out in a certain way.

      **Usage:** intransitive; usually; prediction

      **Example:** His prediction that we didn't stand a chance hasn't aged well, now that we've won the cup.

      **Example:** The sitcom was made in the 1970s and its casual sexism has not aged well.

      **Source context (verbatim):** of a statement

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To cause to grow old; to impart the characteristics of age to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Grief ages us.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To cause to grow old; to impart the characteristics of age to.
   1. To allow to mature.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** We age the whiskey for five years.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To cause to grow old; to impart the characteristics of age to.
   1. To treat or tamper with in order to give a false appearance of age.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** This clock is modern, but it has been deliberately aged in an attempt to make it seem antique.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To determine the age of (the length of time that something has been alive or in existence).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** There are several ways to age trees.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To indicate or reveal that (a person) has been alive for a certain period of time, especially a long one.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I clearly remember hearing the news of Kennedy's assassination. That ages me.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To allow (something) to persist by postponing an action that would extinguish it, as a debt.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** Money's a little tight right now. Let's age our bills for a week or so.

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To categorize by age.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** One his first assignments was to age the accounts receivable.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** accounting; business; finance
