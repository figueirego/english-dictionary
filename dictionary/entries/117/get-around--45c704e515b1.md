## Verb

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To move to the other side of (something, such as an obstruction) by deviating from a direct course or following a curved path.

   **Example:** The tide was too high, and we couldn't get around the rocks.

   **Example:** There's no trail going through. We can't get around to the lake.

   **Example:** We'll get a good view of the mountains when we get around the bend.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To avoid or bypass an obstacle.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** Tax consultants look for ways to get around the law.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To circumvent the obligation and performance of a chore; to get out of.

   **Example:** How did you get around having to write the executive report?

   **Example:** My brother always gets around cleaning his room himself.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To transport oneself from place to place.

   **Example:** How's he gonna get around without a car?

   **Example:** Granny uses a wheelchair to get around.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To visit numerous different places.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To be sexually promiscuous.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Example:** Wow, she really gets around.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see get, around.
