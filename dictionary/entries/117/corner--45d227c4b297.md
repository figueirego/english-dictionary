## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- cornering — participle; present
- cornered — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To drive (someone or something) into a corner or other confined space.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The cat had cornered a cricket between the sofa and the television stand.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To trap in a position of great difficulty or hopeless embarrassment.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The reporter cornered the politician by pointing out the hypocrisy of his position on mandatory sentencing, in light of the politician's own actions in court.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To put (someone) in an awkward situation.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To get sufficient command of (a stock, commodity, etc.), so as to be able to manipulate its price.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The buyers attempted to corner the shares of the railroad stock, so as to facilitate their buyout.

   **Example:** It's extremely hard to corner the petroleum market because there are so many players.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To turn a corner or drive around a curve.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** As the stock car driver cornered the last turn, he lost control and spun out.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** automotive; transport; vehicles

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To handle while moving around a corner in a road or otherwise turning.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** That BMW corners well, but the suspension is too stiff.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** automotive; transport; vehicles

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To supply with corners.

   **Usage:** transitive
