### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Two or more people including or consisting of the speaker(s)/writer(s).
   1. Excluding the person(s) being addressed. (This is the exclusive we.)

      **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

      **Example:** We are here to arrest you.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Two or more people including or consisting of the speaker(s)/writer(s).
   1. Including the person(s) being addressed. (This is the inclusive we.)

      **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

      **Example:** We need to have a talk, you and me.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The institution which the speaker/writer is acting for. (This is the editorial we, used by writers and others when speaking with the authority of their publication or organisation.)

   **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Example:** We will in due course state our reasons for publishing the name of the accused.

   **Example:** As the government, we have a duty to ensure public safety.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Any other entity that the speaker is a part of or identifies with, such as place of employment or education, nation, region, language, etc.

   **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Example:** I went to watch our school football match against Puddletown High. We lost 2-0.

   **Example:** In English, we do not inflect adjectives for gender.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. People in general.

   **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Example:** We live and learn.

   **Example:** We still have much to learn about quantum mechanics.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The sovereign alone in his or her capacity as monarch. (This is the royal we. The reflexive case of this sense of we is ourself.)

   **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun; royal

   **Example:** We are Queen of all these Isles.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Everyone being addressed.

   **Usage:** colloquial; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Example:** Good evening everyone. How are we all tonight?

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An individual being addressed; used especially to a person in the speaker's care, or to whom advice or instruction is being given. (Sometimes called the nurse's we or the doctor's we.)

   **Usage:** colloquial; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun; may sometimes seem patronising

   **Example:** Hello Mrs Miggins. How are we feeling this morning?

   **Example:** You've been spending all your money boozing when you have a wife and family to support. I think we need to get our priorities straight.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Used to refer to a third person, especially someone in the speaker's care.

   **Usage:** colloquial; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun; uncommon

   **Example:** "I've just been to see Mrs Miggins." — "And how is she?" — "I'm afraid we aren't too good today."

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Used to connect to or include readers or listeners.
   1. Used to imply connection between the speaker's experiences or activities and a group of listeners. (Compare the plural of modesty.)

      **Usage:** colloquial; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

      **Example:** Hey guys, in this video I'll show you how I make my upside-down sponge cake. First we take 200 g of butter and we whisk in 200 g of sugar. Next, we ...

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. Used to connect to or include readers or listeners.
   1. Used in explanatory or procedural writing, such as mathematical explanations, to imply inclusion of the reader in the undertaking.

      **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

      **Example:** To solve the equation, we first collect all the terms in x on one side.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. Used when talking to oneself to refer to oneself.

   **Usage:** colloquial; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Example:** Now then ... let me see ... I hope I'm doing this right ... if we just connect these two wires together ...

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. Us.

   **Usage:** West-Country; archaic; first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. The side which is keeping score.

   **Usage:** first-person; nominative; personal; plural; pronoun

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** bridge; games

   **Source antonyms:** they
