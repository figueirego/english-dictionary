### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To touch repeatedly or stroke delicately in a manner which typically causes laughter, pleasure and twitching.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He tickled Nancy's tummy, and she started to giggle.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To feel as if the body part in question is being tickled.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** My nose tickles, and I'm going to sneeze!

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a body part

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To appeal to someone's taste, curiosity etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To cause delight or amusement in.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He was tickled to receive such a wonderful gift.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To feel titillation.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To catch fish in the hand (usually in rivers or smaller streams) by manually stimulating the fins.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To be excited or heartened.
