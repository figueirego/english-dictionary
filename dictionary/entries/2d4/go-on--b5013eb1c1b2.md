## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Forms

- goes on — present; singular; third-person
- going on — participle; present
- went on — past
- gone on — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To continue in extent.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The meeting seemed to go on forever.

   **Example:** This road goes on for another twenty miles.

   **Source synonyms:** endure; abide (archaic); aby (obsolete); astand (obsolete); endure; go on; lie; prevail; hold on; last; live; persist; remain; stick; stick it out; stick out

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To continue an action.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I think I've said enough now; I'm not sure I should go on.

   **Example:** He went on walking even when the policeman told him to stop.

   **Source synonyms:** keep on; carry on; advance; forthgo; proceed; resume

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To proceed (to do something).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He started the competition badly but went on to win the gold medal.

   **Example:** "Do you want to hear a joke?" — "Oh, all right, go on then."

   **Source synonyms:** carry on; continue; carry on (idiomatic); continue; go on; keep on (idiomatic); proceed; roll on; hunker down (idiomatic)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To talk frequently or at great length (about a subject).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Will you stop going on about your stupid holiday?

   **Example:** Sam goes on and on about Pokémon.

   **Example:** Don't ask Sam about Pokémon, because he'll go on and on.

   **Source synonyms:** blather; prattle; drone on; rabbit

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To happen (occur).

   **Usage:** intransitive; progressive aspect

   **Example:** What's going on downstairs? What's all that noise down there?

   **Example:** I really don't want to know what goes on between you and your boyfriend behind closed doors.

   **Source synonyms:** come to pass; take place; bechance; befall; behap; belimp (obsolete); betide (dated; literary); betime (archaic); beworth; chance (archaic); come about; come to pass; crop up; evene (obsolete); eventuate; go on; hap (literary); happen; hold; occur; pass; take place; tide (obsolete); transpire

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To move or proceed so as to become "on" in any of various senses.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Bertha's about to finish her song. Who's going on next?

   **Example:** Smith went on for Jones at left back after Jones got injured.

   **Example:** It was pitch black and then suddenly all the lights went on.
