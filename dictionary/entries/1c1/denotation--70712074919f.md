## Noun

### Pronunciation

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of denoting, or something (such as a symbol) that denotes

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The primary, surface, literal, or explicit meaning of a signifier such as a word, phrase, or symbol; that which a word denotes, as contrasted with its connotation; the aggregate or set of objects of which a word may be predicated.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The denotations of the two expressions "the morning star" and "the evening star" are the same (i.e. both expressions denote the planet Venus), but their connotations are different.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; logic; mathematics; philosophy; sciences; semiotics

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The intension and extension of a word

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; logic; mathematics; philosophy; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Something signified or referred to; a particular meaning of a symbol

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences; semantics

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Any mathematical object which describes the meanings of expressions from the languages, formalized in the theory of denotational semantics

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computer; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; science; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A first level of analysis&#58; what the audience can visually see on a page. Denotation often refers to something literal, and avoids being a metaphor.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; media studies
