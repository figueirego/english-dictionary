## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /klæp/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- claps — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of striking the palms of the hands, or any two surfaces, together.

   **Example:** He summoned the waiter with a clap.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The explosive sound of thunder.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any loud, sudden, explosive sound made by striking hard surfaces together, or resembling such a sound.

   **Example:** Off in the distance, he heard the clap of thunder.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A slap with the hand, usually in a jovial manner.

   **Example:** His father's affection never went further than a handshake or a clap on the shoulder.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A single, sudden act or motion; a stroke; a blow.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The nether part of the beak of a hawk.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** falconry; hobbies; hunting; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A dropping of cow dung (presumably from the sound made as it hits the ground)

   **Usage:** Yorkshire

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- clapped — past

- clapt — archaic; past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To strike the palms of the hands together, creating a sharp sound.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The children began to clap in time with the music.

   **Source synonyms:** applaud; beclap

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To applaud.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The audience loudly clapped the actress, who responded with a deep curtsey.

   **Example:** It isn’t the singers they are clapping; it's the composer.

   **Source synonyms:** acclaim; applaud; hurrah; acclaim; acclamate (rare); applaud; hail; barrack (Australia; New-Zealand); beclap; cheer; cheer on; clap; encheer (obsolete); hurrah; hurray; huzzah

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To slap with the hand in a jovial manner.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He would often clap his teammates on the back for encouragement.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To bring two surfaces together forcefully, creating a sharp sound.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** He clapped the empty glass down on the table.

   **Example:** She clapped the book shut.

   **Example:** He clapped across the floor in his boots.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To slam (a door or window); formerly often construed with to.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To create or assemble (something) hastily (usually followed by up or together).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We should clap together a shelter before nightfall.

   **Example:** The rival factions clapped up a truce.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To set or put, usually in haste.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The sheriff clapped him in jail.

   **Example:** She was the prettiest thing I'd ever clapped eyes on.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To shoot (somebody) with a gun.

   **Usage:** slang; African-American Vernacular

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To defeat.

   **Usage:** broadly; slang; transitive

   **Example:** He started a fight but then got clapped immediately.

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**

10. To have sex, fornicate, copulate.

   **Usage:** slang
