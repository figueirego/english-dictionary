## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈpæn.təˌmʌɪm/ — source labels: UK
- IPA: /ˈpæn.təˌmaɪm/ — source labels: Canada; US

### Forms

- pantomimes — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A Classical comic actor, especially one who works mainly through gesture and mime.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The drama in ancient Greece and Rome featuring such performers; or (later) any of various kinds of performance modelled on such work.

   **Usage:** Ancient-Rome; countable; uncountable; Ancient Greece

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A traditional theatrical entertainment, originally based on the commedia dell'arte, but later aimed mostly at children and involving physical comedy, topical jokes, call and response, and fairy-tale plots.

   **Usage:** UK; countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** panto

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The act of gesturing without speaking; a dumb-show, a mime.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈpæn.təˌmʌɪm/ — source labels: UK
- IPA: /ˈpæn.təˌmaɪm/ — source labels: Canada; US

### Forms

- pantomimes — present; singular; third-person
- pantomiming — participle; present
- pantomimed — participle; past
- pantomimed — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make (a gesture) without speaking.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I pantomimed steering a car; he understood, and tossed the keys to me.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To entertain others by silent gestures or actions.

   **Usage:** transitive
