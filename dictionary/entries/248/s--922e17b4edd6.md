**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used to form regular plurals of nouns.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** one computer → five computers; Kennedy → Kennedys; hanger-on → hangers-on; lapful → lapfuls; cul-de-sac → cul-de-sacs; standby → standbys; push-up → push-ups; gin and tonic → gin and tonics; pair → pairs; beef → beefs

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** shorts

   **Example:** sunglasses

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to form many pluralia tantum (nouns that are almost or entirely without singular forms).
   1. Forms names of sciences and fields of studies from adjectives (See Usage notes)

      **Usage:** morpheme

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. When appended to a number ending in at least one 0, expresses a range of numbers which share the digits before some or (usually) all of the 0s; frequently used for decades, centuries and temperatures.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** We often look back on the 1960s \[the years from 1960 to 1969\] with rose-tinted glasses.

   **Example:** The Industrial Revolution went into full-swing during the 1800s \[the years from 1800 to 1899 – but see usage notes\].

   **Example:** The temperature today will be in the low 30s.

   **Example:** He placed in the 200s in the end.
