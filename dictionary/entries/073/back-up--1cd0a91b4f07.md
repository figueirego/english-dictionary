### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To move backwards, especially for a vehicle to do so.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** That beeping sound indicates that the truck is backing up.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To move a vehicle backwards.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Back up the car a little, you're blocking the driveway.

   **Source synonyms:** reverse

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To undo one's actions.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** I couldn't see how to finish the project, so I backed up and tried it another way.

   **Source synonyms:** back out

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To reconsider one's thoughts.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** This isn't working. Let's back up and think about it.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To copy (data) so that it can be restored if the main copy is lost.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Back up your documents folder before applying the update.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To provide support or the promise of support to.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** You should be careful. This guy is backed up by the local gang.

   **Example:** When he said I wasn't there, I told him I was, and my buddy backed me up.

   **Source synonyms:** back; support

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. For the non-striker to take a few steps down the pitch, in preparation to taking a run, just as the bowler bowls the ball.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. For a fielder to position himself behind the wicket (relative to a team-mate who is throwing the ball at the wicket) so as to stop the ball, and prevent overthrows.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To halt the flow or movement of something.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** When I flushed the toilet, the plumbing backed up and burst.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a blockage

   **Source synonyms:** plug up

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To fill up because of a backlog.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** fall behind

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To run a printing press in reverse.

   **Usage:** obsolete

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** media; printing; publishing

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. If a property backs up to another property, that means it abuts or shares a border with another property.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** We bought this house specifically because it backs up to our son's school.

   **Source synonyms:** abut; border
