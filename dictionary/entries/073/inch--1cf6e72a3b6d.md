## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- inch — UK; colloquial; plural
- in — abbreviation

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An English unit of length equal to 1/12 of a foot or 2.54 cm.

   **Example:** One rule of thumb is that the width of an adult thumb can approximate a length of one inch.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any very short distance.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** Don't move an inch!

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any of various similar units of length in other traditional systems of measurement.

   **Example:** At the time of Augustus, the Roman inch was standardized at a length about 3% shorter than the modern English one.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A depth of one inch on the ground, used as a measurement of rainfall.

   **Example:** Expect around 2 inches of rain on Saturday afternoon.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** climatology; meteorology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A depth of one inch in a glass, used as a rough measurement of alcoholic beverages.

   **Example:** My partner only drinks about a 1 inch pour with dinner, leaving the rest of the bottle for me.
