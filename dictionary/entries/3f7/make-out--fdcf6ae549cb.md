### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To draw up (a document etc.), to designate (a cheque).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Cheques may be made out to the Foo Bar Company.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To send out.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To discern; to manage to see, hear etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In the distance, I could just make out a shadowy figure.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To manage, get along; to do (well, badly etc.).

   **Usage:** US; intransitive; regional

   **Example:** Oh, you were on a TV game show? How did you make out?

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To represent; to make (something) appear to be true.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** His version of the story makes me out (to be) the bad guy.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To get along with (someone).

   **Usage:** US; intransitive; slang

   **Example:** Him and I always make out well.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To make an (often temporary) home.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive; slang

   **Example:** I'll make out in this room for the night.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To embrace and kiss passionately.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive; slang

   **Example:** We found a secluded spot where we could make out in private.

   **Source synonyms:** neck; buss; goozer; kiss; lallygag (US; archaic); lips (Multicultural-London-English); lock lips; osculate; pash (Australia); play tonsil hockey; smooch; snog (UK); suck face; swap spit

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To engage in heavy petting or sexual intercourse.

   **Usage:** US; intransitive; nonstandard; rare; slang
