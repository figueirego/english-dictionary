### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To approach or drive up to a place and come to a stop; to park by driving frontways into a parking spot.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** A car just pulled in our driveway.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a vehicle or driver

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To approach a station; to arrive at a station.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** Quick! The train's pulling in.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a train or bus

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To arrest someone; to take someone to a police station because they may have done something.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** She was pulled in for questioning.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To earn \[money\].

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** He pulls in a lot of money.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To tighten a sail by pulling on a rope.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** pull in the main sheet

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see pull, in&#58; To pull something, so that it comes inside.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** After falling out of the boat, the crew pulled him in.
