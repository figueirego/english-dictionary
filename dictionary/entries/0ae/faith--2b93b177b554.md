### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A trust or confidence in the intentions or abilities of a person, object, or ideal from prior empirical evidence.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The faithfulness of Old Faithful gives us faith in it.

   **Example:** I have faith in the goodness of my fellow man.

   **Example:** Have faith in him, buddy.

   **Example:** You need to have faith in yourself, that you can overcome your shortcomings and become a good person.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A conviction about abstractions, ideas, or beliefs, without empirical evidence, experience, or observation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** I have faith that my prayers will be answered.

   **Example:** I have faith in the healing power of crystals.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A religious or spiritual belief system.

   **Usage:** countable; metonymically; uncountable

   **Example:** The Christian faith.

   **Example:** We seek justice for the Indo-European Folk Faith; what's wrong in our literature for that?

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An obligation of loyalty or fidelity and the observance of such an obligation.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** He acted in good faith to restore broken diplomatic ties after defeating the incumbent.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Credibility or truth.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable
