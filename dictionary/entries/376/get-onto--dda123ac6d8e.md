## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To contact a person or organisation about a particular matter.

   **Example:** You should get onto the manufacturers and complain.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To connect, especially to the Internet or a network.

   **Example:** With my new computer, I can get onto the Internet faster.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To scold someone; to pressure someone; to criticize someone.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** My father got onto me for taking the car without asking.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To introduce someone to something.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; informal

   **Example:** My mum got me onto this new diet and I've lost 5 kilos in the past two weeks.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To take a look at someone or something.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** Hey, get onto the dog and cat playing together.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To believe or suspect something; to understand or come to understand something; to realize something.

   **Usage:** informal; slang; transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see get, onto.

   **Example:** The child will get onto the merry-go-round.

   **Example:** That cat's on the house again. How does it get onto the roof?
