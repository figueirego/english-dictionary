## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈflætɚ/ — source labels: General-American
- IPA: \[ˈflæɾɚ\] — source labels: General-American
- IPA: /ˈflætə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- flatters — present; singular; third-person
- flattering — participle; present
- flattered — participle; past
- flattered — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To compliment someone, often (but not necessarily) insincerely and sometimes to win favour.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source synonyms:** butter up; lay it on thick; puff

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To enhance or gratify someone's vanity by praising them.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source synonyms:** stroke someone's ego

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To portray someone to advantage.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Her portrait flatters her.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To encourage or cheer someone with (usually false) hope.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To cheer or please (with the idea that); congratulate oneself, especially when the perception is false.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; reflexive; usually

   **Example:** Before the results came out, I flattered myself that I had done well on the test. It turned out I was the worst in the class!

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To cheer or please (with the idea that); congratulate oneself, especially when the perception is false.
   1. In phrasal verb "to flatter with"&#58; to encourage, inspire with hope.

      **Usage:** ambitransitive; intransitive; obsolete; reflexive; usually

### Related words
