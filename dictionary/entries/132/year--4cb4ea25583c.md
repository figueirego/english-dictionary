### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.

   **Example:** we moved to this town a year ago; I quit smoking exactly one year ago

   **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.
   1. The time taken for the Earth to return to the same position along the ecliptic, completing a full cycle of seasons; a tropical year or solar year.

      **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.
   1. The time taken for the Earth to orbit the Sun with respect to the fixed stars; a sidereal year.

      **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.
   1. The length of twelve lunations; the time taken for any moon phase to happen twelve times; a lunar year.

      **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.
   1. The length of a year as marked by a calendar, 365 or 366 days in the Gregorian calendar; a calendar year.

      **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A period of time akin to the time taken for the Earth to undergo a full cycle of seasons.
   1. The mean length of a calendar year in the Julian calendar, that is, 365.25 solar days; a Julian year.

      **Source synonyms:** annum; mul&#58;a

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. An orbital period&#58; the period of one revolution in any particular orbit&#58; The time it takes for any astronomical object (such as a planet, dwarf planet, small Solar System body, or comet) in direct orbit around a star (such as the Sun) to make one revolution around the star.

   **Usage:** broadly

   **Example:** Mars goes around the sun once in a Martian year, or 1.88 Earth years.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A period between set dates that mark a year, such as from January 1 to December 31 by the Gregorian calendar, from Tishri 1 to Elul 29 by the Jewish calendar, and from Muharram 1 to Dhu al-Hijjah 29 or 30 by the Islamic calendar.

   **Example:** A normal year has 365 full days, but there are 366 days in a leap year.

   **Example:** I was born in the year 1950.

   **Example:** This Chinese year is the year of the Ox.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A scheduled part of a calendar year spent in a specific activity.

   **Example:** During this school year I have to get up at 6&#58;30 to catch the bus.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A scheduled part of a calendar year spent in a specific activity.
   1. A level or grade in school or college.

      **Example:** Every second-year student must select an area of specialization.

      **Example:** The exams in year 12 at high school are the most difficult.

      **Example:** I'm the oldest student in my year because I've repeated twice.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. The proportion of a creature's lifespan equivalent to one year of an average human lifespan (see also dog year).

   **Example:** Geneticists have created baker's yeast that can live to 800 in yeast years.
