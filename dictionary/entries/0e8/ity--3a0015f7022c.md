### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used to form an uncountable noun from an adjective; especially, to form the noun referring to the state, property, or quality of conforming to the adjective's description.

   **Usage:** countable; morpheme; uncountable

   **Example:** absurd + -ity → absurdity (“the quality of being absurd or inconsistent with obvious truth, reason, or sound judgment”)

   **Example:** anonym(ous) + -ity → anonymity (“the quality or state of being anonymous”)

   **Example:** modern + -ity → modernity (“the quality of being modern or contemporary”)

   **Example:** precar(ious) + -ity → precarity (“a condition of existence without predictability or security, affecting material or psychological welfare”)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Used to form a countable noun from an adjective, referring to someone or something that conforms to the adjective's description.

   **Usage:** countable; morpheme; uncountable

   **Example:** absurd + -ity → \[an\] absurdity (“that which is absurd; an absurd action; a logical contradiction”)

   **Example:** anonym(ous) + -ity → \[an\] anonymity (“that which is anonymous”)

   **Example:** insipid + -ity → \[an\] insipidity (“something that is insipid; an insipid utterance, sight, object, etc.”)

   **Example:** odd + -ity → \[an\] oddity (“an odd or strange thing or opinion; a strange person; an oddball”)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to form other nouns, especially abstract nouns.

   **Usage:** countable; morpheme; uncountable
