## Verb · Record 1

### Forms

- turns up — present; singular; third-person
- turning up — participle; present
- turned up — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To show up; to appear suddenly or unexpectedly.

   **Usage:** copulative; intransitive

   **Example:** Is this your t-shirt that turned up in my drawer?

   **Example:** I don't like people turning up without an appointment.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause to appear; to find by searching, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I spent hours in the archives, but couldn't turn up anything on the alleged criminal.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To increase the amount of something by means of a control, such as the volume, heat, or light.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Turn up the radio and sing along.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To reposition by rotating, flipping, etc., upwards.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He turned up his collar against the cold.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To belay or make fast (a line on a cleat or pin).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Turn up the main halyard.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To party hard, especially when involving alcohol or drugs.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang

   **Example:** We're going to turn up at the concert tonight.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To acquit.

   **Usage:** UK; obsolete; slang; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To sicken.

   **Usage:** UK; slang; transitive
