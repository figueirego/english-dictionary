### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To look with the eyes partly closed, as in bright sunlight, or as a threatening expression.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The children squinted to frighten each other.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To look or glance sideways.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** skew

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To look with, or have eyes that are turned in different directions; to suffer from strabismus.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To have an indirect bearing, reference, or implication; to have an allusion to, or inclination towards, something.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To be not quite straight, off-centred; to deviate from a true line; to run obliquely.

   **Usage:** Scotland; intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To turn to an oblique position; to direct obliquely.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to squint an eye

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Look askance.

   **Example:** Congress clearly is squinting at the prospect of increased funding for the program.

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. An expression in which the eyes are partly closed.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The look of eyes which are turned in different directions, as in strabismus.

   **Example:** He looks handsome although he's got a slight squint.
