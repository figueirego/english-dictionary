### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To perceive the truth or factuality of; to be certain of; to be certain that.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I know that I’m right and you’re wrong.

   **Example:** He knew something terrible was going to happen.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To be or become aware or cognizant.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Did you know Michelle and Jack were getting divorced? ― Yes, I knew.

   **Example:** Malware's sometimes been known to sit dormant for a long time.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To be aware of; to be cognizant of.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Did you know Michelle and Jack were getting divorced? ― Yes, I knew.

   **Example:** She knows where I live.

   **Example:** I knew he was upset, but I didn't understand why.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To be acquainted (with another person).

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To be acquainted or familiar with; to have encountered.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I know your mother, but I've never met your father.

   **Example:** Borja, do you know Pilar? - Sure, we've actually met before.

   **Example:** I got to know her during the pandemic, so we've known each other for years now.

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To be acquainted or familiar with; to have encountered.
   1. To have sexual relations with. This meaning normally specified in modern English as e.g. to 'know someone in the biblical sense' or to 'know biblically'.

**Source sense R1.S7**

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Their relationship knew ups and downs.

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To understand or have a grasp of through experience or study.

   **Example:** Let me do it. I know how it works.

   **Example:** She knows how to swim.

   **Example:** His mother tongue is Italian, but he also knows French and English.

   **Example:** She knows chemistry better than anybody else.

   **Example:** Know your enemy and know yourself.

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To be able to distinguish, to discern, particularly by contrast or comparison; to recognize the nature of.

   **Example:** I wouldn't know one from the other.

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To recognize as the same (as someone or something previously encountered) after an absence or change.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

11. To have knowledge; to have information, be informed.
