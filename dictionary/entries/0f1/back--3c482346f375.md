### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. At or near the rear.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** Go in the back door of the house.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Returned or restored to a previous place or condition.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; predicative; usually

   **Example:** He was on vacation, but now he’s back.

   **Example:** The office fell into chaos when you left, but now order is back.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Not current.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** I’d like to find a back issue of that magazine.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Situated away from the main or most frequented areas.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** They took a back road.

   **Example:** He lives out in the back country.

   **Example:** It's OK that the highway is jammed, because I know a back way. It's another good way to get there.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. In arrears; overdue.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** They still owe three months’ back rent.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Moving or operating backward.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** back action

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Pronounced with the highest part of the body of the tongue toward the back of the mouth, near the soft palate (most often describing a vowel).

   **Usage:** comparable; not-comparable; usually

   **Example:** The vowel of lot has a back vowel in most dialects of England.
