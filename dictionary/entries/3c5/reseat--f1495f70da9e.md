### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To provide (e.g. a room) with more, or new, seats.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We should reseat this cinema&#58; the old seats are worn.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To seat (someone) again, to give somebody a different seat.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We have to reseat you, sir&#58; this seat is reserved for the guest speaker.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To sit down again.

   **Usage:** intransitive; rare

   **Example:** I reseated after standing up to applaud the prizewinner.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To plug (something) back into its socket.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Try reseating your video adapter, and see if that fixes your computer's problems.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; electronics; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To fit (something, especially a valve) back into its place.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** To ensure that there are no leaks, clean the surfaces before you reseat the valve.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences
