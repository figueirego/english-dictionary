### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. More than half (50%) of some group.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The majority agreed that the new proposal was the best.

   **Example:** Those opposing the building plans were in the majority, so the building project was canceled.

   **Source synonyms:** absolute majority

   **Source antonyms:** minority

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any share or fraction that is bigger than any one of its counterpart shares or fractions (of the same whole).

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncommon; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. In a parliament or legislature, the difference in seats between the ruling party and the opposition; (UK) in an election, the difference in votes between the winning candidate and the second-place candidate, or between the winning candidate and all of the other candidates combined.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The ruling party had a narrow three-seat majority in the legislature.

   **Example:** The winner with 53% had a 6% majority over the loser with 47%.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Legal adulthood, age of majority.

   **Usage:** countable; dated; uncountable

   **Example:** By the time I reached my majority, I had already been around the world twice.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The office held by a member of the armed forces in the rank of major.

   **Usage:** UK; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** On receiving the news of his promotion, Charles Snodgrass said he was delighted to be entering his majority.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Ancestors; ancestry.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable
