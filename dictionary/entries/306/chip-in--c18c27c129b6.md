### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make a contribution; help in a small way; especially, to pay for a part of something.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** If we all chip in, we can afford to buy a pizza for lunch.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make a contribution; help in a small way; especially, to pay for a part of something.
   1. To contribute (for, to, or toward something).

      **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

      **Example:** She chipped in for the gift.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To contribute (something).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** He chipped in twenty for the retirement gift.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To ante; to put into the pot the amount of chips or money required to continue.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He seemed to hesitate when he chipped in.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; games

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To interrupt a discussion for the purpose of making a comment.

   **Usage:** UK; idiomatic; informal; intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To put a chip shot in the hole.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He chipped in from 20 yards for a birdie.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** golf; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To join in (something); to butt in or contribute (something) to a conversation; to say (something).

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** She chipped in that she had seen the whole situation play out.
