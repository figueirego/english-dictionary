### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To clean thoroughly; to rid of impurities; to cleanse; to clear of (something unwanted).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After the process, the machine purges the chamber before venting it to remove toxic gases.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To remove by cleansing; to wash away.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To free from sin, guilt, or burden.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To evacuate (the bowels or the stomach); to defecate or vomit.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To cause someone to purge; to operate (on somebody) using a cathartic or emetic, or in a similar manner.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To forcibly remove, for example, from political activity.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Deng Xiaoping was purged twice during the Cultural Revolution, but managed to return to power after Mao's death.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To forcibly remove people by an organization.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Cromwell had Colonel Pride purge Parliament of royalists who opposed Charles I's execution.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To clear of a charge, suspicion, or imputation.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To clarify; to clear the dregs from (liquor).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To become pure, as by clarification.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To have or produce frequent evacuations from the intestines, as by means of a cathartic.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To trim, dress, or prune.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** dermatology; medicine; sciences

## Noun · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /pɜːd͡ʒ/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /pɝd͡ʒ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- purges — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. An act or instance of purging.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. An evacuation of the bowels or the stomach; a defecation or vomiting.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Something which or someone who purges; especially, a medicine that evacuates the intestines; a cathartic.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A forcible removal of people, for example, from political activity.

   **Example:** Stalin liked to ensure that his purges were irreversible.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. An act or instance of the cleansing of pipes.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. A red or reddish liquid that seeps out from raw muscular meat consisting mostly of water and protein; "meat juice".

   **Source synonyms:** weep

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. The temporarily worsening of acne due to the introduction of skincare products that increase cell turnover, bringing underlying blemishes to the surface more quickly.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. A period of time in which normal rules supposedly do not apply; especially, a time for violence and gang war.

   **Usage:** slang
