## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈhækni/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- hackneys — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A horse suitable for riding; specifically, a strong medium-sized horse with a high-stepping trot, often used in harness competitions; also, such horses collectively as a breed.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; historical

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A horse which is hired out.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; historical; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** hireling

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. In full hackney carriage, hackney coach&#58; a horse-drawn carriage which is hired out.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable; historical; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Writing which is dull or follows a predictable formula, produced so the writer can be paid rather than as a creative work; literary hackwork.

   **Usage:** archaic; figuratively; uncountable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A person who does menial work; a drudge, a hireling, a lackey.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A person or thing that is exploited.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A prostitute.

   **Usage:** countable; derogatory; figuratively; obsolete; uncountable

## Verb · Record 3

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈhækni/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- hackneys — present; singular; third-person
- hackneying — participle; present
- hackneyed — participle; past
- hackneyed — past

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To make (something) uninteresting and unoriginal through frequent repetition; to make (something) trite.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To use (a horse) for ordinary riding.

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To transport (someone) on an ordinary riding horse.

   **Usage:** obsolete; rare; transitive; passive voice

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To transport (someone) in a hackney carriage; also (followed by the dummy pronoun it), to ride in or on a hackney carriage.

   **Usage:** obsolete; rare; transitive; passive voice

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To cause (someone, or their mind, etc.) to be less refined or sensitive; to blunt, to coarsen.

   **Usage:** figuratively; obsolete; transitive

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To give or offer (something) for money; also (also reflexive), to hire (oneself, or something) out for money.

   **Usage:** figuratively; obsolete; transitive

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. To prostitute (someone).

   **Usage:** figuratively; obsolete; transitive

<a id="r3-s8"></a>

**Source sense R3.S8**

8. To hurry or rush (someone or something).

   **Usage:** figuratively; obsolete; transitive; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s9"></a>

**Source sense R3.S9**

9. To treat or use (a person) as a menial worker.

   **Usage:** figuratively; obsolete; transitive; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s10"></a>

**Source sense R3.S10**

10. Followed by in&#58; of a person&#58; to be made experienced or used to (something); to become accustomed or habituated.

   **Usage:** derogatory; figuratively; obsolete; transitive; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s11"></a>

**Source sense R3.S11**

11. To use a horse for ordinary riding.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete

<a id="r3-s12"></a>

**Source sense R3.S12**

12. Followed by at&#58; of a person or thing&#58; to be hurried or rushed towards something.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r3-s13"></a>

**Source sense R3.S13**

13. To treat or use a person as a menial worker.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete; chiefly passive voice
