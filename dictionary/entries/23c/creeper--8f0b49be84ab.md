<a id="r1-s3"></a>

3. A one-piece garment for infants designed to facilitate access to the wearer's diaper.

   **Usage:** in-plural; often

   **Source synonyms:** babygro; creepers; diaper shirt; infant bodysuit; onesie; snapsuit

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A metal plate with spikes, designed to be worn with shoes to prevent slipping.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A small low iron, or dog, between the andirons.

   **Usage:** in-plural

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An instrument with iron hooks or claws for dredging up items from a well or other water.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Any device for causing material to move steadily from one part of a machine to another, such as an apron in a carding machine, or an inner spiral in a grain screen.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Any plant (as ivy or periwinkle) that grows by creeping, especially a climbing plant of the genus Parthenocissus.

   **Source synonyms:** climber

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. A treecreeper.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. The lowest gear of some tractors or trucks&#58; one with so low a gear ratio that the vehicle creeps along at the speed of a human's slow walk.

   **Example:** Throwing it in creeper and then getting out to work alongside is strictly forbidden because it is too dangerous if anything goes wrong.

   **Source synonyms:** creeper gear; creeper shift
