### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A measure of cold or heat, often measurable with a thermometer.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The boiling temperature of pure water is 100 degrees Celsius.

   **Example:** The temperature in the room dropped nearly 20 degrees; it went from hot to cold.

   **Example:** The most accurate way to take your temperature is by sticking a thermometer up your butt.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An elevated body temperature, as present in many illnesses; fever.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** You have a temperature. I think you should stay home today. You’re sick.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A property of macroscopic amounts of matter that serves to gauge the average intensity of the random actual motions of the individually mobile particulate constituents.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics; thermodynamics

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; machine learning

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

5. The general mood.

   **Usage:** colloquial; countable; figuratively; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable
