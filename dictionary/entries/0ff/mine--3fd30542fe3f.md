### Senses

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. That or those belonging to me.
   1. Used predicatively.

      **Example:** The house itself is mine, but the land is not.

      **Example:** These books are mine.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. That or those belonging to me.
   1. Used substantively, with an implied noun.

      **Example:** Mine has been a long journey.

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. That or those belonging to me.
   1. Used absolutely, set off from the sentence.

      **Example:** Mine for only a week so far, it already feels like an old friend.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. That or those belonging to me.
   1. My house or home.

      **Usage:** informal

      **Example:** We had the party at mine.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. That or those belonging to me.
   1. As double possessive.

      **Example:** This house of mine is over 100 years old.

      **Example:** You have something of mine.

- IPA: /maɪ̯n/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. An excavation from which ore or solid minerals are taken, especially one consisting of underground tunnels.

   **Example:** This diamond comes from a mine in South Africa.

   **Example:** He came out of the coal mine with a face covered in black.

   **Example:** Most coal and ore comes from open-pit mines nowadays.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. Any source of wealth or resources.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** She's a mine of information about the history of mathematics.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. A passage dug toward or underneath enemy lines, which is then packed with explosives.

   **Example:** The most famous mine of the American Civil War led to the Battle of the Crater.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. A device intended to explode when stepped upon or touched, or when approached by a ship, vehicle, or person.

   **Example:** His left leg was blown off after he stepped on a mine.

   **Example:** The warship was destroyed by floating mines.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. A type of firework that explodes on the ground, shooting sparks upward.

   **Usage:** pyrotechnics

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. The cavity made by a caterpillar while feeding inside a leaf.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; entomology; natural-sciences

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. A machine or network of machines used to extract units of a cryptocurrency.

   **Example:** A change to the blockchain method was contemplated to allow mines to hog less electric power.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences
