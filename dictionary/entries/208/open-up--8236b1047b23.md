### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To open.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Some vacancies have opened up due to retirements.

   **Example:** Open up the door!

   **Example:** The executive pay gap is opening up again.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To widen.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** The corridor gradually opened up into a triangular room.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To bring up (a topic etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To reveal (oneself); to share personal information about oneself; to become communicative.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** After three hours of questioning, he finally opened up.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To commence a literal or figurative battery of attacks.
   1. To commence firing weapons.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** As the convoy entered the pass, we opened up on them with everything we had.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To commence a literal or figurative battery of attacks.
   1. To rain.

      **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

      **Example:** The sky has really opened up. I've never seen this much rain!

      **Source context (verbatim):** of the sky

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To commence a literal or figurative battery of attacks.
   1. To berate; to begin shouting at or insulting someone or something.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** As soon as she saw him, she just opened up!

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To begin running, driving, travelling, etc., at maximum (or faster) speed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Synonym of cheat out (“to face or turn toward the audience more than would be natural”).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; theater
