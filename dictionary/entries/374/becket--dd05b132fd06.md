### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A short piece of rope spliced to form a circle

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A loop of rope with a knot at one end to catch in an eye at the other end. Used to secure oars etc. at their place.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A loop of thread, typically braided, attached at each end to a jacket. Used to pass through the brooch bar of medals to affix them to the jacket without damaging it.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; manufacturing; sewing; textiles

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The clevis of a pulley block.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An eye in the end of a rope.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A pocket in clothing.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A method of joining fabric, for example the doors of a tent, by interlacing loops of cord (beckets) through eyelet holes and adjacent loops.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A spade for digging turf in the Fens.

   **Usage:** England; dialectal; historical

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. chough (the bird)

   **Usage:** obsolete

   **Example:** Cornish choughs, or “beckets”, as they are sometimes known, are seen on Thomas Wolsey’s coat of arms; they are a reference to his namesake, Thomas Becket.
