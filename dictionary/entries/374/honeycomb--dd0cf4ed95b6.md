### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A substance made by bees (clade Anthophila) primarily from beeswax which has hexagonal cells to hold their larvae, and for storing pollen and honey to feed the larvae and themselves when other food is scarce; it is also eaten by humans as part of comb-honey; (countable) a single sheet made up of two layers of this substance.

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Something resembling honeycomb (noun sense 1) in having numerous cells or small holes.

   **Usage:** broadly; countable

   **Example:** The wood porch was a honeycomb of termite tunnels before we replaced it.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something resembling honeycomb (noun sense 1) in having numerous cells or small holes.
   1. A space-filling packing of polytopes in three- or higher-dimensional space.

      **Usage:** broadly; countable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geometry; mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Something resembling honeycomb (noun sense 1) in having numerous cells or small holes.
   1. The texture of the surface of a solar cell, intended to increase its surface area and capture more sunlight.

      **Usage:** broadly; countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; technology

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Something resembling honeycomb (noun sense 1) in having numerous cells or small holes.
   1. A defect in a material (especially metal) where small holes are present; specifically (construction), a defect in concrete consisting of numerous voids resulting from the failure of mortar to effectively fill the spaces among coarse aggregate particles.

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈhʌnikəʊm/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈhʌniˌkoʊm/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- honeycombing — participle; present
- honeycombed — participle; past
- honeycombed — past
- honey-comb — alternative; dated

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To riddle (something) with small holes, especially in a pattern resembling a honeycomb (noun sense 1); also, to cause (something) to become hollow or weakened in this way.

   **Usage:** transitive; often passive voice

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To bore cavities or tunnels inside (something).

   **Usage:** transitive; often passive voice

   **Example:** Termites will honeycomb a porch made of untreated pine.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To decorate (something) with a honeycomb pattern.

   **Usage:** transitive; often passive voice

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To make way deeply into (something) so as to weaken it; to undermine.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive; often passive voice; chiefly passive voice; chiefly passive voice

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To ornament (a ceiling) with honeycomb work (see noun sense 2.4).

   **Usage:** transitive; often passive voice

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To become riddled with small holes, especially in a pattern resembling a honeycomb; also, to become hollow or weakened in this way.

   **Usage:** also; archaic; figuratively; intransitive
