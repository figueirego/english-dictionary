### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To state (something) with less completeness than needed; to minimise or downplay.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** To call this decision a calculated bet understates the risk; it is a Hail Mary pass.

   **Source synonyms:** downplay; trivialize; bagatellize; banalize; belittle; cut down (idiomatic); de-emphasize; denigrate; degrade; deprecate; derogate; disparage; dispraise; downplay; lilliputianize; make little of (idiomatic); make nothing of (idiomatic); minimize; play down (idiomatic); set at nought (archaic); superficialize; trivialize; underestimate; underplay; understate; vilipend (dated; formal)

   **Source antonyms:** overstate; exaggerate; big up (slang); cut it fat (UK; obsolete; slang); exaggerate; hyperbolize (archaic); overcharge (dated); overdo; overemphasize; overstate; play up; stretch (figuratively); three-sheet

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To state (something) with an intentional lack of emphasis, in order to express irony.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** She is the queen of understatement&#58; she understated her critique masterfully.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To state a quantity that is too low.

   **Example:** The company had been understating its liabilities for years.

   **Source synonyms:** underrate; underrate; [misunderestimate](../12e/misunderestimate--4bb137f85fef.md); sell short; underapproximate; underestimate; underguess; underjudge; underrate; underreckon; understate

   **Source antonyms:** overstate; overapproximate; overestimate; overhold; overrate; overreckon; overstate; overthink (rare)
