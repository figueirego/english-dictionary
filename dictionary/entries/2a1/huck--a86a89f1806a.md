**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To throw oneself off a large jump or drop.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To throw one's body in the air, possibly in a way that is ungraceful or lacks skill.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To throw a frisbee a long distance.

   **Usage:** transitive; Ultimate Frisbee

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To make a long throw with the frisbee; to start a point by making such a throw.

   **Usage:** intransitive; Ultimate Frisbee

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To attempt a particularly big jump or drop, often haphazardly.

   **Usage:** mountain biking

   **Example:** A longer fork makes the bike more cumbersome, but you will be able to huck more stuff.

   **Example:** If you huck the take-off, you'll drop about 20 feet.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To make a maneuver in a clumsy or poorly planned way.

   **Usage:** mountain biking

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To paddle off a waterfall or to boof a big drop.

   **Usage:** transitive; whitewater kayaking

   **Example:** I hucked a sweet 25-foot waterfall on the Tomata River.
