### Pronunciation

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To arrange (cargo, goods, etc.) in the hold of a ship; to move or rearrange such goods.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To search a vessel for smuggled goods.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After the long voyage, the customs officers rummaged the ship.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To search something thoroughly and with disregard for the way in which things were arranged.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** She rummaged her purse in search of the keys.

   **Example:** The burglars rummaged the entire house for cash and jewellery.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To hastily search for something in a confined space and among many items by carelessly turning things over or pushing things aside; dig through carelessly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** She rummaged in the drawers trying to find the missing sock.

   **Source synonyms:** ransack; rifle; trawl

## Noun · Record 2

### Pronunciation

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. A thorough search, usually resulting in disorder.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Have a rummage through the attic and see if you can find anything worth selling.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. Commotion; disturbance.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A place or room for the stowage of cargo in a ship.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport
