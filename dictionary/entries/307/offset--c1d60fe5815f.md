## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈɔf.sɛt/ — source labels: US

### Forms

- off-set — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Anything that acts as counterbalance; a compensating equivalent.

   **Example:** Today's victory was an offset to yesterday's defeat.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A form of countertrade arrangement, in which the seller agrees to purchase within a set time frame products of a certain value from the buying country. This kind of agreement may be used in large international public sector contracts such as arms sales.

   **Usage:** international trade

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A time at which something begins; outset.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The offset printing process, in which ink is carried from a metal plate to a rubber blanket and from there to the printing surface.

   **Usage:** attributive; often

   **Example:** offset lithographs

   **Example:** offset process

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** media; printing; publishing

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The difference between a target memory address and a base address.

   **Example:** An array of bytes uses its index as the offset, of words a multiple thereof.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; programming; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The displacement between the base level of a measurement and the signal's real base level.

   **Usage:** signal analysis

   **Example:** The raw signal data was subjected to a baseline correction process to subtract the sensor's offset and drift variations.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The distance by which one thing is out of alignment with another.

   **Example:** There is a small offset between the switch and the indicator which some users found confusing.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A short distance measured at right angles from a line actually run to some point in an irregular boundary, or to some object.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; natural-sciences; surveying

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. An abrupt bend in an object, such as a rod, by which one part is turned aside out of line, but nearly parallel, with the rest; the part thus bent aside.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A short prostrate shoot that takes root and produces a tuft of leaves, etc.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; botany; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. A spur from a range of hills or mountains.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. A horizontal ledge on the face of a wall, formed by a diminution of its thickness, or by the weathering or upper surface of a part built out from it; a set-off.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. A terrace on a hillside.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture
