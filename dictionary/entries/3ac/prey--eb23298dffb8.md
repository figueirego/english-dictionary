## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /pɹeɪ/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. That which is or may be seized by animals to be devoured.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The deer became prey to the lion.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A person or thing given up as a victim.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A living thing, usually an animal, that is eaten by another living thing.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The rabbit was eaten by the coyote, so the rabbit is the coyote's prey.

   **Example:** Stranded without any weapons, we made very easy prey.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Anything, such as goods, etc., taken or got by violence; something taken by force from an enemy in war.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** spoil; booty; plunder

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The act of devouring other creatures; ravage.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The victim of a disease.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** He became the prey of dengue.
