### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To store; to put by.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We must lay up enough stores to get us through the winter.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To disable or incapacitate; to confine to bed.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He was laid up for six weeks with pneumonia.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To recuperate or rest; to remain in a place.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He laid up until recovering from his illness.

   **Example:** She's been laid up in Reno for weeks.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To take out of active service.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The battleship is presently laid up in Portsmouth Harbour.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To go out of active service.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The cruise ship lays up in November for the winter.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To make a layup with (a basketball)

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He takes the pass, he drives, he lays it up and in.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; basketball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To deliberately leave the ball further than necessary from the hole, so as to secure an easier succeeding shot.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Rather than try to reach the green, he chose to lay up.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** golf; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To have sex; to have a sexual relationship.

   **Usage:** US; slang
