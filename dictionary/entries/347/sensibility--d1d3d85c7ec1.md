### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Emotions or feelings arising from or relating to aesthetic or moral standards, especially those which are sensitive and thus likely to be hurt or offended.

   **Usage:** countable; in-plural; often; uncountable

   **Example:** I apologize if I offended your sensibilities, but that’s the truth of the matter.

   **Source synonyms:** susceptibilities

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The ability to feel, perceive, or sense; responsiveness to sensory stimuli; sensitivity; also, the degree to which someone or something (especially a sensory organ or tissue) is able to respond to sensory stimuli.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source antonyms:** insensibility; nonsensibility; unsensibility

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The quality of being easily affected by external forces or stimuli; also, of a measuring instrument&#58; the quality of being able to detect small changes in the environment.

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Keen sensitivity to matters of creative expression or feeling; artistic or emotional awareness.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source antonyms:** insensibility; nonsensibility

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Keen sensitivity to matters of creative expression or feeling; artistic or emotional awareness.
   1. Affected or excessive artistic or emotional awareness; the fact or quality of being overemotional; overemotionality.

      **Usage:** archaic; countable; specifically; uncountable

      **Source antonyms:** insensibility; nonsensibility

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Awareness; also, understanding.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The capacity of something to be perceived by the senses; perceptibility.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

   **Source antonyms:** insensibility

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Of a plant or one of its parts&#58; the ability to move in response to a stimulus.

   **Usage:** archaic; countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; botany; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The ability to perceive or sense as opposed to the ability to understand; also, in the philosophy of the German philosopher Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)&#58; emotion or feeling as opposed to the will.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; philosophy; sciences

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. An emotional sense or understanding of something.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. A sign or token of appreciation or gratitude.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. Synonym of Empfindsamkeit.

   **Usage:** historical; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** art; arts; entertainment; lifestyle; music

   **Source synonyms:** Empfindsamkeit (synonym; synonym-of)
