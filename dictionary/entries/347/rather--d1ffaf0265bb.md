### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used to specify a choice or preference; preferably, in preference to. (Now usually followed by than)

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** I'd rather not have spent all the money, but it really was an emergency.

   **Example:** I would like this one rather than the other one.

   **Example:** I s'pose we'd better clean the kitchen, but we had much rather not do anything right now.

   **Example:** Tomorrow's impossible. We'd rather you came next weekend.

   **Example:** They'd rather've spent the money on a holiday.

   **Source synonyms:** sooner; liefer (archaic)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Used to introduce a contradiction; on the contrary.

   **Usage:** conjunctive; not-comparable

   **Example:** It wasn't supposed to be popular; rather, it was supposed to get the job done.

   **Example:** She didn't go along, but rather went home instead.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Introducing a qualification or clarification; more precisely. (Now usually preceded by or.)

   **Usage:** conjunctive; not-comparable

   **Example:** I didn't want to leave. Or rather I did, just not alone.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Somewhat, quite; to an unexpected degree.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; degree

   **Example:** It's been rather/quite a good meal overall, but this melon is rather too tasteless.

   **Example:** We had some rather worse news today.

   **Example:** We’ll be seeing rather a lot of you over the next few days.

   **Source synonyms:** somewhat; fairly; actually; unexpectedly

   **Source antonyms:** utterly

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. More quickly.

   **Usage:** not-comparable; obsolete

   **Source synonyms:** sooner; earlier
