## Noun

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈbɹeɪ.ʒən/ — source labels: US

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The act of abrading, wearing, or rubbing off; the wearing away by friction.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The metal surface showed signs of abrasion after years of use.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An act of abrasiveness.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The substance thus rubbed off; debris.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The effect of mechanical erosion of rock, especially a river bed, by rock fragments scratching and scraping it.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** geography; geology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. An abraded, scraped, or worn area.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A superficial wound caused by scraping; an area of skin where the cells on the surface have been scraped or worn away.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The doctor cleaned the small skin abrasion on her knee.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The wearing away of the surface of the tooth by chewing.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** dentistry; medicine; sciences
