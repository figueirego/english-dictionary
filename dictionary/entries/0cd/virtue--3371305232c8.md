### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The idea of all that is good or excellent (in every sense of those terms) in a human being, collectively instantiated by a varying number of human traits known as "the virtues", the enumeration of which vary by the many virtue systems which have developed within different cultures, religions, and historical periods.

   **Usage:** uncountable; conceptually

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Accordance with moral principles; conformity of behaviour or thought with the strictures of morality; good moral conduct.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Without virtue, there is no freedom.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An attribute of a personality (a "personality trait") which predisposes a person to behaviors resulting in human goodness; an admirable quality.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The commission of acts by a person which he or she believes to be wrong can cause a diminishment of one or more of the virtues which subsist in the person's personality; in this way such actions may be viewed as significant life events.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A particular manifestation of moral excellence in a person.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Specifically, each of several qualities held to be particularly important, including the four cardinal virtues, the three theological virtues, or the seven virtues opposed to the seven deadly sins.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Better to be excluded from society than to deny the virtue of citizenship.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An inherently advantageous or excellent quality of something or someone; a favourable point, an advantage.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A creature embodying divine power, specifically one of the orders of heavenly beings, traditionally ranked above angels and archangels, and below seraphim and cherubim.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Christianity

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Specifically, moral conduct in sexual behaviour, especially of women; chastity.

   **Usage:** uncountable

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The inherent power of a god, or other supernatural being.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. The inherent power or efficacy of something (now only in phrases).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable
