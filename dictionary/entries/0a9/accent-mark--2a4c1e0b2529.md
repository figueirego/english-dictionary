### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A diacritical mark or character used in writing to indicate the place of stress (spoken accent), or to indicate the nature or quality of the vowel marked (such as long vowel versus short vowel).

   **Usage:** strictly

   **Example:** Written Spanish uses acute accent marks very precisely, to show where the stress falls whenever it does not follow the default patterns; this practice is part of the highly phonemic nature of Spanish orthography.

   **Example:** The name of the French painter Paul Cézanne is written with an acute accent mark.

   **Example:** The Italian word è (which means "is") is written with a grave accent mark.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any diacritical mark.

   **Usage:** broadly

   **Example:** Student&#58; Why does the word Čech have an accent mark on it? Teacher&#58; That mark is called a háček or caron. It tells the reader that the letter sounds like \[ch\] as in "church", not like \[k\] as in "carrot" or \[s\] as in "celery".

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** diacritic; diacritical mark
