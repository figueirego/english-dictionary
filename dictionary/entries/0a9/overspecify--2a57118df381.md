### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To specify in excessive detail.

   **Example:** The customer overspecified the requirements and now we're contractually required to build it this way. Does he think he's an engineer?

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To specify excessive capability.

   **Example:** As usual the customer overspecified the requirements, it's like asking for a car that seats 20 and fits in a compact car's parking space.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To provide redundant or inconsistent information.

   **Example:** An overspecified truth table contains at least one decision that will never be executed because it is already specified in a previous decision...

   **Example:** A noun phrase is overspecified when it is used in a context where a pronoun would have been unambiguous.

### Related words

**Source antonyms:** underspecify — antonym(s) of “specify in excessive detail”
