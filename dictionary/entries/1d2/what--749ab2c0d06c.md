### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Which, especially which of an open-ended set of possibilities.

   **Usage:** interrogative

   **Example:** What colour are you going to use?

   **Example:** What time is it?

   **Example:** What kind of car is that?

   **Example:** Did you buy the groceries? — With what money? We barely have any left.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Which.

   **Usage:** indirect; interrogative

   **Example:** I wonder what colour he is going to use.

   **Example:** I know what colour I am going to use.

   **Example:** That depends on what answer is received.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any ... that; all ... that; whatever.

   **Usage:** relative

   **Example:** He seems to have lost what sense he had.

   **Example:** What money I earn is soon spent.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Emphasises that something is noteworthy or remarkable in quality or degree, in either a good or bad way; may be used in combination with certain other determiners, especially 'a', less often 'some'.

   **Example:** This shows what beauty there is in nature.

   **Example:** You know what nonsense she talks.

   **Example:** I found out what a liar he is.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Emphasises that something is noteworthy or remarkable in quality or degree, in either a good or bad way; may be used in combination with certain other determiners, especially 'a', less often 'some'.
   1. Used to form exclamations indicating that something is remarkable, in either a good or bad way.

      **Example:** What nonsense!

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. Which thing, event, circumstance, etc.&#58; used in asking for the specification of an identity, quantity, quality, etc.

   **Usage:** interrogative

   **Example:** Hammer in that nail —What with? I didn't bring my set of tools.

   **Example:** What is your name?

   **Example:** Ask them what they want.

   **Example:** What d’you say that made him so angry?

   **Example:** I’ve broken the exhaust manifold gasket. - You’ve broken the what?

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. That which; those that; the thing(s) that.

   **Usage:** fused relative

   **Example:** He knows what he wants and what to do to get it.

   **Example:** What is amazing is his boundless energy.

   **Example:** And, what's even worse, I have to work on Sunday too.

   **Example:** What is astonishing, she doesn't want the job. -What’s even more staggering, neither of her friends does either.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Anything that; all that; whatever.

   **Usage:** fused relative

   **Example:** I will do what I can to help you.

   **Example:** What I'll do is, I'll phone you to let you know.

   **Example:** What is mine is yours.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. That; which; who.

   **Usage:** dialectal; nonstandard; relative

   **Example:** Look! It's that bloke again what was 'ere yesterday!

### Senses

<a id="r4-s1"></a>

**Source sense R4.S1**

1. An expression of surprise or disbelief.

   **Example:** What! That’s amazing!

   **Example:** wait this song is so good whattt

<a id="r4-s2"></a>

**Source sense R4.S2**

2. What do you want? An abrupt, usually unfriendly enquiry as to what a person desires.

   **Example:** What? I'm busy.

<a id="r4-s3"></a>

**Source sense R4.S3**

3. Clipping of what do you say?, used as a type of tag question to emphasise a statement and invite agreement, often rhetorically.

   **Usage:** UK; abbreviation; alt-of; clipping; colloquial; dated

   **Example:** It’s a nice day, what?

   **Alternative form of:** what do you say?; used as a type of tag question to emphasise a statement and invite agreement; often rhetorically

<a id="r4-s4"></a>

**Source sense R4.S4**

4. What did you say? I beg your pardon? This usage is often considered impolite, with the more polite "Pardon?" or "Excuse me?" preferred.

   **Example:** — Could I have some of those aarrrrrr mmmm ... — What?

<a id="r4-s5"></a>

**Source sense R4.S5**

5. Indicating a guess or approximation, or a pause to try to recall information.

   **Example:** I must have been, what, about five years old.

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** what-what (British; colloquial; interjection); wot (British; colloquial; interjection); come again — what did you say?; pardon

### Senses

<a id="r6-s1"></a>

**Source sense R6.S1**

1. Emphasizes the truth of an assertion made to contradict an evidently false assumption held by the listener.

   **Usage:** Manglish; Singlish

   **Example:** — Sian sia, nearby here don’t have library. — The National Library is a five-minute walk from here what, no meh?

<a id="r6-s2"></a>

**Source sense R6.S2**

2. Used to invite agreement or acknowledgment when something is assumed to be straightforward or already obvious to begin with; similar in function to “right?” and “as you know”.

   **Usage:** Manglish; Singlish

   **Example:** If it doesn’t get sunlight, it won’t grow what, so you should consider moving it closer to the window.

   **Source synonyms:** mah

<a id="r6-s3"></a>

**Source sense R6.S3**

3. Used after a direct assertion to reinforce a disagreeing opinion.

   **Usage:** Manglish; Singlish

   **Example:** The lava cakes are not that bad what.

   **Source synonyms:** leh
