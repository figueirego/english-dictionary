### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To lower by winding, as with a crank or windlass.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Wind down the car window if you want to talk to me.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of an object that can be raised or lowered

   **Source synonyms:** roll down; lower

   **Source antonyms:** wind up; roll up; raise

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To unwind.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** You'd better wind down that wind-up toy before you take it apart, or pieces might go flying.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a device with a mainspring

   **Source synonyms:** unwind; deactivate

   **Source antonyms:** wind; wind up; activate

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To shut down slowly (by degrees or in phases).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** A program as complex as the Space Shuttle program takes some time to wind down.

   **Source synonyms:** unwind; shut down

   **Source antonyms:** activate; fire up; power up; start up; boot up

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To slow, as if coming to an end; to become calmer or less busy.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** The festival seems to be winding down now.

   **Source synonyms:** taper off

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To relax; to get rid of stress; to destress.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** After a long day at work, she winds down by kickboxing.

   **Source synonyms:** calm down; unwind; chill out; let off steam

   **Source antonyms:** power up
