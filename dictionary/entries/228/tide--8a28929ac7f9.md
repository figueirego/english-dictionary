### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The daily fluctuation in the level of the sea caused by the gravitational influence of the moon and the sun.

   **Example:** The Bristol Channel has some of the world's largest tides.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The associated flow of water.

   **Example:** A lot of driftwood was brought in on the tide.

   **Example:** We can cross to the island when the tide is out, but it won't move out until around five o'clock.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any similar gravitational effect on Earth or other body.

   **Example:** As well as sea tides, there are much smaller land tides.

   **Example:** By far the largest tides on Europa are those caused by the gravitational attraction of Jupiter.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A high-volume flow, literal or figurative; a current or flood.

   **Example:** The sewer burst, and a tide of sewage poured into nearby properties.

   **Example:** A tide of people crossed over the border.

   **Example:** We've encountered a tide of problems.

   **Source synonyms:** inflood; inflooding; inflow; inflowing; influx

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The tendency or direction of causes, influences, or events; course; current.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** The tide of public opinion has turned.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Time, notably anniversary, period or season linked to an ecclesiastical feast.

   **Usage:** obsolete; except in liturgy

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chronology; hobbies; horology; lifestyle

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A time.

   **Usage:** archaic; regional

   **Example:** The doctor's no good this tide.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A point or period of time identified or described by a qualifier.

   **Usage:** archaic; in-compounds; regional

   **Example:** Eventide, noontide, morrowtide, nighttide, moontide, harvesttide, wintertide, summertide, springtide, autumntide etc.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. The period of twelve hours.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; mining

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To cause to float with the tide; to drive or carry with the tide or stream.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To carry over or through a problem or difficulty.

   **Usage:** broadly

   **Source context (verbatim):** in the phrase tide over

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To pour a tide or flood.

   **Usage:** intransitive; rare

   **Example:** The ocean tided most impressively.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To work into or out of a river or harbor by drifting with the tide and anchoring when it becomes adverse.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport
