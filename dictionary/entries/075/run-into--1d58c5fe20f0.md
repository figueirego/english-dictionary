## Verb

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To enter by running.

   **Usage:** literally; intransitive but with prepositional object

   **Example:** He ran into the building.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To collide with.

   **Usage:** intransitive but with prepositional object

   **Example:** He lost control of the vehicle and ran into a tree.

   **Example:** I've sustained some front-end damage after running into a pothole.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cause to collide with.

   **Usage:** transitive and with prepositional object

   **Example:** He lost control of the vehicle and ran it into a tree.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To unexpectedly encounter or meet someone or something (literally or figuratively).

   **Usage:** broadly; intransitive but with prepositional object

   **Example:** I ran into your cousin the other day.

   **Example:** Everything was going according to plan until we ran into the legal problems.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To reach, to flow into (a body of water).

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive but with prepositional object

   **Source context (verbatim):** of flowing water

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To blend into; to be followed by or adjacent to without there being a clear boundary.

   **Usage:** intransitive but with prepositional object

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To cause to blend into.

   **Usage:** transitive and with prepositional object

   **Example:** You can use the paintbrush this way to run the colors into each other.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To reach a large figure.

   **Example:** By the end, the cost of the project ran into the millions of dollars.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To amount to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** debts running into millions
