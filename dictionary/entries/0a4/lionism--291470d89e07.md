### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The 19th-century practice of bringing a lion (“an interesting person or object”) into one’s home as entertainment for visitors.

   **Usage:** historical

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The state of being the lion (see above) of 19th-century hosts.

   **Usage:** historical

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The practice of lionizing (“treating a person as a celebrity or someone important”); also, the state of being lionized.

   **Usage:** broadly

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The state of a person having a leonine facies, that is, facial features which resemble those of a lion as a result of some disease, especially a form of leprosy which causes leontiasis (“a medical condition characterized by an overgrowth of the cranial and facial bones”).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; pathology; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The ideals of Rastafarianism.

   **Usage:** Rastafari
