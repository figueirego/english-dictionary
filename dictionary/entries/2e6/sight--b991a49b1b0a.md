### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The sense or faculty by which objects are perceived through the agency of light; the sense by which we see.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Sight is a precious gift.

   **Example:** He is losing his sight and now can barely read.

   **Source synonyms:** vision; eyesight; ken; sight; perception; vision

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An act, instance, opportunity or possibility of seeing; the perception of objects by the eye; view (see also idiomatic expressions such as in sight, out of sight, catch sight of, etc.)

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The sight of him in his new uniform made us all proud.

   **Example:** I need to have sight of the certificate before proceeding.

   **Example:** to gain sight of land, to lose sight of the aircraft

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something seen.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The sight in front of me seemed somehow familiar.

   **Source synonyms:** view; vision

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Something worth seeing; a spectacle, either good or bad.

   **Usage:** countable; in-plural; often; uncountable

   **Example:** We went to London and saw all the sights – Buckingham Palace, Tower Bridge, and so on.

   **Example:** You really look a sight in that ridiculous costume!

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A device used in aiming a projectile, through which the person aiming looks at the intended target; see also in one's sights.

   **Usage:** countable; in-plural; often; uncountable

   **Source synonyms:** peep sight; scope

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A small aperture through which objects are to be seen, and by which their direction is settled or ascertained.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** the sight of a quadrant

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. a great deal, a lot; frequently used to intensify a comparative.

   **Usage:** colloquial; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** a sight of money

   **Example:** This is a darn sight better than what I'm used to at home!

   **Source synonyms:** brimful; load; ton; abundance; battalion; arseload (UK; slang; vulgar); assload (US; slang; vulgar); bellyload; boatload (slang); brimful; busload; bucketful (informal); bucketload; butt-ton; bumload (UK; slang); buttload (British; US; dated); cartload; crapflood (slang); crapload (slang); deal; fuckload (slang; vulgar); fuckton (slang; vulgar); fuckwad (slang; vulgar); gob; great deal (idiomatic); hantle (British; dialectal); heap; hecatomb (figuratively; literary; poetic); helluvalot; horseload; lashings (Ireland; UK; informal); load; lot; mampus (British; dialectal); many; mass; metric fuckton (slang; vulgar); metric shitload (slang; vulgar); mickle (Northern-England; Scotland; archaic); mountain; muckle (Scotland); multiplicity; multitude; pile; ream; shedload (euphemistic; slang); shitflood (slang; vulgar); shitful (slang; vulgar); shitload (slang; vulgar); shitton (slang; vulgar); sight; slew; spadeloads; sum; superflux; ton; trainload (slang); truckload; tun; vaultful (figuratively); wagonload

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. In a drawing, picture, etc., that part of the surface, as of paper or canvas, which is within the frame or the border or margin. In a frame, the open space, the opening.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To see; to get sight of (something); to register visually.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to sight land from a ship

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To see; to get sight of (something); to register visually.
   1. To observe through, or as if through, a sight, to check the elevation, direction, levelness, or other characteristics of, especially when surveying or navigating.

      **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To apply sights to; to adjust the sights of.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to sight a rifle or a cannon

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To observe or aim (at something) using a (gun) sight.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive
