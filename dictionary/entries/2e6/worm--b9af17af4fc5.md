### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A generally tubular invertebrate of the annelid phylum; an earthworm.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. More loosely, any of various tubular invertebrates resembling annelids but not closely related to them, such as velvet worms, acorn worms, flatworms, or roundworms. See Appendix&#58;English worms.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any creeping or crawling animal, such as a snake (cf. “dragon”; blindworm, slowworm, “legless lizard”), snail, or caterpillar.

   **Usage:** archaic; poetic

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Any creeping or crawling animal, such as a snake (cf. “dragon”; blindworm, slowworm, “legless lizard”), snail, or caterpillar.
   1. A maggot or any other insect larva with similar shape and behavior.

      **Usage:** archaic; broadly; informal; poetic

      **Example:** Those little wriggling worms are the larvae of flies who laid eggs here.

**Source sense R1.S5**

   **Usage:** archaic; poetic

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make (one's way) with a crawling motion.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We wormed our way through the underbrush.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To move with one's body dragging the ground.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To work one's way by artful or devious means.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To work (one's way or oneself) (into) gradually or slowly; to insinuate.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** He wormed his way into the organization.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To effect, remove, drive, draw, or the like, by slow and secret means.

   **Usage:** often

   **Source context (verbatim):** followed by out

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To drag out of, to get information that someone is reluctant or unwilling to give (through artful or devious means or by pleading or asking repeatedly).

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** in “worm out of”

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To fill in the contlines of (a rope) before parcelling and serving.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Worm and parcel with the lay; turn and serve the other way.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To deworm (an animal).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To cut the worm, or lytta, from under the tongue of (a dog, etc.) for the purpose of checking a disposition to gnaw, and formerly supposed to guard against canine madness.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**

10. To clean by means of a worm; to draw a wad or cartridge from, as a firearm.

   **Usage:** transitive
