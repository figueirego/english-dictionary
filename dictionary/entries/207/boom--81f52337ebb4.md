### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make a loud, hollow, resonant sound.

   **Example:** Thunder boomed in the distance and lightning flashes lit up the horizon.

   **Example:** The cannon boomed, recoiled, and spewed a heavy smoke cloud.

   **Example:** Beneath the cliff, the sea was booming on the rocks.

   **Example:** I can hear the organ slowly booming from the chapel.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To exclaim with force, to shout, to thunder.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of speech

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To flourish, grow, or progress.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The population boomed in recent years.

   **Example:** Business was booming.

   **Source synonyms:** flourish; prosper

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To make (something) boom.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Men in grey robes slowly boom the drums of death.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To make a deep, resonant, territorial vocalisation.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a Eurasian bittern

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To cause a sonic boom.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To subject (someone or something) to a sonic boom.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To publicly praise, to rally behind.

   **Usage:** US; obsolete; slang

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To rush forwards with such violent intensity that it generates a sustained, overwhelming, roaring noise; especially from the perspective of a bystander who has been suddenly subjected to it.

   **Example:** The train boomed through the station without stopping.

   **Example:** The ship was booming under the press of sail.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a vehicle

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To rapidly adjust the evaluation of a position away from zero, indicating a likely win or loss.

   **Usage:** slang

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** board-games; chess; computer; computing; engineering; games; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

   **Source antonyms:** moob

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To cause to advance rapidly in price.

   **Usage:** dated; transitive

   **Example:** to boom railroad or mining shares
