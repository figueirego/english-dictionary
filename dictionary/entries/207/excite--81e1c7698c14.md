### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To stir the emotions of; to cause to feel excitement.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The fireworks which opened the festivities excited anyone present.

   **Source synonyms:** affect; move; touch

   **Source antonyms:** relax; calm

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To arouse or bring out (e.g. feelings); to stimulate.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Favoritism tends to excite jealousy in the ones not being favored.

   **Example:** The political reforms excited unrest among the population.

   **Example:** Smugglers operating secretly so as not to excite suspicion.

   **Source synonyms:** instigate; fire; provoke; abet (obsolete); actuate; goose; fuel; light a fire under; send; sting; bring about; cheer (obsolete); draw; drive; encourage; egg on (idiomatic); engender; evoke; excite; foment; goad; grill (Scotland; US; obsolete); ignite (figuratively); impel; incite; induce; inflame (figuratively); instigate; invoke; lash; motive; motivate; move (obsolete); obligate; oblige; push; promote; prompt; provoke; rouse; set off; spark off; spur; [spur on](../327/spur-on--c9c86bd78ae1.md); stimulate; stir; stir up; stoke (figuratively); tempt; urge; whet (obsolete); whip up; animate; awaken; electrify (figuratively); enflame (figuratively); enkindle (figuratively); enthrill; exagitate (obsolete); excite; exhilarate; exsuscitate (obsolete); fillip; fire (figuratively); fire the blood; galvanize (figuratively); gin up; ignite (figuratively); inflame (figuratively); intoxicate (figuratively); kindle (figuratively); quicken; rouse; send (slang); set fire to (figuratively); set on fire (figuratively); sharpen (figuratively); stir the blood; suscitate (obsolete); thrill; vitalise; wake; warm the blood; whet (figuratively); wind up; work up; yerk (Scotland; obsolete)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cause an electron to move to a higher than normal state; to promote an electron to an outer level.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** By applying electric potential to the neon atoms, the electrons become excited, then emit a photon when returning to normal.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To energize (an electromagnet); to produce a magnetic field in.

   **Example:** to excite a dynamo
