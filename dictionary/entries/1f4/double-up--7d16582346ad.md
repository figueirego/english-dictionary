### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To double the quantity, amount or duration of something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'm going to double up my enlistment.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To double one's number of chips, especially in poker by winning an all-in pot.

   **Usage:** broadly; intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** gambling; games

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To bend, bend over; to fold; to stoop.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a person

   **Source synonyms:** double over

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cause (someone) to bend over; to beat up (someone).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I doubled him up with a swift punch to the stomach.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To have a secondary use.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** This unfolding sofa doubles up as a bed.

   **Source synonyms:** double

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To get the second out in a double play, typically referring to getting an out by beating a runner back to a base (often by throwing) after a fly ball has been caught

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Jones snared the liner and then stepped on the bag to double up the runner.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To employ double the usual resources for a particular purpose; to work in pairs.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To marry or live with someone; to share a living space.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang
