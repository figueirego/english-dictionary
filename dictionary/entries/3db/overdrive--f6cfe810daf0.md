## Noun · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈəʊvə(ɹ)ˌdɹaɪv/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- overdrives — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. A gear, on an automobile, higher than the traditional top gear, mainly to allow better fuel economy at highway speeds. In the 21st century, such gears are no longer viewed as "extra" and thus rarely called by this name.

   **Usage:** countable; dated; uncountable

   **Example:** In the 1970s, many transmission designs were revised to add an overdrive gear.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** automotive; transport; vehicles

   **Source synonyms:** OD; O/D; O.D.; top gear

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A state of heightened activity.

   **Usage:** figuratively; uncountable

   **Example:** overdrive pacing

   **Example:** The campaign has been in overdrive since the recent announcement.

   **Source synonyms:** top gear; full throttle

   **Source antonyms:** low gear

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Freewheeling (driving a vehicle with the transmission in neutral) on downhill stretches to achieve higher fuel economy and/or speed than otherwise. In colloquial registers, ethnically bigoted variants have been common, such as Jewish overdrive (alluding to the stereotype of miserly Jews) or Mexican overdrive or Polish overdrive (alluding to stereotypes of foolishness and poverty).

   **Usage:** countable; euphemistic; slang; uncountable

   **Example:** Of course overdrive is forbidden for dump trucks in the quarry because it is unsafe, but unscrupulous workers were found to be using it because of a perverse incentive involving tonnage piece rates.

   **Source synonyms:** see at angel gear

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A gear ratio higher than 1&#58;1&#58; one in which a single revolution of the driving element corresponds to more than one revolution of the driven element.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; mechanical; mechanical-engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences
