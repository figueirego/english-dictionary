### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A physician; a member of the medical profession; one who is trained and licensed to heal the sick or injured. The final examination and qualification may award a doctor degree in which case the post-nominal letters are DO, DPM, MD, DMD, or DDS in the US, or MBBS or BDS in the UK.

   **Example:** If you still feel unwell tomorrow, see your doctor.

   **Source synonyms:** physician; doctor; doc; leech (archaic); physic (obsolete); physician; medic; medician; medical doctor

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A person who has attained a doctorate, such as a Ph.D. or Th.D. or one of many other terminal degrees conferred by a college or university.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A veterinarian; a medical practitioner who treats non-human animals.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A nickname for a person who has special knowledge or talents to manipulate or arrange transactions.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A teacher; one skilled in a profession or a branch of knowledge; a learned man.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Any mechanical contrivance intended to remedy a difficulty or serve some purpose in an exigency.

   **Usage:** dated

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A fish, the friar skate.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A witch doctor.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A ship's cook.

   **Usage:** obsolete; slang

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To act as a medical doctor to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Her children doctored her back to health.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To act as a medical doctor.

   **Usage:** humorous; intransitive

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To make (someone) into an (academic) doctor; to confer a doctorate upon.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To physically alter (medically or surgically) a living being in order to change growth or behavior.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** They doctored their apple trees by vigorous pruning, and now the dwarfed trees are easier to pick.

   **Example:** We may legally doctor a pet to reduce its libido.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To genetically alter an extant species.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Mendel's discoveries showed how the evolution of a species may be doctored.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To alter or make obscure, as with the intention to deceive, especially a document.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** To doctor the signature of an instrument with intent to defraud is an example of forgery.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To adulterate, drug, or poison (drink).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To take medicine.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete
