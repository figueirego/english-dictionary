### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The time when the Sun is above the horizon and it lights the sky.

   **Example:** day and night; I work at night and sleep during the day.

   **Source synonyms:** daylight; day; daylight; daylength; daytime; photoperiod

   **Source antonyms:** night; night; nightertale (obsolete); nighttide; nighttime; scotoperiod; skotoperiod

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A period of time equal or almost equal to a full day-night cycle, being 24 hours long.

   **Example:** I've been here for two days and a bit.

   **Source synonyms:** twenty-four hour day; 24-hour day; nychthemeron

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A period of time equal or almost equal to a full day-night cycle, being 24 hours long.
   1. The time taken for the Sun to seem to be in the same place in the sky twice; a solar day.

      **Source synonyms:** twenty-four hour day; 24-hour day; nychthemeron

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A period of time equal or almost equal to a full day-night cycle, being 24 hours long.
   1. The time taken for the Earth to make a full rotation about its axis with respect to the fixed stars; a sidereal day or stellar day.

      **Source synonyms:** twenty-four hour day; 24-hour day; nychthemeron

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A 24-hour period beginning at 6am or sunrise.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** Your 8am forecast&#58; The high for the day will be 30 and the low, before dawn, will be 10.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** climatology; meteorology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A period of time between two set times which mark the beginning and the end of day in a calendar, such as from midnight to the following midnight or (Judaism) from nightfall to the following nightfall.

   **Example:** The day begins at midnight.

   **Example:** Monday is the first day of the week in many countries of the world.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The rotational period of a planet.

   **Example:** A day on Mars is slightly over 24 hours.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** astronomy; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. The part of a day period which one spends at one’s job, school, etc.

   **Example:** I worked two days last week.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. An observance lasting for a day, such as an annual holiday.

   **Example:** Christmas Day

   **Example:** Remembrance Day

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A day set aside for a specified activity.

   **Example:** leg day, moving day, observation day

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. A specified time or period; time, considered with reference to the existence or prominence of a person or thing; age; time; era.

   **Example:** every dog has its day; in that day; back in the day; in those days

   **Source synonyms:** era; epoch; age; cycle; day (figuratively); epoch; era; generation; Great Year; interval; period; Platonic year; time

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. A period of contention of a day or less.

   **Example:** The day belonged to the Allies.
