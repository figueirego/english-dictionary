### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To discover, find out; to uncover.

   **Usage:** archaic; impersonal; transitive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To change with a specific direction, progress.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Let's see how things develop and then make our decision.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To progress through a sequence of stages.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Isabel developed from a tropical depression to a tropical storm to a hurricane. An embryo develops into a fetus and then into an infant.

   **Source synonyms:** shift; proceed; convert; develop; draw (figuratively; obsolete); evolve; metamorphize; metamorphose; reconvert; shift (figuratively); transfigurate; transfigure; transform; transmogrify; transmute; transume (archaic)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To advance; to further; to promote the growth of.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To create.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I need to develop a plan for the next three weeks.

   **Source synonyms:** contrive; generate; bring forth; cause; contrive; create; ferment; forge; develop; engender; evolve; generate; gin up (North-America); innate; form; forthbring (obsolete); make; produce; spawn

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To bring out images latent in photographic film.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Please develop this roll of film.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To acquire something usually over a period of time.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I have been in England enough to develop a British accent.

   **Example:** You will develop calluses if you play the cello.

   **Example:** She developed bad eating habits.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To place one's pieces actively.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I need to develop my white-square bishop.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** board-games; chess; games

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To cause a ball to become more open and available to be played on later. Usually by moving it away from the cushion, or by opening a pack.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; games; hobbies; lifestyle; pool; snooker; sports

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To change the form of (an algebraic expression, etc.) by executing certain indicated operations without changing the value.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences
