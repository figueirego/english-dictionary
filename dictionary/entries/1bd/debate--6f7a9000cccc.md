**Source sense R1.S1**

   **Usage:** transitive

## Noun · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /dɪˈbeɪt/ — source labels: UK
- IPA: /dɪˈbeɪt/ — source labels: US
- IPA: /di-/ — source labels: US — **partial source transcription; not a complete pronunciation**
- IPA: /də-/ — source labels: US — **partial source transcription; not a complete pronunciation**

### Forms

- debates — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. An argument, or discussion, usually in an ordered or formal setting, often with more than two people, generally ending with a vote or other decision.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** After a four-hour debate, the committee voted to table the motion.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. An informal and spirited but generally civil discussion of opposing views.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The debate over the age of the universe is thousands of years old.

   **Example:** There was a bit of a debate over who should pay for the damaged fence.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Discussion of opposing views.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** There has been considerable debate concerning exactly how to format these articles.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A type of literary composition, taking the form of a discussion or disputation, commonly found in the vernacular medieval poetry of many European countries, as well as in medieval Latin.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. Strife, discord.

   **Usage:** countable; obsolete; uncountable
