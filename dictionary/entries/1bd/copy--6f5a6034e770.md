## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The result of copying; an identical or nearly identical duplicate of an original.

   **Example:** Please bring me at least 200 copies of this report. The photocopier is down the hall on the right.

   **Example:** His new book is out. I have a signed copy. He'll send you a copy if you want one. He's giving free copies to his former colleagues who want one. The press run is 3000 copies, and he hopes to sell more press runs after this one.

   **Example:** I already saw their draft. She emailed me a copy yesterday. I can forward it to you so that you have your own copy.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The result of copying; an identical or nearly identical duplicate of an original.
   1. The result of gene or chromosomal duplication.

      **Example:** Every cell in this organism contains a copy of each gene, but whether that copy will be expressed depends on complex factors of cell differentiation and epigenetics.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; genetics; medicine; natural-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An imitation, sometimes of inferior quality.

   **Example:** That handbag is a copy. You can tell because the buckle is different.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The text (words, content) that is to be typeset or similarly prepared and published.

   **Example:** This organization doesn't publish any copy that hasn't been edited by a good copy editor.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** journalism; media; publishing; typography

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The text (words, content) that is to be typeset or similarly prepared and published.
   1. The output of copywriters, who are employed to write material which encourages consumers to buy goods or services.

      **Example:** ad copy; advertising copy

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** advertising; business; journalism; marketing; media; publishing; typography

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The text (words, content) that is to be typeset or similarly prepared and published.
   1. The text to be set into newspaper articles, magazine pages, or similar.

      **Usage:** uncountable

      **Example:** Submit all copy to the appropriate editor.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** journalism; media; publishing; typography

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A person employed to carry copy and run errands.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** journalism; media

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A schoolwork pad or workbook.

   **Example:** Tim got in trouble for forgetting his maths copy.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A particular instance of a book&#58; a single printed impression or digital file representing it.

   **Example:** Our town's public library has several copies of the Bible, in each of several editions.

   **Example:** Our town's public library has several copies of the second edition of Smith's textbook, but it doesn't yet have any copies of any newer edition.

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A particular instance of an issue of a periodical (e.g., magazine, journal, bulletin)&#58; a single printed impression or digital file representing that issue; (metonymic) the issue.

   **Example:** Have you seen the latest copy of Newsweek yet?

**Source sense R1.S11**

   **Usage:** archaic

**Source sense R1.S12**

   **Usage:** obsolete

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To produce an object identical to a given object.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Please copy these reports for me.

   **Example:** Don't copy that floppy!

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To give or transmit a copy to (a person).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Make sure you copy me on that important memo.

   **Example:** All heads of departments have been copied.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To place a copy of an object in memory for later use.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** First copy the files, and then paste them in another directory.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To imitate.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Don't copy my dance moves.

   **Example:** Mom, he's copying me!

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To receive a transmission successfully.
