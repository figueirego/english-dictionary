### Senses

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The striking of one body against another; collision.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The force or energy of a collision of two objects.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The hatchet cut the wood on impact.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A forced impinging.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** His spine had an impingement; L4 and L5 made impact, which caused numbness in his leg.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; proscribed; uncountable

   **Example:** His friend's opinion had an impact on his decision.

## Verb · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- enPR: im-păktʹ — variety unspecified in the source labels
- IPA: /ɪmˈpækt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- impacts — present; singular; third-person
- impacting — participle; present
- impacted — participle; past
- impacted — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To collide or strike, the act of impinging.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** When the hammer impacts the nail, it bends.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To compress; to compact; to press into something or pack together.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The footprints of birds do not impact the soil in the way those of dinosaurs do.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To significantly or strongly influence or affect; to have an impact on.

   **Usage:** figuratively; proscribed; transitive

   **Example:** I can make the changes, but it will impact the schedule.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To stamp or impress onto something.

   **Usage:** rare; transitive

   **Example:** Ideas impacted on the mind.
