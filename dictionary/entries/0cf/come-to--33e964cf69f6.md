### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To recover consciousness after fainting etc.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** She came to with the aid of smelling salts.

   **Source synonyms:** come to one's senses; come back to one's senses; come to oneself; come round; come around; revive

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To stop a sailing vessel, especially by turning into the wind. See also come about.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To total; to amount to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** so how much does that come to?; the bill comes to £10 each

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To reach; to arrive at.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** come to an end; come to a conclusion; come to an agreement; come to a halt

   **Example:** I don't know what the world is coming to! Everything seems so crazy these days.

   **Example:** Many sprouting crops never come to ear.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To seek help from.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** You can always come to me when you're feeling sad.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To devote attention to in due course; to come around to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'll come to your question in a minute.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To befall; to happen to; to come upon.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I pray no harm will come to you.

   **Source synonyms:** affect

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To regard or specifically pertain to.

   **Usage:** present; regional; transitive

   **Example:** He's the best when it comes to detective fiction.

   **Example:** When it comes to remorseless criminals, this guy takes the cake.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see come, to.
