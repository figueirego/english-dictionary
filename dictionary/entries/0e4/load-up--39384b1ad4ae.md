### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To fill (a vehicle, vessel, room, etc.) with a load of cargo or similar.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We loaded up the wagon with supplies.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To load (things) as cargo or similar.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Q&#58; What should we do with these other boxes? A&#58; Load 'em up, same as the others.

   **Source antonyms:** offload

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To receive a load of cargo or similar.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The wagon halted for an hour to load up with supplies.

   **Example:** Let's stop at the off-licence and load up on booze and snacks.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To fully load (a weapon).

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; idiomatic

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To load (software) fully into memory.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To intoxicate oneself with alcohol or drugs; to get drunk or get high.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang
