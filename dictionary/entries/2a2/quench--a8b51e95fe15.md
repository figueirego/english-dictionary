### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To satisfy, especially a literal or figurative thirst.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The library quenched her thirst for knowledge.

   **Source synonyms:** appease; slake

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To extinguish or put out (as a fire or light).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cool rapidly by direct contact with liquid coolant, as a blacksmith quenching hot iron.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The swordsmith quenched the sword in an oil bath so that it wouldn't shatter.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; metallurgy; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To terminate or greatly diminish (a chemical reaction) by destroying or deforming the remaining reagents.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To rapidly change the parameters of a physical system.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To rapidly terminate the operation of a superconducting electromagnet by causing part or all of the magnet's windings to enter the normal, resistive state.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** If someone is pinned against the MRI magnet by a ferromagnetic object, you may need to quench the magnet in order to free them.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics
