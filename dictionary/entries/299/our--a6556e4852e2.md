### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Belonging to us, excluding the person(s) being addressed (exclusive our).

   **Usage:** determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** Sorry, you're not invited to our party.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Belonging to us, including the person(s) being addressed (inclusive our).

   **Usage:** determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** We must try to save our marriage for the sake of our children.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Of, from, or belonging to any entity that the speaker is a part of or identifies with, such as place of employment or education, nation, region, language, etc.

   **Usage:** determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** Our safety record at ABC Widgets is very good.

   **Example:** In English, our nouns do not generally have gender.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Belonging to people in general.

   **Usage:** determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** As we get older, our reactions get slower.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Belonging to everyone being addressed.

   **Usage:** colloquial; determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** How are we all tonight? Have we got our happy faces on?

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Belonging to an individual being addressed; used especially of a person in the speaker's care, or to whom advice or instruction is being given.

   **Usage:** colloquial; determiner; first-person; plural; possessive; may seem patronising

   **Example:** How are we feeling this morning, Mrs Miggins? Have we taken our tablets yet?

   **Example:** You've been spending all your money boozing when you have a wife and family to support. I think we need to get our priorities straight.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Belonging to a third person, especially someone in the speaker's care.

   **Usage:** colloquial; determiner; first-person; plural; possessive; uncommon

   **Example:** "I've just been to see Mrs Miggins." — "And how is she?" — "Not too good today. We've had a little problem with our tummy."

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Used to imply connection between the speaker's experiences or activities and a group of listeners.

   **Usage:** colloquial; determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** Hey guys, in this video I'll show you how I make my upside-down sponge cake. First, let's get our ingredients weighed out.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Used before a person's name to indicate that the person is in one's family, or is a very close friend.

   **Usage:** Northern-England; Scotland; determiner; first-person; plural; possessive

   **Example:** I'm going to see our Terry for tea.
