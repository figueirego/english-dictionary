## Verb · Record 1

### Forms

- gets out — present; singular; third-person
- getting out — participle; present
- got out — past
- got out — UK; participle; past
- gotten out — US; participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To leave or escape.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** In case of fire, get out by the nearest exit.

   **Example:** You have to get out on a bull to succeed in a rodeo.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To come out of a situation; to escape a fate.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Never take life seriously. Nobody gets out alive anyway.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To be released, especially from hospital or prison.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He's been in prison for six months, but he gets out next week.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To remove one's money from an investment; to end an investment.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I think copper prices will plummet this fall, so I'm getting out while I can.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To help (someone) leave.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We must get the children out first.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To take (something) from its container or storage place, so as to use or display it.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Get the playing cards out and we'll have a game of snap.

   **Example:** It's time to get the Christmas decorations out.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To leave the inside of a vehicle such as a car. (Note&#58; for public transport, get off is more common.)

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I'll get out at the end of the road and walk from there.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To become known.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Somehow the secret got out.

   **Example:** We have to act before that fact gets out on us.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To spend free time out of the house.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** You work too hard. You should get out more.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To publish or make available; to disseminate.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The organization has just gotten their newsletter out.

   **Example:** This candidate is struggling to get her message out.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To say with difficulty.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He could hardly get the words out for the tears.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To remove or eliminate (dirt or stains).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** This detergent will get most household stains out.

<a id="r1-s13"></a>

**Source sense R1.S13**

13. To end.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** School gets out in the afternoon.

## Interjection · Record 2

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. Commanding a person to leave.

   **Usage:** literally

   **Example:** Get out! I never want to see you in here again!

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. Indicating incredulity.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Expressing disapproval or disgust, especially after a bad joke.

   **Usage:** Ireland; UK; slang

   **Example:** Just get out.
