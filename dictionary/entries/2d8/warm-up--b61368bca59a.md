### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To become warmer.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He came inside to warm up.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To heat or reheat (e.g. food).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I couldn't be bothered to cook another meal so I just warmed up yesterday's leftovers.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To reach, or cause to reach, a normal operating temperature (of a car for example).

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** These old valve radios take a few minutes to warm up.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To physically or mentally prepare oneself, something or someone for an activity.
   1. To do gentle exercise, stretching etc., in order to prepare the body for more vigorous exercise.

      **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

      **Example:** It is important to warm up properly before your gym session.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To physically or mentally prepare oneself, something or someone for an activity.
   1. To prepare for an activity by carrying out a practice or preparation routine.

      **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

      **Example:** The band were already on stage, warming up for the gig.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To physically or mentally prepare oneself, something or someone for an activity.
   1. To make (an audience) enthusiastic or animated before a show.

      **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

      **Example:** Rich knew how to warm up the audience of The Price is Right. His good looks and charming personality made even that special.
