## Noun

### Forms

- camelbacks — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The backs of camels.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** We travelled across the desert on camelback.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A flexible water container worn on the back.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** An empty camelback is a blessing and a curse&#58; it is easy to carry, but it means you have no water.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A house with a second storey that does not completely cover the ground floor.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** My friend lived in a camelback; we used to have fun climbing out of the window onto the roof below.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A railway locomotive that has its cab in the middle as opposed to at the end.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** The camelbacks were designed to help with the visibility problems inherent in putting the driver behind the entire engine.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A sofa or chair whose back has a raised middle part.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Example:** He fell back relieved into his favourite camelback with a gin and tonic.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A type of rubber used in tyre restoration.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** We require four million tons of camelback each year.

   **Example:** The camelback rubber produced in this plant is shipped around the world.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. One or more hump-shaped hills on a straight portion of rollercoaster track, designed to lift riders out of their seats and provide a feeling of weightlessness.

   **Usage:** countable

   **Usage:** uncountable
