## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /əˈsɪm.ɪ.leɪt/ — source labels: UK; US

### Forms

- assimilates — present; singular; third-person
- assimilating — participle; present
- assimilated — participle; past
- assimilated — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To incorporate nutrients into the body, especially after digestion.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Food is assimilated and converted into organic tissue.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To incorporate or absorb (knowledge) into the mind.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The teacher paused in her lecture to allow the students to assimilate what she had said.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To absorb (a person or people) into a community or culture.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The aliens in the science-fiction film wanted to assimilate human beings into their own race.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To liken, compare to something similar.

   **Usage:** rare; transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** used with "to" or "with"

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To bring to a likeness or to conformity; to cause a resemblance between.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To become similar.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To be incorporated or absorbed into something.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To undergo phonetic assimilation.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; phonology; sciences

**Source synonyms:** process — incorporate or absorb knowledge into the mind; integrate — absorb a group of people into a community
