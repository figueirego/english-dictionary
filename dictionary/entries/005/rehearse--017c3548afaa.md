### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To repeat, as what has been already said; to tell over again; to recite.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** There’s no need to rehearse the same old argument; we’ve heard it before, and we all agree.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To narrate; to relate; to tell; to recount.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The witness rehearsed the events of the night before for the listening detectives.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To practise by recitation or repetition in private for experiment and improvement, prior to a public representation, especially in theater.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** The main actors spent on average two hours a day rehearsing before the first night.

   **Example:** The lawyer advised her client to rehearse her testimony before the trial date.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cause to rehearse; to instruct by rehearsal.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The director rehearsed the cast incessantly in the days leading up to opening night, and as a result they were tired and cranky when it arrived.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; theater

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To contrive and carefully prepare (a story, etc.) to offer consistency.

   **Example:** The Crown argued that the accused had rehearsed her story.
