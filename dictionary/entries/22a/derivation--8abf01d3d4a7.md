   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

3. The act of tracing origin or descent; an instance thereof (for example, an etymology).

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; genealogy

   **Example:** the derivation of a word from an Indo-European root

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** grammar; human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

6. That from which a thing is derived.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

9. The process of deriving one thing from another, especially in logic; a deduction.
   1. A formal proof&#58; a sequence of statements, each of which is logically entailed by those preceding (with respect to some collection of rules of inference), the initial statements being taken as axioms.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. The process of application of the derivative operator to a function, yielding another function called the derived function of the first.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** calculus; mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. An algebraic generalization of the derivative operator (from its natural setting in the ring of real-valued functions) to a general associative algebra over a field. Formally, (given an algebra A over a field K) a K-linear endomorphism that satisfies Leibnitz's Law.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; differential algebra

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. An algebraic generalization of the derivative operator (from its natural setting in the ring of real-valued functions) to a general associative algebra over a field. Formally, (given an algebra A over a field K) a K-linear endomorphism that satisfies Leibnitz's Law.
   1. Any of several generalizations of this notion&#58; a Hasse–Schmidt derivation, a graded derivation, etc.

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable; differential algebra

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences
