### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To remove by pulling.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Pull off old blossoms so that the plant will keep flowering.

   **Example:** As soon as she got home, she pulled off her clothes.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To achieve, accomplish, succeed at (something difficult).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** to pull off a heist

   **Example:** Six pages is a lot to write in one night. Do you think she can pull it off?

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To turn off (a road onto the side of the road, or onto another road).

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** (transitive) After about a mile, we pulled off the main road onto a dirt track.

   **Example:** (intransitive) I think the car is overheating – we'd better pull off .

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To begin moving and then move away; to pull away.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** As the police approached, the car pulled off and sped away into the distance.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a vehicle
