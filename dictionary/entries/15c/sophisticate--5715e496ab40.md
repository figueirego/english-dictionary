## Verb · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /səˈfɪstɪkeɪt/ — source labels: General-American; Received-Pronunciation

### Forms

- sophisticating — participle; present
- sophisticated — participle; past
- sophisticated — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make (something) less innocent or natural; to artificialize.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** denaturalize; denature

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To make (something) more sophisticated (“complex, developed, or refined”); to develop, to refine.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** complicate; enhance; hone; improve; optimize; perfect; polish

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To make (oneself or someone) more sophisticated (“experienced in the ways of the world, that is, cosmopolitan or worldly-wise”); to cosmopolitanize.

   **Usage:** also; reflexive; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** broaden someone's horizons; deparochialize

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To alter and make impure (something) by mixing it with some foreign or inferior substance, especially with an intention to deceive; to adulterate; (generally) to corrupt or deceive (someone, their thinking, etc.).

   **Usage:** also; figuratively; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** abase; bastardize; dash; seduce; subvert; abase (obsolete); adulter (rare); adulterate; adulterize (uncommon); alloy; balderdash (archaic); corrupt; bastardize; dash (dated); dilute; embase (archaic); sophisticate (figuratively); adulterate; corrupt; debauch; demoralize (dated); detort (dated); file; immoralize; lead astray; pervert; pollute; preposterate (obsolete); seduce; sophisticate (figuratively); suborn; subvert; tamper (dated); unmoralize; vitiate; warp

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To change the meaning of (something) in a deceptive or misleading way.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** distort; pervert; skew; twist

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To apply an artificial technique to (something).

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To practise sophistry (“the (deliberate) making of arguments that seem plausible but are fallacious or misleading”).

   **Usage:** intransitive
