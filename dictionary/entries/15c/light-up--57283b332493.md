<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To illuminate, to bring light to something, to brighten.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** lighten; alight (archaic); belight; beshine (dialectal); bright; enlighten; fire; illume (archaic); illuminate; illumine; illustrate (obsolete); irradiate; light; lighten; light up; luster (obsolete); lustrate (obsolete); shed light on

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To start to emit light; to become activated as a light source.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source synonyms:** gleam; beam; beshine; gleam; glow; illuminate; illumine (rare); light up; shine

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To show an increase in activity or a brightening of mood.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** He saw Mary and his face lit up.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To ignite.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** inflame; kindle; aflame; alight; conflagrate; fire; fire up; ignite; inflame; enkindle; light; light up; set ablaze; set afire; set aflame; set alight; set fire; set on fire; torch

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To light a cigarette, pipe, etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Smoking in this building is not allowed, so I always step outside to light up.

   **Source synonyms:** spark up

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To light a cigarette, pipe, etc. for (someone).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To make happy.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** cheer up; happify; make someone's day; beatify; enrapture; begladden; blissen; bright; cheer; fetch; cheer up; cherish (obsolete); content; delight; elate; elevate (obsolete); enbliss; exhilarate; fain (obsolete); gay (dated; uncommon); gladden; gladden someone's heart (dated); gratify; happify; happy; hearten; jollify; laetificate (rare); light up; make someone's day; perk up; please; raise someone's spirits (idiomatic); rejoice; turn the frown upside down; upcheer (archaic); warm someone's heart (idiomatic); warm the cockles of someone's heart (idiomatic)

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To introduce (someone to something), to make someone aware of or interested in something; turn on.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To open fire on a target or group of targets, especially with rockets, a flamethrower, etc.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** telling me to light it up if it didn't stop approaching

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To shock (someone) with a stun gun.

   **Usage:** US; slang; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** tase

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To activate the emergency sirens on a police vehicle in order to pull someone over.

   **Usage:** US; slang; transitive

   **Example:** I guess he doesn't realize who's behind him, because the way he's darting and weaving, he's practically begging me to light him up.
