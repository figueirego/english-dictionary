### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To kill, especially as punishment for a capital crime.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** There are certain states where it is lawful to execute prisoners convicted of certain crimes.

   **Example:** According to international treaties, it is illegal to execute prisoners of war.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To carry out; to put into effect.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Your orders have been executed, sir!

   **Example:** I'll execute your orders as soon as this meeting is adjourned.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To perform.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to execute a difficult piece of music brilliantly

   **Example:** to execute a turn in ballet

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To carry out, to perform an act; to put into effect or cause to become legally binding or valid (as a contract) by so doing.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to execute a contract

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To start, launch, or run.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to execute a program

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** start; launch; run; open

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To run, usually successfully.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The program executed, but data problems were discovered.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences
