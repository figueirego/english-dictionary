### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Connects at least two alternative words, phrases, clauses, sentences, etc., each of which could make a passage true.

   **Example:** He might get cancer, or be hit by a bus, or God knows what.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An operator denoting the disjunction of two propositions or truth values. There are two forms&#58; the inclusive or and the exclusive or.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; logic; mathematics; philosophy; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to present two or more options from which a choice is to be made.

   **Example:** Do you want tea or coffee?

   **Example:** You may stay or go.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Otherwise (a consequence of the condition that the previous is false).

   **Example:** It's raining! Come inside or you'll catch a cold!

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Connects two equivalent names.

   **Example:** The country Myanmar, or Burma

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Used in between two numbers to denote a range between them.

   **Example:** A man of 18 or 20 years
