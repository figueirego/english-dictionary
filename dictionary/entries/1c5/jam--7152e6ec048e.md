### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To get something stuck, often (though not necessarily) in a confined space.

   **Example:** My foot got jammed in a gap between the rocks.

   **Example:** Her poor little baby toe got jammed in the door.

   **Example:** I jammed the top knuckle of my ring finger.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To brusquely force something into a space; to cram, to squeeze.

   **Example:** They temporarily stopped the gas tank leak by jamming a piece of taffy into the hole.

   **Example:** The rush-hour train was jammed with commuters.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To render something unable to move.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To cause congestion or blockage. Often used with "up".

   **Example:** A single accident can jam the roads for hours.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To block or confuse a radio or radar signal by transmitting a more-powerful signal on the same frequency.

   **Example:** The government jams foreign propaganda broadcasts.

   **Example:** The airstrike suffered minimal casualties because electronic-warfare aircraft were jamming the enemy air-defense radars.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To turn (fruit) into jam.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To add jam to; to cover with jam.

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To throw a pitch at or near the batter's hands.

   **Example:** Jones was jammed by the pitch.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To dunk.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; basketball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**

10. To play music (especially improvisation as a group, or an informal unrehearsed session).

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; music

<a id="r2-s11"></a>

**Source sense R2.S11**

11. To injure a finger or toe by sudden compression of the digit's tip.

   **Example:** When he tripped on the step he jammed his toe.

<a id="r2-s12"></a>

**Source sense R2.S12**

12. To attempt to score points.

   **Usage:** roller derby

   **Example:** Toughie jammed four times in the second period.

<a id="r2-s13"></a>

**Source sense R2.S13**

13. To bring (a vessel) so close to the wind that half her upper sails are laid aback.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r2-s14"></a>

**Source sense R2.S14**

14. To give up on a date or some other joint endeavour; to stand up, chicken out, jam out.

   **Usage:** Canada; informal

<a id="r2-s15"></a>

**Source sense R2.S15**

15. To be of high quality (especially for music).

   **Usage:** colloquial

   **Example:** I love this song! This song jams!
