### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To exploit (a potential weakness or vulnerability) by exacerbating.

   **Usage:** also 'play upon'

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To exploit (a double meaning, similarity in sound, etc.) for humorous or creative effect.

   **Usage:** also 'play upon'

   **Example:** This joke plays on the fact that 'thirsty' sounds similar to 'Thursday'.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To impinge upon in a recurring or nagging way.

   **Usage:** also 'play upon'

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To frequently allude to or return to as a theme.

   **Usage:** also 'play upon'

   **Example:** The pamphlet plays on the idea that all social conflict is class-based.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. to hit the ball into his own wicket, thus being out bowled

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a batsman

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To let the game continue after a foul has been committed, because the situation is advantageous to the team who would be awarded a foul.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To cause (an opposing player) to be onside rather than offside due to one's position on the field of play.

   **Usage:** transitive; sports such as soccer

   **Example:** The ref said that Smith was offside, but I think that Jones played him on.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see play, on.

   **Example:** I knew I was losing, but I played on anyways.
