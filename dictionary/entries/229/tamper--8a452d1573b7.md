## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈtæm.pə(ɹ)/ — source labels: UK

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A person or thing that tamps.
   1. A tool used to tamp something down, such as tobacco in a pipe.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A person or thing that tamps.
   1. A railway vehicle used to tamp down ballast.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

      **Source synonyms:** ballast tamper; tamping machine

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A person or thing that tamps.
   1. An envelope of neutron-reflecting material in a nuclear weapon, used to delay the expansion of the reacting material and thus produce a longer-lasting and more energetic explosion.

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To make unauthorized or improper alterations, sometimes causing deliberate damage; to meddle (with something).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** tamper detection

   **Example:** The alarm had been tampered with and didn’t go off when it should have.

   **Example:** The election monitors found that a large number of ballots had been tampered with.

   **Source synonyms:** interfere

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To try to influence someone, usually in an illegal or devious way; to try to deal (with someone).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Prosecutors argued that he would tamper with witnesses if bail was granted.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To meddle (with something) in order to corrupt or pervert it.

   **Usage:** dated
