## Verb · Record 1

### Forms

- kicks back — present; singular; third-person
- kicking back — participle; present
- kicked back — participle; past
- kicked back — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To relax; to laze.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; intransitive

   **Example:** I'm going to stay at home on Saturday and just kick back.

   **Source synonyms:** take it easy; lie back; kick it; kick up one's heels

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To pay part of the proceeds of a transaction to a person or organization that mediated it, often in a way that is covert, improper, and illegal.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal; intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** It was the local government’s practice to kick back to the police officer a portion of the fines collected from traffic tickets.

   **Example:** It was the insurer’s practice to kick back to the broker approximately 0.12% of the premium paid by the policyholder.

   **Example:** It was the company’s practice to kick back to the negotiator at the client company.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To return (something) to its original or rightful owner.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal

   **Example:** They'll kick it back to you if it turns up.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To drink (something).

   **Usage:** idiomatic; informal

   **Example:** We'll kick back some beers.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To move suddenly and forcefully in reaction to the main action (for example, when a firearm jerks backward when a bullet is fired, or when a table saw thrusts the workpiece in an unexpected direction).

   **Example:** You better watch yourself around that saw. That thing kicks back real hard occasionally, with no warning.

   **Example:** My revolver can handle .357 Magnum cartridges, but I use .38 Special, because I don't need it kicking back harder than a mule.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a weapon or tool

   **Source synonyms:** recoil
