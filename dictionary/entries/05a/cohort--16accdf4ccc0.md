### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A group of people supporting the same thing or person.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A demographic grouping of people, especially those in a defined age group, or having a common characteristic.

   **Example:** The 18–24 cohort shows a sharp increase in automobile fatalities over the proximate age groupings.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences; statistics

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any division of a Roman legion, normally of about 500 or 600 men (equalling about six centuries).

   **Usage:** Ancient-Rome; historical

   **Example:** Three cohorts of men were assigned to the region.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An accomplice; abettor; associate.

   **Example:** He was able to plea down his sentence by revealing the names of three of his cohorts, as well as the source of the information.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Any band or body of warriors.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A natural group of orders of organisms, less comprehensive than a class.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; natural-sciences; taxonomy

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A colleague.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A set of individuals in a program, especially when compared to previous sets of individuals within the same program.

   **Example:** The students in my cohort for my organic chemistry class this year are not up to snuff. Last year's cohort scored much higher averages on the mid-term.
