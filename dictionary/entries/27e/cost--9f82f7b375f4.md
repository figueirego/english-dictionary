**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To incur a charge of; to require payment of a (specified) price.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; transitive

   **Example:** This shirt cost $50, while this was cheaper at only $30.

   **Example:** It will cost you a lot of money to take a trip around the world.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause something to be lost; to cause the expenditure or relinquishment of.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Trying to rescue the man from the burning building cost them their lives.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To require to be borne or suffered; to cause.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cost (a person) a great deal of money or suffering.

   **Usage:** colloquial; transitive

   **Example:** I can give you the names, but it'll cost you.

   **Example:** That's going to cost you!

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The total cost of the new complex was an estimated $1.5 million.

   **Example:** We have to cut costs if we want to avoid bankruptcy.

   **Example:** The average cost of a new house is twice as much as it was 20 years ago.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Spending all your time working may earn you a lot of money at the cost of your health.

   **Example:** The army won the battle decisively, but at a cost of many lives.
