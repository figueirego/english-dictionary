### Pronunciation

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A control or occupancy of something for which one does not necessarily have private property rights.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Something that is owned.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The car quickly became his most prized possession.

   **Example:** I would gladly give all of my worldly possessions just to be able to do that.

   **Source synonyms:** ight (obsolete); aught; belongings; owndom; possession; property; propriety (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An ownership; a taking, a holding, keeping something as one's own.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The car is in my possession.

   **Example:** I'm in possession of the car.

   **Source synonyms:** owndom; retention; owndom; ownership; possession; proprietorship; propriety

   **Source antonyms:** absence

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A territory under the rule of another country.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Réunion is the largest of France's overseas possessions.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The condition or affliction of being possessed by a demon or other supernatural entity.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Back then, people with psychiatric disorders were sometimes thought to be victims of demonic possession.

   **Source synonyms:** enthusiasm (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. The condition of being under the control of strong emotion or madness.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A control of the ball; the opportunity to be on the offensive.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The scoreboard shows a little football symbol next to the name of the team that has possession.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A disposal of the ball during a game, i.e. a kick or a handball.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable; Australian rules football

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A syntactic relationship between two nouns or nominals that may be used to indicate ownership.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Some languages distinguish between a construction like 'my car', which shows alienable possession — the car could become someone else's — and one like 'my foot', which has inalienable possession — my foot will always be mine.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences
