### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To examine diverse documents and so on, to discover similarities and differences.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The young attorneys were set the task of collating the contract submitted by the other side with the previous copy.

   **Example:** collate data

   **Example:** collate findings

   **Example:** collate information

   **Example:** collate results

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To assemble something in a logical sequence.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To sort multiple copies of printed documents into sequences of individual page order, one sequence for each copy, especially before binding.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Collating was still necessary because they had to insert foldout sheets and index tabs into the documents.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To bestow or confer.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To admit a cleric to a benefice; to present and institute in a benefice, when the person presenting is both the patron and the ordinary; followed by to.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Christianity
