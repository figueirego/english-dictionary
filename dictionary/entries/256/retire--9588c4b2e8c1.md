## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ɹɪˈtaɪə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ɹəˈtaɪɹ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- retires — present; singular; third-person
- retiring — participle; present
- retired — participle; past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To stop working on a permanent basis, usually because of old age or illness.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Having made a large fortune, he retired.

   **Example:** He wants to retire at 55.

   **Example:** She decided to retire from her banking job due to stress.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To stop playing their sport and in competitions a sports player.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I have decided to retire from football at the end of the season after the World Cup.

   **Example:** I have decided to retire from test cricket at 45.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To withdraw; to take away.

   **Usage:** reflexive; sometimes; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To cease use or production of something.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The steamship made thousands of trips over several decades before it was retired by the shipping company.

   **Example:** When a hurricane becomes so deadly or destructive that future use would be insensitive, officials may retire the name of the hurricane.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To withdraw from circulation, or from the market; to take up and pay.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The central bank retired those notes five years ago.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To cause to retire; specifically, to designate as no longer qualified for active service; to place on the retired list.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The board retired the old major.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To voluntarily stop batting before being dismissed so that the next batsman can bat.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Jones retired in favour of Smith.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a batsman

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To make a play which results in a runner or the batter being out, either by means of a put out, fly out or strikeout. Also, when such an event ends a team's turn at bat.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Jones retired Smith 6-3.

   **Example:** Jones makes the catch for the third out, and that retires the side.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a fielder

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To go back or return; to withdraw or retreat, especially from public view; to go into privacy.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I will retire to the study.

   **Example:** to retire from the world

   **Example:** to retire from the public eye

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To retreat from action or danger; to withdraw for safety or pleasure.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** to retire from battle

   **Example:** The regiment retired from the fray after the Major was killed.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To recede; to fall or bend back.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Past the point, the shore retires into a sequence of coves.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To go to bed.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** I will retire for the night.
