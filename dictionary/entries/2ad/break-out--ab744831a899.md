## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To escape, especially forcefully or defiantly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** They broke out of prison in the middle of the night.

   **Example:** The brigade succeeded in breaking out of the pocket and reunited with friendly forces.

   **Source synonyms:** bust out

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To rescue someone or aid their escape as in sense 1.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After Big Tony was sent to prison, his friends got together to break him out.

   **Source synonyms:** bust out

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To bring out from storage, use, or present.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** Break out the bubbly and celebrate.

   **Example:** Before you immediately break out calculus, consider that there might be a more elegant way to find the answer in this case.

   **Example:** Picks and shovels had been perfectly adequate for the sporadic digging we’d had to do over the last month, but when faced with 30 cubic yards of earth to move in at once, we decided to break out the heavy equipment.

   **Source synonyms:** bust out

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To separate (something) from a bundle.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Break out the cables from the harness once they are inside the frame.

   **Source synonyms:** sunder out

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To take or force out by breaking.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to break out a pane of glass

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To begin suddenly; to emerge in a certain condition.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He broke out in sweat.

   **Example:** He broke out in song.

   **Example:** The First World War broke out in 1914.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To suddenly get pimples or a rash, especially on one's face.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** break out in hives

   **Example:** break out in a rash

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a person

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. (Of a record, film, product, or company)&#58; to achieve widespread success.

   **Usage:** intransitive; especially recording industry or movies

   **Example:** It wasn't until later that her album broke out and she hit the big time.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To remove snow from a road or sidewalk.

   **Usage:** New-England; obsolete
