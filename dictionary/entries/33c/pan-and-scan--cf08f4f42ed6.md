## Noun · Record 2

### Forms

- pan and scans — plural
- pan-and-scan — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The method or practice of adjusting widescreen film images, especially 2.35&#58;1 or 2.39&#58;1 aspect ratio, so that they can be shown within the proportions of a 1.33&#58;1 or 1.78&#58;1 aspect ratio television screen, by cropping off sides of the original widescreen image, as opposed to letterboxing.

   **Usage:** uncountable

   **Example:** Alicia hated the person who had invented pan and scan, since the heightened panning effect aggravated her nausea when watching action movies.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; film; media; television

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A movie recording employing this technology.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** In the discount bin by the door sat a pile of pan and scans, mostly comedies, that no one would touch.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; film; media; television

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To adjust widescreen film images so that they can be shown with standard television aspect ratios by cropping the original image, i.e. using pan and scan methods.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; film; media; television

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To crop out (a character or object) when performing the pan and scan process, resulting in the deletion of a character or object.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** In the full-screen version of Star Wars Episode II, only Anakin and Obi-Wan appear in the scene because Padmé was panned and scanned out.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; film; media; television
