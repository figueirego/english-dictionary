   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

2. To apply a force or forces to by stretching out.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to strain a rope; to strain the shrouds of a ship

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The gale strained the timbers of the ship.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To act upon, in any way, so as to cause change of form or volume, as when bending a beam.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To exert or struggle (to do something), especially to stretch (one's senses, faculties etc.) beyond what is normal or comfortable.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive

   **Example:** Sitting in back, I strained to hear the speaker.

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To stretch beyond its proper limit; to do violence to, in terms of intent or meaning.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to strain the law in order to convict an accused person

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To separate solid from liquid by passing through a strainer or colander.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To percolate; to be filtered.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** water straining through a sandy soil

<a id="r2-s9"></a>

**Source sense R2.S9**

9. To make uneasy or unnatural; to produce with apparent effort; to force; to constrain.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s10"></a>

**Source sense R2.S10**

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to strain a petition or invitation

<a id="r2-s11"></a>

   **Example:** Taking on more debt would strain our resources to the limit.
