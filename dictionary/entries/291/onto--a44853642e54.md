**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Arriving upon or on top of (speaking of a physical or metaphorical movement).

   **Example:** My cat just jumped onto the keyboard.

   **Example:** A new drug has just come onto the market.

   **Source antonyms:** off; off of (colloquial)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Aware of.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** The thought-police were onto my plans of world domination.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Being an onto function with a codomain of (see below).

   **Example:** The exponential function maps the set of real numbers onto the set of positive real numbers.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. Attaining each of the values in its codomain; having its image equal to its codomain.

   **Usage:** not-comparable

   **Example:** Considered as a function on the real numbers, the exponential function is not onto, as it never takes on values less than or equal to zero.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a function
