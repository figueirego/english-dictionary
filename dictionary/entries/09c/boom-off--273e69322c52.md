## Verb

### Forms

- booms off — present; singular; third-person
- booming off — participle; present
- boomed off — participle; past
- boomed off — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To resound against (a surface).

   **Usage:** literally; transitive

   **Example:** The music boomed off the walls of the venue.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To move away quickly.

   **Usage:** dated; intransitive; literally

   **Example:** The train boomed off to the west.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To boom as a result of (something) (to flourish, grow, or progress).

   **Usage:** dated; transitive

   **Example:** The economy will boom off the exploitation of offshore oil.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To explode (a door) to gain access.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Example:** The cops boomed off my door.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To mark off an area (of water, or sometimes land) using booms (a floating barrier used to obstruct navigation) or something similar.

   **Usage:** dated; transitive

   **Example:** They boomed off the oil slick.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To shoot using (something); to be fired.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang; transitive

   **Example:** He booms off his glock as he enters the room.

   **Example:** The cannons boomed off.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To blow off or brush off (somebody); to dismiss (somebody) as unimportant.

   **Usage:** dated; transitive

   **Example:** The bureaucrats can boom him off when he comes to them with ideas.
