## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈɪm.pɹɪnt/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- imprints — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An impression; the mark left behind by printing something or by pressing on something, or the figurative counterpart of such a mark.

   **Example:** Their footsteps left an imprint in the sand.

   **Example:** The packaging requires an imprint on three of its sides.

   **Example:** The day left an imprint in my mind.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The name and details of a publisher or printer, as printed in a book etc.; (metonymic) the publisher, publishing house, or printer; their brand, or one of their subbrands.

   **Example:** Each of the Big Five publishers has many imprints; for example, the imprints of Penguin Random House include Crown, DK, and Knopf Doubleday.

   **Source synonyms:** colophon; imprimatur

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A distinctive marking, symbol, or logo.

   **Example:** The shirts bore the company imprint on the right sleeve.

   **Source synonyms:** imprimatur; hallmark

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

### Forms

- imprinted — participle; past
- imprinted — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To leave a print, impression, image, etc.

   **Example:** For a fee, they can imprint the envelopes with a monogram.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To learn something indelibly at a particular stage of life, such as who one's parents are.

   **Example:** Baby birds are known for a propensity to imprint; they tend to imprint on whichever creatures they perceive to be their parents — including humans doing wildlife rescue!

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To mark a gene as being from a particular parent so that only one of the two copies of the gene is expressed.
