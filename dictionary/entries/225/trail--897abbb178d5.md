### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To follow behind (someone or something); to tail (someone or something).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The hunters trailed their prey deep into the woods.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To drag (something) behind on the ground.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** You'll get your coat all muddy if you trail it around like that.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To leave (a trail of).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He walked into the house, soaking wet, and trailed water all over the place.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To show a trailer of (a film, TV show etc.); to release or publish a preview of (a report etc.) in advance of the full publication.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** His new film was trailed on TV last night.

   **Example:** There were no surprises in this morning's much-trailed budget statement.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To hang or drag loosely behind; to move with a slow sweeping motion.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The bride's long dress trailed behind her as she walked down the aisle.

   **Example:** Her curly hair trailed along her shoulders and down her back.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To run or climb like certain plants.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To drag oneself lazily or reluctantly along.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Our parents marched to church and we trailed behind.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To be losing, to be behind in a competition.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To carry (a firearm) with the breech near the ground and the upper part inclined forward, the piece being held by the right hand near the middle.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To create a trail in.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To travel by following or creating trails.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

## Noun · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- enPR: trāl — variety unspecified in the source labels
- IPA: /tɹeɪl/ — variety unspecified in the source labels

### Forms

- trails — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The track or indication marking the route followed by something that has passed, such as the footprints of animal on land or the contrail of an airplane in the sky.

   **Example:** The trail has gone cold.

   **Example:** condensation trail

   **Example:** data trail, paper trail

   **Source synonyms:** spoor; sign

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. A route for travel over land, especially a narrow, unpaved pathway for use by hikers, horseback riders, etc.

   **Source synonyms:** dirt track; footpath; path; track

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. A route or circuit generally.

   **Example:** Politicians are on the campaign trail in preparation for this year's election.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. A trailer broadcast on television for a forthcoming film or programme.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** broadcasting; media; television

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. A walk in which all the edges are distinct.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** graph-theory; mathematics; sciences

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**
