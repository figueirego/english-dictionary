## Verb · Record 1

### Forms

- drills down — present; singular; third-person
- drilling down — participle; present
- drilled down — participle; past
- drilled down — past

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To examine something on a more detailed level.

   **Usage:** intransitive; literally

   **Example:** The issue can be drilled down to a simple yes or no.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To examine information at another level or in greater detail; especially in a database, to navigate to a more detailed level or record.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** From the employee list, you can drill down to find addresses and pay history.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences; software

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To replace data with more specific data.

   **Usage:** idiomatic

   **Example:** Clicking here lets you replace the annual sales data with monthly data, drilling down to help you track revenue.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences; software

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To practice maneuvers in order to memorize them; to drill thoroughly (to practice).

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** We've drilled down on guarding players with his skillset.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To score points; to do well in a basketball game.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** They drilled down three pointers throughout the second half.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; basketball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** get down to bedrock; get down to brass tacks

## Noun · Record 2

### Forms

- drill downs — plural

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. an internal competition used to practice marching commands, in which the last person caught improperly executing a command wins

   **Example:** We knew someone had lost when the last two people in the drill down ended up facing each other.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of marching bands
