### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To dislodge or remove (someone) from a seat, especially on horseback.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The frightened horse reared up and unseated its rider.

   **Example:** I was unseated from my bike after hitting an unexpected pothole.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To remove (someone) from an office or position, especially a political one; to dethrone.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** After having lost her seat to a Tory, she succeeded in unseating him in the next general election.

   **Example:** India has recently unseated China as the world’s most populous country.

   **Source synonyms:** depose; oust; overthrow

   **Source antonyms:** appoint; enthrone; empower; install

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cause (something) to be removed or replaced in its role; to displace, to overturn.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The Michelson-Morley experiment prompted scientists to unseat their belief in the luminiferous aether.

   **Example:** Printed newspapers are gradually being unseated by digital publications.

   **Source synonyms:** oust; supersede; supplant

   **Source antonyms:** confirm; preserve

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To upset the composure of (someone); to astound, to shock, to unsettle.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The Matrix (1999) utterly unseated audiences around the world with its mind-blowing plot twist.

   **Example:** I was somewhat unseated by the strange noises outside my window.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To come off or out of a seat.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** We replaced the screws, since the old ones unseated too easily.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; technical
