### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A possessive marker, indicating that an object belongs to the noun or noun phrase bearing the marker.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** Jane's house is bigger than Sarah's.

   **Example:** The cat bit the dog's tail. (the dog + 's)

   **Example:** The cat bit the dog with the shaggy fur's tail. (the dog with the shaggy fur + 's)

   **Example:** women's contributions to science

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. In the absence of a specified object, used to indicate “the house/place/establishment of”.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** We'll arrive at Jose and María's in time for dinner.

   **Example:** I'm going to the butcher's for a steak.

   **Example:** I bought it at Tesco's. (see s-form)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Indicates a purpose or a user.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** You need a driver's license.

   **Example:** Alex can be a girl's name.

   **Example:** That's a girl's toy. (A toy intended for use by girls.) — Homographic to&#58; That's a girl's toy. (The toy of a specific girl.)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Used to indicate a quantity of something, especially of time.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** I took three weeks' holiday.

   **Example:** The rocks lay at about a mile's distance from the shore.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Used to indicate various other kinds of relationship, such as source or origin, object of an action, subject depicted, etc.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** She got angry at my niece's trying to lie to us.

   **Example:** the doctor's help (help provided by the doctor)

   **Example:** the King's capture (event of the King being captured)

   **Example:** my father's portrait (portrait depicting my father)

   **Example:** in Dublin's fair city (in the fair city of Dublin)

   **Example:** JFK Airport is among America's busiest.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Attached to a noun or noun phrase linked to a genitive of, forming a double genitive (Compare of mine, etc.)

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme

   **Example:** a friend of Sarah's

   **Example:** Have you heard this new idea of our boss's?

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. Used to form the plurals of numerals, letters, some abbreviations and some nouns, usually because the omission of an apostrophe would make the meaning unclear or ambiguous.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme; proscribed; sometimes

   **Example:** There are four 3’s in my phone number.

   **Example:** “Banana” has three a’s and one b. (apostrophe "s" used so that the plural of “a” is not confused with the word “as”)

   **Example:** You can buy CD’s in that shop.

   **Example:** These are the do’s and don’ts. (apostrophe "s" used as “dos” may be misread)

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. Used to form plurals of foreign words, to clarify pronunciation, such as “banana’s” or “pasta’s”.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme; obsolete

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Used to form the plural of nouns that correctly take just an "s" in the plural. See greengrocer’s apostrophe.

   **Usage:** enclitic; morpheme; proscribed

   **Example:** Apple’s 50p a pound
