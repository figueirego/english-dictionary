### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To leave, especially a building.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Please go out through the back door.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To leave one's abode to go to public places, especially for recreation or entertainment.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; intransitive

   **Example:** They were going to stay in and read, but instead went out shopping.

   **Example:** On their first date they went out to dinner at a restaurant.

   **Example:** Let's go out tonight and have some fun!

   **Source antonyms:** stay in

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To be eliminated from a competition.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Our team went out in the third round.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To come to an end, by nature or by an unseen external agent.
   1. To be turned off or extinguished.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** The lights went out while I was taking a shower.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To come to an end, by nature or by an unseen external agent.
   1. To go unconscious; to pass out.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** When I hit him, he hit the floor and went out.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To come to an end, by nature or by an unseen external agent.
   1. To be drained from; to disappear from somebody.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** All the fizz has already gone out of the election campaign.

      **Example:** After diagnosis, all his confidence went out of him.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To come to an end, by nature or by an unseen external agent.
   1. To become extinct, to expire.

      **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To come to an end, by nature or by an unseen external agent.
   1. To die.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** As much as you may want to plan your funeral, you can't control when you go out.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Leon made two canastas, then went out by melding treys.

**Source sense R1.S10**

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** He thought Nehru jackets went out in the late seventies.

   **Source antonyms:** come in

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To have a romantic relationship, one that involves going out together on dates; to be a couple.

   **Usage:** intransitive
