### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Past tense of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Used to form the "anterior future", or "future in the past", indicating a futurity relative to a past time.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

      **Example:** On my first day at University, I met the woman who would become my wife.

      **Example:** Hi! I thought I'd come over and introduce myself. My name’s Chema.

      **Example:** I'm really flattered you would call your daughter after me.

      **Example:** They thought anything Elvis did would sell, but that was not true.

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Past tense of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Used to, did repeatedly, habitually; indicates an action that happened several times in the past (cannot describe continuous states, as in I used to live in London)

      **Usage:** auxiliary

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

**Source sense R1.S6**

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A modal verb, the subjunctive of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Used as the auxiliary of the simple conditional modality, indicating a state or action that is conditional on another.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

      **Example:** If I won the lottery, I would give half the money to charity.

      **Example:** I'd never do anything that went against my conscience.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. A modal verb, the subjunctive of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Without explicit condition, or with loose or vague implied condition, indicating a hypothetical or imagined state or action.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

      **Example:** I would love to come and visit.

      **Example:** Look at that yummy cake! I would eat that all up!

      **Example:** Most other people would do it differently, helping anybody who was in trouble, whether they knew them or not.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A modal verb, the subjunctive of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Suggesting conditionality or potentiality in order to express a sense of politeness, tentativeness, indirectness, hesitancy, uncertainty, etc.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

      **Example:** I would ask you all to sit down.

      **Example:** I would imagine that they have already left.

      **Example:** I would say/think we would/might do better to catch the earlier flight.

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. A modal verb, the subjunctive of will; usually followed by a bare infinitive.
   1. Used to express what the speaker would do in another person's situation, as a means of giving a suggestion or recommendation.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

   1. Used to express the speaker's belief or assumption.

      **Usage:** auxiliary

      **Example:** She looked as if she would be sick.

      **Example:** He's very security-conscious, so he would have remembered to lock the door.

      **Example:** They would be arriving in London round about now.

   1. Could naturally be expected to (given the situation, the tendencies of someone's character etc.).

   1. Used interrogatively to express a polite request; are (you) willing to …?
