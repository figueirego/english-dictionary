### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To record the departure or withdrawal of someone or something (such as guests, employees, books, etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He checked his favorite mystery out for the twenty-third time.

   **Example:** The desk clerk checked out the family that had been staying in room 322.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To record one's departure from a workplace, hotel, restaurant, etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Be sure to check out of the hotel before noon.

   **Example:** I'm done shopping, so I'll go check out now.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To examine, inspect, look at closely, ogle; to investigate; to gather information so as to make a decision.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He was hanging out at the beach, checking out the young women in bikinis.

   **Example:** He checked out the rumor, and managed to verify that it was true.

   **Example:** Check it out! Best prices in town.

   **Example:** Check this out! They just arrested the Mayor!

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To obtain source code (or other material) from a source control repository so that one can modify it (and often later check in the modified version).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; programming; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To become uninterested in an activity and cease to participate in more than a perfunctory manner; to become uncooperative.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To become catatonic or otherwise nonresponsive.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To leave in a hurry.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To die.

   **Usage:** broadly; euphemistic; intransitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To prove (after an investigation) to be the case, or to be in order.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The first two leads check out; I'll assume the third one is also valid.

   **Example:** Their stories checked out.

   **Source synonyms:** check; track; figure; make sense; add up

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To visit the oche for the last time and clear one's remaining points to win the game.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** darts; games
