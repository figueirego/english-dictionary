### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To board or mount (something), especially a vehicle.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** Please get on the bus as quickly as possible.

   **Example:** She has no trouble getting off a bus but has difficulty getting on.

   **Example:** I had to get on a chair to change the light bulb.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cope or manage (over time); to handle a situation; to perform or make progress.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** John is really getting on at work.

   **Example:** I hear John has a new job. How's he getting on?

   **Example:** She's getting on very well at school.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To progress (with); to move forward.

   **Usage:** intransitive; often

   **Example:** It's time to get on with improving quality.

   **Example:** We've wasted enough time now. Can we get on, please?

   **Source context (verbatim):** with "with"

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To progress (with); to move forward.
   1. To move forward so as to approach a deadline or become late in the day.

      **Usage:** intransitive; often

      **Example:** Time is getting on. We need to hurry up or we'll miss our flight.

      **Example:** The hour is getting on. It'll be dark soon.

      **Source context (verbatim):** of time; with "with"

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To approach (a value, level, point in time, etc.) (see also getting on).

   **Usage:** intransitive; usually; usually in continuous tenses

   **Example:** The cost was getting on for a thousand pounds.

   **Example:** She must be getting on for 65 years of age.

   **Example:** There were getting on for 40 people last night.

   **Example:** It was getting on towards midnight before I went to bed.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with "for" or "towards"

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To become old.

   **Usage:** intransitive; usually in continuous tenses

   **Example:** My parents are visibly getting on (in years) these days.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To have a good relationship; to get along.

   **Usage:** UK; intransitive; often

   **Example:** John doesn't get on with Jack. They're always arguing.

   **Example:** I wish you and I could learn to get on.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with 'with'

**Source sense R1.S8**
