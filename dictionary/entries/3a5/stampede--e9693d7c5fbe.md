## Noun · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /stæmˈpiːd/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /stæmˈpid/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- stampedes — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A wild, headlong running away or scamper of a number of animals, usually caused by fright.

   **Source synonyms:** crush; flight; jam; rush; stampedo; trampling

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A situation in which many people in a crowd are trying to move in the same direction at the same time, especially in consequence of a panic.

   **Usage:** broadly

   **Example:** The annual Muslim Hajj in Mecca, Saudi Arabia, which is attended by millions of pilgrims, has increasingly suffered from stampedes.

   **Source synonyms:** crush; rush

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An event at which cowboy skills are displayed; a rodeo.

   **Usage:** Canada; US; broadly

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A sudden unconcerted acting together of a number of persons due to, or as if due to, some common impulse.

   **Usage:** figuratively

   **Example:** a stampede toward US bonds in the credit markets

### Pronunciation

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To cause (a drove or herd of animals) to run away or scamper in a wild, headlong manner, usually due to fright.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause animals (owned by a person) to run away or scamper in this manner.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source synonyms:** stampedo

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To cause (people in a crowd) to move in the same direction at the same time, especially due to panic.

   **Usage:** broadly; transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To cause (an individual) to act hastily or rashly.

   **Usage:** broadly; transitive

   **Example:** I was stampeded into buying an unnecessary insurance against earthquakes, fires, and flooding.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To cause (people) to act in a sudden unconcerted manner due to, or as if due to, some common impulse.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive
