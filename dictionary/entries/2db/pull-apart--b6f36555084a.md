### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see pull, apart.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To become separated as a result of pulling.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** A frayed rope will pull apart under tension.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To separate (people or animals that are fighting).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The drunken brawlers had to be pulled apart.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To disassemble, dismantle, take apart (a machine or other mechanical device).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** When his watch stopped working, he pulled it apart to work out what was wrong with it.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To investigate rigorously the basis of (an idea or theory).

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** Starting with a couple of inconsistencies, the detective began to pull apart his alibi.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To criticise harshly and in detail.

   **Usage:** figuratively; transitive

   **Example:** The professor pulled her essay apart in front of the entire class.
