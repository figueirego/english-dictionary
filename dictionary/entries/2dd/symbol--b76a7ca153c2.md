### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A character or glyph representing an idea, concept or object.

   **Example:** "$" is the symbol for dollars in the US and some other countries.

   **Example:** Chinese people use word symbols for writing.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A thing considered the embodiment or cardinal exemplar of a concept, theme, or other thing.

   **Example:** The lion is the symbol of courage; the lamb is the symbol of meekness or patience.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A type of noun whereby the form refers to the same entity independently of the context; a symbol arbitrarily denotes a referent. See also icon and index.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; linguistics; sciences

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A summary of a dogmatic statement of faith.

   **Example:** The Apostles, Nicene Creed and the confessional books of Protestantism, such as the Augsburg Confession of Lutheranism are considered symbols.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The numerical expression which defines a plane's position relative to the assumed axes.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** chemistry; crystallography; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. That which is thrown into a common fund; hence, an appointed or accustomed duty.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Share; allotment.

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An internal identifier used by a debugger to relate parts of the compiled program to the corresponding names in the source code.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; programming; sciences

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A signalling event on a communications channel; a signal that cannot be further divided into meaningful information.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** communications; electrical-engineering; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; telecommunications
