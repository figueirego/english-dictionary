## Noun · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈsɛnsə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈsɛnsɚ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- censors — plural
- censour — alternative

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. One of the two magistrates who originally administered the census of citizens, and by Classical times (between the 8th century B.C.E. and the 6th century C.E.) was a high judge of public behaviour and morality.

   **Usage:** Ancient-Rome; historical

   **Example:** The Ancient Roman censors were part of the cursus honorum, a series of public offices held during a political career, like consuls and praetors.

   **Source synonyms:** censorian; censurer (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A high-ranking official who was responsible for the supervision of subordinate government officials.

   **Usage:** Ancient-China; historical

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. An official responsible for the removal or suppression of objectionable material (for example, if obscene or likely to incite violence) or sensitive content in books, films, correspondence, and other media.

   **Example:** The headmaster was an even stricter censor of his boarding pupils’ correspondence than the enemy censors had been of his own when the country was occupied.

   **Source synonyms:** censurer (obsolete)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A college or university official whose duties vary depending on the institution.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** education

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. One who censures or condemns.

   **Usage:** obsolete

   **Source synonyms:** censurer

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. An algorithm that approves or rejects something on grounds of taste or morality etc.

   **Example:** I tried using a dirty word as my user name for the online game, but the censor rejected it.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

## Verb · Etymology 1 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /ˈsɛnsə/ — source labels: Received-Pronunciation
- IPA: /ˈsɛnsɚ/ — source labels: General-American

### Forms

- censors — present; singular; third-person
- censoring — participle; present
- censored — participle; past
- censored — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To review for, and if necessary to remove or suppress, content from books, films, correspondence, and other media which is regarded as objectionable (for example, obscene, likely to incite violence, or sensitive).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The people responsible for censoring films have seen some startling things in their time.

   **Example:** Occupying powers typically censor anything reeking of resistance

   **Source synonyms:** bowdlerize; expurgate; expunge; redact

   **Source antonyms:** decensor

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To partially obscure an observation.

   **Usage:** transitive; chiefly passive voice

   **Example:** Early dropout is one cause of right-censoring.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences; statistics
