<a id="r1-s2"></a>

## Verb · Etymology 2 · Record 2

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /spɹaʊt/ — source labels: US
- IPA: /spɹʌʊt/ — source labels: Canada

### Forms

- sprouts — present; singular; third-person
- sprouting — participle; present
- sprouted — participle; past
- sprouted — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To grow from seed; to germinate.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The crocuses should be sprouting after 2 months, provided they're well tended.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** agriculture; business; horticulture; lifestyle

   **Source synonyms:** stock

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause to grow from a seed.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I sprouted beans and radishes and put them in my salad.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To deprive of sprouts.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to sprout potatoes

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To emerge from the ground as sprouts.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To emerge haphazardly from a surface.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** Whiskers sprouted from the old man's chin.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To emerge or appear haphazardly.

   **Usage:** figuratively; intransitive

   **Example:** A lot of coffee shops have sprouted up in this neighbourhood since the block of flats was put up.
