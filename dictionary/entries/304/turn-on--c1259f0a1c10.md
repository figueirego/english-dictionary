### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To set a flow of fluid or gas running by rotating a tap or valve.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Turn on the tap/faucet.

   **Source antonyms:** turn off

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To power up, to put into operation, to start, to activate (an appliance, light, mechanism, functionality etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Please turn the lights on so I can see what I'm reading.

   **Example:** Please turn on automatic updates.

   **Example:** He'll turn on the charm if he thinks he's losing the sale.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To start operating; to power up, to become on.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** My computer won't turn on.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a device

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To introduce (someone to something), and especially to fill them with enthusiasm (about it); to intoxicate, give pleasure to ( + to an object of interest or excitement).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Attractive packaging can turn buyers on to a product.

   **Example:** Attractive showroom models can turn buyers on.

   **Example:** Hearing that song turned me on to jazz fusion.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To sexually arouse.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** John's a maid fetishist. Maid outfits really turn him on.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To take drugs.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To cause to take up drugs, especially hallucinogens.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To aim at.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** He turned the searchlight on the passing planes.

   **Example:** Cornered by the authorities, the shooter turned the gun on himself.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To rebel against; to suddenly attack.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Suddenly all his friends turned on him.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To cause (someone) to rebel against or suddenly attack (someone else).

   **Usage:** ditransitive

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To depend upon; to pivot around, to have as a central subject.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** The argument turned on the question of whether or not jobs would be lost.

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To lower, blocking the way and signalling that a train may not proceed.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a railroad semaphore

   **Source antonyms:** turn off

### Related words

**Source synonyms:** turn against

**Source antonyms:** shut off; turn off
