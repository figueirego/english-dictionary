### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To cut in; to furrow; to make trenches in or upon.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; literally

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cut in; to furrow; to make trenches in or upon.
   1. To dig or excavate a trench; to trench.

      **Usage:** ambitransitive; literally

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** archaeology; business; construction; history; human-sciences; manufacturing; sciences

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To cut in; to furrow; to make trenches in or upon.
   1. To surround or provide with a trench, especially for defense; to dig in.

      **Usage:** ambitransitive; literally

      **Example:** The army entrenched its camp, or entrenched itself.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become completely absorbed in and fully accept one's beliefs, even in the face of evidence against it and refusing to be reasoned with.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; figuratively

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To establish a substantial position in business, politics, etc.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; figuratively

   **Example:** Senator Cornpone was able to entrench by spending millions on each campaign.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To invade; to encroach; to infringe or trespass; to enter on, and take possession of, that which belongs to another; usually followed by on or upon.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; figuratively
