### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To announce or record one's own arrival at a hotel, airport etc.

   **Example:** We got to the hotel last night and checked in at the front desk.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To verify a person's information and record their arrival, such as at a hotel, airport, etc.

   **Example:** The clerk checked us in at the front desk.

   **Source antonyms:** check out

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To contact another person in order to keep the other person informed of one's situation.

   **Example:** Kelly has to check in with her parole officer today.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To return (a book to a library, source code to a repository, etc.).

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To visit in order to see how someone is doing.

   **Example:** I just wanted to check in after your accident. How are you recovering?

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To enter solitary confinement at one's own request for protection from other prisoners.

   **Usage:** intransitive; slang

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To send somebody to solitary confinement; to make somebody be admitted to solitary confinement.

   **Usage:** slang; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** send up top

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To integrate new revisions into the public or master version of a file in a version control system.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences; software

   **Source synonyms:** commit

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. to tap on, to touch in, to tap in (to use a smartcard to start a public transport journey)

   **Usage:** Netherlands; intransitive; non-native speakers' English
