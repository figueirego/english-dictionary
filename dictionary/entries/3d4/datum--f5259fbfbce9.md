**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Something known or assumed as fact, and is made the basis of reasoning or inference which an intellectual system of any sort (such as knowledge or theoretical framework) is constructed.

   **Example:** a datum of experience

   **Example:** given this datum, it follows that

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Singular of data&#58; A single recorded phenomenon, especially obtained by scientific work.

   **Usage:** form-of; singular

   **Source synonyms:** data point

   **Form of:** [data](../0e9/data--3a6eb0790f39.md)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A point, line, plane, or surface with reference to which positions (such as elevations) are measured or indicated. (Examples include a permanent benchmark in leveling or mean sea level in a topographical survey).

   **Example:** A geodetic datum is sometimes also called a benchmark.

   **Example:** GD&amp;T tells us that the center of a bolt circle is in fact defined by the locations of the holes themselves, as each is a datum.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; cartography; engineering; geography; manufacturing; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; surveying

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A floating reference point, or SLDMB, used to evaluate surface currents in a body of water, and often employed by coastal search and rescue.
