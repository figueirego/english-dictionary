   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Usage:** countable; uncountable

      **Example:** the Canon of Polykleitos

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A group of literary works that are generally accepted as representing a field.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** the entire Shakespearean canon

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A eucharistic prayer, particularly the Roman Canon.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A religious law or body of law decreed by the church.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** We must proceed according to canon law.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. A catalogue of saints acknowledged and canonized in the Roman Catholic Church.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. In monasteries, a book containing the rules of a religious order.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. A piece of music in which the same melody is played by different voices, but beginning at different times; a round.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Pachelbel’s Canon has become very popular.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

11. The officially recognized continuity, events, characters, relationships, etc. of a work of fiction.
