### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To move on the feet by alternately setting each foot (or pair or group of feet&#58; in the case of some non-human animals with four or more feet) forward, with at least one foot on the ground at all times. Compare run.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** To walk briskly for an hour every day is to keep fit.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To "walk free", i.e. to win, or avoid, a criminal court case, particularly when actually guilty.

   **Usage:** colloquial; intransitive

   **Example:** If you can’t present a better case, that robber is going to walk.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Of an object, to go missing or be stolen.

   **Usage:** colloquial; euphemistic; intransitive

   **Example:** If you leave your wallet lying around, it’s going to walk.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To walk off the field, as if given out, after the fielding side appeals and before the umpire has ruled; done as a matter of sportsmanship when the batsman believes he is out.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; cricket; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a batsman

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To travel (a distance) by walking.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I walk two miles to school every day.

   **Example:** The museum’s not far from here – you can walk it.

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To take for a walk or accompany on a walk.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** ball-games; baseball; games; hobbies; lifestyle; sports

   **Usage:** intransitive
