## Verb

### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make (something) last for more time than is necessary; to prolong; to extend.

   **Example:** It seems the boss tries to draw out these meetings for as long as possible just to punish us.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To physically extract, as blood from a vein.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To extract, to bring out, as concealed information; to elicit; to educe.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To use means to entice or force (an animal) from its hole or similar hiding place.

   **Example:** They used raw meat to draw the beast out of the cave.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To cause (a shy person) to be more open or talkative.

   **Usage:** broadly

   **Example:** She's really a neat person if you just take the time to draw her out.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To redraw an electoral district in order to make it unlikely a particular candidate or party will win it.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; politics

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To improve a losing hand to a winning hand by receiving additional cards.

   **Example:** Jill had a flush on the turn, but Jimmy drew out by completing a full house on the river.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** card-games; poker

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To leave the place (of an army), etc.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete
