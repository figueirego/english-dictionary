### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To allow to, not to prevent (+ infinitive, but usually without to).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After he knocked for hours, I decided to let him come in.

   **Example:** I never let myself worry about the future.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To allow to be or do without interference; to not disturb or meddle with; to leave alone.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Let me be!

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To allow the release of (a fluid).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The physicians let about a pint of his blood, but to no avail.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To allow possession of (a property etc.) in exchange for rent.

   **Usage:** UK; transitive

   **Example:** I decided to let the farmhouse to a couple while I was working abroad.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To give, grant, or assign, as a work, privilege, or contract; often with out.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** to let the building of a bridge; to let out the lathing and the plastering

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Used to introduce a first or third person imperative verb construction.

   **Usage:** auxiliary; transitive

   **Example:** Let's put on a show!

   **Example:** Let us have a moment of silence.

   **Example:** Let me just give you the phone number.

   **Example:** Let P be the point where AB and OX intersect.

   **Example:** "Let there be no doubt&#58; I saw your boyfriend with another girl" "Let him, see if I care!".

   **Example:** Let the show go on!

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To cause (+ bare infinitive).

   **Usage:** transitive; obsolete except with know or be

   **Example:** Can you let me know what time you'll be arriving?
