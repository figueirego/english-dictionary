### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A force compounded by means of a lever rotating around a pivot.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** A crowbar uses leverage to pry nails out of wood.

   **Source synonyms:** mechanical advantage

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any influence which is compounded or used to gain an advantage.

   **Usage:** broadly; uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Try using competitors’ prices for leverage in the negotiation.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The use of borrowed funds with a contractually determined return to increase the ability to invest and earn an expected higher return, but usually at high risk.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Leverage is great until something goes wrong with your investments and you still have to pay your debts.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The debt-to-equity ratio.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

   **Source synonyms:** gearing

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. The ability to earn very high returns when operating at high-capacity utilization of a facility.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Their variable-cost-reducing investments have dramatically increased their leverage.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business

   **Source synonyms:** operating leverage

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually; regression analytics
