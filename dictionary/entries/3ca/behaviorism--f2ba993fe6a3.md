### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An approach focusing on observable behavior which, generally assuming that behavior is determined by the environment and denying any independent significance for mind, largely ignores any pathophysiological processes which may, or may not, underlie subjective, behavioral phenomena.

   **Usage:** US; countable; uncountable

   **Example:** Behaviorism is considered by most philosophers of medicine, to be an expression of the weaker, minimalist interpretation of the 'medical model' of clinical psychology because it focuses the study and classification of mental disorders upon a phenomenological, rather than a pathophysiological, approach to the subject.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; psychology; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** behavioral psychology

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The theory that a mental state is identical to the disposition or tendency to behave in accordance with the physical causes; statements about the mind are reducible to statements of physical behavior.

   **Usage:** US; uncountable

   **Example:** Supporters of behaviorism claim that to be "in pain" is nothing more than the tendency to wince, yelp, scream "ouch!" and so on.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** human-sciences; philosophy; sciences

   **Source synonyms:** logical behaviorism; philosophical behaviorism; analytical behaviorism; analytic behaviorism (US); analytic behaviourism (UK); analytical behaviorism (US); analytical behaviourism (UK); behaviorism (US); behaviourism (UK); logical behaviourism (UK); philosophical behaviorism (US); philosophical behaviourism (UK)
