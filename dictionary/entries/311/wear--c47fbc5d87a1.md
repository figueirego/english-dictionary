### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To have on&#58;
   1. To carry or have equipped on or about one's body, as an item of clothing, equipment, decoration, perfume, etc.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** He's wearing some nice pants today.

      **Example:** She wore her medals with pride.

      **Example:** Please wear your seatbelt.

      **Example:** Can you wear makeup and sunscreen at the same time?

      **Example:** He was wearing his lunch after tripping and falling into the buffet.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To have on&#58;
   1. To have or carry on one's person habitually, consistently; or, to maintain in a particular fashion or manner.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** He wears eyeglasses.

      **Example:** She wears her hair in braids.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To have on&#58;
   1. To bear or display in one's aspect or appearance.

      **Usage:** transitive

      **Example:** She wore a smile all day.

      **Example:** He walked out of the courtroom wearing an air of satisfaction.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To erode&#58;
   1. To eat away at, erode, diminish, or consume gradually; to cause a gradual deterioration in; to produce (some change) through attrition, exposure, or constant use.

      **Example:** You're going to wear a hole in the bottom of those shoes.

      **Example:** The water has slowly worn a channel into these rocks.

      **Example:** Long illness had worn the bloom from her cheeks.

      **Example:** Exile had worn the man to a shadow.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To erode&#58;
   1. To undergo gradual deterioration; become impaired; be reduced or consumed gradually due to any continued process, activity, or use.

      **Usage:** copulative; intransitive

      **Example:** The tiles were wearing thin due to years of children's feet.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To erode&#58;
   1. To exhaust, fatigue, expend, or weary.

      **Example:** His neverending criticism has finally worn my patience.

      **Example:** Toil and care soon wear the spirit.

      **Example:** Our physical advantage allowed us to wear the other team out and win.

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To erode&#58;
   1. (in the phrase "wearing on (someone)") To cause annoyance, irritation, fatigue, or weariness near the point of an exhaustion of patience.

      **Usage:** colloquial; intransitive

      **Example:** Her high pitched voice is really wearing on me lately.

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To endure&#58;
   1. To overcome one's reluctance and endure a (previously specified) situation.

      **Usage:** colloquial

      **Example:** I know you don't like working with him, but you'll just have to wear it.

      **Source context (verbatim):** with "it"

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To endure&#58;
   1. To last or remain durable under hard use or over time; to retain usefulness, value, or desirable qualities under any continued strain or long period of time; sometimes said of a person, regarding the quality of being easy or difficult to tolerate.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** Don't worry, this fabric will wear. These pants will last you for years.

      **Example:** This color wears so well. I must have washed this sweater a thousand times.

      **Example:** I have to say, our friendship has worn pretty well.

      **Example:** It's hard to get to know him, but he wears well.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To endure&#58;
   1. To pass slowly, gradually or tediously.

      **Usage:** intransitive

      **Example:** wear on, wear away.

      **Example:** As the years wore on, we seemed to have less and less in common.

      **Source context (verbatim):** of time
