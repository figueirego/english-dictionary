### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To affect in a sudden, unprecedented or surprising manner; to overwhelm a person's ordinarily contrary impulse.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I apologise for my behaviour last night. I don't know what came over me.

   **Source synonyms:** overtake

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To seem; to come to express a feeling or state.

   **Usage:** informal

   **Example:** That's why he's come over strange.

   **Source synonyms:** come across; come off

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To change one's position or location, especially to someone else's home or to an opposing side in competition or conflict.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** come over for coffee and cake

   **Example:** You'll soon come over to my way of thinking&#58; welcome to the dark side.

   **Example:** He'll come over if we guarantee him a seat on the board.

   **Source synonyms:** come by; drop by; stop by; come across

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To deceive or get the better of; overreach.

   **Usage:** dated; slang; transitive
