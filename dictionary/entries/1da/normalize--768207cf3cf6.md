### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To make normal, to bring into alignment with an established standard.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** There is little hope that the two countries will normalize relations; their governments seem to hate each other and would just as soon stay on bad terms.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To consider normal, to treat as standard in the face of older norms.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To format in a standardized manner, to make consistent.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** We'll need to normalize these statements before we can compare them.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To become normal; to return to a normal state.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To reduce the variations by excluding irrelevant aspects.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** After we properly normalize the measurements with respect to age, gender, geography and economic considerations, there remains little evidence of a difference between the two groups.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences; statistics

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To return a set of points (switches) to the normal position.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

   **Source antonyms:** reverse

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To return to the normal position from the reverse position.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** rail-transport; railways; transport

   **Source context (verbatim):** of points

   **Source antonyms:** reverse

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To subject to normalization; to eliminate redundancy in (a model for storing data).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; databases; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To anneal (steel) for the purpose of decreasing brittleness and increasing ductility.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** engineering; metallurgy; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To divide a vector by its magnitude to produce a unit vector.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To constrain a number's absolute value to be 1 at maximum. More generally, to constrain the magnitude of a mathematical quantity to be 1 at maximum.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** mathematics; sciences
