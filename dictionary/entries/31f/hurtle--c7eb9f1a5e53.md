### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To propel or throw (something) hard or violently; to fling, to hurl.

   **Usage:** archaic; literary; poetic; transitive

   **Example:** He hurtled the wad of paper angrily at the trash can and missed by a mile.

   **Source synonyms:** bung (Australia; UK); cast; chuck; chunk (Southern-US; slang); cook (UK; dialectal; obsolete); dash; dump; feck; fling; jerk (obsolete); heave; hield; hoy (Australia; Northumbria); huck; hurl; hurtle (archaic; literary); launch; lob; peck (regional); peg (slang); pick (obsolete); pitch; precipitate; project; quoit; shy; slight; skew (Yorkshire); sling; thrill (obsolete); throw; toss; traject; warp (obsolete; rare); whang (Australia; UK); whip; whop; wing; yeet (slang)

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To cause (someone or something) to collide with or hit another person or thing; or (two people or things) to collide with or hit each other.

   **Usage:** archaic; literary; poetic; transitive

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To attack or criticize (someone) verbally or in writing.

   **Usage:** archaic; figuratively; literary; poetic; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To move rapidly, violently, or without control, especially in a noisy manner.

   **Usage:** intransitive; literary; poetic

   **Example:** The car hurtled down the hill at 90 miles per hour.

   **Example:** Pieces of broken glass hurtled through the air.

   **Source synonyms:** hightail; move quickly; skedaddle

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Of a person or thing&#58; to collide with or hit another person or thing, especially with force or violence; also, of two people or things&#58; to collide together; to clash.

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive; literary; poetic

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To make a sound of things clashing or colliding together; to clatter, to rattle; hence, to move with such a sound.

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive; literary; poetic

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. Of two people, etc.&#58; to meet in a shocking or violent encounter; to clash; to jostle.

   **Usage:** archaic; figuratively; intransitive; literary; poetic
