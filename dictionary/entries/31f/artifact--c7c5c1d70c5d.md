### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An object made or shaped by human hand or labor.

   **Usage:** US

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. An object made or shaped by some agent or intelligence, not necessarily of direct human origin.

   **Usage:** US

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something viewed as a product of human agency or conception rather than an inherent element.

   **Usage:** US

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A finding or structure in an experiment or investigation that is not a true feature of the object under observation, but is a result of external action, the test arrangement, or an experimental error.

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** The spot on his lung turned out to be an artifact of the X-ray process.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A finding or structure in an experiment or investigation that is not a true feature of the object under observation, but is a result of external action, the test arrangement, or an experimental error.
   1. An appearance or structure in protoplasm due to death, the method of preparation of specimens, or the use of reagents, and not present during life.

      **Usage:** US

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** biology; natural-sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A finding or structure in an experiment or investigation that is not a true feature of the object under observation, but is a result of external action, the test arrangement, or an experimental error.
   1. A perceptible distortion that appears in an audio or video file or an image as a result of applying a lossy compression or other inexact processing algorithm or of physical interference in an acquisition process.

      **Usage:** US

      **Example:** This JPEG image has been so highly compressed that it has unsightly artifacts, making it unsuitable for the cover of our magazine.

      **Example:** The opacity in his chest radiograph turned out to be an artifact due to the film.

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; medicine; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; radiology; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. An object, such as a tool, ornament, or weapon of archaeological or historical interest, especially such an object found at an archaeological excavation.

   **Usage:** US

   **Example:** The dig produced many Roman artifacts.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** archaeology; history; human-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. An object, such as a tool, ornament, or weapon of archaeological or historical interest, especially such an object found at an archaeological excavation.
   1. A powerful weapon, armor or another object, often with magical properties.

      **Usage:** US

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** archaeology; games; gaming; history; human-sciences; sciences
