### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The practice of witches; magic, sorcery, or the use of supernatural powers to influence or predict events.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** Wiccans believe in a modernised form of witchcraft.

   **Source synonyms:** witchdom; witchery; witchwork; bewitchery; black arts; charmwork; conjuration; conjuring; craft (obsolete); dark arts; diablerie; druidcraft; dweomercraft (rare); dwimmer (neologism; obsolete); dwimmercraft; enchantment; goety (obsolete); gypsycraft; hexcraft; hexerei; jugglery (archaic); magic; mojo; sorcery; sortilege; spellcasting; spellcraft; spellwork; thaumaturgy; wandwork; warlockry; whistness (UK; dialectal); witchdom; witchery; witchcraft; witchwork; wizardry; wizardcraft (rare); wonderworking

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Black magic; the use of witchcraft for evil means.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Example:** The Bible warns against the use of witchcraft and sorcery.

   **Source synonyms:** dark magic; goety; sorcery; black magic; cacomagic (rare); dark magic; goety (obsolete); sorcery; witchcraft

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Something, such as an advanced technology, that seems almost magical.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually

   **Source synonyms:** wizardry

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. An irresistible or seemingly magical influence.

   **Usage:** uncountable; usually
