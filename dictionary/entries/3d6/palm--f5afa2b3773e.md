<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. The inner and somewhat concave part of the human hand or other primate hand that extends from the wrist to the bases of the fingers.

   **Example:** On the boys' palms were handfuls of sand.

   **Example:** The chiromancer examined my palms first, and eventually had my hands photographed.

   **Source synonyms:** loof

   **Source antonyms:** hardel; backhand

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. The corresponding part of the forefoot of a nonprimate mammal.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. Synonym of hand, any of various units of length notionally derived from the breadth of the palm, formalized in England as 4 inches and now chiefly used for the height of horses

   **Usage:** historical

   **Usage:** historical

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To hold or conceal something in the palm of the hand, e.g., for an act of sleight of hand or to steal something.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To hold something without bending the fingers significantly.

   **Example:** John palmed the ball.

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To move something with the palm of the hand.

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To use one's palm as identification to get through a door or security checkpoint.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

   **Example:** I palmed in to work.

   **Example:** She palmed the door as she talked to the security guard.

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To bribe.

   **Usage:** UK; obsolete; slang; transitive
