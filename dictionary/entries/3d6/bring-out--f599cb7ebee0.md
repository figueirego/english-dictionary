**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To elicit, evoke, or emphasize (a particular quality).

   **Example:** The herbs really bring out the full flavour of the lamb.

   **Example:** She brings out the best in him.

   **Example:** That outfit brings out the red in your hair.

   **Source synonyms:** encompass

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To elicit, evoke, or emphasize (a particular quality).
   1. To cause a visible symptom, such as spots or a rash, in (a person).

      **Usage:** UK

      **Example:** Eating strawberries always brings me out in a rash.

      **Source synonyms:** encompass

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To elicit, evoke, or emphasize (a particular quality).
   1. To make (a shy person) more confident.

      **Usage:** UK

      **Example:** His new job has noticeably brought him out.

      **Source synonyms:** encompass

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To introduce (someone) to a new lifestyle.
   1. To introduce (a young woman) formally into society.

      **Usage:** historical; transitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To introduce (someone) to a new lifestyle.
   1. To introduce (a person) to gay life and traditions.

      **Usage:** transitive; LGBTQ slang

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To place (something new for public sale) on the market; roll out.

   **Usage:** UK

   **Example:** Acme sweets have just brought out a tasty new chocolate bar.
