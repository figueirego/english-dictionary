### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To sell all of a supply or stock of something.

   **Example:** They sold out of concert posters yesterday and won't get another shipment until next week.

   **Source synonyms:** run out; stock out

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To abandon or betray one's principles to seek profit or other personal advantage; to abandon or betray one's supporters through this change.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** It used to be a pleasant little community, until the leaders sold out to the developers.

   **Example:** He used to be a voice for the underdog until he sold out.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To betray (a person), usually a close friend or family member, for personal gain.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I'll kill him if I see him again; he sold me out.

   **Source synonyms:** [sell](../303/sell--c0f21d451463.md); sell the pass; sell down the river; assfuck (Canada; US; vulgar); betray; burn (slang); cop out (idiomatic; slang); cross out (slang); cross up (Canada; US); double-cross; do someone dirty; do the dirty on (Australia; UK; idiomatic); false (obsolete); go back on; knife; let down (idiomatic); play someone false; quisle (rare); renegade (dated); [sell](../303/sell--c0f21d451463.md); sell the pass (Ireland; idiomatic); sell down the river (idiomatic); sell down (idiomatic); sell out; stab in the back; swike (dialectal; obsolete); traitorize; turncoat

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To sell one's business (with a connotative emphasis on entirety and finality).

   **Example:** The owners long resisted buyout offers, but when they were ready to retire, they finally sold out to a competitor.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To sell one's business (with a connotative emphasis on entirety and finality).
   1. To commit or abandon oneself entirely (to a cause, a team, etc.).

      **Usage:** figuratively

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To sell one's commission.

   **Usage:** historical

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** government; military; politics; war

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To liquidate, sell off, or divest oneself of financial investments, stocks, or government funds.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; dated

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; finance

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To hurry out.

   **Usage:** archaic; intransitive
