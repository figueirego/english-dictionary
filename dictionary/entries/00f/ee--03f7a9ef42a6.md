## Suffix · Etymology 1 · Record 1

### Forms

- -ees — plural

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Added to transitive verbs to form words meaning a person or thing that is the object of that verb (i.e., to whom or to which an action is done).

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** examine + -ee → examinee

   **Example:** interview + -ee → interviewee

   **Example:** train + -ee → trainee

   **Example:** employ + -ee → employee

   **Source synonyms:** -ed

   **Source antonyms:** -er

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Added to intransitive verbs to form words meaning a person or thing that is the subject of that verb (that is, who or that does an action).

   **Usage:** morpheme; uncommon

   **Example:** absent + -ee → absentee

   **Example:** respond + -ee → respondee

   **Example:** stand + -ee → standee

   **Example:** post + -ee → postee

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Used to form words meaning a person who is the other party to a contract or other transaction involving a person described by the corresponding word ending in -or.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** assign + -ee → assignee

   **Example:** legate + -ee → legatee

   **Example:** mortgage + -ee → mortgagee

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

   **Source antonyms:** -or

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Used to form words meaning a person who has undergone a particular medical procedure.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** laryngectomy + -ee → laryngectomee

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Irregularly added to nouns to mean a person somehow associated with the object denoted by the noun.

   **Usage:** morpheme

   **Example:** barge + -ee → bargee

   **Example:** tender + -ee → tenderee

   **Example:** venereal + -ee → venerealee
