**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The rounded east (liturgical east) end of a church that contains or is behind the altar.

   **Example:** Our private tour of the cathedral even allowed us to walk through the apse, which most tours don't.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture

   **Source synonyms:** [apsis](../261/apsis--985bec083327.md); apside; chancel; presbytery; sanctuary\[Appendix&#58;Glossary

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. The rounded east (liturgical east) end of a church that contains or is behind the altar.
   1. The interior portion of this section of the church building, excluding the ambulatory and any apse chapels.

      **Usage:** strictest sense

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** architecture

      **Source synonyms:** [apsis](../261/apsis--985bec083327.md); apside; chancel; presbytery; sanctuary\[Appendix&#58;Glossary

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A semicircular projection from any building that is similar to a church's apse.

   **Example:** The college has an apse that once held a small chapel.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. The bishop's seat or throne in ancient churches.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A reliquary&#58; a case in which the relics of saints are kept.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. Obsolete form of apsis (“the nearest and furthest points to the centre of gravitational attraction for a body in orbit”).

   **Usage:** alt-of; obsolete
