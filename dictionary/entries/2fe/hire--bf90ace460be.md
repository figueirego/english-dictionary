### Forms

- hiring — participle; present

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To obtain the services of in return for fixed payment.

   **Usage:** Australia; UK; transitive

   **Example:** We hired a car for two weeks because ours had broken down.

   **Source synonyms:** rent

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To occupy premises in exchange for rent.

   **Usage:** UK; transitive

   **Source synonyms:** rent

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To employ; to obtain the services of (a person) in exchange for remuneration; to give someone a job.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The company had problems when it tried to hire more skilled workers.

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To exchange the services of for remuneration.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** They hired themselves out as day laborers. They hired out their basement for Inauguration week.

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To accomplish by paying for services.

   **Usage:** UK; transitive

   **Example:** After waiting two years for her husband to finish the tiling, she decided to hire it done.

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To accept employment.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** They hired out as day laborers.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. (neologism) (in the Jobs-to-be-Done Theory) To buy something in order for it to perform a function, to do a job

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** They hired a milkshake.
