### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. A calendar of the days of the month or of all the days of the year divided by month, particularly as a table of information divided in this way and

   **Example:** The Roman farming menologia show the astrological information and religious festivals for each month along with their matching agricultural activities.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A calendar of the days of the month or of all the days of the year divided by month, particularly as a table of information divided in this way and
   1. Synonym of menaion, a book detailing the propers and hagiographic canons for the fixed dates of the liturgical year.

      **Usage:** capitalized; often; Eastern Orthodoxy

      **Source synonyms:** menaion (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. A calendar of the days of the month or of all the days of the year divided by month, particularly as a table of information divided in this way and
   1. Synonym of synaxarium, a hagiography covering similar material equivalent to a Catholic martyrology.

      **Usage:** capitalized; often; Eastern Orthodoxy

      **Source synonyms:** synaxarium (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. A calendar of the days of the month or of all the days of the year divided by month, particularly as a table of information divided in this way and
   1. Synonym of kalendar, any monthly list of saints' days or liturgical readings.

      **Usage:** Eastern Orthodoxy

      **Source synonyms:** kalendar (synonym; synonym-of); any monthly list of saints' days or liturgical readings (synonym; synonym-of)

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A calendar of the days of the month or of all the days of the year divided by month, particularly as a table of information divided in this way and
   1. A work providing biographies of uncanonized members of a religious order in similar style to a martyrology, frequently read within the order but forbidden for use with the liturgy itself.

      **Usage:** capitalized; often

      **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** Catholicism; Christianity; Roman-Catholicism
