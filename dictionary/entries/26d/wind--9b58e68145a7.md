### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. Real or perceived movement of atmospheric air usually caused by convection or differences in air pressure.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** The wind blew through her hair as she stood on the deck of the ship.

   **Example:** As they accelerated onto the motorway, the wind tore the plywood off the car's roof-rack.

   **Example:** The winds in Chicago are fierce.

   **Example:** There was a sudden gust of wind, on which spores were borne away.

   **Example:** The window was banging in the wind.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Air artificially put in motion by any force or action.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** the wind of a cannon ball

   **Example:** the wind of a bellows

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. The ability to breathe easily.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Example:** After the second lap he was already out of wind.

   **Example:** The fall knocked the wind out of him.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. News of an event, especially by hearsay or gossip.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

   **Example:** to catch wind of something

   **Example:** Steve caught wind of Martha's dalliance with his best friend.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A tendency or trend.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

   **Example:** the wind of change

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. One of the four elements of the ancient Greeks and Romans; air.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** alchemy; human-sciences; philosophy; pseudoscience; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. One of the five basic elements in Indian and Japanese models of the Classical elements.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Flatus.

   **Usage:** colloquial; uncountable

   **Example:** to pass wind

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Breath modulated by the respiratory and vocal organs, or by an instrument.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. The woodwind section of an orchestra. Occasionally also used to include the brass section.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** entertainment; lifestyle; music

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s14"></a>

**Source sense R1.S14**

14. A disease of sheep, in which the intestines are distended with air, or rather affected with a violent inflammation. It occurs immediately after shearing.

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s15"></a>

**Source sense R1.S15**

15. Mere breath or talk; empty effort; idle words.

   **Usage:** countable; figuratively; uncountable

<a id="r1-s16"></a>

**Source sense R1.S16**

   **Usage:** countable; uncountable

<a id="r1-s17"></a>

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To blow air through a wind instrument or horn to make a sound.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To cause (someone) to become breathless, as by a blow to the abdomen, or by physical exertion, running, etc.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The boxer was winded during round two.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To cause a baby to bring up wind by patting its back after being fed.

   **Usage:** UK; transitive

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To turn a boat or ship around, so that the wind strikes it on the opposite side.

   **Usage:** UK; transitive

<a id="r2-s5"></a>

**Source sense R2.S5**

5. To expose to the wind; to winnow; to ventilate.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s6"></a>

**Source sense R2.S6**

6. To perceive or follow by scent.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The hounds winded the game.

<a id="r2-s7"></a>

**Source sense R2.S7**

7. To rest (a horse, etc.) in order to allow the breath to be recovered; to breathe.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r2-s8"></a>

**Source sense R2.S8**

8. To turn a windmill so that its sails face into the wind.

   **Usage:** transitive
