### Senses

   **Example:** He felt a surge of excitement.

   **Source synonyms:** torrent

   **Example:** A power surge at that generator created a blackout across the whole district.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business; electrical-engineering; electricity; electromagnetism; energy; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; physics

4. A momentary reversal of the airflow through the compressor section of a jet engine due to disruption of the airflow entering the engine's air intake, accompanied by loud banging noises, emission of flame, and temporary loss of thrust.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

### Pronunciation

- IPA: /sɜːd͡ʒ/ — source labels: UK

### Forms

- surging — participle; present
- surged — participle; past
- surged — past

### Senses

<a id="r2-s1"></a>

**Source sense R2.S1**

1. To rush, flood, or increase suddenly.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Toaster sales surged last year.

<a id="r2-s2"></a>

**Source sense R2.S2**

2. To accelerate forwards, particularly suddenly.

   **Example:** A ship surges forwards, sways sideways and heaves up.

<a id="r2-s3"></a>

**Source sense R2.S3**

3. To experience a momentary reversal of airflow through the compressor section due to disruption of intake airflow.

   **Usage:** intransitive

   **Example:** Use of maximum reverse thrust at low speeds can cause the engine to surge from ingesting its own exhaust.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** aeronautics; aerospace; aviation; business; engineering; natural-sciences; physical-sciences

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a jet engine

<a id="r2-s4"></a>

**Source sense R2.S4**

4. To slack off a line.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport
