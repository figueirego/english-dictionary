### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To remove something and put it in a different place.

   **Example:** Mother took our plates away and came back with some fruit for us to eat.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To remove something, either material or abstract, so that a person no longer has it.

   **Example:** The teacher took my mobile phone away until the end of the lesson.

   **Example:** The new law will take away some important rights from immigrant residents.

   **Example:** The doctor gave me pills to take away the pain.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To remove something, either material or abstract, so that a person no longer has it.
   1. To remove a person, usually a family member or other close friend or acquaintance, by kidnapping or killing the person.

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To subtract or diminish something.

   **Example:** If I have five apples and you take away two, how many do I have left?

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To leave a memory or impression in one's mind that you think about later.

   **Example:** I took away the impression that the play was under rehearsed.

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To make someone leave a place and go somewhere else. Usually not with the person's consent.

   **Example:** The police took him away for questioning.

   **Example:** I'm taking you away to the country for a rest. It's for your own good!

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a person

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To prevent, or limit, someone from being somewhere, or from doing something.

   **Example:** My job takes me away from home most weekends.

   **Example:** Using the internet so much can take you away from your studies.

   **Source context (verbatim):** of a person
