### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To enjoy, be pleased by; favor; be in favor of.

   **Example:** I like reading books.

   **Example:** I very much like hamburgers.

   **Example:** I like skiing in winter very much.

   **Example:** I liked her much better after I got to know her.

   **Example:** I like it when you sing.

   **Source antonyms:** dislike; hate; mislike; disapprove

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To enjoy, be pleased by; favor; be in favor of.
   1. To please (same meaning as above but with subject and object reversed).

      **Usage:** archaic; transitive

      **Source antonyms:** dislike; hate; mislike; disapprove

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To enjoy, be pleased by; favor; be in favor of.
   1. To derive pleasure \[with of or by or with ‘from someone or something’\].

      **Usage:** obsolete

      **Source antonyms:** dislike; hate; mislike; disapprove

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To prefer and maintain (an action) as a regular habit or activity.

   **Example:** I like to go to the dentist every six months.

   **Example:** She likes to keep herself physically fit.

   **Example:** We like to keep one around the office just in case.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To find attractive; to prefer the company of; to have mild romantic feelings for.

   **Example:** I really like Sandra but don't know how to tell her.

   **Source synonyms:** fancy (British); enjoy; love

   **Source antonyms:** dislike; hate; mislike

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To want, desire. See also would like.

   **Example:** Would you like a cigarette?

   **Example:** We could go to the museum if you like.

   **Example:** May I leave now? ~ If you like.

   **Example:** I don't like to disturb him when he's working.

   **Example:** You can come over whenever you like.

   **Source context (verbatim):** with 'would' and in certain other phrases

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To show support for, or approval of, something posted on the Internet by marking it with a vote.

   **Usage:** Internet; transitive; social media

   **Example:** I liked my friend's last status on Facebook.

   **Example:** I can't stand Bloggs' tomato ketchup, but I liked it on Facebook so I could enter a competition.

   **Source antonyms:** unlike; dislike

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. Of inanimate objects&#58;
   1. To be prone to.

      **Usage:** informal; personification

      **Example:** Too bad this cheese likes to stick to the pan and ruin the whole meal.

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. Of inanimate objects&#58;
   1. Of a computer or other system&#58; to tolerate as an input; to accept.

      **Usage:** informal

      **Example:** This self-service checkout doesn't seem to like my credit card.

      **Example:** We were frustrated that our seeming innocent choice for a team name was rejected by the game. Apparently somewhere in the name is a word that the algorithm doesn’t like.

      **Example:** email delivery failed&#58; remote host does not like recipient

      **Source context (verbatim):** in the negative

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. To come near; to avoid with difficulty; to escape narrowly.

   **Usage:** archaic

   **Example:** He liked to have been too late.

<a id="r1-s11"></a>

**Source sense R1.S11**

11. To have an appearance or expression; to look; to seem to be (in a specified condition).

   **Usage:** obsolete

<a id="r1-s12"></a>

**Source sense R1.S12**

12. To liken; to compare.

   **Usage:** obsolete
