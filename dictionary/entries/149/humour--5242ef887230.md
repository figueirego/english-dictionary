### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. The quality of being amusing, comical, funny.

   **Usage:** UK; uncountable; usually

   **Example:** She has a great sense of humour, and I always laugh a lot whenever we get together.

   **Example:** The sensitive subject was treated with humour, but in such way that no one was offended.

   **Source synonyms:** amusingness; comedy; comicality; wit

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. A mood, especially a bad mood; a temporary state of mind or disposition brought upon by an event; an abrupt illogical inclination or whim.

   **Usage:** UK; uncountable; usually

   **Example:** He was in a particularly vile humour that afternoon.

   **Source synonyms:** mood

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Any of the fluids in an animal body, especially the four "cardinal humours" of blood, yellow bile, black bile and phlegm that were believed to control the health and mood of the human body.

   **Usage:** UK; archaic; historical; uncountable; usually

   **Source synonyms:** bodily fluid

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Either of the two regions of liquid within the eyeball, the aqueous humour and vitreous humour.

   **Usage:** UK; uncountable; usually

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** medicine; sciences

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. Moist vapour, moisture.

   **Usage:** UK; obsolete; uncountable; usually
