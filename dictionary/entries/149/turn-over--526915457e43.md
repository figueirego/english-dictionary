### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To flip over; to rotate uppermost to bottom.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Turn over the box and look at the bottom.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To relinquish; give back.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** They turned over the evidence to the authorities.

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To transfer.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To produce, complete, or cycle through.

   **Usage:** idiomatic; transitive

   **Example:** They can turn over about three hundred units per hour.

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To generate (a certain amount of money from sales).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The business turned over several million last year.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** business

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To mull, ponder

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. To spin the crankshaft of an internal combustion engine using the starter or hand crank in an attempt to make it run.

   **Usage:** intransitive; transitive

<a id="r1-s8"></a>

**Source sense R1.S8**

8. To give up control (of the ball and thus the ability to score).

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** The Giants didn't turn the ball over in their last four games.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** hobbies; lifestyle; sports

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

9. To cause extensive disturbance or disruption to (a room, storage place, etc.), e.g. while searching for an item, or ransacking a property.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I've turned over the whole place, but I still can't find my glasses.

   **Example:** Thieves turned over the apartment while the owners were away on holiday.

<a id="r1-s10"></a>

**Source sense R1.S10**

10. Used other than figuratively or idiomatically&#58; see turn, over.
