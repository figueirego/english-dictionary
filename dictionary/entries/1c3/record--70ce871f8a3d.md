### Forms

### Senses

<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. An item of information put into a temporary or permanent physical medium.

   **Example:** The person had a record of the interview so she could review her notes.

   **Example:** The tourist's photographs and the tape of the police call provide a record of the crime.

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. Any instance of a physical medium on which information was put for the purpose of preserving it and making it available for future reference.

   **Example:** We have no record of you making this payment to us.

   **Example:** My remarks were struck from the record.

   **Source synonyms:** log

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. Ellipsis of phonograph record (“a disc, usually made from vinyl, on which sound is recorded and may be replayed on a phonograph”).

   **Usage:** abbreviation; alt-of; ellipsis

   **Example:** I still like records better than CDs.

   **Source synonyms:** disc; [phonograph record](../051/phonograph-record--144e4c93f20b.md); vinyl

   **Alternative form of:** [phonograph record](../051/phonograph-record--144e4c93f20b.md)

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. Ellipsis of phonograph record (“a disc, usually made from vinyl, on which sound is recorded and may be replayed on a phonograph”).
   1. A music album; broadly, any music released as a single, EP, etc.

      **Usage:** broadly; informal

      **Source synonyms:** disc; [phonograph record](../051/phonograph-record--144e4c93f20b.md); vinyl

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. A set of data relating to a single individual or item.

   **Example:** Pull up the record on John Smith. What's his medical history?

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; sciences

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. A data structure similar to a struct, in some programming languages such as C and Java based on classes and designed for storing immutable data.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** computer; computing; engineering; mathematics; natural-sciences; physical-sciences; science; sciences

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

7. The most extreme known value of some variable, particularly that of an achievement in competitive events.

   **Example:** The heat and humidity were both new records.

   **Example:** Australia set a record of 10 back-to-back T20I wins.

   **Example:** He broke the record for the youngest English captain.

   **Example:** The team set a new record for most points scored in a game.

### Senses

<a id="r3-s1"></a>

**Source sense R3.S1**

1. To make a record of information.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** I wanted to record every detail of what happened, for the benefit of future generations.

<a id="r3-s2"></a>

**Source sense R3.S2**

2. To make an audio or video recording of.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** Within a week they had recorded both the song and the video for it.

   **Source synonyms:** catch

<a id="r3-s3"></a>

**Source sense R3.S3**

3. To give legal status to by making an official public record.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** When the deed was recorded, we officially owned the house.

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** law

<a id="r3-s4"></a>

**Source sense R3.S4**

4. To fix in a medium, usually in a tangible medium.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r3-s5"></a>

**Source sense R3.S5**

5. To make an audio, video, or multimedia recording.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r3-s6"></a>

**Source sense R3.S6**

6. To repeat; to practice.

   **Usage:** intransitive; obsolete; transitive

<a id="r3-s7"></a>

**Source sense R3.S7**

7. To sing or repeat a tune.

   **Usage:** ambitransitive; obsolete

<a id="r3-s8"></a>
