<a id="r1-s1"></a>

**Source sense R1.S1**

1. To retrieve; to bear towards; to go and get.

   **Usage:** ditransitive; transitive

   **Example:** You have to fetch some sugar in order to proceed with the recipe.

   **Example:** I'm thirsty. Can you fetch me a glass of water, please?

<a id="r1-s2"></a>

**Source sense R1.S2**

2. To obtain as price or equivalent; to sell for.

   **Usage:** transitive

   **Example:** If you put some new tyres on it, and clean it up a bit, the car should fetch about $5,000

<a id="r1-s3"></a>

**Source sense R1.S3**

3. To bring or get within reach by going; to reach; to arrive at; to attain; to reach by sailing.

   **Example:** to fetch headway or sternway

   **Source topics (extractor hierarchy; not curated):** nautical; transport

<a id="r1-s4"></a>

**Source sense R1.S4**

4. To bring oneself; to make headway; to veer; as, to fetch about; to fetch to windward.

   **Usage:** intransitive

<a id="r1-s5"></a>

**Source sense R1.S5**

5. To take (a breath); to heave (a sigh).

   **Usage:** literary; rare; transitive

<a id="r1-s6"></a>

**Source sense R1.S6**

6. To cause to come; to bring to a particular state.

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s7"></a>

**Source sense R1.S7**

   **Usage:** obsolete; transitive

   **Example:** to fetch a man to

   **Usage:** transitive

<a id="r1-s9"></a>

**Source sense R1.S9**

   **Usage:** archaic; transitive
